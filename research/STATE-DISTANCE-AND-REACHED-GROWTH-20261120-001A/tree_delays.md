# State distance and the three delays on a tree

On a funded not-all-equal tree, two live knots have a common descendant exactly when their ordinary route has even length and every other knot along it, starting at either queried knot, is live. The delay is then half the ordinary length. Adding one loose distinction at the sender changes a receiver birth one step after the meeting. It changes a matched birth's fresh count at the meeting unless that meeting exhausts a whole finite part. In that exceptional case every matched receiver birth has its original fresh count.

## Which distinctions the states measure

Let T be a finite connected tree of knots. Every ordinary meeting shares a single distinct name, all other places are loose, and each knot v has arity qᵥ≥3. Choose distinct live holders R and S, and a single loose mark x and y in each. Permuting the private marks within either holder preserves its states, so the particular ranks give the same answer. The allowed words on all the holdings receive equal weight. Write ρ for the probability of disagreement and C=1−2ρ. On the unique ordinary route v₀=R,…,vₖ=S,

\[
 C=\prod_{i=0}^k\frac{-1}{2^{q_{v_i}-1}-1},\qquad
 D_\tau=-\log_3|C|-1=\sum_{i=0}^k\log_3(2^{q_{v_i}-1}-1)-1.
\]

Deleting an ordinary meeting separates the holdings into two independent completions conditioned on its bit. Complementing all bits gives the same number of completions at either value of a boundary bit. Thus branches off the route contribute a constant, and conditional on one incoming bit the local allowed words are uniform. Of the `2^(q−1)−1` allowed assignments to the remaining places, `2^(q−2)` have the chosen outgoing bit opposite and `2^(q−2)−1` have it equal. Multiplying these conditional signs gives the product, including the two endpoint holders. In particular q=3 throughout gives Dτ=k. Its unshifted predecessor counts k+1 transfers *between distinctions*. With variable arity the weights change. In a tree ρ<1/2 forces an odd k and therefore no ordinary meeting. For even k the loose supply still matters. These are statements about the frozen marked reader. The earlier disagreement theorem already proves the three-place mark formula. [Original disagreement ruler](../DISTANCE-ORDER-20261117-001/distance_order.md)

For parity on this tree the two private bits are independent, so ρ=1/2. Indeed, in the sum of any old parity rows supported on loose names alone, the selected holders cannot have a shared name meeting an unselected one. Thus the only nonzero such row in a connected start contains *all* loose names. There are at least `∑q−2(|T|−1)≥|T|+2` of them. For the frozen canonical bond, wire all loose names to one exterior holder for the calculation. A minimal parity support is exactly a cut with connected sides. A support containing the two chosen loose names has a connected side excluding the exterior and containing the full R–S route. The route has cut size `∑route q−2k`; every component of its complement contains an original leaf with a loose name, and hence reaches the exterior. Adding an off-route point increases the cut by q−2≥1, so the route is the unique minimizing side. It gives

\[
 D_{\rm bond}=\sum_{i=0}^k q_{v_i}-2k-1.
\]

For three-place holders D_bond=k+2. The offset of two is a consequence of using one private mark in each holder. The algebraic two-name cut that can occur at a later non-tree stage is excluded by the canonical reading. [Parity bonds](../BOND-CLOSURE-20261120-001/decision.md)

## Common descent

Let H be the witnessed graph on the **live** holders of this cut. A step of H covers exactly two ordinary meetings through any single intervening holder, live or spent. A path in H therefore gives an even walk in T with a live holder at each even-numbered position. Every walk R to S in T visits every holder on the unique route, and the parity of a visit is fixed by the tree's two classes. Thus k must be even and v₀,v₂,…,vₖ all live. Conversely those holders give a shortest H path of length d=k/2. No other path of that length is possible because the corresponding `2d` ordinary meetings exhaust the shortest ordinary route. If the tests fail set d=∞.

All pairs in H are covered by actual co-inputs on a funded cut. On a tree every pairwise-witnessed set of three has one common physical witness. This follows from the unique routes between three mutually distance-two holders. The same common holder sees all members of a larger such set. Thus every initial candidate pair makes its own binary. At the next cut two births are witnessed together exactly when their actual parent sets intersect. Every live parent has a child. If I takes all children of a set and P all parents, `PI` and `IP` give the original and next closed one-step neighborhoods respectively. Consequently the distance between all descendants of R and all descendants of S drops by exactly one per continuing cut until zero. At infinity no later common birth exists. This is the previous exact meeting theorem, here evaluated in the physical tree. An initial global funding veto gives zero births and ends the comparison there. Under not-all-equal a funded nonempty plan pays the next plan and can continue indefinitely. Under parity a sole closing birth is counted if it is the first common child. [Proved ordinary ancestry formula](../COPIES-AND-MEETINGS-20261120-001/propagation.md) · [Whole-cut funding and closing](../ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/end_operator.md)

A fully funded tree made only of three-place knots can have finite d>0 only at k=2. If a shortest route had k≥4, the live knot v₂ would require two distinct binary groups, to v₀ and v₄. It already has two or more ordinary shared distinctions, leaving at most one loose. So on this restricted class the scalar function of Dτ is one at 2 and infinity at every other positive realized value. Adjacent distinct holders give the smallest near/never discrepancy. Two three-place holders sharing once have five names, two loose each, Dτ=1, D_bond=3 and ρ=4/9. They have no witnessed pair. Their two families keep growing separately. This is minimal in number of queried original holders and, within the specified simple-tree class, in number of names at that size. The older four-knot comparison had already shown the separation of direct contact from common descent. [Earlier four-knot comparison](../ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/rulers.md)

Two fully funded all-live paths give another exact scope test. Give their holders arities (3,7,3) and (3,3,4,3,3) and query their endpoints. Because `63=3²×7`, the two marked correlations equal −1/567 and both disagreement probabilities equal 284/567. Ordinary first common descendants arrive at steps one and two respectively. Thus on variable-arity trees even two queries within one witnessed part can have equal marked state distance and different actual delays.

## Exact actual response to one added loose distinction

Start on a funded not-all-equal tree, add one loose distinction at the already live S and keep everything else. Both preparations have identical initial witnesses. Write h(v) for its old loose supply, a(v) for the number of binary groups through v, and s(v)=h(v)−a(v). At d>0 finite write G for the d+1 **original live** knots on the unique shortest witnessed path. Define the integer

\[
 b=|N_H(G)\setminus G|+\sum_{v\in G}s(v).
\]

This counts distinct one-point extensions before any special continuation of a completed part. The three delays, in the order common descendant, first changed fresh count on a matched birth, first unmatched receiver birth, are

| Initial condition | Delays |
| --- | --- |
| d=∞ | ∞, ∞, ∞ |
| 0<d<∞ and b>0 | d, d, d+1 |
| 0<d<∞ and b=0 | d, ∞, d+1 |

The diagonal is separately defined. A holder is its own ancestor at cut zero. The +1 changes its original loose count immediately and adds a unary among its next births. A changed fresh *at birth* is a later observation on that comparison.

**Why the identification applies.** A tree has no pair of distinct common witnesses and no initial unheld triple. The first cut is funded by hypothesis and at least two original loose names exist. The relayed identification therefore applies. On a tree it has no quotient identifications. Its initial points are the live originals, with the live neighbors of each physical witness in one block, and s(v) private surplus points adjacent to v. The continuation attached to each such point has zero, one or two chains according as h(v) is at least three, two or one. A birth at age t has a connected set of t+1 points containing an original, true parents given by its permitted one-point deletions, and fresh count equal to the number of allowed one-point extensions. A finished finite part receives one relay even when it is the last part under not-all-equal. A relay can be added only to a finished part. This last feature matters for a small counterexample below. [Identification with spent knots](../../proofs/DOUBLE-IDENT-20261119-001A/DOUBLE-IDENT-20261119-001A-P001_doubles/proof.md) · [Finished finite parts](../RELAY-IDENT-20261119-001/relay_ident.md) · [Not-all-equal continuation](../NAE-GROWTH-20261119-001/nae_growth.md)

Only a birth containing both R and S can be their common descendant. Before a relay, membership implies ancestry since one can repeatedly delete points outside a chosen connected subcollection containing the desired original. Conversely a deletion cannot introduce a missing original. A completed relay retains all originals of its part. At age d the only possible common birth is G. Every intermediate cluster on that shortest route is unchanged in both arms and its true parents match. G has at least two distinct originals, hence its parents include deleting either end. Its ordinary one-point extensions are its outside original neighbors and a point for every spare of its members, exactly b. In the edited arm there is one more spare at S. Its possible continuations and changes to the chains beyond older surplus points affect no first extension of G. If b>0 the matched G has prices b and b+1. No other matched R-descendant can change price earlier. No receiver birth can be unmatched until the following cut, by the existing matched-edit theorem. If the two marked holders are in separate parts this theorem protects every complete cut. Under not-all-equal the finite-part relay is independent of any remote part. [Full-parent matching and the sharp earliest bounds](../INFLUENCE-CONE-20261120-001/fixed_edits.md)

There is a direct proof for the changed birth that also works without acyclicity, on funded continuing cuts at finite d>0. In the edited arm one extra unary u is born from S at step one with no matched copy. None of the step-one children of R equals u. Their combined true parents comprise the closed neighborhood B(R), because every co-input pair is covered. In the child graph, two disjoint birth sets have distance one plus the minimum distance between their old parent sets: a shortest route between old parents lifts using co-input coverage, and the shared parents along any new route project back. Thus u is distance `1+dist(S,B(R))=d` from the full step-one children of R. Their first common descendant follows d further steps. It has u in its recursively matched history, so it is an unmatched receiver birth at d+1. The matched-edit protection rules out anything earlier. Counting the final closing child requires the stated stages actually to exist. This argument uses the supplied +1 and the earlier true-parent distance identity.

The extra extension of G by the new spare z has G as a parent and also the parent obtained by deleting R from G∪{z}. The latter contains S and z. It descends from the new unary at S and has no recursively matched counterpart. This is an actual unmatched receiver birth at d+1, including when the old arm continues through a different relay at that cut.

If b=0, G exhausts its whole original part, with no spare. The part is exactly the path G. Both endpoints have witnessed degree and supply one, and each internal holder has supply two. The old top G therefore gets the single fresh distinction for its relay. The edit gives S supply two, with one surplus point and one endless chain extending it. The edited G also gets one fresh distinction. The receiver has a single descendant at age d in each arm and precisely one at d+1. In the old arm the latter takes one input from G. In the edited arm it has the two distinct parents just described. It follows that **no receiver-descended birth from d+1 onward can match recursively**. Each has a receiver-descended parent and no such parent has a counterpart at the previous step. The first changed matched price is therefore infinite in this case. The receiver's unlabelled total fresh count does change at d+1, from two to one. It reads different actual births.

As a control, take the R and S roles at the two ends of the physical arities (3,4,3). Their witnessed distance is one and their first common birth changes from two to three fresh. Add one terminal three-place holder at either end, converting one previous private name at each end into a shared name, and retain arities (3,3,4,3,3). Use the same original three roles with R and S now at positions one and three. Their marked ρ=32/63 and D_bond=5 agree in both starts. Here the fresh count of the only matched receiver birth remains one. The next receiver births have one and two parents respectively. The ordinary parent matcher detects the first unmatched birth at age two in both preparations. These are lawful, funded examples under both laws. ## Other edits and global observations

For a general change of loose counts at already live holders, the fixed-witness protection gives lower bounds d and d+1. For an edit changing witnessed holders, the initial charge includes **all** participants in every changed witnessed set. If erasing S gives the same outside data in both preparations, measure d on the union of their two cuts. The sharp funded nonclosing bounds at d≥2 are d−1 and d. The held multiplicity comparison can leave the entire end and every future matched birth unchanged. In the replay at d=3 the altered witness first changes a receiver price at 2, before the first common descendant at 3, and its roster differs at 3. That construction has ordinary cycles and supplies sharpness for the general theorem. A distance alone cannot give the actual edit-specific response, even on the two acyclic examples with exactly the same pair reading above.

A stage-wide veto is decided before any eligible birth is made. On the seven-holder odd-cycle control the deficient preparation cancels the entire first plan in both laws. The other preparation grows. At a parity terminal, a remote private opening can instead change a formed solitary birth from zero to one fresh. The same two preparations under not-all-equal give one in both. Separate the genuine common birth, its price, a completely absent cut and a comparison whose named participant is inactive. All such controls have complete physical receipts.
