# Referee report on whether the two countings factor over knots
**Refuted.** **Block** on factorization over canonical knots and on the STOP inferred from it. Both countings factor over the listed rules of a fixed growth history. The study skipped any pair held by a listed rule. Two of its own sampled parity starts contain a pair that this screen skips, although no knot holds it in the whole structure and its weighting has rank two.

## A. Two tests on the same sample

I rebuilt growth and all three weightings from the census of 7,075 starts. The sample contains 300 distinct entries with equal sampling weight, seed 20261129. A history needed a funded round and at most fourteen distinctions after up to two rounds. In ordinary, one-fewer and arity-floor order, parity retained 69, 70 and 70 histories, and not-all-equal retained 17, 21 and 21. These are 268 start/law/arm combinations. A table fixes every distinction except the tested pair. Forbidden states have weight zero.

The second sampled start (census index 3480) has five even-parity rules. Their supports are

A=(0,1,2,3,4),\quad B=(0,5,6,7,8),\quad C=(1,2,5,6,9,10),\quad D=(3,4,7,8),\quad E=(9,10,11).

The ordinary arm adds the implied rule fixing distinction 11 to zero and then closes. Summing \(A,B,C,D\) gives the equality of distinctions 9 and 10. **Fix 2 · Narrowing — what growth supplies.** The decision says “The growth law divides weight birth by birth, so it builds exactly such a product: separated readers in the model are classical because its weightings are local.” The defining study explicitly supplies both countings, the uniform weighting of starting states and equal division. Growth supplies the history. The separability argument requires factorable **amplitudes**, as given by the positive square roots of a product weighting. Uniform probabilities also admit the amplitude matrix \(\tfrac12\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), which has rank two. That sign is an additional choice. In the five-rule witness, the equal-positive encoding itself gives \((|00\rangle+|11\rangle)/\sqrt2\) on 9 and 10, independently of the unread remainder. A shared fair bit already reproduces their value readings. A game also needs a supplied preparation and specified reads. [Decision](../decision.md#L41) · [Origin of the countings](../../TWO-COUNTINGS-20261125-001/decision.md#L92) · [Predecessor's amplitude argument](../../THIRD-READER-20261126-001/referee-R1/report.md#L8)

For a birth \(b\), let \(L_b(o,f)\) be its law indicator on its old inputs and fresh distinctions, and \(c_b(o)=\sum_f L_b(o,f)\). On one history every birth in a round reads old names and has private fresh names. The joint completion count is \(\prod_b c_b(o)\), checked against enumeration on **97,002** old-state occurrences. Counting from the finish multiplies all listed law indicators. Counting from the start multiplies the starting law indicators and one \(L_b/c_b\) per birth, defining that factor as zero when \(c_b=0\). A cut birth with nothing fresh thus contributes its law indicator. Division by the surviving mass is one constant per nonempty history. Each cut arm lost weight on one retained parity history and five not-all-equal histories. Fixing an entire starting assignment contributes one indicator per distinction. Uniform weighting on **allowed** starting states uses the start's rules and can already correlate distinctions. Pooling histories or giving simultaneous births a shared fresh distinction falls outside this argument. Waiting preserves the listed-rule argument. The executing engine on a seven-rule source joined to a ring of five recorded 222 births and 66 waits over four rounds under each of two rations. Each input existed before its round, and all 499 fresh names had one initial holder. The recorded one-fewer waiting rule can allocate zero fresh distinctions to a non-implied birth. On three not-all-equal rules with supports \((0,2,3),(0,1,4),(1,2,5)\) it adds a rule on the three loose distinctions \((3,4,5)\), keeping 18 of 26 old states. Renormalisation applies. The corresponding waiting-law census replay retained 23 histories, five losing weight and three others with actual waiting. Its 1,548,540 occupied listed-rule tables gave zero failures for either counting. Under the waiting ration capped at three, the parity histories reproduce the two canonical counterexamples. ## B. The five facts on three rings of four

The rebuild constructed the rings, their births and the five fact carriers, then computed \(e\), the number of independent shared pairs for the chosen treatment of the middle. The frozen rounds were **1, 4, 5 and 6**. For each of 31 nonempty selections of facts I fixed their sum to zero and checked all three pairs of ring readers. Of the **372** restriction/pair cases, all 372 gave zero with the middle unread, and all 372 gave zero with values read. With changes read, **260 gave one and 112 gave two**. Fixing the third ring's whole fact raised \(e\) from one to two in all **12/12** relevant comparisons. No ring-local change flips the whole-ring facts \(R,S,T\). A first-ring change can flip its alternating facts \(P,Q\) together, affecting 16 selections. Neither other ring can flip any. The local change ranks per ring were 16, 46, 60 and 76 at the four frozen rounds. All 324 saved stage fields and 16 control fields matched. The same conclusions held in additional rounds 2, 3 and 7. **Fix 4 · Narrowing — the proof for sums and the restriction that raises \(e\).** The README says “Restricted to any of the 31 sums of the five facts, **e = 0 for every pair of ring readers with the middle unread or read for values**: each fact has a representative on one ring, so the restriction is one more knot missing a reader, and the separability argument applies unchanged (the advisor's shorter proof).” The equations \(R+S+T=0\) and \(P+Q+R=0\) give eight classes of sums. Three nonempty selections are identically zero. Only 23/31 selections admit a single-ring representative, counting those three. The other eight represent \(P+S=Q+T\) or \(P+T=Q+S\). For **each chosen reader pair**, a representative avoids at least one reader. Its added indicator therefore preserves conditional separation for that pair. This argument needs no K1–K3 claim about the added rule. The decision says “Restricting to a bound whole-held fact leaves ring readers separated with the middle unread or read for values (e = 0 on every restriction, rounds 1 to 6); with the middle read for changes it raises e from 1 to 2 for the other two rings, still sorted.” Specify the four frozen rounds and the *third ring's whole fact* for that increase. ## Counterexample check, prior results and disposition

**Fix 8 · Not a block — the shared-fresh example in the advisory note.** The note says of the two setting rules sharing a fresh distinction on the eleven-rule start, “At z = 1 the weights on (x, y) each in {00, unequal} are 1, 1, 1, 1/2, and 1 · 1/2 ≠ 1 · 1.” This table holds with otherwise free old inputs. Across all four settings, the local factor times the law indicators matched recursive division in all 512 comparisons. The four finished state counts were 64, 80, 64 and 64. **Prior results.** I found no claim of novelty for the literature's Markov random field implication or for graph-state separability. At fixed unread values \(u\), a product of rule factors meeting at most one reader apiece groups into one factor per reader. For amplitudes this gives a product vector at fixed \(u\). Tracing out \(u\) gives a mixture of those products, which can still share value information. This is the forward implication and allows zeros. Hein, Eisert and Briegel's Proposition 3 gives the graph-state partial trace. The predecessor also gives the direct argument for general product amplitudes. The study contributes measurements on specified growth histories and reader choices. The counterexample changes which rules meet the mathematical hypotheses. [Study's attribution](../decision.md#L41) · [Predecessor's attribution](../../THIRD-READER-20261126-001/README.md#L33) · [Geiger, Meek and Sturmfels](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608054) · [Hein, Eisert and Briegel, Proposition 3](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.69.062311)
