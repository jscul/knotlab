# Maximum junction size along one start

Let \(p\) be the number of parents of a newly born junction and \(f\) the number of its fresh distinctions. Its size is \(p+f\); later rounds never alter its support. In a lawful complete round every old loose distinction is taken, in a group or a one-parent offshoot. When a next complete round occurs, every row that was live becomes exhausted. If all newly born rows have size at most M, every live and exhausted row is bounded by the larger of M and the starting maximum.

For the order-three partnered start of WALL-READ let \(N_t\) be the number born in round \(t\), \(m_t=N_t/2\). Its accepted recurrence is \(N_0=6,\ N_1=8,\ N_{t+1}=2^{m_t}-N_{t-1}\). At \(t\ge1\) the junctions all have \(p=m_{t-1}\) parents and \(f=2^{m_t-1}-m_{t-1}\) fresh distinctions. **Every one therefore holds exactly \(2^{m_t-1}\) distinctions.** This is 8, 16, 2,048 and \(2^{2042}\) at the first four positive rounds. Each row exhausts at the next round. WALL-READ proves that \(m_t\) increases without bound and every round is lawful, so one finite connected start has unbounded largest exhausted junction. The earlier test's finite observation about size eight does not assert a universal bound. There is also an all-age bounded class. Take \(b\ge3\) live points with one common block, one open distinction per required pair with the other \(b-1\) points, and no leftover open distinctions. This is the binomial tail of a single-block burst. At the next stage the points are exactly the 2-subsets of the original \(b\). At subsequent tail levels they are the \(k\)-subsets for \(2\le k\le b\). A \(k\)-subset has \(k\) parents given by deletion of one of its points. It occurs in \(b-k\) succeeding subsets. Before the last level this is at least one and meets the arity floor, so the cheapest law supplies \(b-k\) fresh distinctions. At the last level the sole row is implied and takes all loose distinctions, with zero fresh distinctions. Thus **every junction newly made in this tail has exactly \(b\) distinctions**, also when its round population is \(\binom bk\). Counting the finite prefix gives the all-age bound \(\max(M_{\rm prefix},b)\). MERGE-BURST's named finite bursts have \(b=9\) and \(781\). Their pre-tail rows may be larger.
