Setting. Structures: two-valued distinctions; knots hold 3 or 4 of them under a law (even parity, odd parity, or not-all-equal). A distinction held by one knot is loose; by two or more, shared. Growth rule Q, per round: a knot with a loose distinction is live; two live knots that some one knot meets form a group and make a joint birth; a live knot's remaining loose distinction gives a one-parent birth, which takes that loose distinction AND one distinction its parent shares (the rule does not say which), and gets max(D, 1) fresh distinctions (D = what the next round will ask of it; 1 in the case below). Every newborn is not-all-equal. Births never depend on values.

Bell-type reading used so far (supplied): each observer has three distinctions; "setting a" adds a not-all-equal knot on the first and the (2+a)-th with one fresh distinction; every allowed word of the finished structure counts equally; "hidden excess" = CHSH above 2 with each observer's complete record (its three distinctions and its fresh one) having exactly the same distribution for either far setting. Until now the setting knots were added by hand, and the ceiling for this weighting at one birth per setting is 20/9 (proved).

Claim (found by hand, exploratory). An 11-knot structure:
Alice: even(x1,x2,x3,c), nae(c,x2,s1), nae(c,x3,s2), even(x2,h1,h2), odd(x3,h1,h2).
Bob: even(y1,y2,y3,t), even(t,m1,m2), even(s1,m1,m2), even(y2,k1,k2), odd(y3,k1,k2), odd(s2,k1,k2).
Only x1 and y1 are loose; their holders have no common neighbour. So one round of rule Q makes exactly two births, both one-parent: one takes x1 and one of {x2, x3, c}, the other y1 and one of {y2, y3, t}, each with one fresh distinction. Reading "which shared distinction the birth takes" as the setting: for options (x2, x3) against (y2, y3) each of the four stage-1 structures has 144 allowed words, both complete records are hidden, and the largest CHSH over all outcome functions of the records is exactly 20/9; the other eight pairs of option pairs give 19/9 or 37/18, all hidden. Checked by enumeration with code separate from the recorded scorer. Five distinctions (x2, x3, c, k1, k2) have three holders. So nothing is added by hand any more: the two "setting births" are the births the rule makes, and the setting is the rule's open choice.

Proposed frozen study. (a) An independent implementer re-derives round 1 under rule Q and enumerates the four (and the nine, and the 3x3) stage-1 structures: hidden, score. (b) Controls: ordinary growth (no re-read: no choice exists); parity newborns (no re-weighting); the same structure with Bob's first knot holding s1 directly (then one knot meets both live knots and the rule makes one joint birth). (c) Smallest: a census of pairs of sides in which each observer's knot holds its loose distinction and two shared ones, sides of up to 4 knots (5 where feasible), with the conditions "exactly two loose distinctions" and "no knot meets both live knots". (d) Rounds 2 and 3 under rule Q from each stage-1 structure: is the table on the same records still hidden and above 2? Predictions: (a) replicates; hiding holds for the 3x3; no structure with at most two holders per distinction works; none with fewer than 10 knots; the excess is kept through round 3.
