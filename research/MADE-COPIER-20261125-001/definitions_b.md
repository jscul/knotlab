# The copier study, ticket B: definitions for the owner's second addition

Epic `MADE-COPIER-20261125-001`, ticket `MADE-COPIER-20261125-001B`. These definitions ("DB section.item") extend
[ticket A's definitions](definitions.md) ("D section.item"), and through them [the fold study's
definitions](../PARTS-MEET-AGAIN-20261124-001/definitions.md) ("FD") and [the shared
definitions](../RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001/definitions.md) ("SD line n"). They change nothing in D, FD or SD. They
serve [ticket B's claim](claim_b.md) and [its pre-registration](../MADE-COPIER-20261125-001B/preregistration.md) ("PB").
The starts are in [starts_b.json](../MADE-COPIER-20261125-001B/starts_b.json), the round-0 rosters in
[rosters_b.json](../MADE-COPIER-20261125-001B/rosters_b.json). The canonical K1–K3 test is unchanged. Versions 1 and 2 of
this text are filed in the advisor folder as sent; where a reading changed, the change is said where it is made.

## 1. The laws

1. **Arm 1, no taking rule.** The six base laws COPY-LAW(t) (the fold study's name for (FLOOR1, t, RELEASE), not a law
   of copying) and CAP-3(t), t = CANCEL-RETIRE, CANCEL-OFFSHOOT, CANCEL-WAIT (D 1.4), with RELEASE and no taking rule:
   one complete round as SD lines 34–75 with FD 2.1's order of births and names. No choice rule is used. With no taking
   rule a birth's distinct inputs are its parents, so D's FBP(t) is COPY-LAW(t) here (PB, Settled 2); version 1 counted
   it as a third base law.
2. **Arm 2, MET.** MET (D 1.3) under CAP-3(t) and FBP(t) (D 1.6), with each of D's four stated rules (D 1.7), on ticket
   A's designs (D 2.2) with each design's own s, and on the starts of DB 2. MET under COPY-LAW(t) is one check row (PB):
   by ticket A's S4 nothing of the classes can be left from both there. **Arm 2 gives the numbering back**: takes choose
   by name, so every case of arm 2 must survive the renamings registered for its design: D 4.5's for ticket A's designs;
   for a start of DB 2, D 4.5's n1, n2 and n4, with `design` the start's name in starts_b.json.
3. Knot laws: parity with IMPLIED, and not-all-equal; every run under both.
4. A law is fixed for a whole run and never changed for a pattern. Nothing here adds a rule for copying, or for ending a
   pattern.

## 2. The starts of arm 1

1. **Parts.** A part of kind k is, for k one of D 2.2's six kinds, D 2.2's dead knot z and members of that kind, with the
   shared distinction e (label 1000); for **pairs-3** and **pairs-4**, three or four members each holding two loose
   distinctions, with one dead knot for each consecutive pair (in the order listed) holding two places of each of the
   two, the first of these dead knots also holding e; its maximal met sets are the consecutive pairs. In a start, X's
   part has kind i and labels from 0, Y's part kind j and labels from 100 (starts_b.json, "parts"). Both first dead knots
   hold e, so without the start's meeting X and Y are wholly apart. **X** = X's members, **Y** = Y's members; s = 0.
2. **The first member** of a part: D 2.2's first member of that kind (for two-23 the member holding two loose
   distinctions); for pairs-3 and pairs-4 the first member listed, which is in one met set. A part the same as one-2 is a
   lone member holding two usable loose distinctions, k = 0.
3. **One part at the start: the start's meeting** (each adds new shared places, labels from 501 in the order listed; a
   new place is shared by exactly the two knots named, so no member gains a loose distinction):
   - **W1**: a new dead knot, **the start's common knot of X and Y**, holding two new places of X's first member and two of
     Y's first member;
   - **W2**: X's first dead knot (the one holding e) also holds two new places of Y's first member;
   - **W3**: Y's first dead knot also holds two new places of X's first member;
   - **W4**: a new dead knot holding two new places of every member of X and of Y.
   **The other-member swap**: W1 to W3 with the member used being, for two-23 the member holding three, for pairs-3 and
   pairs-4 the second member listed (each carried to the first member by no renaming of the start roster onto itself).
   Under W1 both members are exchanged when both kinds have one; W2 uses Y's member only and W3 X's only. For the other
   kinds every member is carried to the first member by a renaming of the start roster onto itself (one-2 and one-3 have
   one member), so the swap has no run (Settled 1 and 3). Version 1's "last member" was so carried for every kind but
   two-23, and was the same run.
4. The 256 starts (eight kinds of X × eight of Y × W1 to W4) and the 87 starts of the other-member swap (W1 to W3
   wherever the meeting uses a member of two-23, pairs-3 or pairs-4: 39 under W1, 24 under W2, 24 under W3) are listed in
   starts_b.json. **The control**: Y of X's own kind.
5. **The marked roster** of a start, or of a run after a round: its roster (SD lines 14–30: members with h and k, and
   the inclusion-maximal met sets) with each member marked by its descent (X only, Y only, both, neither); two marked
   rosters are **the same** when a renaming of members carries h, k, the marks and the maximal met sets of one onto the
   other. rosters_b.json lists, for each start, the class of its round-0 roster and of its marked round-0 roster, and
   the kinds whose alone start has the same roster.

## 3. Descent and the readings

0. **On ticket A's designs** (arm 2): read "round 0" as "round s" and "t ≥ 1" as "t > s"; Y's form is the part Y's
   members form in Y alone (D 4.6) after round s; Y′ as the second pattern is D 4.4's run; the class is the design's own
   kinds (D 2.2); there is no start's meeting.
1. **Descent** is D 2.6 with the design's s (s = 0 in arm 1): the knots descended from both, from X only, from Y only;
   the knots descended from Y only include Y's members. After the first births with a parent descended from X only and
   one from Y only, every birth with such a parent among its parents is descended from both, so descent from both says
   little by itself.
2. **The first joint births**: births with one parent descended from X only and one from Y only.
3. **Y's form**: the part Y's members form in **Y alone** at round 0 (Y's part, all its dead knots, e barred): h, k and
   inclusion-maximal met sets. **X alone** and X's form likewise, from X's part.
4. **Two parts the same as Y, each descended from both**: after a round t ≥ 1, at least two parts of the roster each
   the same as Y's form (SD lines 29–30) and each with every member descended from both. Parts of Y's form with members
   of several descents are reported apart as **mixed**.
5. **The round t of a case**: the first round t after which there are two parts the same as Y, each descended from both,
   and the knots descended from Y only have all ended (SD line 91, read for that set of knots; FD 1.1 keeps them ended).
   With it is reported: **exactly two** (the number of such parts at t is two); **the other parts** at t (their number,
   and the class forms among them by descent); **how the knots descended from Y only ended** (each by giving every usable
   loose distinction to births, by being barred when it retired, or by giving its last to a birth that closed); whether
   the knots descended from X only have ended; whether **X returns** (SD line 85: a part the same as X's form, every member
   descended from X only) or a part of X's form descended from both is left. The first round with two parts the same as
   Y each descended from both is reported even when the knots descended from Y only have not ended by then.
6. **The paired runs** (D 4, with s = 0 in arm 1): run A; **B_X**, X barred after round 0 (D 4.3); **B(i)**, Y barred
   after round 0 (D 4.2), reported; **Y alone**; **X alone**; **Y′ as the second pattern**: the start with Y's part
   replaced by a part of kind j′, the same X's part and the same start's meeting (the 256 starts include every one).
7. **The conditions**, each read at every round t ≥ 1 through the last round read, with parts of any descent:
   - **(a)** in B_X, fewer than two parts the same as Y's form;
   - **(b)** in Y alone, fewer than two parts the same as Y's form;
   - **(c)** with each member Y′ of the class other than Y and other than the control as the second pattern, fewer than
     two parts the same as Y's form; the control's run is reported apart;
   - **(d)** in X alone, fewer than two parts the same as Y's form.
8. **The last round read** T: the last round every run the reading names reached (D 7.1's rule). A comparison run that
   stops before run A's round t is reported "not compared" at the rounds it did not reach. The round t of a case is
   sought through T only; a first such round after T is reported "not compared", and the prediction's verdict for that
   start is "undecided past round T".
9. **Bounds**: 8 rounds, 10 under CAP-3(t); a run stops after the first round past 2,000 knots; the fold engine's guard
   limits (`pm.GUARD`) multiplied by ten in this study's engine, set at import, the fold engine's file unedited.

## 4. The two readings of the claim

1. **CB-1, the second pattern with the first** (his sentence read literally; the literature's autocatalysis reading,
   PB): under one law (arm 1: a base law, no taking rule, a knot law; arm 2: a base law, MET, a stated rule, a knot law),
   one start's meeting (arm 1) and one pair X, Y, run A has a round t (DB 3.5), and (a), (b) and (d) hold; in arm 2 the
   case also survives every renaming registered for its design (DB 1.2).
2. **CB-2, the first pattern for the class** (the thread's stricter reading): one X, law and start's meeting such that
   CB-1 holds for every member Y of the class other than the control, and (c) holds for each.
3. **Some members and not all**: CB-1 with (c) holds for at least one member and not for every member, the same X, law
   and start's meeting.
4. **By argument or by census**: each failure of CB-1 or CB-2 is reported as **by argument** when PB's settled statements
   (Settled 4 or 5) exclude it, and **by census** otherwise. A failure by census is reported with the clause that failed
   (no round with two; the knots descended from Y only not ended; (a); (b); (c); (d)). A failure on (b) alone is a fact
   about the law and Y, not about X. Under W1 to W3, and under W4 for every Y but pairs-3 and pairs-4, (a) is the same
   condition as (b) (PB, Settled 6), and (d) is a fact about the law and X alone; so a failure whose failed clauses are
   only (b), (d) and (a) where (a) is (b) counts as a failure on (b) alone. Failures on (b) alone, and failures by
   argument, are not counted as bearing on the owner's inkling (A248).

## 6. Verdicts: three questions

Asked of every case:
- **Who made the two**: descended from both, by definition; reported with the first joint births they descend from (the
  round and the parents).
- **What fixed any takes**: in arm 1 nothing (no birth takes a shared distinction, and Settled 1 says the case cannot
  depend on the numbering); in arm 2 a stated rule whose case survives every registered renaming, or one whose case does
  not.
- **Where X, Y and their meeting come from**: in arm 1 X and Y are marks on one start roster and **the meeting is the
  start's**; in arm 2 as D 5.2.
Arm 1 is a census of marked start rosters: no case of it can be made, and the texts say so. A case of arm 2 is made only
when all three answers are the growth's.

## 7. The renaming statement (argued in PB, Settled 1)

Under a law with no taking rule (DB 1.1, with RELEASE or HOLD, either knot law), for a start S and any renaming σ of its
knots and distinctions, after each round t there is a renaming σ_t carrying the structure grown from S onto the
structure grown from σ(S), such that σ_t agrees with σ on the start's knots and on the distinctions shared or barred at
the start, and on each member's usable loose distinctions agrees with σ only up to a permutation of that member's usable
loose distinctions. The bounds on rounds and knots and the guard are counts, so σ(S) stops when S does. Every reading of
D and DB defined up to renaming (forms, descent, the conditions, the knot test, the counts) is then the same for S and
σ(S). **Version 1's wording**, "σ_t agrees with σ on the start's knots and distinctions", is false: a start member holding
loose a before b makes one join and one offshoot; σ exchanges the names of a and b; in S the join takes a, in σ(S) it
takes σ(b), so σ_1 carries a to σ(b). Nothing in ticket B uses a start distinction's name, so no reading changes; a
values reading would need the repaired wording. Under a taking rule the statement fails in general.
