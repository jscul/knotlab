# Recovering not-all-equal holdings from the complete states

**Standard statement.** Fix a finite carrier of named distinctions. Consider connected presentations in which each distinction has one or two different holders, each holder contains at least three distinct names, each holder has the full local not-all-equal relation, and erasing any one rule strictly enlarges the allowed set. Two such presentations with the same named allowed words have the same holdings, except at one six-distinction relation up to renaming. Both presentations of that relation are closed. Under the supplied ordinary growth rule the words therefore determine every later birth, with the usual freedom to rename fresh siblings and exchange equivalent loose distinctions.

**Nearest known result.** The earlier study proved state recovery under a sufficient condition on mutual meetings and exhausted the lawful presentations through nine distinctions. It exhibited the closed six-name witness. Reconstruction from known co-held pairs also has a classical antecedent. The advance here is recovering those pairs from allowed words across arbitrary repeated meetings, then classifying every connected ambiguity using only those data. ## Argument

1. For distinct names x and y, pin every other name to the same exterior word and seek allowed values 01, 10 and 11 with 00 forbidden. This test reads exactly the pairs that have a holder in common. For necessity, both names must belong to every rule violated at 00. For sufficiency, erase their holder, fix its other names to zero and use essentiality and full local projection to extend all four patterns. If x and y share their remaining holder, an extension of 11 also permits either single change. Otherwise shorten their separate remaining holders. Any contradiction from a forced value still contradicts one original extension, because each shortened holder can force a value only once. After all such implications, there are at most two fully unpinned two-place requirements. The alternating colouring argument completes all remaining rules, including the possible odd sequence, which must have a holder already pinned to one colour. This gives the *same* exterior for the three successful patterns. [Two derivations with the exact residual cases](../../../research/THREE-READERS-AND-GROWTH-20261120-001B/recovery_argument.md).
2. Collect x and the names that pass this pair test with it. If x is loose, projection on that collection is precisely its holder’s full not-all-equal rule. If x is shared, the collection strictly contains one of the two incomparable physical holdings and cannot have the full rule on the union. Thus the words reveal every loose distinction and its holder. A second test overwrites a pair of names by either assignment of opposite bits in every allowed word. It succeeds precisely when the names have the same holders. A holder containing only one of them has a local word where that distinction is the only one of its value. Given a known H and a shared x in H, the other holder consists of x’s co-held names outside H and the names with exactly the same holders as x, including x. Connectedness propagates that reconstruction.
3. In a closed presentation with a repeated meeting, conditioning two names of that meeting to opposite values discharges their two holders in both representations. Every surviving connected component contains a newly loose distinction. Its holder is recoverable and is shared by both original presentations. Propagation then recovers the originals. If all meetings are single, any four distinct mutually co-held names identify a common holder. Thus two competing presentations with no common holder are ternary throughout. A rival triple must use the three different meeting names of three mutually meeting original holders. Each meeting occurs in two rival triples. This forces exactly four mutually meeting original holders and exhausts all their holdings. Group the six pair names by which of the four holders each mentions or avoids. Both groupings permit the same 18 words. Both have zero loose names and zero births. [Closed cases, induction and full argument](../../../research/THREE-READERS-AND-GROWTH-20261120-001B/classification.md).
4. The stipulated growth law acts on the identified holdings. Every old word and every old private erasure witness extends at the newly created names. Each new rule has a private erasure witness, and every mixed word on each new holder extends. Consequently growth preserves the local and essentiality assumptions, and the same argument applies again after any finite number of parent steps. [Growth and scope](../../../research/THREE-READERS-AND-GROWTH-20261120-001B/classification.md#growth-and-scope).

## Checks, scope and review

The mathematical argument covers every finite size. Independent same-task programs exhausted the ten-distinction cases and replayed the state-only reader on all 2,349 not-all-equal representatives through ten. Another implementation checked partner and overwrite tests on 108 lawful irredundant constructed presentations, including 33 with loose names. These are finite controls for the proof. A parity comparison already gives four or three births on the same eight five-name words. A repeated holder can change growth if the requirement that every rule be necessary is lifted. A disconnected presentation can adjoin a closed ambiguous part to a live one. [Census](../../../research/THREE-READERS-AND-GROWTH-20261120-001B/census.md). The theorem is about the stipulated model. The extension law, connected carrier, full local projections and named-state input delimit the conclusion. Reconstructing the arguments of an extension law does not derive the law itself. The joint-order reading and its fresh-source measurements are recorded in the companion study.
