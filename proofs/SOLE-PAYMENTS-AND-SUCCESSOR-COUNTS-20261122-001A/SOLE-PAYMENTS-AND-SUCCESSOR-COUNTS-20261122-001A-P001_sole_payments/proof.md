# A return paid with both parents’ sole distinctions can happen only once

**Theorem.** Follow one structurally selected receiver through a true child in each complete funded cohort. Count at most one return per cohort, each an actual birth with exactly two distinct paying parents. At a return read whether the two paid values differ. Fix any admissible sequence of physical payment allocations and an enrollment independent of values. Starting from any lawful founding word, **all postfounding return readings can be prescribed simultaneously except possibly one**. Here “postfounding” means that the two payers themselves were born during growth. If only the founder is marked and each next receiver must be uniquely identifiable through current incidences and prior enrolled holders under renaming, the possible exceptional return cannot occur on an indefinitely continuing enrollment.

Consequently, on every such course that returns infinitely, every infinite binary sequence can be prescribed after a finite prefix. Every pair of lower and upper limiting signal fractions $0\leq a\leq b\leq1$ is attained. This holds separately for even parity and not-all-equal. One concrete reached return is forced silent on a fixed older word under both laws. Its forced payment and the later freedom coexist in the same growth. ## What is fixed before a payer writes

Start with a finite connected lawful preparation. Each distinction belongs to one or two knots. The complete-cut rule forms a group from current witnessed meetings and allocates one actual loose distinction per parent of its child. It spends every remaining loose distinction in its own unary birth. Thus a holder with $h$ fresh distinctions in its birth cohort makes exactly $h$ payments to $h$ distinct children in the next funded cohort. Before birth the next fresh demand counts the groups that would contain the newborn. For $p$ true parents and next demand $d$, the ordinary grant is $\max(1,3-p,d)$, except for a sole already implied parity birth. That birth spends every loose input, writes zero fresh distinctions, and closes the whole growth. At a funded nonempty not-all-equal birth there is a positive fresh grant. Preserve one complete reached word, all older coholdings, and a choice of the physical payments and grants of the next cohort. Each newborn has its own fresh names. A tuple of fresh banks extends the whole older word exactly when each bank satisfies its newborn’s relation on its actual paid inputs. This follows by checking each new holding and leaving every old one intact. Two newborns can inherit different distinctions from the **same** old parent. Once that old word and its payments are fixed, these two new banks are still independently assignable within their legal sets. A global zero-fresh parity closure adds only the relation already imposed on the earlier word. [Product of the actual fresh choices](../../../proofs/PAYMENT-HISTORY-AND-FIRST-WRITE-20261122-001B/PAYMENT-HISTORY-AND-FIRST-WRITE-20261122-001B-P001_history_extension/proof.md#extension-of-that-particular-history)

A material course in the theorem specifies the actual births, payments, receiver handoffs and scored returns. Its structural data can include physically marked names supplied independently of values. The same rules find the same receivers on every assignment to that course. A founding payer’s original private value was already fixed at enrollment. If an entire finite history is supplied later, the values at every already issued name in it are fixed as well. The freedom considered below is tested when the *paying parents* are written. By the time of their next child’s birth, the actual two slots already carry their values.

Let $D,E$ be a positive-age pair of payers of a scored return $C$, and let the material binding identify the name each will pay to $C$. If $D$ has at least two fresh distinctions, one may set this particular name to either value. Under even parity choose the remaining fresh values arbitrarily except for one companion that makes the whole holding even. Under not-all-equal give a companion the opposite value. This works for every old paid word. First choose any lawful entire bank of $E$. Then choose the designated name at $D$ to equal or differ from the value supplied by $E$, as requested. All other new banks can be lawfully completed on their own actual older values. The proof also applies with $D,E$ exchanged. One free payer suffices.

Call the case where both $D$ and $E$ have exactly one fresh distinction a *structural exception* in this proof. It is a necessary condition for a restricted postfounding reading. Under parity a one-slot bank must equal the parity of its paid inputs. Under not-all-equal, homogeneous paid inputs force the opposite bit, while mixed paid inputs allow either fresh value even when there is only one slot. Having two sole names therefore does not always fix their disagreement. The comparison is in a common Boolean convention. Renaming distinctions with their payments transported preserves it.

## After the pair spends both sole names

Suppose a binary return $C$ consumes the sole slots of both its parents $D,E$. In the witnessed live component just before their payment the only neighbor of either is the other. Indeed every adjacency is covered by a required group. A third neighbor would need a group other than the actual pair $\{D,E\}$, hence a second loose name at one of the two parents for the same cohort. After the birth, two different newborns share an old witness precisely when their actual parent sets intersect. No other child can use $D$ or $E$, so $C$ has no witnessed neighbor. This argument uses the ordinary grouping, the actual inputs, and the exhaustion of their funding. Their older physical holdings need not be disconnected from the rest of the preparation.

Distinct current witnessed components cannot later exchange descendants under the ordinary complete-cut rule. Every nonsingleton parent group lies within a component, every unary child has just one parent, and two of the new births can be witnessed together only by a shared parent. Induction gives the claim at each further funded cut. The global funding and sole-parity exceptions can stop growth but cannot make a child across those components. In particular an enrollment that continues through true children of $C$ cannot take parents from another component, even with arbitrary intermediate handoffs.

The next possibilities are exhaustive.

1. **Sole global parity birth.** If $C$ alone consumes all loose distinctions, the sum of all old even-parity equations makes its two inputs equal. Its implied two-place relation admits 00 and 11 and it receives no fresh name. This is the ordinary closing convention. There is no later child of $C$. The closing two-place equation is not a new three-variable foundational knot.
2. **A continuing birth.** Otherwise $C$ has $p=2,d=0$ and one fresh distinction. This includes a sole global not-all-equal birth. At the next funded cut its one slot pays one unary child $U$. That child has no sibling in its component and receives exactly two fresh distinctions by the unary arity floor. If the law is parity, the earlier nonsole cut also had a live child outside this component. That outside component contributes a birth whenever the next complete cut is available, so this unary cannot be a sole global parity closure. If a whole cut stalls, there is no later enlisted receiver beyond it.
3. **The two children of $U$.** They are distinct unaries, each on one of its two loose names. They form an isolated witnessed pair, sharing $U$ as old witness. Each has one upcoming group and gets two fresh distinctions by the unary floor. More generally, suppose the isolated current component is a path on $m\ge2$ holders, each with two loose names. Its groups are the $m-1$ adjacent pairs. Each end also pays a unary child, and the other holders spend both names on pairs. In the next component the $m-1$ pair births, with a unary at each end, form a path on $m+1$ holders because precisely consecutive children share an old payer. Each binary has two next groups, and each end unary has one. All receive exactly two fresh distinctions. This proves the description at every later funded cohort. The only child to which $C$ can pay its own one slot is the unary $U$. Every later holder in its continuing component has two fresh names. Thus a second exceptional scored receipt cannot occur on this receiver. This conclusion also covers a score with ternary or unary births *between* two genuine binary observations. The argument concerns a single continuing family. Separate components can produce their own exceptional receipts.

There is a further limit for enrollment by physical structure alone. At the birth of the two unary children of $U$, interchange those children, the two new loose distinctions of $U$ that pay them, and their corresponding fresh banks. This is a symmetry of the entire structural history through this cut, preserving all founder marks, earlier enrolled holders and the rest of the preparation. The two fresh names of $U$ were born together into its single holding. Keeping their birth and coholder records preserves that symmetry. Any rule invariant under coherent renaming with only those structural data must either return both children or leave the next singleton unresolved. Therefore an indefinitely recognizable one-member enrollment with no additional designation cannot traverse this split. A supplied value-independent mark on one of $U$’s new names, or a recorded choice of child, can resolve the split. Such a choice is a scaffold. It also leaves the two-fresh-name induction intact. Whole-history physical connectivity or a known numerical age supplies no orientation here.

## Signal streams and their denominator

Fix the structural course and any lawful founding word. Disregard the possible scored birth whose two parents are founders. Its values can already have been determined even if they own many loose names. Prescribe an arbitrary bit at each nonexceptional postfounding return. At each positive payer cohort there is at most one such prescription for the following cohort. Use the free-payer argument above to satisfy it. At an exceptional return, use any lawful banks and retain the reading they actually give. Choose ordinary legal banks at all other newborns and continue. Old words stay fixed and the next step has its prescribed funding. This is one compatible history, even for consecutive returns when one of today’s payers was itself yesterday’s observed member. The single structural exception can depend on the fixed route; the finite prefix length needed to pass it need not be uniform over all possible routes.

If a later finite past is held pointwise fixed, the same construction starts at the first future cohort whose paying banks can still be chosen. A return already funded by previously written names keeps their values. On a course with infinitely many returns every binary tail is attainable beyond these finitely many readings. For the first $N$ actual returns, let $S_N$ count disagreements of the actual two incoming bits. Each $S_N/N$ lies in $[0,1]$. A silent tail and an all-signal tail attain lower and upper limiting fractions 0 and 1. For any $c\in[0,1]$, the tail $s_j=\lfloor jc\rfloor-\lfloor(j-1)c\rfloor$ has limiting fraction $c$. Alternating increasingly dominant blocks of this construction at rates $a$ and $b$ attains any $0\le a<b\le1$ as lower and upper limits. For example let each new block have length $(1+M)^2$ after $M$ returns. Its prefix rounding error is less than one, so the total error over all blocks divided by $N$ tends to zero. Each last completed block determines its own limiting endpoint. Any fixed initial count changes the fraction by at most the prefix length divided by $N$.

This covers all fills in the upper and lower bounds. The constructions apply to **every fixed legal sequence of material bindings**, and hence supply support when all such bindings are admitted. A policy allowed to see a bank before choosing which name goes to which child can route a different value under a counterfactual fill. A guarantee against each such policy would be a different quantifier. A history with finitely many actual returns has its finite numerator and denominator. No later return is added for a missed handoff, a closed end or a reread. A completely quiet history with unequal values already in its paid reference would score that actual disagreement. On the earlier five-founder receiver the quiet reference has equal arrivals and there really are infinitely many silent returns. Its physically oriented succession gives a nonvacuous instance of the stronger, founder-marked case. At most one receiver return per cohort is essential to the unrestricted prescription argument. On the same five-founder parity preparation score **all five** distinct pair births simultaneously. The XOR of their five disagreement bits is the parity of all incoming paid values, which is zero. Each previously loose name is paid once, and summing the old parity equations cancels each previously shared name twice. Thus every individual bit may be free for its own receiver while some simultaneous five-bit prescriptions are impossible. Under not-all-equal at a positive donor one can instead pay 0 to its same-first-phase child and 1 to its other child. Its two fresh names are then mixed, and every subsequent pair child receives different values. This comparison concerns a different enrollment and only tests the scope of the one-member conclusion.

## A reached physical exception

Use the source proposed in the freeze. The old ordinary meetings form A–X–M–Y–B. Only A, M and B have private supply, respectively one, two and one. Each displayed meeting has at least three actual distinctions, with a distinguishable meeting at the A end. In the continuing comparison, join M by two successive spent holders to one additional live founder Z, far enough to have no common witness with A, M or B. Two ones on each old meeting and zeros on all private names satisfy both laws and give a common lawful reference at the source. Multiple distinct shared names leave both values represented on every old holding. The one-fresh postfounding reference may differ between laws.

The original rows are full local projections of the unconditioned source. For even parity, remove a holder whose allowed row was prescribed. Each remaining connected part has a vertex with a free private name, a consequence of the loose endpoints A, B, and Z when present. Use one previously free shared name at each step toward that private name to satisfy the successive parity equations, then fix the last private bit. If the prescribed holder is a leaf, the rest is still connected to free private supply. For not-all-equal, on every old meeting away from the prescribed holder give two free names opposite values. Any remaining holder with no such meeting is a leaf adjacent to the prescribed holder. Its private slot can oppose a constant prescribed boundary. This extends every allowed row under each law.

The initial witnessed payer pairs in the scored component are A,M and M,B. Thus the first two pair children $D,E$ share an actual ancestor M and have one upcoming joint child $C$. Both $D$ and $E$ have $p=2,d=1$, hence one fresh name each. The marked A pays only its child $D$, and $D$ pays only $C$. Both $D,E$ initially receive 00. Under parity they must each write 0. Under not-all-equal they must each write 1. In both laws the new pair $C$ receives two equal values on two genuinely different names, and is forced silent on that fixed older payment history. There is a changed legal original word on the same extended holdings. Flip A’s private bit and one private bit of Z from 0 to 1. On every ordinary meeting along the route from A to Z flip a single old 1 to 0. Each interior holder has two flips, and each endpoint has one shared and one private flip. Every meeting still contains both values, so both founder laws hold. Now $D$ receives 10 and $E$ receives 00. Parity forces their respective sole payments to be 1 and 0. Not-all-equal lets $D$ write either value and forces $E$ to write 1, admitting both a signal and silence from the identical changed older word and binding. Thus the two payers’ common ancestor does not by itself fix their disagreement. In the isolated source without Z, the parity birth $C$ is the sole global closure. With Z it receives one fresh name and its future component follows the unary and path cases above. The forced return and infinitely many subsequent pair receipts can occur on **one** enrolled history when the fresh choice is supplied. Begin at the marked A and score each actual binary birth of its succession. Continue from $C$ through its unary $U$. Designate one of $U$’s two fresh names independently of all values and follow the unary child that receives it. Call this chosen end $L$ and select its unique binary child at the next cut. At that point the selected receiver is the immediate neighbor of the continuing $L$ end, identifiable by the end’s unary child. Thereafter choose the binary child paid by the current receiver and that current end, and track the end by its unique unary child. In the $m$-holder path these two children are the first binary and the adjacent end of the next path. This relation repeats by the path induction. On the reached fixture the founder-paid and exceptional receipts occur at cuts 1 and 2, the next two handoffs are unary, and every cut from 5 onward contributes a genuine binary receipt with two-fresh-name payers. No new receiver enrolls at the split. Hiding the supplied designation leaves the first two unary children exchangeable. Separately, the quiet five-founder source checks a structurally identifiable infinite receiver under founder-only marking.
