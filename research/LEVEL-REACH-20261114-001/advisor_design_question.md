## Setting (from two finished studies)
Three-place not-all-equal (NAE) constraints ("knots") on Boolean variables ("distinctions"). Addresses are binary words; level n = words of length n. A binary invertible automaton with s states (swap bit, two successors; reads from the first letter) gives maps on addresses; words of length <= 2 in the maps are allowed. Census ADDR-KNOTS: knots {g1 x, g2 x, g3 x} within one level ("placements"); under a fair-coin description weighting most patterns lie in the exponential activity class; fates read to level 12. Follow-up LEVEL-RETURN: some patterns are rigid (exactly one state up to complement) at every living level read, e.g. one whose only states are the address's parity and its complement.
A separate line on nested patterns with bounded interfaces (copies of a definition meeting through 3 ports) measured the "reach": L(s) = the least bond (a minimal non-shattered set: the projection of the allowed states onto it is not the full cube, every proper subset's projection is) containing a top distinction and some distinction s levels down, members anywhere. Proved there per family: L(s) >= 2 + floor(s/r) whenever every product of r per-level port relations is rectangular ("every way down forgets"), by a gap argument: top and bottom of a stretch meet only through the stretch's projection onto its two boundary port sets; if that projection is a product, no set without a member inside the stretch is a bond across it. Forgetting is decided exactly on the finite semigroup of port relations.

## The new study (John approved: "does the top bind the deep levels, where parts depend on the whole, and is that typical?")
Object B': distinctions are addresses of every length 0..N; a description = an automaton plus placements of three TERMS, a term t = (word g, optional appended letter a): t(w) = g(w) or g(w)·a; at every w where the three results exist (length <= N) and are distinct, a knot. ## What I think is settled or hard (check)
1. Knots span at most one level step, so the pattern above level k and below level k+r meet only through levels k and k+r: the gap argument applies with the stretch's projection onto (level k, level k+r) in place of a port relation. But these sets have 2^k and 2^(k+r) addresses, so there is no fixed finite semigroup; rectangularity of one stretch is a forall-exists question.
2. The plain tree {w, w0, w1} alone is a ruler (no cycles): L(s) = s + 2 (known in the program).
3. A pattern rigid on levels 0..N has every pair tied, so L(s) = 2 at every s: the top binds everything at once. So rigidity (found in LEVEL-RETURN) and forgetting are the two extremes.
4. Exact least bonds: ties (size 2) and size-3 bonds can be searched exhaustively by SAT queries for N <= 9 (1,023 distinctions); size >= 4 needs restriction or a smarter method; a ruler at s = 6 has L = 8, beyond reach of exhaustive search.
