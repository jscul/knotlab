# What the commutation equations imply

Write the choice as a symmetric binary form on the distinctions, with zero diagonal. The bit for two products is the sum of the bits for their constituent pairs. This counts the interchanges needed to exchange the products. Every binary form here can be realized by giving distinction $i$ its own bit flip $X_i$, together with $Z_j$ for each earlier distinction $j$ whose pair with $i$ fails. A product with nonempty binary support then moves some bit. This realization introduces no extra scalar relations.

**A1 and A2.** At the decision round a permitted pair is held apart and one member has two holders. That member can never enter another knot. Its pair stays held apart. The conditions between old checks also persist. A complete choice satisfying them has commuting checks at every earlier round. A finite connected holding with a loose distinction has independent knot supports. Indeed, a zero sum of supports must give both holders of each shared distinction the same coefficient, and the holder of a loose distinction must have coefficient zero. Thus one can prescribe every check sign on such a holding. In the realization above, the product of the prescribed sign projectors has positive trace because only the empty product has nonzero trace. When the structure closes, the only dependence is the sum of all supports. The closing sign specified by the cost satisfies it. An already closed start has the same dependence and no signing birth. The required product of its initial signs must be stated if those signs are prescribed. The all-commuting choice always admits the all-even signs.

**A3 to A5.** For the support of a birth's old inputs to equal a sum of old supports, the two holders of every already shared distinction must again have equal coefficients. The old structure is connected. A nonempty implied input set therefore contains every old loose distinction. The funded round uses each once, so the implied birth is the only birth, receives no fresh distinction and closes the whole. A continuing birth receives a loose distinction. At closure each distinction occurs twice. Move its second occurrence to the first and cancel them. A failing pair contributes once modulo two exactly when its two occurrences alternate with those of the other distinction. Swapping two consecutive knots changes the cost by their commutation bit, zero on the choices. At a first-round closure, every pair of initially loose distinctions shares the closing knot. Every other unheld pair already had a twice-held member at its decision.

**A6.** Inside the stated block, take a minimal tree joining the three parents. It either supplies three disjoint arms to a fourth knot, or is a path $a,b,c$ through the three. In the latter case the block stays connected after deleting $b$. On a bypass from $a$ to $c$, take two consecutive meetings with the old path, $u$ on the $a$ side and $v$ on the $c$ side. If $u=a$ and $v=c$, the bypass joins the old path to give a cycle through all three parents. Otherwise use the internal meeting $u$ or $v$ as the common knot, with the bypass leading to the opposite side. The three arms meet only there. Choose one actual shared distinction for each step.

For either construction, represent closing distinction $i$ by $X_i$ on its own bit. Give each selected path distinction a $Z_i$ for precisely the closing distinctions it must fail with. In the cycle form these are the distinctions on the opposite path. In the three-arm form they are the distinctions on the other two arms. Every path distinction already has both holders before the close, so it is older than the three closing distinctions, and each designated pair was held apart when decided. Internal path contributions cancel. On the three bits, the parent checks are $X_i Z_j Z_k$, the other old checks act as identity, and the close acts by $X_1X_2X_3$. The product of the three old parent checks is the negative of the closing check. The state with amplitudes $2^{-3/2}(-1)^{x_1x_2+x_2x_3+x_3x_1}$ has sign $+1$ on every old check and sign $-1$ at the close. Attaching a distinct extra $Z$ in its +1 state to each distinction also gives distinct nontrivial observables. The saved certificates test these signs directly. [Witnesses](witnesses.json)

**Why the sequential weighting is uniform.** In an ordinary funded round, all the old loose inputs commute with one another. They were loose at their common birth round. For each new knot with fresh distinctions, choose one fresh distinction $f$. Against each old distinction $d$, assign $f$ the sum of the failing bits between $d$ and the knot's old inputs. Give all other new pairs zero. Every nonzero assignment is legal. At decision time the funded round has given every old distinction its second holder if it lacked one. An old distinction in the new knot gives sum zero because the old inputs commute among themselves.

The contribution from $f$ cancels that from the inputs, so the new check commutes with every old distinction. Checks of different new knots commute as well. Their old inputs commute, an $f$ has zero bit against every other old input of this round, and fresh distinctions commute with one another. For an implied birth, its support is the sum of old supports, so its check commutes with every old check. This extends any prefix that met the commuting conditions. For fixed holdings the new equations always have the same coefficients. Their solutions above each prefix are translates of the same kernel and hence equal in number. Multiplying the uniform conditional weights over rounds gives equal weight to every complete choice for that start. Where an odd choice exists, adding it pairs each even choice with one odd choice. No new distinction is born in the closing round, so the sign has already been decided by then. [Exact projection tests](sequential.json)
