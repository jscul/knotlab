## The question

The owner's thesis places the base of emergence at the step from pairs to
triples. This proof asks what that step creates, for every law on bits (every
set of relations on {0, 1}).

The first prediction was that knots and extension coincide: pairs give
neither, and triples give both. **That prediction is false.** There are three
regimes, not two, and the second axis is the direction in which a knot forces
values.

## Definitions

- **A law on bits** is a set of relations on {0, 1}. Its systems conjoin
  copies of the relations, identify variables, and forget witnesses.
- **Forcing.** A knot K forces variable v to the value a at a state p of its
  other variables when p extends in K only with v = a. K forces the value a
  when it forces some variable to a at some state.
- **The forced set** of a law is the set of values that some knot of some
  system forces. It is a subset of {0, 1}.
- **One-signed knots.** NAND_m forbids exactly the all-ones pattern of m
  variables: "not all on". OR_m forbids exactly the all-zeros pattern: "not
  all off".

## Standard statement

1. **Pairs** (Theorem 1). If every relation of the law is closed under
   majority, then:
   - in every system a set is free exactly when all its pairs are free;
   - every circuit is a tie, and there are no knots;
   - the shape is a flag complex.
2. **Every knot forces** (Lemma 1). At every excluded pattern q of a knot,
   each variable v is forced, at the rest of q, to the opposite of q_v.
3. **The transmission rule** (Theorem 2). Let two knots, on X ∪ {e} and
   Y ∪ {e}, share only e. Forget e:
   - if the two knots can force e to opposite values, the result is one knot
     on all of X ∪ Y;
   - otherwise nothing remains constrained, and the pair is a hub.
4. **One-sided knots** (Theorem 3). A knot forces only 0 exactly when it is
   NAND_m, and forces only 1 exactly when it is OR_m.
5. **Three regimes** (Corollary 4). Every law on bits is in exactly one of
   three regimes.
   - **Pairs:** the forced set is empty; there are no knots.
   - **Frozen:** the forced set is one value; every knot is "not all on", or
     every knot is "not all off". No two knots ever compose, so the knot sizes
     are those of the law's own clauses.
   - **Transmitting:** the forced set is {0, 1}; knots compose, and chains grow
     without bound (Theorem 5).
6. **Growth** (Theorem 5). In a transmitting law, for every k there is a chain
   of k knots that composes into one knot on Σ|K_i| − 2(k − 1) variables. With
   knots of three variables that is k + 2, so the bond distance from end to
   end is k + 1.
7. **Frozen laws** (Theorem 6).
   - **The proved direction.** A law whose relations are all definable by
     negative clauses, implications and unit literals is frozen toward 0 (the
     "implicative hitting-set" laws), and dually.
   - **The converse, and "every non-pairwise law has a knot".** Both are read
     from Post's lattice, and checked exhaustively on every law generated by
     one relation on at most three bits (Proposition 7).

## Nearest known results

- **Majority and two-variable determination:** Baker and Pixley; Schaefer.
  Theorem 1 restates them for free sets.
- **Resolution.** Two clauses resolve exactly when they hold the shared
  variable to opposite signs. Theorem 2 is the same rule for arbitrary
  relations on bits, read through forcing. The program's space note
  (`KNOT-FOUNDATION-20261019-001`, ticket D) stated it for single clauses.
- **Post's lattice and its families.** The implicative hitting-set families
  (negative clauses with implications and units, and duals) are the infinite
  chains near the bottom of Post's lattice. Creignou, Khanna and Sudan use
  them in their classifications. Resolution never widens their clauses.

**Classification: elementary; new to the program.**
- **The reading is new to the program:** the three regimes defined by forced
  values, and the identification of frozen knots with "not all on" and "not
  all off".
- **The pieces are classical:** resolution, majority closure, and the bounded
  width of implicative hitting-set resolution.

## Theorem 1 (pairs)

*Proof.*
1. **Determined by pairs.** Relations pp-definable from relations closed under
   majority are closed under majority, so they are determined by their
   two-variable projections (Baker and Pixley).
2. **Free sets.** If every pair of a set Y is free, the projection onto Y is
   the product of its ranges. So Y is free.
3. **The consequences.** Every constrained set contains a constrained pair, so
   every circuit is a tie. The free sets are the cliques of the free-pair
   graph, a flag complex. ∎

**Composition collapses.** Forgetting the shared variable of two ties leaves
at most a tie (resolution of two-literal clauses). So the bond distance takes
only the values 0, 1 and ∞.

## Lemma 1 (every knot forces)

*Proof.* Let q be an excluded pattern of a knot on V, and v ∈ V. The
projection onto V − v is the full product (K2), so q − v extends. It cannot
extend with q_v, so it extends only with the opposite value. ∎

**Forcing needs every other variable.** A partial state S that forced v would
make S ∪ {v}, a proper subset of V, constrained.

## Theorem 2 (the transmission rule)

Let K on X ∪ {e} and L on Y ∪ {e} be knots, with X, Y and {e} disjoint, and
let C be their conjunction with e forgotten. Each knot variable takes both
values.

*Proof.*
1. **Allowed sets.** For states p of X and r of Y, let A_K(p) and A_L(r) be the
   values of e allowed with them. Each is nonempty, since X and Y are free in
   their knots. Each is {0, 1} or a single forced value.
2. **No opposite forcing.** Then A_K(p) ∩ A_L(r) is never empty. Every pair
   (p, r) extends, so C is the full product: a hub.
3. **Opposite forcing.** Suppose p forces e to a and r forces e to ¬a. Then
   (p, r) is excluded, and X ∪ Y is constrained.
4. **Every proper subset is free.** Let Z miss some x₀ ∈ X; the case of y₀ ∈ Y
   is the same. Given values on Z, choose any values for Y − Z. L allows some
   value of e with them; choose it. In K, the set X − {x₀} ∪ {e} is a proper
   subset, hence free, so x₀ extends.
5. **So C is a knot on X ∪ Y.** ∎

## Theorem 3 (one-sided knots)

*Proof.*
1. **NAND_m forces only 0.** Its only excluded pattern is all ones. By Lemma 1
   it forces each variable to 0 when all others are 1, and never to 1.
2. **A knot forcing only 0 is down-closed.** If K never forces 1, then
   (p, 0) is allowed for every state p of V − v (p extends, and it is not
   forced to 1). So lowering any coordinate of an allowed pattern keeps it
   allowed.
3. **A down-closed knot is NAND_m.** Let q be excluded, with q_v = 0. Then
   q − v extends, and only with v = 1, to some q′ ≥ q. Down-closure gives
   q ∈ K, a contradiction. So the only excluded pattern is all ones, and
   K = NAND_m.
4. **OR_m** is the dual. ∎

## Corollary 4 (three regimes)

Every knot forces some value (Lemma 1), so a law has knots exactly when its
forced set is nonempty.
- **Frozen.** If the forced set is {0}, every knot is NAND by Theorem 3. Two
  knots sharing a variable can only both force it to 0, so by Theorem 2 they
  form a hub. Every circuit of three or more variables in every system is one
  of the law's "not all on" knots, and composition never makes a new one.
- **Transmitting.** If the forced set is {0, 1}, some knot forces 0 and some
  knot forces 1, and Theorem 2 composes them.
- **Pairs.** By Theorem 1, laws closed under majority are in the pairs regime.
  The converse, that a law not closed under majority has a knot, is read from
  Post's lattice: every co-clone outside the two-literal ones contains a
  one-signed clause, a Horn clause with a positive literal or its dual, a
  parity, or not-all-equal. Proposition 7 checks it on every small law. ∎

## Theorem 5 (growth)

*Proof.* Start from any knot C₁ of the law. Given a knot C, Lemma 1 gives a
variable u of C and a value b that C forces u to. The law has a knot M forcing
some variable w to ¬b. Rename M's variables apart from C, except w = u. By
Theorem 2, C and M compose into a knot on |C| + |M| − 2 variables. By
induction, a chain of k knots gives a knot on Σ|K_i| − 2(k − 1) ≥ k + 2
variables. ∎

**The bond distance grows by one per atom**, as the space note observed for
clause atoms: one more for each further three-variable knot.

## Theorem 6 (frozen laws)

*Proof of the direction proved here.* Let every relation be a conjunction of
negative clauses, implications (¬x ∨ y) and unit literals.
1. **Resolution never widens.** Forgetting a variable of such a system keeps
   it of the same kind, because every resolvent stays among these forms:
   - implications chain into implications;
   - units propagate through implications into units;
   - a negative clause resolves with an implication into a negative clause of
     the same width, and with a positive unit into a narrower one;
   - two negative clauses never resolve.
2. **A knot's prime implicates.** Let K be a knot of a system, projected onto
   its circuit. Every prime implicate of K is a negative clause, an
   implication or a unit.
   - An implication or a unit would constrain one or two variables, which K2
     forbids.
   - A negative clause on a proper subset of K's variables would constrain
     that subset, which K2 also forbids.
3. **So K is NAND on its variables,** and the law is frozen toward 0 by
   Theorem 3. The dual case gives OR. ∎

**The converse.** A frozen law is of this kind. In Post's lattice, the
co-clones whose knots are all one-signed are the implicative hitting-set
families. We do not re-prove the lattice. Proposition 7 checks the converse
on every small law.

## Proposition 7 (the census of small laws)

Every law generated by one relation on at most three bits (273 laws) is
classified by exact relational tests:
- **pairs** when the relation is closed under majority (183 laws);
- **frozen** when it is definable by one-signed clauses, implications and
  unit literals and is not closed under majority (2 laws: "not all on" and
  "not all off");
- **transmitting** otherwise (88 laws).

In every case the observed behaviour matches the class:
- **Pairs.** Sampled systems of pairwise laws show no circuit of three or more
  variables.
- **Frozen.** Frozen laws show only one-signed knots of one sign, of at most
  three variables.
- **Transmitting.** Every transmitting law has an explicit witness: a knot
  forcing both values, or a circuit of four or more variables.

Eleven laws with several relations agree as well:
- **frozen:** "not all on" with an implication, a constant, a two-variable
  "not both", or "not all on" on four variables, and the duals;
- **transmitting:** "not all on" with "not all off", with a Horn clause with a
  positive literal, or with parity, and "not all off" with "not both".

**One methodological lesson.** Random systems alone miss the witness for 8 of
the 88 transmitting laws. An example is the relation {000, 011, 101}, where
z is the exclusive or of two inputs that are never both on. It defines
parity, but only through a cyclic gadget: three copies sharing their inputs in
a ring, R(h₁, h₂, a) ∧ R(h₂, h₃, b) ∧ R(h₃, h₁, c), with the h's forgotten. The
cyclic gadgets find all 88.

## What this does not show

- **No space and no geometry.** Extension here is bond size along chains.
- **Only bits.** Larger domains are not treated.
- **Two directions rest on Post's lattice** (Corollary 4's pairs converse and
  Theorem 6's converse). They are checked on all small laws, not re-proved.
- **No native-loop claim.** No claim is made about native loops beyond the
  K1–K3 reading, and none about physics.
