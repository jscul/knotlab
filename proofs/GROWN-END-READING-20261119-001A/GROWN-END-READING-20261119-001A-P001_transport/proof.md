# When three-distinction bond readings carry growth

**Standard statement.** Consider the cut space of a finite wired graph with simultaneous replacement of its exterior attachments. Which edge permutations preserving the minimum bond size on all pairs and triples and fixing each growing terminal incidence pointwise extend to a permutation preserving the next pair table?

**Nearest known result.** The marked meet-once and repeated-meeting counterexamples change successor pair entries. The established EXTEND-ROUND equation-span transport theorem carries every subsequent round. The classical common-circuit existence problem takes a binary matroid as input. Here the available data are the minimum sizes and the marked growing end. The proof below is independent of the external classification. [Ding and colleagues, Bonds with Parity Constraints](https://www.math.lsu.edu/~ding/bonds.pdf)

Write $K$X$$ for the cardinality of the smallest bond that contains every distinction in $X$, with infinity for absence. The published pair bond distance is $K-1$. All results here concern genuine bonds, the inclusion-minimal nonempty parity equations. The classical calculation represents each shared distinction by a link between its two junctions and each loose one by a link to the common exterior. A bond is a cut with both sides connected. A pointwise mark fixes each distinction in every live junction, including its shared distinctions.

## Pair flips and the growing end

Flipping just $x,y$ changes each junction parity by the sum of its two incidence indicators. The flip is allowed exactly when the two distinctions have exactly the same holders. If ordinary junctions meet at most once, two distinct shared distinctions cannot have the same holders. The only true flip pairs therefore consist of two loose distinctions at the same junction, and both are already fixed. A lawful round keeps the ordinary meet-once property because a new junction takes at most one distinction from any given parent, and two junctions born together share none. Consequently the flip observation leaves the marked pair-table group unchanged at every positive age of a meet-once start. On repeated meetings it can distinguish closed distinctions with the same two holders.

For a transposition of two unfixed distinctions in any parity system, there is also a direct equation test. Swapping them changes any equation by either zero or the word supported on just those two distinctions. Thus the transposition preserves the equation space exactly when their incidence columns are equal or that two-distinction word is an equation. In our admissible graphs no single distinction is forced, so in the second case the two-distinction equation is itself a bond.

## All shortest bonds at any one distinction can be read

Fix a distinction $x$ joining $s,t$. Its least bond size $k_x$ is the least finite $K$x,y$$ over the other distinctions. This uses the absence of singleton bonds. A cut through $x$ separates $s,t$, and a minimum cut separating them has two connected sides. A disconnected component away from its required endpoint could otherwise change sides and strictly reduce the cost. Thus the minimum bonds through $x$ are precisely the minimum cuts separating its ends.

Orient each such minimum cut by recording the side $A$ containing $s$. These sides are closed under union and intersection. Indeed for two sides $A,B$, the sum of their two cut sizes equals the sum for $A\cap B,A\cup B$, plus twice the number of distinctions from $A\setminus B$ to $B\setminus A$. The union and intersection still separate $s,t$, so both attain the same minimum and this last number is zero.

Any other distinction $y$ cut by one or more of these minimum cuts has a consistent orientation across all its witnesses. Opposing orientations would give a link between the two forbidden differences. The minimum sides cutting $y$ therefore form an interval, from their intersection $L_y$ to their union $U_y$. Every minimum side between these bounds contains the source endpoint of $y$ and excludes its other endpoint. A finite collection of these intervals that meet in pairs has a common member. Each $L_y$ is contained in each $U_z$, so the union of all their lower bounds lies in every interval.

This gives an explicit reader. Keep the $y$ with $K$x,y$=k_x$. Link $y,z$ exactly when $K(x,y,z)=k_x$. Then the maximal sets linked throughout, together with $x$, are **all the smallest bonds through $x$**. Each such set has a joint minimum witness by the interval argument. Each true minimum bond supplies a set of this form, and no strict inclusion between two minima is possible. The statement holds in every finite admissible wired graph, including those with repeated meetings, without using names, growth history or the presenting junctions at read time.

There is a further operation on a pair with minimum size $m$. The set consisting of $x,y$ and every $z$ with $K(x,y,z)=m$ is exactly the union of all its minimizing bonds. If that union has $m$ distinctions, it is the unique minimum bond through the pair. The span of both families of recovered bonds together with the live equations is invariant under the marked reading. If this span is the full parity equation space at the source, every such self-permutation carries all later rounds. To see this, the new equations use the fixed live distinctions and fresh ones that can be fixed as well. Their span with the old equations is carried, and the new live supports are again fixed. Induction gives the same matching on every later table. The accepted growth theorem gives this transport more generally for matchings of different presentations with matching growing ends. On all three minimum meet-once examples the one-distinction minima and the live equations give rank 10. The unique pair minimizers raise this to the full rank 12 in each case. On the repeated-meeting example the first family already gives the full rank 10. These ranks give a direct explanation for all-round transport on the named examples, beyond selecting permutations by enumeration. ## A limit of these reconstruction operations on a reached stage

Take two junctions $A,B$ and $k\ge3$ middle junctions. Each middle junction meets $A$ twice and $B$ twice. Add two further junctions attached once to $A$ and $B$ respectively, each with two loose distinctions. These two live junctions have no common ordinary neighbor, and the first lawful round attaches four unary junctions, each with two fresh distinctions. Consider this positive-age stage.

Any cut separating $A,B$ takes two distinctions at every middle junction. At each one it can select either pair, putting that middle junction on the corresponding side. Moving an unmarked middle junction to the other side of such a bond preserves connectivity of both sides and preserves size. Each distinction at a middle junction belongs to its four-distinction star, and each distinction elsewhere has a bond of size at most three there. Consequently no minimum bond through one distinction separates $A,B$. No *unique* minimum bond through a pair separates them either, since there is always an unmarked middle junction to move. All the recovered equations and all marked live equations therefore keep $A,B$ on the same side. This property is linear (even parity along any path between them). The actual equation at $A$ separates the two. The rank deficit is one in the literal checks at $k=3,4,5$. It is a limitation of those two reconstruction operations. The complete triple readings still contain information about the alternative minimizing bonds.

## A reached self-renaming that changes the equations

There is an actual meet-once reached stage where a permutation preserves the full pair and triple readings, fixes the growing end pointwise, and changes a parity equation. In this example the same permutation extends, with every fresh distinction fixed, through **every later lawful round**. This separates two claims that have the same answer throughout the starting-width census.

Start with the split version of the following ordinary graph $G$. There are 21 junctions $u_i$ indexed modulo 21 and one junction $w_{ij}$ for each unordered index pair. Each $w_{ij}$ meets $u_i,u_j$ and one of two further junctions $h_0,h_1$. In the unique representation $\{i,i+t\}$ with $1\le t\le10$, its third distinction belongs to $h_0$ for $t\le5$ and to $h_1$ otherwise. All meetings occur once. Index exchange $\sigma=(2\ 3)$ acts on distinction names by transporting both indices. In the graph in which the two $h$ junctions are identified this is an ordinary symmetry. In $G$ it changes the equation at $h_0$. Indeed, the subgraph obtained by removing the 210 distinctions incident to the two $h$ junctions is connected on all the other vertices. Therefore the cuts supported on those 210 names are exactly the zero word, the two original groups, and their sum. The exchange preserves the group of the distinction at $\{0,1\}$ and changes that of the one at $\{2,8\}$, so it sends the equation at $h_0$ outside that span.

The two $h$ junctions are joined by 105 mutually distinction-disjoint paths. In the graph with identified $h$ every one-, two- or three-distinction mark has a bond of size at most 80. The proof constructs a small connected side containing at most four $u$ junctions, checks the connectedness of the opposite side, and counts the crossings. Both graphs have exactly the same bonds below size 105. Splitting or identifying the two $h$ junctions preserves a cut of that size and the connectivity of its sides, since at least one entire connecting path survives. Consequently the specified index exchange preserves every pair and triple minimum in $G$. The construction and the check of every type of three marks under index relabeling are given in full. [Core proof and check](../../../experiments/GROWN-END-READING-20261119-001/GROWN-END-READING-20261119-001-E001_readings/theory.md#a-hidden-equation-in-a-simple-wired-graph)

Take the distinction $e=u_0w_{01}$, fixed by $\sigma$, with ends $s,t$. Remove it to form $H$. Add two new ordinary junctions $U,V$, one shared distinction $sU$, one shared distinction $tV$, and two loose distinctions at each of $U,V$. All vertices of $G$ are now ordinary. The resulting connected start has **235 junctions and 635 distinctions**. It is admissible. The core $G$ stays connected after deletion of one vertex: the other index vertices remain connected through their intervening junctions, and each remaining junction joins that component. The added part with a virtual $st$ likewise stays connected after any single vertex deletion, witnessed by the circuit $s,U,\text{exterior},V,t,s$. Deleting the virtual $st$ from both pieces and joining them preserves this property. When a surviving path formerly used $st$, the other piece supplies its replacement, and on deletion of $s$ or $t$ both surviving sides meet at the other one. Thus every ordinary support is a bond, and in this finite parity system its full even-parity projection satisfies the ordinary open-junction test.

At the start $U,V$ have distinct parents and no common neighbor. The first round adds four one-parent junctions with two fresh distinctions apiece. The first reached stage therefore has **239 junctions and 643 distinctions**. Every live support is in the added part $Q$. At later positive stages the two branches still have disjoint parent sets. No new junction takes every loose distinction, since the other branch has one. Each successful round leaves a loose distinction in each branch. Their wired part remains connected through the exterior, touches $H$ only at $s,t$, and has minimum $s,t$ cut exactly one because its only distinction at $s$ is $sU$.

Here is why old and new readings really match. If a mark of size at most three lies wholly in $H$, replacing the unit distinction $e$ by any connected $Q$ with unit minimum $s,t$ cut leaves its minimum unchanged. A bond separating the terminals uses connected separating cuts in both pieces, and the optimal price in the unmarked $Q$ is one. If the terminals are on the same side, the other side lies entirely in $H$ and the terminal side is connected using $Q$ exactly as it was using $e$. If both pieces are marked, let $X$ consist of the marked distinctions in $H$ and $Y$ those in $Q$. Both are nonempty. The terminals must be separated, and the two choices of connected terminal cuts are independent. The new minimum is

$$
\bigl(K_G(X\cup\{e\})-1\bigr)+L_Q(Y),
$$

where $L_Q(Y)$ is the minimum size of a connected $s,t$ cut containing $Y$, with infinity for absence. Here $|X|+1\le3$. The core term is invariant under $\sigma$ and the outside term is fixed. Every mark confined to $Q$ is fixed pointwise. Thus every pair and triple agrees at every such stage without a further reading. The only possible nontrivial pair flips are fixed loose twins. The false image of the $h_0$ equation is still false: deleting all the $h$ distinctions leaves the rest connected, including the added part. ## What remains open

In any fixed part attached to the rest at exactly two junctions, an outside distinction on a minimum separating cut measures the interior separating cost of any marked pair when it is read with that pair. If future growth stays outside and its terminal cut cost never increases, this one measurement and the old pair table determine every later pair comparison for a permutation supported in the fixed part. Ordinary exterior replacement cannot increase that cost while the outside stays connected. The proof distinguishes mixed pairs from pairs wholly inside either part and includes missing readings. [Two-attachment theorem and scope](../../../experiments/GROWN-END-READING-20261119-001/GROWN-END-READING-20261119-001-E001_readings/obstruction.md)

For arbitrary reached stages, an equation-changing renaming can therefore be invisible to all the proposed readings. To refute one-round transport one must also show a change in a successor pair that no legal naming of the fresh distinctions can repair. To prove transport in general one must show that every hidden difference is immaterial to those successor minima. The two-attachment reduction gives this for its specified geometry. In a part with several attachments, complete bonds can depend on the simultaneous partition of several attachment junctions, and independent one- or two-mark minima have not been proved to determine those joint choices. The unrestricted one-round theorem and its unrestricted iteration remain open.
