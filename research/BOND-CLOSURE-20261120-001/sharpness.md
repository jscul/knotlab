# Which marked distinctions can determine parity closing?

**On-paper bounds, independent program referee pending.** For a funded unheld group with \(p\ge3\) parents, one prescribed query needs exactly \(p-1\) loose inputs in the worst case. In particular any one pair settles a ternary proposal. With every reading up to a specified order on the proposed loose inputs, the worst-case order grows linearly with \(p\). Even when the complete parent supports are available, the multiple-tree construction rules out every constant order across all parent sizes. A bound on parent size supplies a uniform sufficient order for this stronger reader. A separate proposed quantitative strengthening below awaits an independent audit and is outside these conclusions. The lower examples are lawful physical sources with a funded first birth. A positive-age restriction or membership in the earlier two-parent-at-source identification class would impose additional hypotheses. [Registered observation models](preregistration.md)

Write \(b(T)\) for the least physical parity bond size through every marked distinction in \(T\), with infinity when none exists. In the cut representation put each loose distinction between its holder and an exterior point, and retain parallel shared distinctions separately. A bond is the nonempty crossing set of a partition with both sides connected. Denote the side omitting the exterior by \(U\). If the proposed \(p\) loose inputs exhaust a connected presentation, the complete exterior star is a bond of size \(p\). If an extra loose distinction exists, a cut through all \(p\) proposed inputs costs more than \(p\). These statements concern parity. Under not-all-equal the same physical diagrams encode a different allowed-state relation. [Previous parity cut and closing correspondence](../ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/rulers.md)

## A single prescribed query, including the ternary case

Take any \(p-1\) of the proposed inputs, with \(p\ge3\). **They have \(b\le p\) if and only if the proposal exhausts.** Suppose a nonexhausting preparation had a cut of size at most \(p\) through these marks. Its ordinary shore contains the \(p-1\) selected parents. It cannot contain the missing parent as well, since the entire ordinary shore would then contain an extra loose distinction and a proper shore would have at least one shared crossing. It therefore has exactly the selected loose distinctions and a single ordinary crossing \(x y\), with \(x\) inside. This is a bridge of the connected ordinary presentation.

At least one selected parent differs from \(x\). Its length-two path through a common holder to the missing parent must cross the bridge as the second step. Thus \(y\) is the missing parent, and every selected parent other than a possible \(x\) meets \(x\). If \(x\) is one of the proposed parents, the pair \(x,y\) requires a common holder, contradicting the bridge. If \(x\) is outside the proposal, it meets every proposed parent, including \(y\). One holder then holds the entire set, excluding an unheld group of arity at least three. This proves the claim for whichever input was omitted. In the closing case the answer is exactly \(p\), since a cut of size \(p-1\) would consist solely of the chosen loose distinctions and a connected ordinary presentation has no such proper loose-only cut. The ternary pair bond distance is therefore two at closing and strictly larger or infinite otherwise.

**Why a smaller single query fails.** Fix \(p\ge4\) and \(m=p-2\). Use \(m\) parents in \(I\), each meeting two spent holders \(H_1,H_2\) once. One more parent \(a\) meets \(H_1\) once and a third holder \(W\) twice. The last parent \(b\) meets \(H_2\) once and \(W\) once. Give each of the \(p\) parents one loose distinction. In the other preparation add one loose distinction at \(W\). Each pair of parents has a common holder, and no holder meets all \(p\). When \(W\) becomes live it shares no common holder with any of them, since every ordinary meeting joins a parent to one of the three holders. Thus the same full parent group forms in both sources. Every parent funds it. Only the second source makes an additional unary birth. Both sources are connected, all arities are at least three, and deleting any one knot leaves each remaining spent holder attached to at least one parent with a loose distinction. The complement of every declared parity star is connected, proving its canonical projection.

For every nonempty subset \(T\) of the loose distinctions at \(I\), a minimum can put \(a,b,W\) on the exterior side in both preparations. To see this, keep the sides of \(H_1,H_2,I\) fixed. Moving all three remaining ordinary knots outside costs one for each included \(H_i\). This meets a lower bound from the two disjoint routes \(H_1,a,\mathrm{exterior}\) and \(H_2,b,\mathrm{exterior}\). The remaining ordinary shore is connected, since any included hubs meet the same selected member of \(I\), and every remaining included parent meets each included hub. If no hub is included, a connected shore through a selected member of \(I\) can only be that single parent. Every outside hub connects to the exterior through \(a\) or \(b\), and every outside member of \(I\) has its own loose distinction. Both shores are therefore connected after moving the three knots.

The common answer is \(3\) for a singleton and \(p\) for every \(|T|\ge2\). The ordinary graph has no bridge, and the star of a member of \(I\) has size three. For two or more marks one needs an inside hub. With both hubs inside each member of \(I\) costs at least one and the outside part costs two, attained by taking all of \(I\). With exactly one hub inside each member costs at least one, each marked member costs an extra one and the outside part costs one. This is at least \(p\). Any prescribed query of order at most \(p-2\) can be placed within \(I\) by renaming. This proof compares *all* subsets of this fixed part and does not imply equality on subsets containing \(a\) or \(b\).

For \(p=3\) the same smaller-query witness works with \(|I|=1\) after doubling each of its two hub meetings. Every declared star then has lawful arity. The ordinary graph still has no bridge, and \(I\cup\{H_1,H_2\}\) realizes size three through the one specified loose mark in both preparations.

In fact every true singleton reading on the three parents' whole supports can agree. Use an alternating six-knot cycle with three parent knots and three different pair witnesses. Give each parent one loose distinction. Connect each witness once to a new spent knot of its own, then connect that knot twice to a common spent \(Z\). Add one loose distinction to \(Z\) in the second preparation. The same unheld triple forms and \(Z\) is candidate-isolated. Removing a parent leaves each corresponding witness attached to its other parent. Removing a witness or an intermediate knot leaves the other paths from \(Z\) to parents intact. All complements of stars are connected and all arities are at least three. Every distinction on a parent has singleton minimum three in both preparations. Its parent star supplies the upper bound. For the loose mark there are three disjoint routes from its parent to the exterior, directly and through each of its two other parents. For a meeting of parent \(u\) with pair witness \(H_{uv}\), use their direct meeting, the route through the exterior and parent \(v\), and the route from \(u\) through its other witness, the two intermediates and \(Z\) into \(H_{uv}\). These routes share no distinction. Thus order two is also sharp for the entire ternary input or parent-support deck.

**The binary distinction.** For two parents with canonical actual stars of at least three places, their two genuine singleton minima are both two exactly when their proposal exhausts. In a closing source the two loose distinctions are the full exterior bond and neither one can be a singleton constraint. In a nonclosing source a size-two bond through one selected loose distinction must cut exactly one ordinary bridge and have precisely that one loose distinction in its ordinary shore. Orient both such shores away from a third loose distinction. Sides cut off by ordinary bridges away from the same holder are nested or disjoint, and these shores contain different, exclusive loose inputs, so they must be disjoint. A two-meeting path between the parents through a common holder must traverse both exit bridges. Each bridge therefore leaves its own shore directly at its loose parent. If the shore contains another knot, deleting this parent isolates those other knots from the wired exterior, contradicting its canonical star. If it contains only that parent, the parent has just two places. Both possibilities are excluded. A third loose distinction at either selected holder already makes such a pair of singleton shores impossible.

One prescribed singleton can still read two on a continuing binary proposal. Let \(u,h\) meet twice and let \(h,v\) meet once. Put \(v\) on a six-knot ordinary cycle, double every meeting on that cycle, and take \(z\) three steps from \(v\) around the cycle. Give \(u,v,z\) one loose distinction each. The sole pair is \(u,v\) through \(h\), and \(z\) makes a unary birth. The shore \(\{u,h\}\) gives a size-two bond through the loose mark of \(u\). Deleting \(h\) leaves \(u\) with a loose mark, deleting \(v\) leaves the cycle remainder with the mark at \(z\), and the other single deletions also keep every component attached to a loose holder. Thus every original star passes the parity projection gate. A closing comparison has a spent common holder joined twice to each of two one-loose parents. It gives the same prescribed singleton answer. This binary conclusion concerns exhaustion under the registered last-birth convention; enforcing a three-place minimum on an implied two-input last birth would separately change its output.

## A comparison that protects exact minima globally

Suppose the wired closing source has \(L\) distinction-disjoint routes from an ordinary knot \(A\) to the exterior. Add a new loose distinction at \(A\), comparing only markings on the original distinctions. **Every old marked minimum strictly below \(L\) stays equal.** Indeed, in either preparation a cut with fewer than \(L\) crossings leaves an original route intact. The points \(A\) and the exterior are on the same side, connected on that side even without the added distinction. Adding or removing this distinction therefore preserves the crossing cost and the connectedness of both sides. The argument runs in both directions and compares actual global minimum bonds. Merely exhibiting the same candidate bond in both preparations would only give a shared upper bound.

Use three spent knots \(A,B,C\). Each proposed parent meets two of them once and has just one loose distinction. Take \(m\) parents of each of the three types \(AB,BC,CA\), so \(p=3m\). Give the same parents additional links as the leaves of \(t\) separate trivalent trees. Each tree has its own central knot with three branches, each branch a complete binary tree with \(m=2^\ell\) leaves. Only the physical parent leaves are shared across the copies. All internal tree knots are spent. In the second source add one loose distinction at \(A\). The \(2m\) routes from \(A\) through its distinct parent neighbours and their loose distinctions to the exterior give \(L=2m\).

Every two proposed parents share at least one of \(A,B,C\). None of these three meets every parent, and no tree knot of degree three does so for \(p>3\). The new live knot \(A\) meets ordinary parents only, whereas each parent meets only hubs and internal tree knots. Hence \(A\) shares no common holder with a proposed parent. The sole group with several parents is the full set of \(p\) in both preparations. It gets zero fresh distinctions in the closing source and one in the continuing source, where the additional unary birth has a different parent. Both updates are fully funded. Removing any one leaf leaves every tree connected through its remaining leaves. Removing an internal tree knot leaves every resulting branch with a parent leaf connected to the exterior. The three hubs retain parent neighbours if one parent is removed. These facts also cover removing a hub and prove full canonical parity projection on every star. The physical stars and loose counts of the marked parents, their restricted witness family and their chosen inputs match under the identity correspondence. Arbitrary consistent renaming preserves all statements.

Take \(s\) distinct selected parents. In one tree take them with all internal knots on their routes to its central knot. This ordinary shore is connected. Each outside branch of that tree still ends in an unselected parent. Every other full tree with the selected leaves omitted reaches an unselected parent, and so does every hub when \(s<2m\). All those parents connect through loose distinctions to the exterior. If \(h\) is the number of included internal tree knots, the number of tree meetings leaving this shore in the selected tree is \(h+2-s\), by counting the three children of the central knot, the two children of each other internal knot, and the \(h+s-1\) meetings within the shore. The \(s\) leaves contribute their own loose distinctions, two hub meetings each and their \(t-1\) meetings in the unused trees. The exact bond cost is

\[
h+2+(t+1)s,\qquad h\le 1+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\min(3\cdot2^{j-1},s).
\]

**All proposed loose-input markings.** Set \(t=1\), so every proposed parent has four places. For \(r=2^k\ge2\), take \(m=4r\), \(p=12r\). For \(s\le r\), the displayed sum gives \(h\le 9r/2-2\). Indeed the terms before reaching \(r\) sum to \(3(2^{k-1}-1)\) and the last three terms are at most \(r\) each. The bond through those \(s\) marks costs at most \(13r/2<2m\). Thus **all exact proposed-loose-input readings through order \(p/12\) agree**, even though the next birth has opposite closing verdicts. The total \(p\) and the names of the marked parent set are available in both cases.

This gives a linear lower bound at every sufficiently large \(p\) as well. For \(p\ge24\) divide the parents into three types with sizes differing by at most one. Let \(r\) be the largest power of two at most \(\lfloor p/3\rfloor/4\). Build each of the three tree branches on its assigned number of leaves as a full binary tree of height at most \(\log_2r+3\), which is possible since the largest type has size at most \(8r\). There is at most one extra level in the preceding sum. Hence \(h\le11r/2-2\), the bond costs at most \(15r/2\), and \(A\) has at least \(8r\) disjoint routes. Here \(r\ge\lfloor p/24\rfloor\). Combined with the sufficient prescribed \(p-1\) query, the minimum worst-case order of the whole loose-input deck is \(\Theta(p)\). This does not determine its optimal constant or exact order for each \(p\).

**All markings in the parents' whole supports.** A marked distinction now may be loose, a hub meeting, or a meeting connecting a parent to one of the routing trees. Take \(t=r+1\) tree copies. At most \(r\) copies contain a marked tree meeting, so choose one without any. Use every parent incident to a marked distinction as a selected leaf in that copy. All the marked distinctions cross the connected shore just constructed, since the hubs, the other tree copies and the exterior are outside. The same global protection applies as soon as \(1+r\ell+2+(r+2)r<2m\). For example, the least power of two \(m\ge4(r+1)^2\) always suffices, since \(\ell<3+2\log_2(r+1)\le3+2r\). The resulting parent count is less than \(24(r+1)^2\), and each parent has exactly \(r+4\) places.

At order three one may use \(m=16\), \(p=48\), and four trees. All markings of at most three distinctions in the 48 seven-place parent supports have matching exact minima. Their constructed bonds cost at most \(13+2+5\cdot3=30<32\). There are 235 ordinary knots, with 516 distinctions before and 517 after adding the loose distinction. The old parent supports comprise 336 distinct physical marks. The proof covers them all by the displayed cut bounds, without enumerating their subsets.

## Proposed quantitative strengthening for the complete parent supports

**Pending separate independent audit.** The preceding multiple-tree construction proves unbounded complete-support order on its own. This additional argument proposes linear dependence on \(p\) for the same reader. Its separate obligation is the common private-cut lower bound under every mark orientation and every connected bipartition. The study's main result uses the preceding construction.

Keep the three hubs, their parent assignments, the one loose distinction per parent and the added loose distinction at \(A\). Replace the routing trees by the following physical presentation. Start with **one** of the balanced trivalent trees on \(p\) leaves used above. At its leaf corresponding to each parent \(x\), put the root of a private binary tree \(I_x\) with \(r+1\) leaves. Take a second copy \(O_x\) of the **same** binary tree, with separate internal knots but identically corresponding leaves shared between the copies. Each shared leaf is a new knot with one further ordinary meeting to \(x\). Join the root of \(O_x\) once to a common spent knot \(Z\). No identification involves two different parents' private trees. Every physical parent thus has \(r+4\) places. A private leaf has three places, as do the two roots after attaching them to the common tree or to \(Z\).

The new knots are all spent. Only the three old hubs meet multiple live proposed parents, so all group and funding checks above still apply. Every ordinary knot remaining after any one deletion reaches a parent with a loose distinction. Deleting an internal knot of a private binary tree leaves a private leaf in each remaining branch; deletion of one of its leaves leaves the other leaves accessible in both copies. After deleting the parent \(x\) itself, its private trees reach other parents through the main tree or \(Z\). Every branch created by deleting a main tree knot leads to a surviving private tree, and deleting a hub or \(Z\) leaves the parent connections through the main tree. This proves full parity projection at every declared knot and works equally with the extra loose distinction. The \(2m\) distinction-disjoint routes \(A,x,\mathrm{exterior}\) through its public parent neighbours are unchanged.

Take a nonempty marked set \(T\) of at most \(r\) distinctions from the parent supports, and let \(S\) be their \(s\) incident parents. If \(s=1\), the actual star of its parent is already a common bond of size \(r+4<2m\) in the parameter range below. Now take \(s\ge2\). For each \(x\in S\), write \(J_x\) for the leaves where the physical meeting with \(x\) is marked. For nonempty \(J\) in the underlying private binary tree, let \(c(J)\) count the child branches leaving the union of the root paths of \(J\). Put

\[
L_T=\sum_{x:\,J_x\ne\varnothing}\bigl(|J_x|+c(J_x)\bigr).
\]

**Every qualifying bond, in either preparation, spends at least \(L_T\) crossings on the private parts alone.** The \(|J_x|\) marked leaf meetings cross. For one such leaf, its side of the bond is opposite \(x\). This side reaches outside the private part at \(x\), because a mark at another parent supplies a crossing with both endpoints outside this part. Take a path on that connected side from the marked leaf to its first exit. The path avoids \(x\), so it reaches one of the two private roots. Project the two identical trees onto one abstract tree, mapping their shared leaves to themselves. The projected walk from leaf to root passes every ancestor of that leaf. Hence at every node of the union of the marked root paths at least one physical copy belongs to the side opposite \(x\). For each of its \(c(J_x)\) outgoing branches choose an unmarked leaf below it. If the chosen leaf is opposite \(x\), its own unmarked meeting with \(x\) crosses. If it is on the side of \(x\), start at the opposite-side copy of the branch's attachment and follow that copy down to the chosen leaf. At least one meeting on the branch crosses. These charges are distinct across branches and disjoint from the marked leaf meetings. This proves the lower bound. It excludes the two-meeting routes from the public hub \(A\) to the exterior from every charge.

**A connected realizing upper bound.** Include all \(x\in S\) and their corresponding root paths in the main tree. Put the three hubs, \(Z\) and the exterior outside. In each selected private part take all internal knots of \(I_x\) inside, and put its shared leaves outside exactly at \(J_x\). If \(J_x\ne\varnothing\), put the root paths of \(J_x\) in \(O_x\) outside and its other knots inside. If \(J_x\) is empty, put the entire \(O_x\) inside. Every selected parent has an unmarked leaf since there are \(r+1\) leaves. It joins the main tree through that leaf and \(I_x\). Every included branch of \(O_x\) reaches an included leaf; every excluded marked leaf reaches \(Z\) in \(O_x\). The omitted main-tree branches have unselected parents, and \(Z\) and the hubs reach the exterior through unselected parents. Both sides are connected. All marked distinctions cross, including hub meetings and loose marks.

The main-tree boundary and the selected parents' public meetings and loose distinctions cost \(h+2+2s\), as in the one-tree case. For nonempty \(J_x\) the private cost is exactly \(2|J_x|+c(J_x)\), one crossing from each marked shared leaf to \(x\) and one into \(I_x\), in addition to the outgoing branches in \(O_x\). For empty \(J_x\) the only private crossing is the link to \(Z\). The total of \(\sum_x|J_x|\) and the number of selected parents with empty \(J_x\) is at most \(|T|\), because each of those latter parents has its own other marked distinction. The constructed cost is therefore at most

\[
 L_T+h+2+3r.
\]

For \(r=2^k\ge2\), \(m=4r\) and \(p=12r\), the excess over \(L_T\) is at most \(15r/2<2m\). Every minimizing bond in either preparation consequently uses fewer than \(2m\) crossings on the public routes. An original \(A\)-to-exterior route remains intact on its side, so adding or deleting the extra loose distinction preserves both connected shores and the exact minimum. This comparison permits a large value of \(b(T)\) itself. Subtracting the common private lower bound is essential.

For every \(p\ge48\), divide the three parent types as evenly as possible and take the largest power of two \(r\le\lfloor p/3\rfloor/8\). Each main branch now has height at most \(\log_2 r+4\). The level sum gives \(h\le13r/2-2\). The excess is at most \(19r/2\), strictly below the at least \(16r\) public routes at \(A\). The proposed stronger conclusion is that all parent-support readings through order \(\lfloor p/48\rfloor\) can agree, which would give \(\Theta(p)\) when parent arity is unrestricted. The construction leaves the least sufficient order for each \(p\) open.

## A bounded parent size suffices with complete physical supports

Assume \(p\ge2\). Supply each proposed parent's **complete** physical support, which mark is its chosen loose input, and which of its other distinctions are loose. If a parent has another loose distinction, the birth fails the exhaustion test immediately. Suppose from here on that there is exactly one loose distinction \(e_u\) per proposed parent \(u\). Write \(J_u\) for its full set of shared distinctions and \(d_u=|J_u|\), with parallel occurrences counted separately. Then

\[
\text{the proposal exhausts}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists u\quad\forall v\ne u\text{ proposed},\quad
b(J_u\cup\{e_v\})\le d_u+p-1.
\]

To prove necessity, ordinary deletion of at least one proposed parent \(u\) leaves a connected ordinary presentation in any lawful closing source. Choose \(u\) farthest in ordinary meeting steps from a fixed different loose holder, maximizing over the loose holders. If deleting \(u\) disconnects the ordinary presentation, every resulting component must still contain a loose distinction, since the complement of the canonical star of \(u\) is connected in the wired presentation. A loose holder in a component away from the fixed starting holder would be farther, a contradiction. With this \(u\), put every ordinary knot except \(u\) inside. The complement connects \(u\) to the exterior through \(e_u\), so this is a bond of cost \(d_u+p-1\) through every required marking at once.

For sufficiency, each qualifying bond puts \(u\) outside. Otherwise all of \(u\)'s shared meetings cross, isolating it in the ordinary shore, which also contains \(v\) because \(e_v\) is marked. It therefore puts **every ordinary neighbour of \(u\)** inside. In the common family of all cuts with those neighbours inside and \(u\) outside, the \(d_u\) meetings already cross. Each other proposed parent \(w\) costs at least one more. If it is inside, take \(e_w\). If it is outside, it has a shared meeting with a common holder for \(u,w\), and that holder is inside. These are distinct crossing distinctions, also distinct from the meetings of \(u\). Thus every cut in this family costs at least \(d_u+p-1\). This argument uses the common-holder condition for proposed parents; mere ordinary adjacency would not suffice.

All the qualifying shores for a fixed \(u\) attain that common lower bound. Their union attains it too. Indeed for any two allowed ordinary shores, their union and intersection still obey the pinning, and the sum of their two cut sizes is at least the sum for union and intersection, by counting each physical distinction. Repeated union preserves the minimum. The final union contains every proposed parent except \(u\), and every neighbour of \(u\). Its \(d_u\) crossings at \(u\) and \(p-1\) marked loose distinctions consume the whole budget. In a connected ordinary presentation every component left by removing \(u\) meets some neighbour of \(u\). With no other ordinary crossing possible, all these components lie inside. Every extra loose distinction would now cost one more, except at \(u\), whose loose count was supplied and is one. This proves sufficiency, including the convention that infinity fails the test.

If the ordinary holder identities of the shared distinctions are additionally supplied, one representative mark per distinct neighbour of \(u\) pins all of \(J_u\) across the cut. The threshold still uses its physical multiplicity \(d_u\). In particular, if every proposed parent has at most \(w\) places, order \(w\) suffices however large \(p\) becomes, even with no compression of parallel meetings. The multiple-tree examples show that order \(w-4\) can fail for \(w=r+4\). Thus in the uniform parent-width question the worst required order lies between \(w-3\) and \(w\) for \(w\ge5\). This is compatible with the unbounded loose-only order even for four-place parents. All these statements concern the marked information for the closing decision of an already legal funded join, without weighting possible sources or inferring a local rule for every part of the update.
