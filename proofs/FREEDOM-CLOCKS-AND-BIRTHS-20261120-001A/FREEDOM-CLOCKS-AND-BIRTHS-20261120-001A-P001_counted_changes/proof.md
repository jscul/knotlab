# Counts of pairs of allowed words and the chances of a change

**Standard statement.** Supply a second binary question that reports the sign of a change and the calibration of that sign. Let S be any finite nonempty set of binary words with positive uniform preparation. For a flip set T and a disjoint ordinary read set U, the proposed product mean counts the words in S whose translate by T is still in S, using the ordinary parity on U as sign and dividing by the size of S. This mean and its compatible combined-question extension determine exactly the earlier squared-weight probabilities.

**Nearest known result.** Finite character inversion and the sign-change multiplication rules are classical. The preceding studies used a real positive preparation and the squared-weight rule to compute the six-rule and three-reader tables. Here both the probabilities and the ambiguous alternative calibrations are described using unsigned sets of ordinary words. [Detailed derivation and sources](../../../research/FREEDOM-CLOCKS-AND-BIRTHS-20261120-001A/counting.md).

For fixed ordinary answers and a fixed unread word, count allowed assignments of the change marks separately for the two signs of the observed change-answer parity. The signed sum over ordered pairs in that class is the square of the difference between these two counts. Summing these squares and dividing by the number of setting-compatible change strings times the size of S gives each joint probability. Summing over the unobserved outcomes gives the required margins. The probability that a sign is plus is half of one plus its mean. For a single flip free in four of the six isolated not-all-equal words, the mean is 2/3 and the plus probability is 5/6.

For combined questions, applying one translation before evaluating an ordinary sign changes that sign once per overlapping mark. An odd overlap reverses the sign. Commuting real involutions have simultaneous signed answer records. Expanding the product of their two-outcome projectors gives precisely the signed composition counts. Equivalently, summing the squares of the differences of ordinary counts for the commuting choices proves nonnegativity even if the actions have dependencies. This supplies an answer convention for the combined questions. The distinction between a native physical action and a question about that action is preserved.

Ordinary margins, compatible multiplication, independent reader choices and certainty for every globally allowed flip still admit another rule. Divide S into orbits of the flips which preserve all of S and retain matched word pairs only within an orbit. The resulting mixture is nonnegative and invariant under relabeling. On the isolated ternary not-all-equal relation it leaves the sign of the triple change certain, removes the single-change correlations and gives score 1. An additional sufficient calibration resolves each two-word orbit of the *chosen* flip, keeps ordinary conditional weights, assigns certainty to a full pair and an unbiased change answer to a singleton. The difference between the global orbits and these conditional pairs matters. No assertion that this is a globally shortest axiomatization is needed to exhibit the rival.
