## The question, and the verdict line

The owner's direction, as the thread put it to him: "a count of descent held by the structure itself, which would remove
the last supplied record" ([his request](owner_request.md)). The record it means is the stamp of A247: each new knot's
parent steps from the start counted in threes, written by whoever runs the growth (the least-record study's word is
"generation").

**Verdict:** *shown in the structure's places; written by the law; relative to a start supplied flat.* The supplied record
is moved into the law, not removed.

## Argument 1. The restart lemma (argued, not refereed)

**Hypotheses.**
- (R1) A law L that makes births, with any ration, treatment, release rule, extras, number of holders or taking rule.
- (R2) A class 𝒮 of allowed starts, stated in terms of what a start may carry (DH 1.2: for the program's two-holder laws,
  knots of at least three places, one or two holders per distinction, no barred distinction). This statement is supplied
  (DH 10), with its swap: a start may carry barred places.
- (R3) A start S0 in 𝒮 and a structure T reached from S0 under L after at least one birth, such that T is, up to renaming
  with marks kept, a member of 𝒮.
- (R4) A population Π of pairs (structure, record of parents) that contains both (T, its record from S0) and (T, as a start:
  no parents).

**Conclusion.** No function of the structure, at any distance, names the parent places correctly on every member of Π.
More exactly: at every parent place of T, any such function is wrong in one member or the other, so it makes at least as
many errors on Π as T has pairs (new knot, parent).

*Argument.* At a parent place of T the place is a parent place in the first member and a place between two start knots in
the second; any function of the structure gives one answer at both.

**Three ways a structure carries two records,** each enough for the conclusion: (i) the restart, as above; (ii) two starts
of the class reaching one structure, up to renaming, with different records; (iii) a renaming of one structure onto itself
that exchanges a parent place with one that is not (M1 below, and the 25 structures of the drafter's check).

**The consequence.** A positive result needs, under the laws it names, a population in which no structure carries two
records. Naming a class of starts that no reached structure belongs to is necessary for that, not sufficient.

**The complete-round extension.** Where the round uses only the present structure (SD's round does: groups, owing,
shortness, births, demand and the fresh count use usable loose distinctions, met sets and counts, never the record or the
round number; SD lines 34-77), and T is the structure after a complete round, the growth from T as a start equals the
growth from S0 after T, round by round, names included (seen in scratch: equal names in 57 of 57 tests, four laws, five
starts, t = 1, 2, 3). Then every later structure also carries two records. M1's structure and the 515 below are structures
within round 1, so for them only the one-structure conclusion is claimed.

**Its smallest instance, M1.** Under each recorded ration with the numbers a below, the lone start knot of a places makes a
offshoots in round 1, each with demand a − 1. If the ration gives the first offshoot a − 1 fresh distinctions, the
structure after that one birth has two knots of a places, each with one shared and a − 1 usable loose places. Exchanging
the two knots and their loose distinctions, the shared distinction fixed, is a renaming that exchanges the offshoot's place
to its parent with the start knot's place to its child; and the structure is an allowed start. **Ten cases:** ORD with
a = 3, 4, 5; CAP-3 with a = 3, 4; RATION-3 with a = 4; RATION-4 with a = 5; FLOOR1, FLOOR0 and ONE-FEWER with a = 3. Any
treatment and release rule (round 1 has no group); with or without FILLERS (there f = D, so nothing is barred); not under
NO-OFFSHOOT, which makes no birth. For a = 3 the structure is the record's named two-knot start `[[0,1,2],[0,3,4]]`,
reached under ORD, CAP-3, FLOOR1, FLOOR0 and ONE-FEWER. M1 is about one start per ration: it refutes any one function meant
to name the parents on every structure reached from every allowed start under those rations, and says nothing about a
population confined to one start's histories.

**The record's own population carries two records** (scratch, to be checked by the run). Of the 3,348 census starts of up to
five knots kept under parity, 515 are, up to renaming with marks kept, the structure made of a census start and a prefix (in
the engine's order of new knots) of its round 1's new knots, under some recorded law; the sources are the census starts of at
most four knots (the 220 kept under parity and all 223 give the same 515). 145 to 234 by law. Of the 515, 4 are the
structure after a complete round 1 under some law; 511 are within round 1 only. Taking any parent-closed subset of round 1's
new knots in place of a prefix gives 1,234. So, without a stamp, no claim pooled over the census and admitting structures
within round 1 can hold at any d.

**What this does to the earlier studies.** It does not touch the stamp result of A247: the stamp is written from the record,
so a restart is stamped all 0. It fixes the reading of "on every structure reached": every structure of the listed runs, each
stamped from its own start; "a start knot is 0" is the supplied item.

**The drafter's check of the lemma, as far as it went (scratch, unchecked by the run).** The conclusion has no
counterexample once both members are in Π; only (R3) can fail. A search over eight laws and six small starts (structures after
complete rounds 1 to 3 of at most 40 knots, and half-built structures of the start and a prefix of round 1's new knots of at
most 12 knots) was cut at 20 minutes with 26 of its 48 cases of law and start finished, 176 structures. 173 are allowed
starts; the 3 that are not carry a barred distinction (FLOOR1 with retiring); that FILLERS and the closing exception can also
exclude a structure follows from the definition and was not seen. 148 of the 173 have no renaming exchanging a parent place
with one that is not, and 25 have one; the cut removed the slow, symmetric cases, which are the likelier to have one, so the
148 is biased upward. The restart grew as the continuation by a signature match in 68 of 68 tests (not an isomorphism test).

**Narrowed with it.**
- **L3.** Under a ration that is a function of the new knot's number of parents p and demand D alone, at a birth that is not
  a closing birth and whose (p, D) equals that of a parent that is not a start knot, the two knots have equal numbers of
  places, so no rule on the two counts can answer differently from the two sides of the place between them. Its scope is
  each such birth.
- **M2.** On the census of starts of up to five knots (each counted once up to isomorphism; 3,348 kept under parity), under
  each of ten recorded laws, through 8 rounds or 3,000 knots: every start that reaches a knot at three parent steps has some
  birth whose number of places is not one more, counted in threes, than some parent's. Seen, not argued; the threshold of
  three parent steps was chosen after the scratch run.
- **Withdrawn:** "every ration of p and D alone".

## Argument 2. Counting places in threes, written by the ration (argued, not refereed)

**The laws (arm W).** On a base law B (SD's form, two holders, no taking rule, RELEASE or HOLD), a new knot x with parents
y_1, ..., y_p and base count F receives s more fresh distinctions, s the least of 0, 1, 2 that makes x's count one more,
counted in threes, than its parents' common count (DH 3.2): NEXT3-PLACES (usable), whose count is the number of places and
whose s distinctions are usable; NEXT3-PLACES (barred), the same count with the s distinctions barred at birth; NEXT3-BARRED,
whose count is the number of barred places, with the s distinctions barred at birth. Under parity a closing birth receives
nothing, as under B (DH 3.2 step 5).

**The place rule** at a shared place of x whose other holder is y: "a parent place" exactly when y's count is one less than
x's, counted in threes; it uses the two holders' counts (shown within 1). No knot uses it; only the confirming run applies it.

**H1. Hypotheses.** (a) every two start knots that meet have the same count in threes (for NEXT3-BARRED, the convention that a
start has no barred place); (b′) every birth's parents are at numbers of parent steps that agree counted in threes; (c) no
knot's count changes after its birth (a start knot's, after the start) by a number not divisible by 3; (d) no closing birth
under parity at which s would be more than 0. **Conclusion:** within each connected piece of the start, every knot's count is
the piece's common start count plus its parent steps, counted in threes, and the place rule is right at every shared place
of every structure of the run and of every parent-closed part of one (DH 4.4). **Conversely,** given (a), (c) and (d), if
(b′) fails, the first birth where it fails has parents of different counts, and the place rule is wrong at one at least of
its places to its parents. So, given (a), (c) and (d), the place rule is right on a run exactly when (b′) holds. For the
place-count forms (c) is automatic: right exactly when (a), (b′) and (d).

*Argument.* Induction on births in order of rounds. A join's parents lie in one connected piece of the start, since
joinable members have a common knot. Before the first birth the knots of a piece have one count c (by (a), pieces being
connected through meetings). If every earlier knot has count c plus its parent steps, and a birth's parents agree in parent
steps counted in threes, they have one count, and the ration makes the new knot's count one more, which is c plus its parent
steps (one more than the greatest of its parents', which agrees with each counted in threes). By (c) counts keep their
count in threes. At a shared place the holders are parent and child (counts one apart, the right way) or two start knots
(equal). The rule's verdict at a place uses only the two holders' counts, fixed at birth, so it is the same in every
structure that holds both. For the converse: the first birth whose parents disagree in parent steps counted in threes has
parents of two different counts, and the new knot's count is one more than at most one of them.

*(b′) against (b).* (b), equal parent steps, implies (b′). The census counts below are of (b) failing; they equal the counts of
(b′) failing there only because no birth's two parents differ by three parent steps (the greatest difference seen, child less
parent, is 3). Under RELEASE, (b) is LQ's sketch (every knot acts in at most one round; the leak is a start knot that waits
while one it meets acts). It does not hold on the census (history lines): under the four waiting bases parents at different
parent steps occur on 510, 510, 510 and 223 of 3,348 starts; under holding with retiring or offshoots on 369 and 462, where no
knot waits.

*Who can see (b′) fail.* Given (a), (c) and (d), the first birth at which (b′) fails is a birth whose parents' counts differ,
which DH 3.2 step 3 counts: the ration meets it. What no knot has afterwards is which holders are its parents. This gives
the run a free cross-check: the births counted at step 3 against the births whose parents' parent steps disagree.

*(c), the general rule.* Only retiring changes a count after birth here. For NEXT3-BARRED let δ(u) be the barred places
retiring added to knot u, counted in threes, and e = δ(y) − δ(x). At x's place to y the place rule is wrong exactly when y is a
parent of x and e ≢ 0; or y is a child of x and e ≡ 1; or both are start knots and e ≡ 2. (Under HOLD a retired knot can have
children, and two meeting knots can both retire; the rule covers both.) Seen in scratch: the predicted and actual wrong places
equal on 16 runs (FLOOR1 with retiring, RELEASE and HOLD, eight named starts each); in them no parent of a retired knot
retired, so the case of two retired holders is not exercised.

**Population.** H1 is about every run of arm W from a start that meets (a), on which (b′), (c) and (d) hold. The census strata
count them. **No structure reached under a place-count form meets (a),** by the ration (its last new knot and a parent have
counts one apart), except a structure closed by a closing birth under parity, which grows no further: the cycle of three
knots with one loose place each closes, under parity, to four three-place knots, which meets (a). The two-knot start is
reached under ORD and meets (a); the exclusion is the ration's, not argument 1's.

**H3. With the s distinctions barred, the births are the base law's (argued, not refereed).** A barred distinction is never
taken and never counted in h (SD lines 17-18, 22-24, 67). Groups, owing, shortness, births, demand and the base count use only
usable distinctions and met sets, so under both barred forms every birth, its parents and its round are the base law's,
through the base law's closing round under parity. The one place a barred distinction acts is the closing exception: one
barred distinction anywhere switches it off (SD lines 72-74). So from the first s > 0 the exception is off for the rest of the
run, and **where the base law closes, the barred form grows on without end**: for those runs "no birth changes" is false
after the base law's closing round. Under NEXT3-BARRED the first birth from a start with no barred place leaves a barred
distinction (s = 1 without FILLERS; a barred filler or s with it). H3 needs shortness counted on usable places: SD lines 71-73
say the pilot's count of all loose places agrees only because a member holding barred places acts once, which fails here.

**So the barred forms are the stamp of A247 written in barred distinctions.** Without FILLERS, NEXT3-BARRED's write rule is the
stamp's rule word for word (one more than the parents', counted in threes; a start knot is 0, here as "a start has no barred
place"), and H3 is the proof that it factors out of the growth.

**What does change under the barred forms.** Nothing but the ration uses the count; no birth depends on the comparison.
- A knot with a barred added place stays live for good.
- Under parity such a knot never constrains its shared places against one another, and under not-all-equal its loose place
  satisfies it; so the allowed states change. Seen (scratch, 12 named starts × 7 bases, 8 rounds or 3,000 knots): the share of
  new knots with s > 0 is 0.62 under NEXT3-PLACES (barred) and 0.78 under NEXT3-BARRED, 0.25 to 1 by run.
- The closing exception is off from the first s > 0, and a structure that would close goes on growing.
- SD's sameness counts barred loose places (k, line 29), so returns, periods and census kinds change. If a part returns with
  period T under the base law, and under the renaming that makes the returned part the same as the first each member moves
  the same number q of parent steps, and the part's members agree in parent steps counted in threes, then with barred marks it
  returns every T · 3 / gcd(3, q) rounds (otherwise that is only an upper bound, as a renaming can absorb the shift). Seen with
  descent checked on the rings of five and seven knots with three loose places each, under the two and the three added rules:
  T = 2, q = 1, period 6 under both barred forms. What the members show is parent steps counted in threes, which equals the
  number of returns only because each return is one parent step on. The ration wrote it, and nothing but the ration uses it.
  (For the count-of-returns study: any annotation in barred places changes periods as SD defines sameness.)

**The usable form** is in the price list: its added distinctions become offshoots ([FRESH-COUNT-20261118-001](../FRESH-COUNT-20261118-001/fresh_count.md):
a count rule only places offshoots), so its births differ from the base law's. With FILLERS it bars the added distinctions
whenever F + s > D, and then equals the barred form at those births.

**Knot test.** Hypotheses: two holders; every new knot that is not a closing birth receives at least one fresh distinction
(true where F_B ≥ 1, which covers every registered base); under parity a closing birth receives nothing (DH 3.2 step 5).
Then every new knot passes under parity: a closing birth as under the base law, and any other new knot because the loose
distinctions it takes are not all the loose distinctions of the structure, so its rule is the only constraint on its places
(`research/RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001A/preregistration.md` lines 98-103). The hypothesis on closing births is needed:
given fresh distinctions, a closing birth fails under parity, its taken places being forced even by the other knots (seen:
the cycle of three knots with one loose place each, under FLOOR0, FLOOR1 and ORD, each count form). Under not-all-equal
there is no closing exception; that every new knot passes is seen in scratch (51,063 knots in 162 runs), not argued here.

**Numberings.** Argued for every numbering (every step uses counts and met sets; which usable loose distinction goes to which
birth is a renaming, SD lines 76-77; s uses counts only). No difference found in 89,910 scratch runs (every numbering of four
starts of at most six distinctions, and the 4n and the 200 registered numberings of five others, 4 rounds).

## The three tests

For a law and a stated class of starts, each answer exhaustive:

1. **Inputs.** Does some input of the ration determine the parents' counts that the place rule compares? *Written*: yes (arm
   W). *Not written*: no input does (every recorded ration: p and D only).
2. **Factoring.** Delete the rule (s = 0 always). *Factors*: births, parents and rounds are the same with and without it (the
   barred forms, H3, through the base law's closing round). *Does not factor*: they differ (the usable form). *No rule*: the law
   has none (the recorded laws).
3. **Restart.** *Two records*: some structure of the population carries two records (by restart, by two starts, or by a
   renaming); then by argument 1 nothing the structure shows names the parents on that population. *One record*: none does.

The order would be **made**, in the only sense this note admits, if inputs says *not written*, the class of starts is stated
without the count, the population is *one record*, and some function of the look within a fixed d names every parent place on
every structure of every round through R, on runs several parent steps past the start, and again through round R + 1. No law
here does that: the recorded laws are *not written* and, on the record's populations, *two records*; arm W is *written*. With
every allowed start admitted, the restart test answers *two records* wherever a reached structure carries no mark a start may
not carry, so "made" with nothing supplied about starts is ruled out by argument 1, once it is refereed.

**Parked as an idea, not designed:** from a class of starts fixed without the count (lone knots, say), whether some ration with
no parent count among its inputs shows the order at a fixed distance.
