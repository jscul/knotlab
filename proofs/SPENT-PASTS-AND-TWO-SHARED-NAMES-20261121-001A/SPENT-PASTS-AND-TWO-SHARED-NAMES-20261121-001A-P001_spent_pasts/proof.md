# Which pasts can change a reader's chances?

For a fixed structure and the positive preparation tested here, a loose word with \(n\) compatible spent words receives ordinary probability proportional to \(n\) and proposed erased probability proportional to \(n^2\). The equality of all ordinary old-word probabilities after growth has a simple criterion. Every old word must have the same positive number of extensions. An observer seeing only a part satisfies a weaker criterion. For coherent addition the criterion changes again, since two different inputs can contribute to the same visible output. A funded not-all-equal comparison below gives a difference at a local bank of genuinely loose outputs arbitrarily many holder meetings from the change of remote offers. The compared banks have the same local input density under the ordinary source reading. A complete unannounced remote operation cannot change a fixed input's local law.

## Counts on the same physical words

Let \(A(s,e)\in\{0,1\}\) test a given full written word, with its twice-held names \(s\) and once-held names \(e\). A past here is one assignment of spent names. Two construction histories ending in that assignment count once. Write \(n_e=\sum_s A(s,e)\), \(N=\sum_e n_e\) and \(W=\sum_e n_e^2\). The zero support supplies no launch. A closed nonempty stage has one empty loose word with probability one. Uniform full words and the proposed erased words give the two chances in the opening. On the same entire end these distributions agree exactly when all positive \(n_e\) are equal, by dividing their proposed equality by \(n_e\). On a subset of loose names, sum the respective *full* chances over the other names. Squaring after forgetting additional loose names would perform a different reading.

For the supplied change question, ordinary retention gives the reduced real density \(D_{ef}=\sum_s A(s,e)A(s,f)/N\). The coherent clean reading gives \(Q_{ef}=n_e n_f/W\). The full densities coincide precisely when the original support is a product between the spent and loose words, on their nonempty supports. Indeed equality with \(Q\) forces rank one for the Gram matrix \(A^T A\). All nonzero binary rows of \(A\) must then be identical. The converse is direct. Equal diagonal entries alone need no product property. On the parity first stage of an original ternary, all supported loose words have one past. A supplied change on one loose name at each of two different children has mean **zero** with the written inputs retained and **one** on the clean end. In the first calculation each old fixed parent bit prevents that change within its child. After summing the pasts the complete loose parity law admits the joint two-bit flip everywhere. [Exact common-word control](../../../research/SPENT-PASTS-AND-TWO-SHARED-NAMES-20261121-001A/controls.py)

On a connected parity holding with \(v\) knots and \(d\) twice-held names, fix a loose word. Each spent column toggles the equations at its two distinct holders. Such columns span precisely the vectors of even total parity on the holders. Every vector in their span is even, and adding the columns along a chain toggles any specified two end holders, so the reverse inclusion follows from connectedness. The rank is \(v-1\), including one isolated holder of rank zero. The loose word must have the same total parity as the sum of the right-hand sides, and every such loose word has \(2^{d-v+1}\) compatible pasts. A closed component instead has the unique empty loose word when its overall equation is consistent. Each open component contributes its own parity equation and independent multiplicity. A single loose bit in a connected abstract parity relation would be determined; the genuine canonical source and reaching rules impose their own restrictions. A contradictory closed signed component gives no normalization. This extends the earlier connected loose-bond statement to multiplicities and component conventions. [Connected support and the closing criterion](../../../research/BOND-CLOSURE-20261120-001/criterion.md)

If \(k\) spent bits are coherently measured in the supplied change basis, character \(h\) has unnormalized exterior amplitudes
\[
 b_h(e)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^kN}}\sum_s(-1)^{h\cdot s}A(s,e).
\]
The all-plus branch therefore succeeds with probability \(W/(2^kN)\). Orthogonality of the characters gives \(\sum_h b_hb_h^T=D\), including the other, potentially signed, branches. A physically copied past tag \(t\) replaces \(n_e n_f\) on the clean port by \(\sum_t n_{t,e}n_{t,f}\) before normalization. This holds even when the tag is lost to the reader. Coherently restoring every tag changes the instrument. A second, fully classical comparison draws **two independent full words** and selects equal complete loose strings. It gives \(n_e^2/W\) on acceptance with launch probability \(W/N^2\). Its classical retained records give no unique X-reading law. A diagonal preparation with those chances gives every genuinely changed bit a fair answer independently of the ordinary outcomes on the other bits. A supplied all-clean herald, a deterministic change of postulate, an unannounced complete instrument and this collision experiment have different trial records. ## An all-stage bound for three loose readers on one actual knot

Suppose three measured loose names are all held by one not-all-equal knot. If it has at least one further name, fix the other loose values. Each compatible spent exterior gives the six mixed three-bit words exactly the same amplitude. It can forbid at most one of the two constant triples, since at least one other value belongs to that same knot. Discard exteriors already forbidden by an unrelated knot. In a nonempty exterior sector, after summing the spent alternatives the resulting eight amplitudes in lexicographic order are proportional to
\[
(a,1,1,1,1,1,1,b),\qquad 0\le a,b\le1,\quad a+b\ge1 .
\]
The unobserved loose exterior is traced, so the complete local density is a probability mixture of these normalized sectors. For any one sector the XXX numerator is \(2ab+6\). The numerator for each of the three settings changing one place and reading two is \(2a+2b-4\). The signed score is
\[
M=\frac{2ab+18-6a-6b}{a^2+b^2+6}\le \frac{25}{13}<2.
\]
The numerator is positive. With \(s=a+b\), multiplying the upper bound by the positive denominator leaves exactly \(6(s-1)(s+14)+19(a-b)^2\) after clearing the factor 13. Equality in the scalar bound requires \(a=b=1/2\). For the ordinary retained-past reading each fixed exterior has either one forbidden homogeneous triple or none, giving score \(12/7\) or 1. Mixtures preserve both bounds. On a single ternary with all three names loose, all exteriors factor and both homogeneous words are forbidden, so the score is 3. In a connected source this is the isolated ternary. Under parity every setting in this score changes an odd number of names. In the complete written relation at least one parity row is violated, and in the clean loose relation at least one component parity changes. Thus all the corresponding moments vanish even for three separate loose locations.

Every newborn has at least one spent parent input. An older holder has no loose name left after a successful round. Therefore **every operative same-holder triple at every positive ordinary growth age is below two** under both not-all-equal preparations. This also covers large or censored growth histories. It makes no claim that a collection of different holders has the same bound, or that a score below two certifies locality of an entire eight-setting table. In the built four-place source, four first-generation holders really do supply three loose marks each. Their not-all-equal scores are \(12/7\) and \(336474/175063\) for retained and erased pasts, with the latter below \(25/13\). In the isolated ternary itself, the three original loose readers score 3 initially and do not have a same-holder loose read at the first grown stage. Three *different* child holders do have a table then. Their signed scores are \(10/27\) and \(54/95\), with a common explanation for the full table in each case. Both denominators, all full tables and every subsequent eligible or absent age have independent replays. ## Whose old chances survive?

For ordinary words \(x\) with prior \(\mu\) and a physical event having \(c(x)\) distinct child words, reweighting by the number of children gives probability \(\mathbb E_\mu[1_E c]/\mathbb E_\mu c\) to an old event \(E\) when the denominator is positive. For the uniform old prior this is the old marginal of uniform new full words. For an arbitrary prior it defines the explicitly tilted comparison, which need not be uniform over new words. The event is stable precisely when \(\operatorname{Cov}_\mu(1_E,c)=0\). A fixed old record \(r\) is stable in distribution precisely when \(\mathbb E[c\mid r]\) is the same global mean on every occupied record. Allow *every complete old-word event*, with positive old support everywhere, and one recovers the positive constant-extension criterion. Equal division among each old word's children instead keeps whatever old prior survives. A dead old word has its own refused mass. A collection of many fair singletons can fail to determine a nontrivial joint change. This ordinary theorem was already established by the preceding study. [Scoped earlier result](../../../research/UNREAD-COMPANION-AND-ORDER-FREE-WEIGHTING-20261121-001B/results.md)

A full successful round consumes every available old loose name. Each eligible group pays one distinct name per parent. A shortage stalls the *whole* round. On success the rule makes one unary birth for each remaining loose name and uses it. Every old loose name then has two holders. Every newly loose name is fresh. Thus a literally identical name cannot serve as a loose ordinary before-and-after record across a successful complete round. A stall adds no stage. It can leave the old name available without having made the proposed birth. Holding a retained pointer, comparing equivalent names at the same age, and reading an old name already spent give three different experiments. The all-size argument applies under both laws. [Actual allocation rule](../../../research/ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/end_operator.md)

Let \(B(e,y)\) count allowed alternatives from old end \(e\) to the new end \(y\). In these ordinary rounds \(B\) is the product of binary child predicates on disjoint fresh sets. All old loose distinctions have become past values. With no further records the new past count is exactly \(n'_y=\sum_e n_e B(e,y)\). This follows by partitioning old complete words on \(e\), hence extends by matrix multiplication to every fixed funded finite history. Since all old loose words become spent, the new retained-past matrix is
\[
D'_{yy'}=\frac{\sum_e n_e B(e,y)B(e,y')}{\sum_{e,y}n_e B(e,y)}
\]
for the ordinary binary incidence. Equality of the complete old continuation in an off-diagonal term fixes the same old loose and spent words on both sides, giving precisely the factor \(n_e\). Thus the loose count vector and the *actual paid allocation* suffice for both new readings. This does not make the allocation itself recoverable from the loose count vector. Separate structural branch choices and repeated histories are not additional words. If choosing a different kind of history is part of the preparation, its amplitudes, probabilities or tags must be given separately.

For a finite unaltered spectator record \(r\), let \(f_r(e)\) be the old nonnegative amplitudes in its block. The old squared mass is \(w_r=\|f_r\|^2\) and the new one is \(w'_r=\|f_r B\|^2\). A fixed record distribution is stable if and only if the ratios \(w'_r/w_r\) are common on its occupied blocks, with all lost mass reported. For a single event only the summed masses of the event and complement need the same ratio. For *every* nonnegative preparation and every classical spectator partition the criterion is \(BB^T=\lambda I\) on the declared input, with \(\lambda>0\). To prove necessity, put two test amplitudes in distinct spectator blocks. Their relative masses can change only through their two norm ratios. Single basis words first give the common diagonal value of \(BB^T\). Taking the positive coherent sum of any two basis words in a test block then gives a zero off-diagonal entry. Sufficiency follows by multiplication. On a restricted set of preparations only their quadratic forms are tested. With binary entries equal child counts specify the diagonal; distinct inputs also need disjoint possible outputs for that whole class. With general nonnegative multiplicities the diagonal itself is the *squared* row norm. This derivation uses no signed trial weights.

The actual single not-all-equal ternary with three funded unary births shows why equal extensions are insufficient for the clean comparison. Each of its six full words has 27 extensions. A physical copy of the old predicate “the first two bits agree” retains chance \(1/3\) with ordinary full growth and has clean-conditioned chance **7/33**. Copy the full two-bit word instead, group equal answers *afterward*, and the clean probability of equality is **3/13**. In the first case alternatives inside each copied predicate value still add; in the second the orthogonal pair labels distinguish more histories. Copy all three original bits and their entire old law is unchanged, since every branch now records its input and its child count is constant. Every individual bit remains fair with the complement-symmetric NAE source. Attachment across a single boundary bit likewise gives cases where the ordinary old density is preserved by equal exterior completion counts while a traced versus erased boundary changes the local clean answer. A disconnected closed companion multiplies all counts by one constant per independent component, preserving both local readings. [Exact record, one-interface and product controls](../../../research/SPENT-PASTS-AND-TWO-SHARED-NAMES-20261121-001A/controls.py)

For any offered set of questions on a local subsystem, equality of the old and new answers is exactly the vanishing of every offered effect on the difference of the two reduced densities. A complete density test implies it for every local question. Separate Z/X choices need not identify an entire joint real density. For instance the two real two-bit states \((I\pm Y\otimes Y)/4\) have the same probabilities for all product X/Z settings, while one joint real measurement distinguishes them. This is a comparison about the named effect set. A purported universal over unspecified readers has no single fixed quantifier.

## A funded far comparison with four loose output names

Take an even integer \(m=2k\ge6\). Old holders \(0,1,\ldots,m+1\) share two names between each consecutive pair. A spent holder meets each end on two additional names. The two ends hold separate loose names \(p,q\). Add two loose names \(u,v\) to holder \(h=k\). In arm A these are all four loose names. In arm B a new ternary meets the first end at \(p\) and has two other loose names. This neutral attachment extends every full old core word the same number of times, three under not-all-equal and two under parity. Its old word records and its selection of which names are loose do change. All old local ternaries and wider holders are actual projections of the respective sources. For NAE, fix any desired local allowed word and make every unspecified doubled meeting mixed, satisfying its neighboring holders. The one new remote ternary has two adjustable bits at its source. In the reached forms the added local and remote children each have fresh choices. Conversely fix their allowed word and choose their old paid inputs on the original source, with every unspecified doubled link mixed. The other children have nonempty extensions. Under parity, projection by the connected-component argument gives every permitted local parity row; after specifying one old holder's string the remaining checks are solvable by propagation up a connected remainder or a remaining loose bit on each separated portion. In particular when removing the endpoint adjoining the cap, the separated cap side has loose outputs of its own. New parent incidences do not impose any additional old restriction in these nonclosing funded rounds. The exact projected-word certificates test every individual source and reached row for each measured instance. [Actual structures, grants and canonical projection checks](../../../research/SPENT-PASTS-AND-TWO-SHARED-NAMES-20261121-001A/far_growth.py)

Only the far ends in A share a common old witness among the live holders. They generate one binary birth on \(p,q\) with one fresh place. In B the attachment makes the original near endpoint spent; no two remaining far and central live holders have a common witness. The far loose inputs feed three unary births. In both arms \(h\) has exactly two unary births on \(u,v\), with two fresh names apiece. For \(k\ge3\) it is at least three old holder meetings from the nearest remote endpoint and has no unplanned common-neighbor payment. All groups are funded and the two local new holdings have the same full ternary relation. Correspond local labels by the old input and the slot in its own child's fresh pair. In both reached arms all four are physically loose. The minimum number of meetings from the A binary birth to either of the two local child holders is \(k+2\). In B it is a different far offer structure. The local measurement reports whether the *first* fresh bit at each local child agrees with the first one at the other child.

With NAE, a unary child has three local completions per old bit. Its first fresh bit is opposite the old bit with probability \(2/3\) under the counted law conditional on the old input. The two local children are independent given \(u,v\). Thus the chance of local agreement is \(4/9+\Pr(u=v)/9\). Alternatively both output pairs are constant with probability \(1/9\); on this herald the two old bits are the opposites of the displayed pair values. The herald and its complement are both counted. At the far binary birth A each old word has one available fresh value if \(p=q\), two otherwise. Every B remote unary instead contributes a constant factor. The complete ordinary reduced matrix on \(u,v\) is identical before growth in the two arms: in an off-diagonal entry both old words coincide off \(u,v\), including \(p\), so the neutral cap supplies the same constant to every common completion. Assigning the remote births equal total weight per old input also makes the two complete local packet distributions match after the step. A new ordinary uniform full word gives a difference because the far offer's completion count is correlated with the prior local event.

Here is the all-size check on that correlation. Use link-word order \(00,01,10,11\). Let \(J\) have all entries one, let \(D=\mathrm{diag}(1,0,0,1)\), and write \(T=J-D\). At the central holder summing \(u,v\) replaces its link matrix by \(4J-D\); summing only the cases \(u=v\) gives \(2J-D\). Let \(L_i\) forbid only the all-\(i\) pair of constant links at an endpoint with its loose bit held at \(i\). For the rest around the chain set
\[
 V=T^k(L_0+L_1)T(L_0+L_1)T^{k-1},\quad
 V_- =T^k(L_0-L_1)T(L_0-L_1)T^{k-1}.
\]
Here \(X=\operatorname{tr}(JV),Y=\operatorname{tr}(DV)\); define \(Z,W_-\) the same way with \(V_-\). These stand for integer counts; the subscript on \(W_-\) distinguishes the signed sum from the squared normalization above. The old \(u=v\) probability in B is \((2X-Y)/(4X-Y)\). In A, to weight by the remote extensions, replace \(X,Y\) by \((3X-Z)/2,(3Y-W_-)/2\), because the far fresh count is \((3-(-1)^{p+q})/2\). The sign of the difference between these two probabilities is the sign of \(XW_- - ZY\).

Resolve the complement-even link combinations \((00+11)/\sqrt2,(01+10)/\sqrt2\) and the two differences. On the first pair \(T_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&2\\2&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). The unsigned middle factor in \(V\) has even block \(C=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}89&100\\100&112\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and the signed one has block \(-\mathrm{diag}(1,0)\). Their odd constant-pair eigenvalues are \(-1,+1\) respectively. The odd powers of \(T\) add \(+1\) to Y and \(-1\) to \(W_-\). Writing \((a,b)\) for the top row of \(T_0^k\), multiplication gives
\[
 XW_- - ZY=4(-2)^k(53a^2+64ab-11b^2)-2(106a^2+281ab+186b^2).
\]
For \(k\ge2\), \(1\le b/a\le4/3\): the ratio starts at two when \(k=1\) and updates by \((2+2r)/(1+2r)\), which sends \([1,2]\) into \([1,4/3]\). The first quadratic is positive. Odd \(k\) gives a strictly negative expression. For even \(k\ge2\) it is at least \(2a^2(318+231r-274r^2)>0\), using \(r\le4/3\). The ordinary difference is therefore nonzero with alternating sign for arbitrarily large separations. The independent exact transfer and the actual engine plus full-symbol elimination agree for \(m=6,8,10\). ## Selected and unconditional physical comparators

For the same finite old loose input write the matrix of positive amplitudes as \(F\) on the old local and remote inputs and normalize by its squared norm. The exact local and remote Boolean offer matrices are \(L,B\), old names indexing rows. Plain coherent selection gives exterior matrix \(L^T F B\) before normalization. The comparison with rows divided by \(\sqrt{c_i}\) gives unit row norm and the comparison with rows divided by \(c_i\) gives unit row sum. Their Gram matrices can have nonzero off-diagonals in either case. Row normalization does not make them unconditional maps on coherent superpositions.

For each of the three remote matrices \(R\), use a supplied local success operator \(K_R=R^T/\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(RR^T)}\) and complementary operator \(H_R=\sqrt{I-K_R^TK_R}\). Define the same complete instrument for the fixed local \(L\). For an all-zero success map take the failure map to be the identity. Otherwise these are well-defined because the largest eigenvalue of \(RR^T\) is at most its trace. One input on which a raw zero row refuses is retained with identity in the complementary input space. The joint output in any port pair is \(A_L F A_R^T/\|F\|\), with \(A=K\) or \(H\); its squared norm is the unconditional port probability. Separate port names keep their potentially different output spaces apart. Every nonzero full output and both reduced local outputs are fixed by the matrices. This fixture supplies the operation; observing a uniform full word does not realize it.

The manifest also records the comparison mapping \(i\) to its own orthogonal recorded label times \(\sum_y B(i,y)|y\rangle/\sqrt{c_i}\), with separate input-identifying refusals on zero rows. Even overlapping successful rows are now orthogonal on the complete terminal record. Discarding all remote tags, outcomes and flags leaves the input local density untouched, followed only by the chosen fixed local channel. In general for any remote maps \(K_j\) with \(\sum_j K_j^*K_j=I\), the unannounced local effect \(E\) has chance \(\sum_j\operatorname{tr}[(E\otimes K_j^*K_j)\rho]=\operatorname{tr}[(E\otimes I)\rho]\). This argument permits arbitrary original correlations and an arbitrary fixed local reader, including its own flags. At the five-meeting A source, the first fresh-bit equality on both selected raw-success ports has probability \(684723287857/1372153750138\), and on local success with the remote flag unannounced it has \(1215754100059/2436314758533\). The latter is identical for all three completed remote filters and the tagged map. The former requires the remote herald. An across-source or globally normalized probability difference alone is insufficient evidence for sending a signal or for an internal physical eraser.

The unannounced remote-channel identity is the usual no-signalling result for a complete local measurement. The general result predates this study. Here it separates the physical remote offers from their globally selected weightings on a named, reached source. [Ghirardi, Rimini and Weber (1980)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02817189)

The complete positive-uniform character formula was already proved in the erasure record. This study adds actual reached loose-bank scores, explicit recording-dependent counterexamples to extending the ordinary stability rule, and matched local descendants of arbitrarily distant physical birth offers. The ordinary growth law retains old distinctions and their holdings. Simply deleting them from the description loses information about who can fund a future offer. For example an isolated four-place even knot and two even ternaries meeting once have the same four-name loose count vector (one past per even loose word). The respective unary children have three siblings at one old parent or one sibling at each of two old parents; their next demands and grants differ. An end-only dynamics therefore also needs to specify which current holder and payment data survive. Equal loose-word amplitudes alone cannot determine it.
