# An arriving pulse can exchange two stored ring states under tied events

An arriving ten-distinction pulse can determine **which ring receives the state** while leaving several choices for **which four physical distinctions change next**. On the three specified arrangements every strictly single-valued, renaming-equivariant rule fails to exchange the rings. A time-independent relation supplied with a rule for matching the two rings succeeds. Every maximal sequence of its individual four-distinction events reaches the same physical assignment, including the identity of every loose distinction. The matching and the transported zero reference are part of the law. [Study definitions](definitions.md)

## Why the first loose change requires a choice

Work on the fixed, completed size-two stage. From the current co-knotted pairs one can recover each actual parity knot, its loose distinctions, the old knots and their shared distinctions. Each pair of consecutive old meetings has an ordinary four-response \(W_Q\). It changes those two old distinctions and the two private inputs of their unique new child \(Q\). Every other eligible four-response changes one private input at each of two distinct children of a common old knot, and one loose distinction at each of those children. Call the latter an exterior response. Its four physical distinctions are all real. The auxiliary exterior in the parity proof supplies no distinction. These facts use the first-layer hypotheses checked on the three starts. **Strict obstruction.** One may permute each child's loose distinctions independently, fixing every other distinction. These permutations preserve the current nearness, transported reference, stored ring and arriving pulse. They continue to fix the state after any ordinary response. The first exterior response would select one loose distinction from a pool of \(f\) siblings and one from a second pool of \(g\) siblings. Its orbit already has \(fg\ge4\) members under these permutations alone. A single-valued equivariant choice at a fixed input must be fixed. This rules out the first exterior response at every read radius, even if the pulse first moves, deforms or returns. While loose differences stay zero, subtracting the ordinary responses indicated by their private inputs leaves a unique even subset of old distinctions, the stored ring state. Ordinary firings preserve it. Allowing least two-distinction changes as well does not help a strict selector. Such a change can toggle an entire two-sibling pool, but every selected state still retains the original stabilizing permutations.

There is a stronger necessary condition if additional permanent marks are supplied. Let \(a_Q,a'_Q\) be the ordinary-response coefficients of two endpoints with loose differences zero, and \(o_Q\) the parity of the ordinary firings at \(Q\). The old pairs of the children with \(o_Q\mathbin\oplus a_Q\mathbin\oplus a'_Q=1\) sum to the difference of the stored states. Both private inputs of each such child need an odd number of exterior corrections. For two disjoint five-rings at separation \(d\), any representation of this difference needs at least \(d+5\) distinct old pairs. Pair the ten odd distinctions by trails of consecutive meetings. At least one trail crosses between rings at cost \(d+1\), and each of the other four costs at least one. Thus a successful strict physical rule would need an initially individually distinguishable loose member in **at least 9, 13 or 25 different child pools** on these respective starts, even if the final ordinary pulse is allowed to move. This is a necessary condition for independent decorations of the otherwise symmetric pools. It does not assert that those marks suffice. Summing all \(fg\) tied responses gives another well-defined operation. Each private input toggles \(fg\) times, each loose member of the first pool toggles \(g\) times, and each of the second toggles \(f\) times. Two even pool sizes give the empty change. With one odd size the whole even-sized pool toggles. With both sizes odd both private inputs and both full pools toggle. None of these complete sums is the requisite individual least four-response. A physical schedule can instead take one tied alternative and later read which loose member it changed.

## A rule that reads progress from the present state

The input has two five-rings on a path. Each ring has one knot away from its attachment with a recognizable unary child. Its nearer leg at the attachment is unique. Write \(s,t\) for the two attachments in the order indicated by the incoming pulse. The notation is for the proof. The reader tries both directions and retains the unique one whose outer approach has occupied straight-through private pairs exactly two and three places before its attachment. The private inputs of the through response at the attachment stay zero. In the supplied arrival the leading occupied location is one place inside the interval. All moves below preserve the outer approach. Reflection of the entire input reflects all these choices. The rule can use the whole connected component. In particular its progress test reads the intervening path as the separation increases. [Recognizing the through route](../MOTION-AT-EDDY-20261119-001/nearness.md)

Form an ordered list \(e_0,\ldots,e_{d+1}\) of old shared distinctions. Use the nearer leg of the first ring, the \(d\) shared path distinctions between attachments and the nearer leg of the second ring. Choose each ordinary response on a consecutive pair of this list. On each ring, removing its chosen leg leaves four consecutive distinctions. Pair the first with the second and the third with the fourth. These four additional responses bring the total to \(n=d+5\). The sum of the chosen old pairs is precisely the two whole rings. Their old pairs are linearly independent. On the long list the first old distinction appears in just the first pair, after which the next pair can be read successively. The other four pairs have disjoint supports.

For the correction at an old knot \(z\), collect the private inputs there belonging to the chosen children. Each nonempty collection has exactly two inputs, from different children. In fact the chosen children and these old knots alternate in one cycle. To see this, list the old knots met along \(e_0,\ldots,e_{d+1}\). The child on a consecutive pair connects the knots two positions apart, giving an even-position chain and an odd-position chain. On each ring the two additional responses connect those two chains' ends through one further old knot. Hence there are exactly \(n\) old knots to correct.

From the protected approach and the recovered knots, form \(x_0\), the ten-distinction arrival plus the first whole ring, measured from the transported zero. This is an input formula in the rule. It requires no saved row. Compare the present state with \(x_0\).

1. Decode \(w_Q\) from the differences on the old distinctions using the independent old pairs. For the long list the differences are \(w_0,w_0\oplus w_1,\ldots,w_{d-1}\oplus w_d,w_d\). The other four pairs give four coefficients directly. Reject an inconsistent reading.
2. At either private input of its chosen child \(Q\) at \(z\), read its current difference from \(x_0\), then add \(w_Q\) modulo two. Call the result \(u_z\). Both inputs at \(z\) must agree on \(u_z\). A value \(u_z=1\) requires both incident \(w\)'s to be one. A child joining \(z,z'\) must have exactly \(u_z\oplus u_{z'}\) occupied loose distinctions. All unselected differences must vanish.
3. Permit each ordinary response with \(w_Q=0\). Permit an exterior response at \(z\) if \(u_z=0\) and both its \(w\)'s are one. At either of its children use its unique occupied loose distinction if present, and otherwise allow every loose sibling. If the protected orientation or the checks fail, this exchange rule has no fire.

An ordinary event changes exactly its \(w_Q\) from zero to one. Its two private changes cancel the same coefficient in the two readings of \(u\). An exterior event changes exactly its \(u_z\) from zero to one. A child's first such event marks one loose sibling. Its other event uses that same sibling and clears it. Every move passes the checks again. Initially all \(2n\) indicators are zero. If any \(w\) is zero its ordinary response is available. Once all \(w\)'s are one, every remaining \(u=0\) has an available correction. Each event increases the sum of the indicators by exactly one. Consequently **every maximal schedule ends after exactly \(2n\) events**. At the end all private inputs have their arrival values and all loose distinctions have zero difference. The old changes equal the two rings. This restores the identical ten-distinction packet and exchanges the stored state. The endpoint is identical even before quotienting by names. The proof allows a correction before unrelated ordinary firings. It needs neither delay bounds nor scheduler fairness.

The predecessor's lower bound applies to this same pair of endpoints. At least \(n\) ordinary responses must fire oddly to realize the old differences. Each has two private inputs that must be restored. Each exterior response repairs at most two. The counts 18, 26 and 50 are therefore minimal in the full physical four-event menu. The ring supports and their growth stay fixed. During a write the complete packet need not be visible in its original shape. The protected flank and its reconstruction of \(x_0\) suffice.

## Counting all intermediate states

Modulo permutations of loose siblings, a reachable state is determined exactly by the sets \(\{Q:w_Q=0\}\) and \(\{z:u_z=1\}\). No adjacent pair in the alternating \(2n\)-cycle belongs to their union. Conversely any such selection is attainable. Fire exactly the required ordinary responses, then the required corrections. Thus these selections are the independent sets of that cycle. With \(F_0=0,F_1=1\), the number is \(F_{2n-1}+F_{2n+1}\) by conditioning on one position. The number of one-event transitions after identifying siblings is \(2nF_{2n-1}\). Each transition deletes or adds a particular position, and with that position selected its two neighbors must be absent, leaving a path of length \(2n-3\) in numbers of positions.

All literal states can also be counted without enumerating them. Fix \(u_z,u_{z'}\) for two consecutive old knots in the cycle. Their intervening child \(Q\) has \(f_Q\) loose members. Two zero indicators allow two ordinary coefficients; unequal indicators force \(w_Q=1\) and allow any one loose member; two ones force \(w_Q=1\) and clear all loose members. The choices at different children can be realized independently by the first-use and reuse rule. Summing the product around the cycle gives

\[
\#\{\text{physical states}\} =
 \operatorname{tr}\prod_{Q\ {\rm cyclic}}\begin{pmatrix}2&f_Q\\ f_Q&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

## Approach, release and scope

The exchange-only rule stops at the reconstructed endpoint. It can be composed explicitly with the preceding four-phase rule on the recovered through route. Give an enabled exchange priority. Otherwise let the through rule transport an isolated four-phase pulse in the closed ordinary coefficients. To check for unintended writes, use coordinates only in this argument. For a clean through pulse accepted by the exchange's protected checks, the first two occupied locations in the tested orientation must be \(s-3,s-2\), with both \(s-1,s\) vacant. Among the four phases and their reflections this occurs only for the forward arrival \(\{s-3,s-2,s+1\}\). Indeed the other forward phases occupy \(s-1\). Among the reflected phases only \(\{0,1,3,4,5\}\) starts with two consecutive occupied locations, and it occupies \(s\) after that displacement. Once the switch finishes, the next ordinary fire occupies \(s-1\), and the pulse can proceed in the original direction. It cannot arrive again in the opposite orientation on the same pass. On the long start the literal predecessor reader reached the arrival after 104 ordinary events from a wholly exterior launch. Its first two translations from the released state cost another eight ordinary events and preserved the receiving ring.
