# Why only three- and four-leaf founders can close

The calculation treats a founder as a tree of ordinary knots whose meetings each consist of one shared distinction. At a knot of degree \(d\), the permitted loose count is \(\max(0,3-d)+b\) with \(b\in\{0,1\}\). The all-size exclusion below applies to this specific support rule and the ordinary funded birth law. The parent investigation independently proposed the exclusion. We tested it against every decorated class at each registered size *after* computing the full four-cut outcomes. ## A necessary condition at any finite age

Call a holder live when it has a loose distinction. Among live holders, join two when they have a common physical witness (one holder meeting both). Each multi-parent group lies inside a single connected component of this relation. Each outstanding unspent opening produces a unary child in the component of its one parent. A paid cut consumes every old loose distinction. Children can be related at the next end only if their groups had a parent in common. Thus descendants of different initial components of live holders never combine. Every nonempty component has at least one child in each funded cut. In a connected physical holding with two or fewer holders per name, a birth costs zero precisely when its inputs are every loose distinction in the physical holding. This follows by summing old parity rows: to cancel a shared name its two holders must have the same inclusion coefficient, which propagates throughout the physical component. A funded cut with at least two surviving groups therefore gives every child at least one fresh name. Distinct initial components of live holders prevent a closing birth at any finite age.

A tree admits two holder classes, with each original meeting between different classes. Live holders with a common witness are two original meetings apart, hence in the same class. To close, all live holders must lie in one of the two classes, say \(U\), and be connected by common witnesses. Every leaf is live under the declared opening rule. Every internal holder of \(U\) is live as well: deleting a missing internal holder separates two branches of the original tree that each contain a leaf. A walk through live holders with common witnesses lifts to a walk alternating a live member of \(U\) and a witness in the opposite class. It cannot cross the deleted member of \(U\), so the leaves could not have belonged to one live component. All holders in the opposite class are spent and have degree at least three.

At the first cut, any three live holders pairwise sharing witnesses have one common witness. Indeed, fix two of them. Their two-step path has a unique middle holder. A third at distance two from both must meet that middle holder, by uniqueness of paths in a tree. This extends to every pairwise joined group of at least three. Such groups are already witnessed as wholes, so the initial multi-parent proposals are exactly the witnessed pairs. The initial demand at a live \(u\in U\) is
\[
 D(u)=\sum_{w\text{ meeting }u}(\deg w-1).
\]
Each term counts different partners of \(u\); two distinct witnesses serving the same pair would contradict the tree condition. If \(d=\deg u\ge2\), then \(D(u)\ge2d\ge4\). Yet its loose count is at most two. The whole cut stalls. Consequently every holder of \(U\) must be a leaf in any founder that can close. Connectedness then leaves a single central spent holder and its leaves. With \(l\) leaves each leaf owes \(l-1\) pair births and has at most three loose names. Thus \(l\le4\). In particular **no tree in this source family with at least six starting knots can close at any later finite cut**.

There is a useful independent check of the other endpoint, failing to fund a cut. At a general tree founder let \(r_w\) be the number of live neighbours of \(w\). For each live \(u\), the initial demand is the sum of \(r_w-1\) over its neighbours \(w\), by the same common-witness argument. Compare this sum directly with the opening count. After the first paid cut, either the parity system has already closed or every child has received at least its next multi-parent demand. The funded lookahead computed exactly those demands from shared parents and the new loose counts cover every one. This repeats at each nonclosing paid cut under both laws. Every actual funding stop is therefore an initial stop. Every decorated class in the census was checked against the initial demand formula.

For independent extra-opening probability \(q\), the conditional-on-size four-cut rates therefore have a short expression. Write \(C_n\) for any closure and \(J_n\) for an admitting closure. At size four the star has four labelled realizations among sixteen trees. At size five the four-leaf star has five labelled realizations among 125 trees. This gives
\[
 C_4=\frac{(1-q)(1-q^3)}4,\quad J_4=\frac{3q^2(1-q)^2}{4},\qquad
 C_5=J_5=\frac{(1-q)q^4}{25}.
\]
For \(n\ge6\) both are zero. The treatment of the fully decorated three-leaf case here uses the four-cut horizon. No claim about its later stages is needed. Under equal size weights, the mixture conditional is \((J_4+J_5)/(C_4+C_5)\) when the denominator is positive. The per-size conditionals cannot be averaged.

## Why counting one representative per class is exact

Each Prüfer word of length \(n-2\) describes one labelled tree. The independent code explicitly decodes every such word at each size, compares its named meetings with the trees generated by repeatedly adding a leaf everywhere, and checks that both give exactly the same set. Simultaneously it builds all coloured rooted tree words by sorting the words of the child branches. Equal rooted words characterize rooted isomorphism by induction. The least rooted word over all possible roots characterizes the decorated unrooted tree. For a fixed minimal root, multiply the child automorphism orders and the factorials of the multiplicities of identical child words. The number of roots giving the same least word is the orbit size of that root. Multiplying it by its rooted automorphism order gives the unrooted decorated symmetry order \(a\).

A decorated class with \(k\) extras has \(n!/a\) labelled realizations. At \(q=1/4\) each contributes numerator \(3^{n-k}\) over \(n^{n-2}4^n\). We independently counted every binary mask at every uncoloured shape, checked its symmetry orbit, counted each uncoloured shape directly among the labelled Prüfer words, and checked \(n^{n-2}\binom{n}{k}\) labelled decorated pairs separately at each \(k\). The three-leaf founder with a single extra illustrates the need for this test. An extra at the centre has four labelled realizations and leaves the whole open through four cuts. An extra at a leaf has twelve and gives the three-cut negative closure. Weighting decorated isomorphism classes equally would change the answer. The later random permutation of distinction names contributes total conditional mass one. To see why, fix an isomorphism of two current holder structures. It identifies the same funded parent groups and opening counts. Alternative assignments of a parent's loose names to those children are permutations among names with exactly that single holder; composing with such a permutation preserves all earlier holdings. Children with the same unary parent can be matched in any order, preserving their multiplicity and next demand. Match all their new private names by a bijection. This extends an isomorphism to the complete next stage. The induction works even when the rule for choosing the next free name reads its numerical label. It proves invariance of closure, all the prices and terminal admission, not recovery of the same literal named slot assignments.
