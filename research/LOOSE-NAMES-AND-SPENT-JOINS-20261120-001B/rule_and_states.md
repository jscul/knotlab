# A paid birth through two spent witnesses

Two spent knots can jointly authorize a birth on both sides of a distinction they still share. Every true parent pays with one of its own loose distinctions. The rule can preserve every old allowed state under either knot law. It exacts a price in loose supply and can depend on a physically distant change to the holdings.

## The rule at a complete cut

Keep all ordinary proposed parent groups. For each old name e held by two spent knots w,z, forget **only that one meeting** in the physical holder graph. Require that w and z then belong to different connected parts. Wires used solely as a calculation device for parity bonds are absent from this test. A second shared name between w and z prevents either name from qualifying. Let L be all live holders directly sharing a distinction with w, and R be all live holders directly sharing one with z. If both sets are nonempty, add the one parent group L∪R. Require every distinct parent in every group to surrender one currently loose name; each remaining loose name pays for a unary birth. Shortage cancels the complete cut. We compare this single supplied policy to ordinary growth throughout. The ordinary law remains the program's law. [Frozen rule](preregistration.md)

The two donor sets are disjoint. A common donor would itself reconnect w and z after removing e. No ordinary group spans both sides: a single witness between two live donors would give them a two-meeting route across e, yet e has two spent endpoints. Two different eligible names e and f cannot give the same group either. Both holders of f lie on one side of e, and all their directly neighboring donors lie there as well. The group paid through e uses both sides. The implementation still deduplicates identical full parent sets. All these tests respect an arbitrary renaming. The literature studies an individual meeting whose removal disconnects its endpoints as a bridge. Here that test supplies a new rule for who can witness growth, with distinct payments, state constraints and physical ancestry. [Tarjan, *A Note on Finding the Bridges of a Graph*](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1974/29303.html)

Each old name retains its holders. The authorizing name e never acquires a third. A true parent gains a child through an actual former loose bit that now has precisely two holders. Fresh bits are new and singly held. The rule admits every bijection of a donor's private bits onto its prescribed child slots. Any two are related by a permutation of that donor's private bits, so the priced parent map is unchanged. The future recipient of a particular externally named private bit can depend on that choice. Distinction order in a stored file only selects a representative. Independent recodings preserved the full multisets of parents and fresh counts across twenty preparations under both laws. The prospective control erased each candidate physical name from a multigraph retaining all parallel names. [Erasure and recoding receipts](../../artifacts/LOOSE-NAMES-AND-SPENT-JOINS-20261120-001B/LOOSE-NAMES-AND-SPENT-JOINS-20261120-001B-E001_spent_joins/verify.json)

A stricter demand that w or z become a true parent by donating one of its old bits is impossible for **any** policy preserving every old support, defining parenthood by contribution of an already-held name, and allowing at most two holders per name. Every bit of a spent knot already has two holders. Making a fresh, third holder for any of them violates the bound. The authorization here depends on the actual identity and both old holdings of e. Suppressing eligibility only for e on identical holdings removes its paid group. Thus the two spent knots have a verifiable role even though they supply no input bit.

## Pricing, payment and closure

Construct the complete roster of paying parents and unary remainders. For forecast purposes give each newborn one provisional private bit, mark the former parents spent, then evaluate the same two rules on all actual old and provisional supports. The individual parental inputs are already fixed. The temporary private names add no holder meeting, so changing their positive number cannot change any direct neighboring holder, separating shared name or group. A newborn with p actual parents and next multi-parent demand d gets `max(1,3−p,d)` fresh except at a sole implied parity closing. If there is just one birth, its provisional private bit cannot make a two-donor group: a donor cannot be on both sides of the separating name. Next demand is zero, including after a final zero grant. Thus the forecast is consistent including the terminal case. Every continuing funded cut pays for its complete successor. [Ordinary closing and global funding](../ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/end_operator.md)

For old live h let b(h) be its loose supply, o(h) its ordinary multi-parent charge, and a(h) the number of extra authorized groups containing it. The new rule is funded exactly if `b(h)≥o(h)+a(h)` for every h. It cannot repair an ordinary veto. On a common preparation where both fund, if A is the set of extra groups, their first birth totals satisfy

\[
 N_{\rm extra}=N_{\rm ordinary}-\sum_{G\in A}(|G|-1).
\]

Each new group replaces |G| separate unary remainders by one birth. This exact count applies at a **matched current cut**. Different earlier grants or a different first roster can change the next cut's inputs and prices, preventing a comparison by the same subtraction at all ages.

The two originally sharing holders test this difference. With ternary originals, their four unary children get two fresh apiece under both rules. On that fixed reached cut, permitting the one old shared name gives two ordinary binaries and one four-parent birth; its suppression gives two binaries and four unaries. With four-place originals there are six unary children. Ordinary growth gives each two fresh; forecasting the subsequent joint birth requires three apiece on the new rule. Suppressing the authorization **on that same already-funded reached cut** gives twelve children in place of seven. The unchanged original ordinary history would have different available funds at that cut. The receipt records the per-donor budget, charge and unary remainder in each preparation. ## Old words, current projections and essentiality

A stall leaves all old words on the existing holders. At a funded nonclosing cut every child has disjoint private fresh bits. Given any old allowed word, make the parity sum even by choosing the last fresh bit, or make a not-all-equal row nonconstant by choosing a fresh bit opposite a constant old input. Choices for different children are compatible. The proof works for every old word, and independently for every prefix. It supplies at least one state along an indefinitely continuing history by successive extension. When a sole parity row consumes the whole active loose set, its old equation is the sum of all old parity equations: every previously shared bit cancels twice. A zero-fresh closing row is therefore already implied. These are the survive-or-end tests of A126. [Earlier survival proof](../FORBIDDING-JOIN-20261120-001/decision.md)

Local projection is stronger. For parity the only nonzero old row sum supported solely on loose names contains all of them: otherwise in the connected presentation a shared name would cross from a selected to an unselected holder. Thus every proper subset of loose input bits is jointly unrestricted. A nonclosing new holder then realizes its full local even-parity law. Its own private fresh coordinate makes its constraint independent of the other newly added rows. At a final parity closing, exactly the old whole-loose equation holds on its inputs, so its parity projection is also full there; a terminal support of arity below three is only an algebraic remnant. Adding the implied row can make old rows individually redundant even though all old projections persist. On the three-leaf ternary control, the rank is seven before and after the final row. Every one of the eight rows then has its full four local words but is individually redundant. This is visible only by checking projection and erasure separately.

For not-all-equal the bits paying a particular group were loose in different holders. Assign them arbitrary values, at most one pin per old holder. Every old holder retains at least two free places. The previous two-colour completion argument colours the remaining free names so every holder sees both colours. In a closed odd sequence with exactly two remaining free places per holder, a holder whose third place was pinned can instead receive its opposite twice and the rest alternate. Such a holder exists because the original arities are at least three. Hence every word on the selected old inputs is allowed. Choose the fresh bits for any prescribed nonconstant word at the new holder and fill the other children with their own private names. Erasing a new holder permits an all-equal word on it; erasing an essential old holder also has its old witness extend through the new rows. Old and new constraints are individually essential in this class. At the same three-leaf control the single not-all-equal child actually receives a fourth, fresh place, has fourteen local allowed words and is essential.
