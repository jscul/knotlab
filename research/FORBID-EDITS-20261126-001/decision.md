## What stands

1. **Forbidding that splits is local rejection (argument).** When each reader's setting is a free local edit and no
   birth or stall depends on both settings, the four tables are a local model with local rejection: a preparation that
   does not depend on the settings, and responses that each depend on one setting, zero where a join forbids. So a law
   in which a later join can forbid gives, on such placements, no nonlocality in Bell's sense, whatever the size of an
   excess. The literature's detection loophole has exactly this form.
2. **The class can reach 4, so the size proves nothing (example).** Eight equally weighted worlds where each reader's
   edit keeps half of its own values give CHSH 4 with every complete record unchanged; the three-reader form wins the
   Mermin game with certainty. Above the quantum bound 2√2, so 2√2 is not singled out.
3. **Parity is bounded (proof).** Under the parity knot law, with each reader's equations its own and each reader's
   complete record of the same support for either far setting, CHSH ≤ 2; three readers stay at 3/4 in every small case
   checked.
4. **In the starts read, nothing exceeds the classical bound.** Every start of three to five knots (7,075) and 3,000
   sampled starts of six to eight knots, one round, under both forbidding laws and the ordinary count, both knot laws,
   both weightings: no CHSH above 2 and no Mermin win above 3/4, with or without signalling, also where a newborn knot
   holds distinctions of two readers. Forbidding answers one reader's setting in up to 72 placements of a class, never
   both readers'. Where both were built to forbid, the two sides carried no correlation (CHSH below 0.001).

**Not shown.** Whether growth gives a rejection excess where both sides forbid: no grown start does, and the built ones
were uncorrelated, so this was not tested in effect (not a negative). More rounds, other edits, larger starts. A proof
of the three-reader parity bound. Nothing here bears on physical entanglement.

**Intuition (an exact link, not evidence).** A join that forbids is a detector that never fires, in a world that never
exists. Bell's theorem survives it the way it survives the detection loophole: the bound moves to 4/η − 2 for a
surviving fraction η (Branciard), and every such table is still a local model with rejection. What lies beyond rejection
in the literature is negativity: every no-signalling table has a local model with signed weights (Abramsky and
Brandenburger). The cheapest test is direction 1's first step.
