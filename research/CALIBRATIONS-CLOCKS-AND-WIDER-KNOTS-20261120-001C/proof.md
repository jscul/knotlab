# What uniform pair rates determine at repeated wider meetings

Use the finite unsigned not-all-equal law on distinct binary names in each holder, with one or two holders per name, at least three names per holder, full local projection and essential rules. These hypotheses hold at the ordinary not-all-equal stages reached from an eligible start. The input is the complete named pair disagreement table under the uniform weighting on the allowed words. Put a sign +1 on zero and −1 on one, and write $C_{xy}=1-2r_{xy}$ for its two-sign mean. Global complement makes every one-name mean zero. The prior full-state theorem then gives every later ordinary birth count once the full words are known. Its sole named holding ambiguity is closed. ## Two holders, all arities

Write the two holders as $L=S\cup A$ and $R=S\cup B$ with $|S|=k\ge2$, $|A|=a\ge1$, $|B|=b\ge1$. Write $n=k+a+b$. Every mixed word on either holder extends by choosing a suitable private value on the other, and each rule is essential. Excluding one monochromatic holder costs $2^{b+1}$ or $2^{a+1}$ words. Both are monochromatic together in just the two global constant words. Thus the total is
$$
N=2^n-2^{b+1}-2^{a+1}+2.
$$
In the unrestricted cube the total product of two distinct signs is zero. In the choices with a holder constant, the signed sum equals the size of that family if both observed names are in the holder, and otherwise zero. The intersection of the two forbidden families contributes two. Set $U=2^{b+1}-2$, $V=2^{a+1}-2$ and $W=U+V+2$. The following table gives the resulting signed numerator.

| Positions of the two names | $N C_{xy}$ |
| --- | ---: |
| Both in $L$, with at least one in $A$ | $-U$ |
| Both in $R$, with at least one in $B$ | $-V$ |
| Both shared | $-W$ |
| One private in each holder | $+2$ |

Consequently the positive pairs are precisely all pairs between A and B. Their two sides are unique up to exchange. The names outside them form S. This determines both physical holders and every allowed word within the class of two intersecting holders. At a single shared name the formula still holds; it gives exact multiplication at that common name.

For $k\ge2$, every pair within these two holders satisfies $|C_{xy}|>|C_{xz} C_{zy}|$ at every third distinct name z within them. First $N\ge UV+U+V+2>W$ (expand the expression above at $k=2$). For a pair with numerator $W$, both factors in the product are at most $W$, so the assertion follows. With numerator $U$, a third name on the same side or in S gives product bounded by $UW$. A third name in B gives either 4 or $2V$; both are at most $UW$, since $U,V\ge2$. Exchange sides for $V$. For a positive pair, locating z in S requires $2N>UV$. This follows also from $2N-UV=2^{a+b+1}(2^k-2)>0$. A z in A or B requires only $N>U,V$. All inequalities are strict.

At an ordinary source consisting of exactly these two holders they have no common third holder witnessing a group of old parents. Each individual private name funds one unary child. The left side makes a children and the right side b children. At a side with t such siblings their demand is $t-1$, and each child receives $f(t)=\max(2,t-1)$ fresh names. Every sibling pair then makes a child. The remaining unused names make unary children. Hence the next count after the first reached stage is $g(a)+g(b)$, with
$$
g(t)=\binom t2+t\,[f(t)-(t-1)].
$$
Every first child meets its old parent at one name. No second-cut count depends on which old private name occupied a funded slot. Swapping such assignments permutes those old names inside the same parent, with fresh copies permuted afterwards. In all four requested cases each first child is ternary. The first reached widths are respectively 9, 14, 12 and 17, and the next counts are 4, 6, 5 and 6. ## A bare pair can exclude unrestricted physical rivals

A bare two-holder table also determines the *number* of allowed words of any competing lawful not-all-equal presentation on the same named carrier. One opposite-private pair has correlation $2/N=1/(N/2)$, with $N/2$ odd. A competitor's uniform signed numerator is an integer, so its total $N'$ is a multiple of $N/2$. Both its complement symmetry and the oddness of $N/2$ give $N\mid N'$. Since both physical holders have at least three names, each disallows at most one quarter of the cube. Their intersection has positive size, so $N>2^{n-1}$. It follows that $N'=N$. The word count is inferred from a measured rational entry and the width.

Put $M=\max(a,b)$. The complement contains $F=2^{a+1}+2^{b+1}-2<2^{M+2}$ words. Any competing holder of arity q excludes $2^{n-q+1}$ of them by itself, so $q\ge n-M$. If $n>3M$, three such holders require more than the $2n$ available incidences. At $n=3M$ three would each have arity $2M$ and each name would occur twice. With three holders any two of them then cover all names and have $M$ names in common. The union of their forbidden words has size $3\cdot2^{M+1}-4$, strictly greater than F. Four holders are impossible by the same incidence bound. A single holder cannot give any positive pair correlation and disjoint holders give a zero across their separation. Nested or duplicate holders would impose only their smaller rule. Thus every rival at $n\ge3M$ has two incomparable intersecting holders, and the positive-pair description recovers the same named supports. This is an all-size sufficient condition against arbitrary lawful competitors.

It covers both four/four cases and four/five sharing three. In the remaining four/five case, $k=2$, $n=7$, $N=106$, $F=22$. Every competing row has at least four places and four rows cannot fit. Two distinct four-place rows have at most three places in common. Each excludes 16 words. Their common failure has at most eight words, since their union has at least five names. They would already exclude at least 24. Thus a three-rule competitor could have only one four-place holder. Its other two must both have five places to fit in 14 incidences. All seven names would occur twice. Any two such holders meet and cover the seven names, so inclusion–exclusion gives $16+8+8-3\cdot2+2=28$ forbidden words. This too is impossible. A repeated identical four-place support plus a third holder would reduce to a two-rule relation. Adding that duplicate to the recovered two intersecting holders would use their shared names three times. The bare named supports and their births are therefore unique in all four cases, with no promise about competing holder shapes. ## Recognizing a single-name join from pair readings

In the measured relation on names join a pair if it belongs to a triple whose three disagreement rates sum to two, or if its absolute correlation strictly exceeds the product through **every** third distinct name. Each mixed word on a triple contributes exactly two disagreements and each constant triple none. Thus the first test retains exactly the triples that are mixed in every allowed word. Split the resulting relation at every single name whose removal disconnects it, using the maximal sets that stay connected after removal of any single name (the literature's biconnected components). The same definition applied to the physical co-holding relation splits it into intrinsic parts. Every physical holder lies in one part because its own names are pairwise co-held and its arity is at least three. Parts meet at most once and their pattern of attachments is a tree in each connected component.

Two physical parts sharing a single name t have their original uniform word marginals inside the global system. Complement symmetry makes the extension counts equal at either value of t, and the outside pieces are conditionally independent given t. It follows that $C_{ab}=C_{at}C_{tb}$ across that join. Pairs with names strictly on opposite sides fail the strict-product test. Triples spanning that join have full projection: prescribe two values on one side, and assign the remaining name together with the inherited value of t on the other. The case when t itself belongs to the triple uses one pair on each side. This uses the previous proof that every two-name projection is full. It applies to cycles and repeated meetings. In particular $|C_{ij}|<1$ for all distinct named positions. [Single-name conditioning and the two-name projection lemma](../FREEDOM-CLOCKS-AND-BIRTHS-20261120-001C/proof.md)

For a measured strict pair x,y inside a physical part, a third name z outside that part must enter at a single named t. If $t=x$ or $t=y$, the product through z has magnitude $|C_{xy}|C_{tz}^2<|C_{xy}|$. At another t it has magnitude $|C_{xt}C_{ty}|C_{tz}^2$, bounded by the inside test. Forced triples also retain their local status after joining. Thus the measured relation restricted to a true physical part is precisely the one calculated on that part in isolation. Physically disjoint components have zero correlations across them; neither test makes a cross-component link.

## The reconstruction theorem

Allow every physical part of a stage to be any of the following.

1. All its holders are ternary, with arbitrary mutual or repeated meetings.
2. It is one holder of any eligible arity, or two holders sharing at least two names with at least one private place on each side.
3. It has at most ten names and is among the finite table entries defined below.

In every such part the measured relation remains connected after deleting one name. In type 1 every physical ternary appears as a retained triple, so the measured relation contains every physical co-holding. For a single holder of width q the correlation is $-1/(2^{q-1}-1)$ and all its pairs satisfy strict domination. For a double holder the preceding inequalities give the same conclusion. The type 3 test verifies the required property directly. As no measured link crosses a true part boundary, its maximal surviving sets are therefore exactly the physical parts. This proof also identifies separate connected components. An individual catalogued part has at least three names; no single physical name is isolated.

Read the restriction to each measured part. If it is in the finite lookup, use the local word set carried to the observed names by the table permutation. Otherwise, first check whether the forced mixed triples cover all its names. If so, conjoin all of them. Under type 1 this includes every actual constraint and only adds already implied constraints. For a single or double holder, the only three-name sets forced to be mixed are actual ternary holders: pin any other triple to zero and put ones elsewhere; any holder not touched is then made mixed by changing one private name in it, and this cannot affect the other holder. A single holder is immediate. Hence a double holder with any wider rule has an uncovered private name, and the coverage test does not misclassify it as an all-ternary system. With names left over, a single wider holder has no positive pair, whereas the two-holder case has exactly the positive pairs between its two nonempty private sets. Each remaining analytic case therefore decodes uniquely.

Conjoin the recovered part relations. Every physical rule belongs to some part and each inferred constraint holds in its part, so this gives exactly the global allowed words. The supplied full-state classification gives the future birth counts. An exceptional closed component can be replaced by its other named holding without changing any funding or implication in any other component. The theorem holds among all stages satisfying its structural promise; it does not require recognizing ages, promised parts, or the given source. The bare result above and the full finite census test broader competitor populations. Multiplication of conditional correlations on trees is known mathematics. The project adds exact recovery through a repeated meeting, recovery of its unnamed parts and a bound against arbitrary competing presentations. ## What the small local dictionary certifies

Consider all locally full irredundant connected not-all-equal state types on three through ten used names with at most two holders per name, using the independently checked source catalogue. Group by the full uniform pair table up to one common permutation. A table has a unique *named* state set when it has one state type and its symmetry order equals the state-set symmetry order. Indeed the state symmetries form a subgroup of the table symmetries, and the quotient of their orders counts literal named state sets within that fixed table. Keep only tables whose physical names form one part and whose measured relation remains connected on deleting any one name. A disconnected physical rival cannot give such a connected measured relation. Any connected physical rival on the same carrier was included, even if physically separable at a single name. The table alone now fixes the exact named word set in each retained case. Isolating a physical part preserves full projections and essentiality by complement-symmetric extensions at every single attachment. Consequently the local catalogue applies to the part when embedded in an arbitrary large admitted stage.

The catalogue supplies 1,828 readable parts, including 1,742 beyond the single/double/all-ternary list. Every input state type through ten has a different *unmarked* pair table at its width. Three closed types have more than one named word set under one fixed table, one on eight names and two on ten. One further uniquely named, physically indivisible ten-name type fails the measured connectivity gate. The lookup is an independently reproducible finite computation, not an unrestricted theorem about larger physical parts. The proof using it applies to arbitrarily many parts and arbitrarily large full stages.
