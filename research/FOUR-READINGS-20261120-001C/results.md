# What two settings inside a not-all-equal state can hide

**Setting dependence.** The settings here are two distinctions of the same state as the answers. A Bell comparison assumes free settings. With the settings inside the state, the pair-pattern reading is bounded by 2 at every finite size under any weighting that preserves simultaneous complementation. For the single-bit reading, the proposed perfect relation is impossible in ordinary not-all-equal. A score strictly between 2 and 4 with both full local registers hidden remains open beyond the classes below. Independent referee pending. [Frozen readers and stop](preregistration.md)

## When the settings belong to the state, the pair-pattern score is at most two

Write the state marks as `a0,a1,s,b0,b1,t`. Reading I returns `a_s,b_t`. Reading II returns one precisely when the two marks on a side both equal that side's selector. Simultaneously complementing every distinction preserves every unsigned not-all-equal rule and is a bijection on complete states, including every unmarked extension. It exchanges cell 00 with 11 and cell 01 with 10. **Both** answers to reading II stay fixed. For any weighting invariant under that bijection, its four correlations therefore have the form (p,q,q,p), whenever all four cells have positive weight. In an orientation of CHSH with an odd number of minus signs, exactly one of the two pairs of coefficients on p and q sums to zero. Its value is ±2p or ±2q, with maximum at most 2. Two disjoint ternary readers attain 2 under uniform weights because both answers are invariably zero. This theorem concerns the specified reading and weighting symmetry. ## With settings inside the state, ordinary not-all-equal cannot give that perfect table

**Completion.** In a finite not-all-equal presentation with at least three distinctions per holder and at most two holders per distinction, fix some distinction values. Suppose each holder already sees both values or keeps at least two unfixed distinctions. Every such partial assignment extends.

To see this, remove the already satisfied holders and their incidences. Each remaining holder has at least two unfixed distinctions. Represent an unfixed distinction held only once among the remaining holders by giving it a separate auxiliary holder with just that distinction. Unheld distinctions can be assigned either value. On each connected part, assign the unfixed distinctions alternating values on a traversal using each distinction once after the following completion to even holder counts. If there are odd counts, add one temporary holder sharing a new distinction with each odd holder. Begin the closed traversal at that temporary holder. Each original even holder has an opposite pair at every visit. Each original odd holder that needs coverage had at least three distinctions before completing the counts. Removing its one added distinction leaves an opposite pair from another visit. If all counts are even already, begin at a holder of count at least four whenever one exists. A possible mismatch at that initial visit leaves its other visit with opposite values. If every count is two, alternate around the resulting cycle. An even cycle is covered. On an odd cycle one holder must instead receive two equal values. Every holder in such a residual cycle came from an original holder of count at least three and has a fixed value. Choose the two equal values opposite that holder's fixed value. All its other cycle holders still see both. This proves extension even when several distinctions have the same two holders. The underlying coloring result is classical. The extension at a partly fixed odd cycle supplies the application here. [Bondy and Murty, Lemma 6.1.1](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-349-03521-2) · [Earlier application to ordinary growth](../NAE-GROWTH-20261119-001/nae_growth.md#why-it-holds)

**The proposed relation.** Fix both selectors to 0 and fix a1=b1=1. Each reader already sees both values. The frozen separation forbids any holder from carrying marked distinctions from both readers. Every other holder therefore sees at most one pin, or sees the two opposite values from the same reader. The completion argument applies. This event occurs in every eligible ordinary not-all-equal presentation and the proposed relation forbids it. The argument concerns support and survives arbitrary renaming, repeated meetings, larger reader knots and any finite number of hidden distinctions.

**All eight single-bit orientations.** For any fixed s,t instead give each selected bit the value *opposite* its own selector. The same argument gives at least one complete state for each of the four choices. Those four possible answer pairs have correlations of signs (+,−,−,+) for this particular event in their respective cells. The product of those signs is positive. A perfect CHSH orientation requires an odd-minus sign pattern, hence it disagrees in at least one cell with one of the four possible events. Any strictly positive weighting of complete states assigns that event positive conditional probability. Thus every orientation is **strictly below 4** at every finite size, even without the full-register test. The proof gives no fixed gap below 4 independent of size. Under separation one and fully lawful ternaries the selected 11 event can disappear from cell 00. Three successive binary inequalities also obstruct a pin pattern at separation three when the arity floor is released. All eight pin patterns in a residual three-holder odd cycle extended in the direct check. ## With settings inside the state, one shared binary distinction suffices for a bound of two

Suppose the supports can be divided into two parts, each containing the complete marks of its reader, such that the parts have only one distinction H in common. Under uniform complete-state weighting the two parts are independent conditional on H. Write Aᵢʰ and Bⱼʰ for the distributions on the full marked pairs, conditional on their own selector i or j and H=h. Choose any distribution for an unreachable local condition. Let qᵢⱼ be the conditional probability of H=1 in setting cell ij. In a populated cell the observed joint register distribution is

$$
 (1-q_{ij}) A_i^0 B_j^0 + q_{ij} A_i^1 B_j^1 .
$$

If the two local pair distributions Aᵢ⁰ and Aᵢ¹ differ at an index i, hiding the remote selector at that index forces qᵢ₀=qᵢ₁. The same argument down Bob's column gives q₀ⱼ=q₁ⱼ whenever Bⱼ⁰ and Bⱼ¹ differ. If both readers have at least one such index, every affected row and column takes the same value q* by their intersections. In a cell in neither an affected row nor an affected column, neither register depends on H. Thus one may replace all four conditional weights by q*. Sample H once at that weight, and sample each reader's two potential registers separately conditional on H using its own local laws. This recovers all four register tables from one joint distribution. If one reader has no affected index, each cell factors and the hidden-register condition makes both factors depend only on their own setting. Sampling the four local marginals separately then gives the joint distribution. Disconnected readers under the ordinary weighting also factor.

This proves the bound **2** for any functions of the two local registers in this class and includes every tree of holders with just one distinction at each meeting between the two readers. It also holds under any weighting that factors across that distinction in the stated manner. As a finite check, 4,128 of 20,736 tested binary mixtures passed both register equalities, and every one obeyed the bound. The argument leaves open a stronger dependence through several shared distinctions.
