# A finite range for a fixed bond-radius reading

**Theorem.** Fix a nonnegative integer bond radius \(r\) and put \(K=r+1\). On any of the singly attached, arbitrarily padded paths, compare the quiet ring with the same ring loaded once, using the cold-pass/hot-veto law and quiet old loose values. Take an original shared path distinction \(e_i=b_i b_{i+1}\) to the right of the attachment \(s\). If its nearer holder is at least

\[
                  i-s\ \ge\ 2K+7=2r+9
\]

old knot steps away, then at every common finite age the two histories have the **same bond-radius-\(r\) ball**, up to a common naming of the local intervals and their physical distinctions. This identifies every bond of size at most \(K\) through \(e_i\), the union of its distinctions with \(e_i\), and all their actual holders. Within either history the resulting distinctions and holders stabilize by age \(K\). The same statements hold by reflection on the left and for the union of balls about any set of qualifying marks on one side. No margin from the physical path end is required. For radius seven the sufficient offset is 23. For a bond-size cutoff of seven it is 21.

The offset is a sufficient bound. In particular the theorem allows \(e_i\) to be the outermost original mark. A pair reading with an arbitrarily distant second mark and unrestricted bond size can behave differently. **Coordinates used in the proof.** In the wired holder presentation a bond is a cut with two connected shores. The exterior represents current loose distinctions. Write \(T\) for the current age. In both preparations there is a next complete layer at every finite age. The cluster description assigns a cluster of \(u+1\) points to a knot of age \(u\). Give the rightward points coordinates \(m\) in original path indices, continuing by twos along the two virtual end chains beyond \(N\) and \(N-1\). The other coordinates are capped at \(B=s+2\), as in the preceding proof. Put \(h=\max m-u\). Each ordinary physical meeting changes \(h\) by at most one. For each \(k>B\), write \(W_k\) for the links between heights \(k,k+1\). To classify a current loose distinction, use the height of its allotted child in the next complete layer, then terminate its route at the **current** exterior. These sets of physical distinctions are pairwise disjoint. [Cluster representation, including the surrogate for a loaded start](side_bonds_proof.md)

We need only one upward route for this proof. At either height of \(W_k\), a knot has a route to the exterior staying in \(W_k\) and rising in age. From height \(k\), extend the unique highest right tail outward by a point two coordinates higher. From height \(k+1\), add an inward point without increasing the maximum. The first operation raises \(h\) by one and the second lowers it by one. Each tail has an infinite inward continuation, including on the side disconnected from its original left continuation by a hot veto, where a two-open holder gains an endless spare. The two operations work for \(k\ge N\) as well: their use does not require an original meeting \(e_k\). The uniqueness fact used below is worth recalling. Two different lifted tails attaining \(M\) and at least \(M-1\) in a cluster would require at least \(M-B\) steps between them through the capped part. A cluster of age \(u\) has at most \(u=M-h\) steps between any two points. For \(h>B\) these are incompatible.

**Height and age of a small shore.** Let \(S\) be the connected shore away from the exterior of any bond of size at most \(K\) containing \(e_i\). It contains an old endpoint of \(e_i\), at height \(i\) or \(i+1\). Set

\[
                     a=i-K-1,\qquad b=i+K+2 .
\]

All knots in \(S\) have \(a<h<b\). If one had \(h\le a\), a path in \(S\) from it to the old endpoint would meet every level \(W_a,\ldots,W_{i-1}\). From an inside knot at each such level the upward route ends at the exterior, forcing a crossing there. These are \(K+1\) distinct crossings. The same argument using \(W_{i+1},\ldots,W_{b-1}\) excludes \(h\ge b\), including when some of these levels are beyond the last original mark.

Let \(U\) be the largest birth age of any knot in \(S\). Connectivity to an old endpoint supplies a knot at every age \(u=0,\ldots,U\). From one at any \(u<U\), add an inward point keeping its maximum \(M\) fixed. In this child at age \(u+1\), remove the point of coordinate \(M\). Its maximum drops by two, and the result is again a physical cluster of age \(u\). To check this removal, the intermediate height is at least \(a\) as long as the starting knot is in \(S\). Thus \(M\) lies strictly past the tail entrance, has just its inward neighbor in the cluster and no other tail reaches \(M-1\). The neighbor has coordinate \(M-2\). Removing this leaf preserves an original point. If the maximum is virtual this is immediate. If it is original, an inward step beside a high ordinary point uses its inward original neighbor. More generally, a cluster with just one original point that reaches this high and contains other points could only sit on that original point's outward end spare, in which case its maximum is virtual.

Each two-step operation lowers \(h\) by two at age \(u\). Repeat only while still inside \(S\). The strict lower height bound forces an exit using a physical link between ages \(u,u+1\). Different \(u\)'s use disjoint such strips. The marked original link \(e_i\) belongs to none, so \(1+U\le K\). Every ordinary holder of any crossing distinction is consequently present by age \(K\).

**Transporting the complement as well as the cut.** Let \(R\) include all ordinary knots of heights \(a\) through \(b\) and ages at most \(\min(K,T)\). It contains \(S\) and all its ordinary neighbors. The exterior together with all knots outside \(R\) is connected in the wired presentation. Knots of age above \(K\) can ascend directly to the current exterior. From a lower-age knot of height less than \(a\), ascend along any continuing route until it reaches \(a-1\), if ever. From there use the upward route within \(W_{a-2}\), avoiding \(R\) throughout. For heights above \(b\) use \(W_{b+1}\) upon reaching \(b+1\). Thus contracting this entire connected outside part to the exterior preserves whether the complement of any \(S\subseteq R\) is connected. It also preserves each physical distinction incident to \(S\); parallel distinctions to the contracted exterior are counted separately.

Connectivity can also be checked at the boundary alone. Every component outside \(R\) either contains the wired exterior or touches \(R\), since the whole wired presentation is connected. A neighboring knot outside \(R\) at age greater than \(K\) has height at least \(a-1>B\). Its upward route within one of the stated \(W_k\) stays above age \(K\). A neighbor outside the height range can instead use \(W_{a-2}\) or \(W_{b+1}\), according to the side it touches. Each such component therefore reaches the exterior.

Both histories give the same contracted presentation on \(R\), dropping loops entirely within the contracted part. To see all incident links, examine the slightly larger height interval \([a-1,b+1]\) to age \(K+1\). For a cluster in this interval of age \(u\), every one of its points has coordinate at least \(\max m-2u=h-u\ge i-2K-3>B\). It is therefore an interval on a single unbranched right tail. Its point coordinates are exactly \(h-u,h-u+2,\ldots,h+u\), and it occurs precisely when at least one of these points is original. Lifts of a given interval form a single orbit since one can start at any lift of a prescribed original point on it. On each parity line the points through its physical end \(N\) or \(N-1\) are original and all beyond are virtual. Both histories have precisely these same two tails. Birth links add or remove one end point while retaining an original point. Old links join consecutive original holders. The physical end has just its actual old neighbor. At age \(T\) each interval's two one-point extensions specify its two loose distinctions, which terminate at the current exterior. This describes the whole local incidence and the full multiplicity of its links to the contracted outside. It also gives a consistent naming on overlapping patches when several marks are scored.

For a bond in either history, transport its inner shore in this common presentation. Its marked crossing distinctions, literal size, and connectivity of both shores survive. Undoing the outside contraction gives a genuine bond in the other history. Applying the argument in both directions identifies all the stated small supports and their holders. For \(T\ge K\), the contracted patch is independent of \(T\): links going up from age \(K\) go to the contracted exterior, whether still loose or already consumed. Each bond's inner shore itself stops by age \(K-1\), so all physical holders of its crossing links are fixed from age \(K\) on. Include the marked distinction itself, whose self-distance is zero, even if no bond falls below the cutoff. This proves the fixed-radius result without a new measured panel.
