# Which finite packets have an unending line of their own?

A proper packet on a bare odd cycle cannot support an unending all-parent line. A relay has a different answer. Each of its two current ends supports such a line even while the ends and their mixed descendants share a live component. Bare live holdings can conceal the witness that decides which continuation occurs. [Frozen comparisons](preregistration.md)

## Three distinct tests

A whole connected part of the witnessed live end has every child born within it. An all-parent packet can be smaller. Starting with its specified members at one size, retain a new member only when **every** actual parent belonged to the previous retained packet. A child that takes from this packet and from elsewhere is counted in the inclusive test, which requires just one retained parent. All three tests refer to the actual ordinary continuation. The separate reduced-end test reruns the standard growth calculation from the chosen holders, their current opens and their surviving witnesses, with incoming shared distinctions retained as boundary data.

## Sharp loss on the bare cycle

Take a unit odd cycle on $m\ge5$ original knots and give each two loose distinctions. Every original has four places. Its live candidates form another $m$-cycle, in the order obtained by taking two ordinary holder steps along the old cycle. Each witness supplies one pair and every live knot belongs to two pairs. All births have two parents and two fresh distinctions. The next live cycle has one member for each adjacent pair, and neighbours share precisely the intervening old parent. This construction repeats indefinitely under both laws. Each step has $m$ births, so parity encounters no sole closing birth.

Index one current witnessed cycle cyclically for counting. A child at position $i$ has the adjacent parents $i,i+1$. At depth $t$ beyond this current cycle, the initial ancestors of position $i$ are exactly the positions from $i$ through $i+t$, inclusive and interpreted cyclically. This follows by taking the union of the two adjacent intervals at each step. Thus at $t\ge m-1$, every child has all $m$ current members as ancestors.

For a proper initial packet, let $r_j$ be the lengths of its maximal consecutive runs. All-parent descendants at depth $t$ number
\[
\sum_j\max(r_j-t,0).
\]
Each term counts exactly the intervals of length $t+1$ held inside that run. The packet is empty after $\max_j r_j$ further steps. This is sharp for every proper width. One run of $r$ members has $r,r-1,\ldots,1,0$ pure members at successive sizes. The largest proper lifetime is $m-1$ steps to extinction, and the full width $m$ is both sufficient and necessary for indefinite all-parent survival.

Inclusive retention uses the union of the two parent tests. For a nonempty packet with complementary gaps of lengths $g_j$, the count is $m-\sum_j\max(g_j-t,0)$. A single member covers the whole cycle after $m-1$ steps and can supply one parent of an indefinitely followed line. The other parent can lie outside that chosen line at every step. From a singleton, no child qualifies at the very first all-parent step. The labels only index the proof. The witnessed cycle, consecutive runs and both counts are invariant under rotations and reversal.

The exact physical replays covered all 32 and 128 initial subsets for $m=5$ and $m=7$, at every depth through 5 and 7, respectively. That gives 192 and 1,024 subset-depth comparisons per law. The sources have 5/15 and 7/21 knots/distinctions. A one-spare five-cycle control has 5/16. It writes one unary and five pair births at the first size. The unary has two opens and exactly two next groups through its old parent, so it contributes no all-parent child of its own. Its inclusive descendants persist throughout the six checked sizes. This control illustrates that the occurrence of a unary alone does not supply a lasting pure line.

## The two ends of a relay

Take a center $S$ joined once to each of three leaves. Give each leaf two loose distinctions and give $S$ one. This is a 4-knot, 10-distinction source. Both laws make the three leaf-pair births at age 1, with one fresh each. At age 2 their ternary top gets one fresh. The simultaneous descendants of $S$ leave a separate live part, so parity cannot take all current loose distinctions at that top. At age 3 the top forms a unary relay with two fresh. This relay is the single member of its live part at age 3. The part has widths $1,2,3,\ldots$ from there, with two fresh at every birth.

The relay emits two unary children at age 4. They have two loose distinctions each and degree one in their witnessed live path. At the next size both still make an all-parent unary child, and their common pair birth receives two fresh distinctions. That is the first actual birth with an input from each endpoint after the specified release. The two endpoints already have the relay as a shared witness at release. This is a controlled case of continuing pure descendants alongside a mixed output.

At every later size a current endpoint owes one distinction to a pair with its next neighbour, leaving one distinction for a unary. The new witnessed live path has a unary at each end and a pair birth for each old adjacent pair. It is one longer, and all new members again have two fresh distinctions. Thus each endpoint has exactly one pure unary descendant at every later size. No entire connected part has to detach. At depth $d\ge1$ after release there are two pure unary births and $d$ mixed births, all with two fresh distinctions. The live reader can select the smaller of the two paths, which is always two members shorter than the other path, and return its two degree-one members with two loose distinctions. This recognizes the unordered pair of continuing ends, whose individual names may be exchanged. It provides a one-time release with persistent tips. Recurring production of new detached packets is a separate question.

The whole relay part, when restricted at age 3 or later with the incoming boundary and opens retained, has the same subsequent ordinary parent sets and budgets. For an individual endpoint the reduced end has two unaries, each with two fresh. Its actual ambient roster instead spends one of its two opens on the pair and one on its pure unary. The pure unary can be replayed indefinitely with the same budget, while the whole isolated roster differs. One layer earlier, treating the top alone as a one-open end gives a unary with zero fresh under parity and two under not-all-equal. The ambient unary has two in both. The distinction between parentage and budget independence therefore enters even before the two tips emerge.

These restrictions retain the original incoming distinctions as boundary data. Physically deleting the older holders and treating those shared distinctions as loose changes the supply. In a connected multi-knot parity source with only one live knot, the sum of all star equations already constrains the parity of the loose set. That set is a proper subset of the live knot's star because the knot has an ordinary neighbour. Its full intended star projection would therefore fail. A reduced live end and a new physical source have different lawfulness tests.

In the literal quiet control, remove $S$'s one loose distinction at the original source. The source retains 4 knots and has 9 distinctions. The first leaf-pair births survive. The resulting sole ternary top closes with zero fresh under parity and receives one under not-all-equal. The latter then relays with a two-fresh unary. The checker followed the active relay through age 10 under both laws and the quiet control through its parity closure or five not-all-equal steps. ## What spent-witness erasure hides

Use four spent knots $S,T,P,Q$ with a unit meeting between every pair. Take two live knots $U,V$, each with two openings and two incoming ordinary meetings. In both arms $U$ meets $S,T$. Connect $V$ to $S,P$ in one arm and to $P,Q$ in the other. Transfer the same physical distinction when changing its spent holder. Each arm has 6 knots and 14 distinctions. The complete stars of the live knots and their loose/shared flags agree literally.

The first arm has a shared witness $S$. Ordinary growth gives the pair $U,V$ and one unary from each. In the other arm the live two knots have no shared witness and each gives two unaries. All first births get two fresh in both laws. The continuing live widths in the joined arm form one increasing path. In the separate arm they form two increasing paths. The complete physical and witnessed-end readings detect the distinction, while a reading of the live stars after erasing the spent witnesses cannot. The causal question depends on who holds the original shared distinctions.

As a small competition control, two three-place knots $A,W$ share one distinction and each has two opens. Add a third three-place knot $C$ using a loose distinction of $W$, keeping the full support and the two opens of $A$. The two starts have 2/5 and 3/7 knots/distinctions. The added candidate pair $A,C$ uses one of $A$'s two outputs. Its pure descendant widths change from $t+1$ to one at every positive depth $t$. Both pure continuations persist under both laws. A multi-parent witness needs at least two eligible parents and a third holder. The three-knot case realizes that minimum for this source competition while preserving the physical star of $A$. The shared witness is already present at the source. [The original competition proof](../ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/lineage_proofs.md)

## Literal checks

The checker verifies every source's exact parity star by connectivity after deletion with loose distinctions wired to an auxiliary exterior. It checks every allowed local not-all-equal row by satisfiability with the indicated physical distinctions pinned. It then applies the archived ordinary operator to the literal stars, allocating one distinct old slot per parent and newly named slots for the fresh count. The ancestry checks take unions over these actual parent sets independently of the run and path formulas. Representatives of cycle, relay and spent-witness controls also passed independent simultaneous renamings of holders and distinctions. These are deterministic fixture checks and the formulas above supply the unrestricted conclusions for the stated families.
