## The answer

The owner asked whether there is a growth law under which parts that have come apart can meet again, so that what one
part builds depends on whether another part is there; and he added his conviction that it must be possible, at some cost,
through a knot that both sets of descendants come from.

1. **Under the three recorded laws, no, for a stated reason.** Rule R, rule Q and the move of A225 as a law all have a
   birth take only what its own parents hold. Whatever a parent holds is already met by everything the parent meets, so
   such a birth can rejoin only what is still joined. This is an argument, written as one induction for the referee. The
   runs bear it out: no pair of parts that share nothing is ever one part again, in 504 runs, in about 1,600 pairs made by
   switching the rule on only after the copies exist, and in every choice of distinction tried so far (1.94 million
   histories). On the two objects themselves these rules undo them: the ring never returns and nothing copies itself.
2. **Under laws that reach further, yes.** If a birth may also take a distinction from a knot its parent meets, or from one
   or two steps back along its own descent, parts that came apart become one part again and the first then builds
   differently with the second there. Over the registered runs 216 such cases are confirmed as going through a meeting
   that births made.
3. **The owner's picture occurs.** In 62 of those cases a knot that both parts descend from is among the knots that link
   them, an ancestral common knot, and in 6 it is the only link. It is always a start knot.
4. **The cost.** The cheapest law that works looks two holder steps out and needs no record of descent. Every law needs a
   supplied way of choosing which distinction is taken, and the dependence comes and goes with that choice. One
   distinction can come to have more than a hundred holders within eight rounds.
5. **What is the scaffolds'.** No case for parts that came apart survives a change of its ration on its own start. The one
   case that survives every swap joins two parts of a start that were never one part.
6. **What crosses.** Whether the other part is there: one bit, by crowding or, in a third of the cases, by a birth with a
   parent in each part. In no case did a part's course depend on what the other part is. A made pattern that reads another
   in that fuller sense, and builds something of its own from it, was not found.
