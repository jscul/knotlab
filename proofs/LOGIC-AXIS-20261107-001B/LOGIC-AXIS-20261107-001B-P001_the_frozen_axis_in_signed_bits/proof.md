# The frozen axis: Ledrappier's law measures distance in signed bits

Status: Proved (items 1–3); item 4 is a conjecture with finite evidence

Ticket `LOGIC-AXIS-20261107-001B`, 2026-11-07.

## Standard statement

Consider Ledrappier's shift: the configurations x ∈ F_2^{Z^2} with x(i,j) + x(i+1,j) + x(i,j+1) = 0 for all
(i,j). A finite set S ⊆ Z^2 is a *minimal relation* when Σ_{s∈S} x(s) = 0 for every configuration but
no nonempty proper subset has this property. Equivalently, Σ_{(a,b)∈S} X^a (1+X)^b = 0 in F_2(X)
minimally.

1. The three-point minimal relations are exactly the triangles {c, c + 2^e e_1, c + 2^e e_2}.
2. If n = Σ_{i=1}^k ε_i 2^{e_i} with ε_i = ±1 and distinct e_i, there is a minimal relation of k + 2
   points containing (0,0) and (n,0).

So the least size of a minimal relation through two points n apart on a lattice line is at most
2 + w(n), where w(n) is the number of nonzero digits of n's non-adjacent form. It is 3 exactly when n
is a power of two. **Conjecture:** it equals 2 + w(n).

## Nearest known result

- **Ledrappier (1978).** This shift is mixing but not 3-mixing. The witnesses are exactly these
  Frobenius triangles.
- **Masser (2004); Derksen and Masser (2012–2018).** In Schmidt's terms, minimal relations are
  "non-mixing shapes". Masser, and Derksen and Masser, describe the solutions of linear equations over
  finitely generated multiplicative groups in characteristic p. The solutions form finitely many sets
  generated by at most n − 1 Frobenius operators, which locates the non-mixing sets of each size
  effectively.
- **Arenas-Carmona, Berend and Bergelson (2008).** Ledrappier's system is mixing of all orders away from
  certain small constructible sets.
- **Item 1** is Mason–Stothers in characteristic 2, with Frobenius descent.
- **Non-adjacent forms.** They have minimal weight among signed-binary representations (Reitwiesner
  1960).
- **Classification:** `known-with-delta`. No source found states the least size of a minimal relation
  through two given points, or its signed-binary form. Item 2's construction, with its proof of
  minimality by (1+X)-adic valuations, is the delta. So is the reading as the bond distance of a frozen
  law.

## Setting

- **The ring map.** Let R = F_2[X^±, Y^±] and I = (1 + X + Y).
  - Configurations are functionals on finite-support Laurent polynomials. The allowed states are the
    annihilator of I, and by Pontryagin duality their annihilator is I.
  - The map φ: R → F_2(X), Y ↦ 1 + X, has kernel I, since R/I ≅ F_2[X^±, (1+X)^±] is a domain.
- **Implied checks.** A finite S is an implied parity check exactly when Σ_{(a,b)∈S} X^a (1+X)^b = 0.
  The knots (K1–K3) are the minimal such S with |S| >= 3.
- **No small checks.**
  - There are no one-point checks, since units are nonzero.
  - There are no two-point checks: X^a (1+X)^b = 1 forces a = b = 0, because X and 1 + X are distinct
    primes.
- **Hence a vanishing set of at most five points is a knot.** A vanishing proper subset would leave a
  vanishing complement of one or two points.
- **The model on patches.** On a finite patch {i, j >= 0, i + j <= N} the bottom row is free and
  determines the rest by Pascal's rule. The column of the point (a,b) is the coefficient vector of
  x^a (1+x)^b. Check 1 compares this with Gaussian elimination on the patch's own checks.
- **Ledrappier's law is frozen.** It has no finite moves (FROZEN-WORLDS B; LOGIC-AXIS A,
  Proposition 4). So under the source-aware reading none of these knots is native. Under the
  source-blind K1–K3 test all of them are knots. Both verdicts are recorded here.

## 1. The three-point knots are the Frobenius triangles

**Theorem 1.** A three-point set is a knot of Ledrappier's law exactly when it is
{c, c + 2^e e_1, c + 2^e e_2} for some c ∈ Z^2 and e >= 0.

*Proof.*
1. **These are knots.** (1 + X + Y)^(2^e) = 1 + X^(2^e) + Y^(2^e) in characteristic 2. Translate by c.
   Minimality is automatic.
2. **Reduction to polynomials.** Conversely, a three-point vanishing sum, divided by one of its
   terms, reads u + v = 1 with u, v ∈ G = ⟨X, 1 + X⟩.
   - Clear denominators by the least X^m (1+X)^{m′} that makes all three terms polynomials.
   - This gives A + B = C with A, B, C ∈ {X^i (1+X)^j}, pairwise coprime: each prime divides at most one
     of them.
3. **Some derivative nonzero.** Mason–Stothers in characteristic p gives
   max(deg A, deg B, deg C) <= deg rad(ABC) − 1 <= 1.
   - So {A, B, C} = {1, X, 1 + X}.
   - These are the triangles with c = 0, c = (0, −1) and c = (−1, 0), the origin taking each corner's
     place in turn.
4. **All derivatives zero.** In characteristic 2, A′ = 0 means A is a square, since F_2 is perfect. If
   this holds for all three, every exponent is even and A₁ + B₁ = C₁ with A = A₁², because Frobenius is
   injective.
   - The point set halves. Induction on the exponents gives the triangle at scale 2^e.
5. **Translation.** Restore it. ∎

**Corollary 1 (infinite bond balls).** Every point has bond distance 2 to the six points p ± 2^e e_1,
p ± 2^e e_2 and p ± 2^e (e_1 − e_2), for every e >= 0. So every ball of bond radius r >= 2 is infinite,
and the law has no finite growth dimension in its own distance.

## 2. Signed binary digits give knots

**Theorem 2.** Let n = Σ_{i=1}^k ε_i 2^{e_i} with ε_i ∈ {±1} and e_i distinct. Put z_0 = 0 and
z_i = z_{i−1} + ε_i 2^{e_i}. Then

  S = {(0,0), (n,0)} ∪ {(min(z_{i−1}, z_i), 2^{e_i}) : 1 <= i <= k}

is a knot of k + 2 points.

*Proof.*
1. **Each hop is a triangle.** Hop i is the Frobenius triangle T_i with corner (min(z_{i−1}, z_i), 0)
   at scale 2^{e_i}. Its two points on the axis are z_{i−1} and z_i.
2. **The sum is S.** Take the sum of the T_i (mod 2).
   - Each inner z_i lies in exactly two triangles. Coincidences among the z's change only even
     multiplicities.
   - z_0 = 0 and z_k = n survive.
   - The apexes have distinct heights 2^{e_i} >= 1, so they neither cancel nor meet the axis.

   So the sum is S, and S vanishes.
3. **Minimality by valuation at X = 1.** The valuation of X^a (1+X)^b is b. In a vanishing sum the
   least valuation occurs at least twice. Let W ⊆ S be nonempty and vanishing.
   - If W contains exactly one of the two height-0 points, valuation 0 occurs once.
   - If it contains neither, its points have distinct valuations.

   Both are impossible, so W contains both height-0 points. Then S ∖ W consists of apexes and
   vanishes, so it is empty. ∎
4. **Reading.** The non-adjacent form gives k = w(n), its least possible value (Reitwiesner). So
   δ((0,0),(n,0)) <= 1 + w(n) <= (log_2 n)/2 + 5/2, since a non-adjacent form of length
   L <= ⌊log_2 n⌋ + 2 has at most ⌈L/2⌉ nonzero digits.
5. **By Theorem 1,** the bond distance is 2 exactly when n is a power of two.
6. **The other two lattice lines.** The law's symmetries (i, j) ↦ (j, i) and (i, j) ↦ (−i − j, i) map
   up-triangles to up-triangles, so they carry the same bounds to the lines along e_2 and e_1 − e_2.
   Check 3 verifies this for n <= 256.

## 3. What the axis measures

- **Bond distance reads signed binary digits.** Along a lattice line of this frozen plane, the bond
  distance between two points reads the signed binary digits of their separation, not its size.
  - Points 2^e apart are bonded as tightly as neighbours.
  - Separations with many alternating digits, such as 0b1010101, need more places.
  - The segment of length n has bond diameter at most (log_2 n)/2 + 5/2.
- **The native contrast.** LOGIC-AXIS A's gauge plane measures taxicab distance with balls growing like
  r^2. Here every ball is infinite.

## 4. The lower bound: conjecture and evidence

**Conjecture.** δ((0,0),(n,0)) = 1 + w(n) for every n >= 1. More generally, δ(p, q) = 1 + |q − p|_D,
where |v|_D is the least number of terms ±2^e v_0 with v_0 ∈ {e_1, e_2, e_1 − e_2} summing to v. The
axis case is |(n,0)|_D = w(n), from projecting onto the first coordinate.

**Evidence** (check.py, all inside stated windows):
- **n <= 128.** No knot smaller than 2 + w(n) through (0,0) and (n,0), up to four points, in the window
  [−8, n+8] × [−(n+8), 2n+8].
- **The five-point case.** No five-point knot for n = 43, 45, 51 and 53, the n <= 64 with w(n) = 4.
- **The plane.** For all 168 vectors v ∈ [−6, 6]^2, the smallest knot through 0 and v has 2 + |v|_D
  points when |v|_D <= 3, and more than four points when |v|_D = 4.

**A warning for proofs.** Not every four-point knot is two Frobenius triangles sharing an outside
point. For example {(0,0), (3,0), (0,3), (1,1)} is a knot (1 + X^3 + Y^3 + XY vanishes at Y = 1 + X),
and a window computation found 12 such knots among the 232 four-point knots through the origin in
[−12, 12]^2. So the obvious decomposition argument fails. All pairs inside these knots still satisfy
the conjecture. Derksen and Masser's structure theorem is the likely route to a proof. It is not used
here.

## Checks

`check.py` runs six checks, each with a rejected control (`result.json`):
- dependency digests;
- the polynomial model against Gaussian elimination on a side-24 patch: 3,000 random sets and placed
  triangles, with no disagreement;
- the exhaustive list of three-point knots through the origin in [−32, 32]^2: 18, all Frobenius
  triangles, giving 36 bond-2 neighbours;
- hop knots for n <= 2048 and their images on the other two lines for n <= 256;
- the windowed lower bounds;
- the plane window.

## Nonclaims

- The lower bound for every n is not proved.
- Nothing here is native under the source-aware reading. Ledrappier's law has no finite moves, so its
  knots all bind distinctions opened by infinity.
