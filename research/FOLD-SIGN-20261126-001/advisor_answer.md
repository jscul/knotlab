# The advisor's answer (26 November 2026), before the fold study was frozen

**Short version:** Part A holds with two repairs. Part B is a quantity on ends, not on paths. Carried along growth, it is either the exchange sign of fact (v) or a sign of the hand-built start. I would freeze the argument as the stop and run the census as its check. These are arguments I have not run or had refereed.

## 1. Part A

Keep the square argument and demote the "c(b)" argument: c may read I as well as the step's output, and only the square argument covers that.

- **Strike "needs an antisymmetric relation".** The sign around a square is symmetric in the pair. What −1 needs is a native way to tell the two births apart.
- **Add the twin lemma.** If a renaming fixes I and exchanges independent births a and b, any renaming-invariant c gives c(I,a)=c(I,b) and c(I+a,b)=c(I+b,a), so the square is +1. No native sign exists on exchangeable pairs.
- **Narrow the conclusion.** Fact (ii) only covers one round. Independent births that differ in a native attribute (depth in the prerequisites, fresh count) do admit a native −1: count inversions against that attribute. That is path-dependent, so "every path quantity is trivial" is false as stated. The true statement is that each such sign is the choice of an attribute (a scaffold), and the law selects none.
- **Add the scope.** The argument is for labelled states. Identify states up to renaming and new closed paths appear that squares do not generate; that is where Part B lives.
- **Add what is missing.** A sign cancels nothing unless something adds over orders, and nothing in the law does. I think this is the owner's missing piece, more than the sign.

## 2. Part B

The group of renamings of an end is native. Which two-valued character of it you take is supplied.

- **The domain is a choice.** Exchanging two parts with m knots, e shared distinctions and d distinctions gives (−1)^m, (−1)^e or (−1)^d. With distinctions as the domain (the program's primitive), swapping two loose distinctions of one knot is odd, so the sign is ill defined almost everywhere. Ignoring those renamings is a second choice.
- **The cancellation is group theory.** A nontrivial character sums to zero over a group. Choosing sign over the trivial character is the fermion-or-boson choice, which the literature's configuration space also offers without deciding.
- **It does not travel along growth.** Suppose renaming g maps one growth path to another. With invariant step signs, sign(g on the end) = sign(g on the start).
  - If g fixes the start pointwise, its sign is +1. A twin swap has odd knot parity, so knot parity is consistent there only with −1 on the twin square, which is the added exchange sign.
  - If g permutes the start, the sign is inherited from the start.
  - If g moves the start (the 16-knot case), the sign is a free convention.

## 3. The test

It is well posed as a computation on renaming groups, but close to foregone.

- **Q1:** fails in every end with twin offshoots, which I expect is nearly all of the census.
- **Q2:** holds only where Q1 has already failed.
- **Q3:** no instance that survives Q1.
