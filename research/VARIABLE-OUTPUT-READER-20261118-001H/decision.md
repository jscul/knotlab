# Distance five is enough on a mixed three-dimensional growth family

**Proved for every finite size in this family.** Take any finite portion of the three-dimensional growth arrangement that includes the predecessors of every site it contains. Every three-parent join may supply two or three fresh distinctions. When it supplies two, the omitted direction must have no successor in the chosen arrangement. The choices may cluster. From the resulting pair table alone, distances through five determine every junction, every allowed state and every legal next group. The theorem compares the source with **every** reachable rival under the mixed law. A rival can grow by a different history and need have no coordinate description. The coordinates describe which sources the proof covers. The reader receives only the anonymous current table. **Five is sharp.** In a 51-distinction arrangement, two members of a legal triple are at bond distance 13. A swap carries that triple to a forbidden one by placing two proposed inputs at the same junction. The swap preserves all 1,275 entries through distance four. At distance five a pair on that common junction reveals the difference. Thirteen is also the greatest bond distance that two members of any legal triple can have under the unrestricted rule. What carries the verdict here is the pattern of short entries across the table. **The result also covers arrangements without a supplied coordinate pattern.** It holds when every addition supplies three fresh distinctions. It still holds with up to five two-output joins anywhere if each has three parents, or with any number of such joins when no two meet or share an ordinary neighbor. A separate argument covers a high last junction after an arbitrary all-two-output prefix of one- and two-parent joins. An additional case handles a two-parent, two-output join whose parents were single-parent additions on the same seed or high junction. **What could still defeat the general reader.** A pair of full tables with opposite three-parent verdicts would require at least 27 distinctions and a large junction on both sides within the part that actually contains the marks. One small example shows exactly where a proof can go wrong. Six distinctions pass every short pair test for a six-place equation although they form no equation. The unresolved step is whether such a change can agree with every surrounding pair and change a live triple. No tested full table did so. In four targeted families, all 17,105 high-bearing presentations with a legal triple passed the distance-five self-symmetry test. Among 116,997 equal-width pairs of targeted continuations, 1,518 opposing marks matched through four and all separated at five. These populations are specified construction families. A universal distance cutoff, or the necessity of a three-way reading, remains open.
