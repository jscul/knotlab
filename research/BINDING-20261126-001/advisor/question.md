The question (the coordinator's, refined): under parity, a layer-one parity knot is exactly "one distinction is the sum of the others", so a parity relation among facts held by the whole is not automatically trivial. What does not count is a third fact WE define as the sum. What would count: three or more whole-held facts each with its own independent definition (for example each the parity of its own ring or piece, a "carrier"), where the growth forces their parity, as three distinctions of a layer-one parity knot each exist on their own account. Test: does the linear space the carriers' values fill have dimension smaller than the number of carriers (a relation among independently carried facts), and does growth create such relations where the start had none? Under not-all-equal the space need not be linear; check separately.

My draft argument and plan, to attack:
(1) By the refereed fact that ordinary growth leaves the allowed states' projection onto old distinctions unchanged, the joint values of carriers that are functionals of OLD distinctions (the start's ring parities) are fixed at the start forever: growth can create no relation among them.
(2) So a growth-made relation needs at least one carrier on NEW distinctions (a cycle among births). A relation sum_i c_i = const over the allowed states holds iff sum_i c_i lies in the row space of the knots' laws; with loose values free that is the cut of a set K of knots with no loose distinction plus nothing else (the cycle/cut duality), so after growth, when every old knot is spent, K can be a set of old knots: such relations are "forced" by spent knots' laws, as opposed to identities where sum_i c_i = 0 as edge sets (one cycle the symmetric difference of others, which I exclude as not independently carried).
