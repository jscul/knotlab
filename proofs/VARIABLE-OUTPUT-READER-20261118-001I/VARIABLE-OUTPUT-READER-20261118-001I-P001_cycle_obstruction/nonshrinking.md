# When a later site cannot reduce a boundary

Write \(p\) for the number of inputs to a junction and \(h\) for its number of fresh distinctions. In this section a source satisfies \(h\ge p\) at every join. Under the present law the sole excluded choice is three parents and two fresh distinctions. The ordinary graph is the incidence representation of the parity equations. Minimal equations are called bonds. The ordinary shore is the set of sites on the side of a cut away from the wired exterior. See the [parity representation](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/separator.md).

## Exactly when three sites can have a short boundary

**Lemma.** A bond of size at most six has three or more sites on its ordinary shore in such a source if and only if the ordinary graph contains a three-site path whose site degrees sum to at most ten.

For any chosen set of sites build its boundary in their original birth order. Adding a site of degree \(p+h\) with \(j\) previously selected neighbors changes the boundary by \(p+h-2j\). This is nonnegative since \(j\le p\le h\). The seed, if selected, contributes three. If the final boundary has size at most six, so does that of its first three sites. Their degrees are at least three apiece. With at most one internal meeting, their boundary would be at least seven. With two meetings, the three form a path and their degree sum is at most ten. Three internal meetings would contradict bipartiteness.

In the reverse direction, the boundary around such a path has size at most \(10-4=6\). If the complementary side is disconnected, include in the ordinary shore all complementary components except the one containing the exterior. Each added component meets the previous shore, and adding it removes its boundary. The new shore and its complementary side are connected and have a nonempty boundary because the entire wired graph is connected. This gives a bond of size at most six whose ordinary shore contains the path. In particular the short-shore condition holds whenever the seed is the only ternary site. The minimum degree sum in that case is eleven. The qualifier on the number of ternary sites matters. A chain of three ternary junctions each grown on one parent with two outputs after the seed has a genuine five-bit bond enclosing all three. [Exact control](families.json)

**Pair reader within this class.** If a source and a rival both satisfy \(h\ge p\) and neither has such a path, the table through \(D=5\) determines all junctions, states and legal choices. Here is the explicit reader. Work with \(K=D+1\). For \(k=3,4,5,6\) link all pairs with \(K\le k\). For each pair of previously recovered stars \(S,T\) meeting on a bit with \(|S\triangle T|\le k\), erase the links between \(S\setminus T\) and \(T\setminus S\). The maximal groups of at least three still linked in every pair are precisely the stars of arity at most \(k\).

Indeed, the only bonds under consideration are single stars and sums of two adjacent stars. The cut around an adjacent pair is a bond. After deleting two adjacent sites, the latest site in any remaining ordinary component has at least two outputs and cannot send them both to the two deleted sites of opposite colors. Its component reaches the exterior. Each star in an adjacent sum of size at most \(k\) has strictly fewer than \(k\) bits, so the previous stage has recovered both of them. Two bits at the same holder can never be among the erased cross-pairs. Such a meeting elsewhere would give a triangle of ordinary holders or a repeated meeting. Conversely every surviving link has a common holder of arity at most \(k\). In a simple triangle-free graph with private leaves on loose bits, three or more pairwise meeting bits have one common holder. This proves the induction. Checking distinct loose holders and their shared ordinary neighbors gives every permitted group. The proof uses no birth times in the reader. [Earlier within-class reader](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001H/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001H-P001_high_junctions/high_inverse.md#an-extra-equation-would-need-too-many-close-pairs)

In particular, take a source with \(h\ge p\) and a single ternary site. If a rival matches the table through \(D=5\) yet changes a marked verdict, **the rival must use a three-parent, two-output addition** in every realization. Every reachable ternary bond is a ternary star, and the ternary stars can be read from the entries \(K=3\), so the rival has the same single ternary site. If it also admitted a realization with \(h\ge p\) the two sources would fall under the reader just proved. This obstruction allows arbitrarily many binary two-output additions in the source and arbitrarily many intersecting four-place cycles. Its conclusion restricts a possible rival. The separate [one-exception and cactus theorem](cycle_inverse.md) actually recovers the answer against all rivals in its stated cases.

**A necessary seven-to-six step.** The cutoff reader just proved uses only the condition on short shores. Thus two sources each satisfying that condition have identical incidence whenever their tables match through distance five, independently of their output counts. At least one side of any opposing pair must have a bond of size at most six with at least three ordinary sites on its shore. Suppose there is only one ternary junction. Along the selected shore in chronological order, the first three sites have boundary at least seven. The only possible negative increment \(p+h-2j\) is \(-1\), attained when a three-parent site makes two outputs and **all three** of its parents lie inside the selected shore. The first step to a boundary of size at most six therefore goes from exactly seven to exactly six. It uses three of those seven bits and replaces them by two fresh bits. The resulting six-bit word is itself a bond in the full final source. A decomposition would need two disjoint ternary bonds despite the unique ternary site. The earlier seven-bit boundary need only be a cut. As a reachable control, three unary children of the seed making three outputs each, followed by a two-output binary site on each pair, admit one such triple. On this prefix it reduces a boundary of nine to eight. [Literal shrinking control](families.json) This gives a precise target whenever an opposing example is sought in the single-ternary branch, including when the four-place blocks are more complicated. It requires neither the cactus condition nor the identifying high pairs.

## Five-place source equations survive arbitrary rivals

Assume \(h\ge p\) and the source has a unique ternary site. Every five-place star \(S\) then has two inputs and three outputs. For any two of its outputs the minimum common bond has size five. A smaller one could neither use the five-place holder in an adjacent sum nor connect the distinct same-color children. A loose output has no close outside bit through this threshold and can be paired with any other output.

If all are used, a bit outside \(S\) close through five to the output ending at \(c\) must meet \(c\). The only other possible holder is the seed, and it occurs only if \(c\) is four-place and meets the seed. This follows from the complete list of short bonds above. There are at most two such exceptional children. The first child using both the seed and the five-place site already needs a common earlier neighbor, consuming a distinct third seed bit if there are two such children. If there is at most one, choose two ordinary children whose short outside bits can meet only themselves. If there are two, choose one and the remaining child. In this last case the remaining child cannot meet the seed either, since all three seed bits have already been used at the older witness and the two exceptional children. In either case no bit can meet both children of the same ordinary color, and the chosen remaining child avoids the seed whenever that matters. Thus no outside bit lies within \(K\le5\) of both selected outputs. The minimum forces all five bits of \(S\) to form a single rival bond in every presentation with the same clipped table. A six-place site with a physically loose bit has the identical argument at \(K=6\) using that bit and any other from its star. These conditions are statements about the source. Their certificates use just the table.

This also fixes **every parity equation through size five**, against any reachable rival, at \(D\le4\). The three- and four-bit equations agree in both directions by the [short-equation theorem](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F-P001_finite_interval/structural.md#recovering-every-equation-through-size-four). A source five-bit equation is a bond because the minimum equation size is three. Its short shore makes it a five-place star, just forced, or the sum of the seed and an adjoining four-place star, already forced as equations. Conversely let \(W\) be a rival five-bit equation. It too is a bond. If it contains two seed bits, adding the seed equation gives a common word of size at most four, proving \(W\) is in the source. Otherwise at least four bits in \(W\) avoid the seed. Their pair minima have size at most five. Under the short-shore condition each such pair meets, since a cross-pair of a five-bit adjacent sum requires a seed bit. The four bits therefore share a single holder. Its degree is at most five. Indeed two distinct bits at a six-place holder have minimum six, as their opposite endpoints are distinct, of the same color, and cannot give a competing two-site bond. The holder's support is a common equation. Adding it to \(W\) leaves a rival word of weight at most two, hence zero. This identifies \(W\) with the source star.

Consequently **every actual rival junction of size at most five is an actual source junction**. An adjacent sum small enough to be confused with a star must use the seed and a four-place site. Its two bits at the common actual seed rule it out as another physical rival star. If the source also has maximum degree five, a competitor having no six-place sites at all has the identical actual junctions. Indeed its stars are a subset of the source stars and must span them all by the common-equation result. The source stars are independent. This last implication holds at \(D\le4\), with no condition on the rival's fresh counts or the source's intersecting four-place cycles. It is sharp, since the [live width-26 example](fixtures.md) changes a marked verdict at distance three while both presentations have maximum arity five. In particular an opposing unrestricted rival for a maximum-five source in this section must contain a six-place junction as well as a three-parent, two-output junction if the full table agrees.

A six-place site with three neighbors of arity at least five also has an identifying pair. At most two of those neighbors can meet the seed. The seed's first neighbor had to grow unary and has arity four under the single-ternary premise. Choose a high neighbor avoiding the seed and any other high neighbor of the six-place site. A close outside bit on either of these arms must meet the corresponding high neighbor, or possibly the seed if that neighbor is five-place and meets it. The two neighbors have the same ordinary color and the first avoids the seed. This proves the required disjointness. Thus this test can also be used for six-place sites whose loose outputs have all been used.

By the reverse theorem, any such source whose four-place components are classical cacti and whose six-place sites pass either test has a complete reader against all reachable rivals. Other pairs certifying the six-place stars can replace the two tests individually. [Structural reverse proof](cycle_inverse.md)

## A bounded obstruction in the four-place graph

There is a second useful consequence of the [shortest-word argument](cycle_inverse.md#what-an-extra-equation-would-force). Assume a unique ternary site, only one- and two-site shores for bonds of size at most six, and an identifying pair for every five- and six-place star. Allow the ordinary graph \(G_4\) on the four-place sites to have arbitrary cycles. Every possible extra equation of minimum weight in an unrestricted matching rival would have weight six. At least five bits \(e\) of this word avoid the seed. Let \(V_e\) be their one or two four-place endpoints. The subgraph \(J\) of \(G_4\) induced by their union has at most twelve vertices, **diameter at most three, and contains a cycle of length four or six**.

Indeed if \(V_e,V_f\) meet or are adjacent, any two chosen endpoints in these sets are at most three links apart, using the internal link of either set if needed. Otherwise \(e,f\) share a non-four-place holder, so each has just one four-place endpoint. They have used up their possible partner on which this second route is necessary. Any third \(V_g\) therefore meets or is adjacent to both. It connects their singleton endpoints by at most three links inside \(J\). The forest case of the shortest-word proof excludes an acyclic \(J\). A shortest cycle in a graph has an opposite pair at distance half its length when the graph is bipartite. A shortcut between them, together with a half-cycle different from it, would give a shorter cycle. The diameter bound now gives the assertion. This bound counts ordinary links in \(J\). The observed table gives bond distances on distinctions.

Thus a source satisfying the first two structural conditions with no four- or six-cycle in \(G_4\) also has a complete inverse against unrestricted rivals. The argument allows longer intersecting cycles. More generally it bounds the four-place sites occupied by a shortest extra word, even in a much larger arrangement. It bounds neither the width of an opposing pair nor the part of the table that would have to match to certify one.

## The sharp horizon with a four-cycle in the marked part

Take the 51-bit open arrangement with three legal marked bits on the parents of the last site of a two-by-two-by-one predecessor box (these numbers are the maximum coordinates, beginning at zero). Two marked sites \(A,B\) have six places. At \(A\) take one spare output and grow the seven-site fixture of the main proof, identifying the fixture's earlier parent with \(A\). It adds six sites \(t,p,q,w,b,c\) and 17 bits. Add one more binary three-output site on \(A,p\), witnessed by \(t\). Use the other spare output of \(A\) and \(p\)'s free output. Only \(w\) makes two outputs. These seven additions put the new four-cycle and the three old marked holders in a single component even after all ordinary bridges are deleted. [Original 51-bit witness](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001H/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001H-P001_high_junctions/marked_distances.md)

The resulting 71-bit source has nine legal triples. Exchange its marked loose bit at \(A\) with a spare loose bit at \(B\). The marked triple now uses \(B\) twice. The swap preserves all 2,485 clipped entries through \(K=5\). Neither bit belongs to a short bond up to that size since its only holder has six places. At \(K=6\), twenty entries change. In particular the two loose bits at \(B\) share a six-bit bond, whereas the original marks at \(A,B\) cannot be on any bond that short. Thus even with this cycle in the same marked part and exactly one exceptional binary join, **bond distance five is sharp for the complete join reader**.

To test the boundary and reader with intersecting cycles, let both \(b,c\) of the fixture take two inputs and make only two outputs. Its six four-place sites have eight internal meetings. Append a three-output junction on \(c,q\) and a three-output junction on \(b\) and that new site. One loose bit each on \(p,c\) and the last site gives a legal triple. Repeat this entire construction from a spare output at the last site. The incidences and witnesses in earlier groups are preserved, providing arbitrarily many intersecting cycles overall. At one, two and four repetitions the reader recovered all 9, 17 and 33 junctions, and all 3, 5 and 9 allowed triples, respectively, including after relabeling. The boundary argument covers every length within the specified class. The [block proof](blocks.md) also settles these repeated examples against every reachable rival.
