## What holds

**Parity proof (A2).** Use fixed complete local records containing the old distinctions on which each reader’s equations and readings depend. Project out setting-independent middle distinctions and allow unused setting-private fresh distinctions to vary freely. At each nonempty setting the allowed states satisfy affine parity equations. A linear reading parity is constant or balanced under uniform counting, so each correlation is 0 or ±1. Projection onto surviving edited states has constant fibre size at that setting, so equally weighting grown states (U) and equally weighting surviving edited states then splitting their mass among completions (S) agree.

Suppose three setting pairs have constant reading parities. Relabel the settings so the fourth is 11. Take **any** allowed record pair $(a,b)$ at 11. Equality of the complete local supports supplies $(a,b')$ at 10 and $(a',b)$ at 01. Adding these three assignments modulo two gives $(a',b')$ in the old affine relation. Its two records obey the local conditions at 00. Adding the three known output parities gives the output parity of the original pair at 11, with all constant signs accounted for. Thus either at most two correlations are nonzero, or the four signs multiply to +1. Both cases give CHSH ≤ 2.

For three readers I independently exhausted the two homogeneous cases in the corrected README. With one value each, 756 of 1,024 rule tuples pass the support test. With two values for one reader and one each for the others, 16,500 of 26,800 pass. Both maxima are 3/4. That check requires each **pair’s** support to be unchanged by the third setting, which also gives the individual support equalities. The growth census checks each reader individually. The two-reader argument supplies no three-reader theorem. [Finite checker](arguments.py) · [Corrected proof text](../README.md)

**Guessed settings (A3) and local factors (A4).** In the two-reader example each setting keeps two of eight states, with the required reading parity and a fair local output. The four correlations are $(1,1,1,-1)$. In the three-reader example each setting keeps four of 32 states. Every one- and two-reader record distribution agrees at fixed settings of those readers, and all four scored settings win. These are supplied examples.

For A4, fix the prepared state, including any setting-independent common data. If the compatible edited inputs and completions form a product of separate reader choices, let $n_i(r)$ count local completions yielding record $r$ for a given local edited input. U uses these counts. S uses $n_i(r)/\sum_{r'} n_i(r')$ within each surviving edited input, and zero for an input without a completion. Sum each reader’s factors over its compatible local edit values, multiply, average over the common preparation and normalize for the setting pair. This gives the stated local-rejection representation. Finite local factors can be scaled to acceptance probabilities. The preparation is setting independent before selection. Its conditional distribution among accepted runs may depend on the settings.

## Fixes

1. **Block — the flag does not establish the hypothesis of A4 or A2.** The decision says, “When each reader's setting is a free local edit and no birth or stall depends on both settings, the four tables are a local model with local rejection: a preparation that does not depend on the settings, and responses that each depend on one setting, zero where a join forbids.” The source condition needed for this inference is a product of the **actual** one-setting responses given the preparation. Even a dependence solely on the wrong reader’s setting can violate that condition. The flag compares only round status and the parents, old inputs and fresh counts of births with **no reader parent**. In the main census a shared birth gets a separate disposition. In the addendum it can pass the flag.

   The seven-knot sampled start numbered 1007 has addressed readers (3,6) and (2,0). All four settings fund, all births belong to one reader apiece, and all 386,384 kept not-all-equal assignments survive each setting under the ordinary grant. At Bob’s setting 0, switching Alice changes the inputs of Bob’s birth with parents (2,5) from (14,16) to (14,17). Give value one to exactly old distinctions 1, 3, 8 and 17, and zero to both additions. The first input pair is 00 and allows seven fresh triples. The second is 01 and allows eight. The probability of fresh triple 000 changes from 0 to 1/8 under either weighting conditional on this assignment. The archived old-reading scores are 24/54131 under U and 0 under S. A six-knot addressed placement, number 699 with readers (5,0) and (3,2), also changes Bob’s birth inputs at fixed Bob setting. Conversely, both built addressed starts fail the flag although their two copies factor given their one shared distinction. 2. **Narrowing — a rejection representation can come from positive completion counts.** The README says, “So on placements that split, no forbidding law can give more than such a model gives: it can exceed 2 only by rejection, and then nothing in it is nonlocal.” Give each side of the eight-state guessed-setting example three local completions for a correct guess and one otherwise. Every prepared state survives every setting. U gives correlations $(7/8,5/8,5/8,-1/8)$, hence CHSH 9/4. Rescaling the counts gives a probabilistic rejection representation. Locality here describes the model **including** its rejection outcome. The accepted-only A3 table itself violates ordinary Bell locality with CHSH 4. [README, A4](../README.md) · [Exact example](arguments.py)

3. **Narrowing — qualify “never” by how a birth belongs to the readers.** The README says, “Forbidding never answers both readers' settings in a grown start.” I rebuilt all 880 nonshared law/placement rows reporting old-state loss in the census and extension pre-count populations. None had two separate readers whose own edits changed the assignments forbidden by their own births. I also rebuilt **all 780 shared-birth rows reporting loss**. Under one fewer, changing either reader’s own edit changed the assignments rejected by shared births in **163** one-holder placements under not-all-equal and **68** under parity. In **one** three-reader not-all-equal placement, all three edits mattered. At that five-knot start, numbered 4821, the common five-parent birth at 000 retains 1,410 of 1,514 assignments. At 011 all 6,056 survive. No shared-addendum row had separately owned births answering every reader. The code’s `on every side` includes a shared birth for each participating reader. Its `two-sided forbidding` requires separately owned zero-fresh births in the same setting. 5. **Narrowing — distinguish the declared record, the stored record and a single reading.** The README specifies, “**Read per placement and setting pair:** the four (eight) tables; the largest CHSH over the eight sign conventions (Mermin win over relabellings); whether each reader's single value and its complete record (every value of its holders and of births with one of its holders as a parent, with the shapes of those births, named from the reader's own side) have the same joint distribution for every far setting; the surviving fraction per setting pair.” The stored record contains the holder values, the birth descriptions and the fresh values. It omits the **values** of inputs contributed by other parents. At the five-parent birth above, the first reader holds distinctions (1,4,9) and the code omits input values 7, 8, 10 and 11. Once all local old inputs are given, the fresh values of each birth are uniform on its allowed completions under either weighting. Fixed birth descriptions and equality of the full old-input distributions therefore suffice for this last check. 8. **Narrowing — specify the efficiency in the literature bound.** The README says, “Its bound is CHSH ≤ 4/η − 2 for a local survival fraction η (Branciard, *Physical Review A* 83, 032123, 2011, cited from the advisor; to check), which A3 meets at η = 1/2.” For the conditional-efficiency CHSH bound, η is the minimum over setting pairs and directions of one reader’s acceptance conditional on the other’s. Branciard’s equations (3)–(5) impose setting-independent efficiencies and independence from the far outcome, including rejection. The symmetric case of (13)–(14) gives the bound. The archived fraction of edited states with a completion is a joint quantity and does not identify η. In A3 each local efficiency is 1/2, joint acceptance is 1/4, and the uncapped right side equals **6**. A3 attains the algebraic cap of **4**. This is a two-reader inequality. [README, A3](../README.md) · [Branciard, §§II–III](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1010.1178) · [Larsson, conditional-efficiency bound](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.57.3304)
