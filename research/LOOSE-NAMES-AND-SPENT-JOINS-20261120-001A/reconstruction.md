# A second check that exhausts the named holdings

The reference stage was selected from the terminal catalogue by the frozen width and tie order. This computation compares its full named table with **every** genuinely reached finite ordinary not-all-equal presentation. Rivals can have another age or source, use another allocation of old names to parents and have different physical symmetries. The separate compact proof works directly with the number of possible parents. ## Assignments considered

Write \(C_{ij}=1-2r_{ij}\) and put \(D=\{0,\ldots,28\}\). For a proposed loose \(f\), its support \(P\) must consist of \(f\) and every other name attaining its negative row minimum. For any true loose \(f\), its loose siblings are exactly the other names of this support whose rows agree with its row at every exterior position of the pair. A spent name would need another preceding holder. Uniformly the row difference against \(f\) is strictly negative at a name of that older holder. Under recursive division an older input and \(f\) cannot be interchangeable. With at least two old inputs, the older row would have its minimum only on the other inputs of this same child, each from a different preceding parent. With one input its conditional correlation with \(f\) is a negative multiple of its own exterior row, which is nonzero at an actual older companion. This argument uses no source ages. [Detailed proof](role_proof.md)

Mandatory tests include all local proper three-name projections, the probability that the companions are constant, and the full exterior-row relation when the child has one or two old inputs. For the latter, conditional on all old names the mean sign of a fresh name is \(-M_U/(2^q-1)\), where \(q\) is the number of fresh names and \(M_U\) is the common sign of the old inputs \(U\) when they are constant and zero otherwise. For one or two inputs \(M_U\) is their average sign. Conditioning also on another child's fresh names leaves the equality valid by conditional independence. A proposed wider holder cannot contain a forced mixed triple properly. For a proposed four-place holder, combine the observed companion and exterior pairs with the conditional loose identity to infer the fourth signed moment and require all the resulting pattern probabilities to be nonnegative. These checks leave nine uniform orientations and ten inherited orientations. Different orientations can refer to the same named support. A later ordinary birth always has at least one fresh name. Different births in one cut have disjoint supports. We take all nonempty collections of disjoint feasible orientations, including collections omitting familiar-looking supports. For each proposed input \(x\), suppose an older holder \(K\) supported it when it was loose. Uniformly the other names of \(K\) are among the minimizers of \(C_{xz}-C_{fz}\) for another, fresh \(f\) in the birth. Under recursive division they are among the minimizers of \(C_{xz}\) on all names remaining after that whole candidate cut's fresh names are removed. At a source with repeated meetings there can be additional minimizers, so every allowed subset of them is considered. Each \(K\) has at least three places. It meets each present newborn in at most one place, avoids every newly born name, permits all mixed words and uses at most two incidences per old name. All old inputs assigned to one \(K\) must nominate that entire same support. Distinct previously loose inputs of the same child have distinct \(K\)'s. These are all necessary conditions on arbitrary rivals.

The grouping of the new holders by their preceding holders supplies every current common-witness set. The current ordinary proposal list gives the demand at which those holders would have been formed; a proposed current group of \(p\) inputs with \(q\) fresh names must have received exactly \(\max(1,3-p,d)\). For the preceding cut the pairs of older holders found together in a current birth are exactly its witnessed pairs. Every group of size at least three among the births must be a maximal complete set, all remaining witnessed pairs must be covered in the specified way, and every maximal complete set which did not become a birth must fit in an actual joint witness. Count all previously shared slots available to realize these witnesses. This last test is a necessary relaxation: it may allow extra candidates. A unique survivor in a collection of necessary relaxations suffices once a real history attains it. The single nearly feasible alternative under each weighting uses the correct five current supports and proposes preceding live holdings \(\{0,6,7\}\) and \(\{1,2,3,4,5\}\). The latter must previously have had exactly its five displayed names loose: each funds a current birth. With no earlier shared name it cannot meet a witness permitting its joint birth with the former knot. The surviving interpretation gives it a sixth name, 8. At any positive not-all-equal age the entire physical predecessor, if it exists, is uniquely obtained by removing all live holders and their fresh names. Every previous loose name was consumed, every actual new holder is live, and all shared old inputs still appear in their previous holders. Verify whether that physical cut really pays all the groups and prices. Repeating this operation presents every possible choice of the original source along a single finite chain; each funded cut added a name. For this 29-name presentation the recovered nine-name predecessor has two live holders and one spent knot on 6,7,8. It cannot itself be a reached stage. Deleting its two live holders would leave just that one ternary, whose first cut makes three unary births. The required two holders do not form at that cut. Thus even the complete physical founding presentation and positive age are forced here. The equal-division comparison did not assume them.

Direct enumeration on the nine-name source gave 310 words and full local projections of 6, 62 and 6 words. Deleting the three rules in turn added 62, 10 and 62 words. All the proposed current children have four fresh places. Each of the four unary children has 15 extensions for every old word. The two old inputs of the one binary child are equal on 154 source words, giving 15 choices there. They differ on 156 source words, giving 16. The whole extension counts are respectively 759,375 and 810,000. Their weighted sum is the full uniform word count 243,303,750. This independently certifies the realized surviving presentation and the actual source of the change under weighting.
