## 4. When pair rates suffice at all sizes

Under the not-all-equal law, take every connected group of three-place holders as one part, leaving each larger holder as its own part. Suppose the parts of each component form a tree and each join between parts uses one shared distinction. Then the uniform pair table reconstructs the named **full allowed words among all presentations with this stated property**, even without the part labels or ages. By the prior full-word result, those words fix all later ordinary birth counts. The statement allows cycles and repeated meetings of arbitrary size inside each ternary part. It also allows multiple physically disjoint components, whose names can be identified from nonzero pair correlations. [Proof and disconnected extension](proof.md)

Three names are forced to be mixed precisely when their three disagreement rates sum to two. Those triples identify the ternary parts. At an individual larger holder with q places the signed pair correlation is −1/(2^(q−1)−1). It multiplies through each single shared distinction. An intervening named distinction can thus be detected from the measured correlations, which identifies the wider holders. The observation of all named pairs and uniform weighting are supplied. For example, if words on one ternary knot in which a chosen pair differs receive twice the weight of the others, that pair’s rate changes from 2/3 to 4/5. The positive-weight test for forced mixed triples still works, but the uniform coefficient used for higher arity has a specified weighting. [Derivation, relation to known mathematics and checks](proof.md#what-the-uniform-rates-measure)
