# First-layer bond distance on paths of arbitrary finite length

**Corollary.** Take a path of knots \(b_0,\ldots,b_N\), with original shared distinction \(e_i\) between \(b_i,b_{i+1}\). Attach the five-ring \(b_s,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4,b_s\), where \(4\le s\le N-4\). Keep the four path spares at \(b_0,b_1,b_{N-1},b_N\). Each path knot has two loose distinctions except \(b_{s-1},b_s,b_{s+1}\), which have four each. On the ring, \(c_1,c_4\) have four each and \(c_2,c_3\) start with two each. The three registered versions add no spare, one at \(c_2\), or one at each of \(c_2,c_3\). Let all loose distinctions have their reference value. Compare the quiet preparation with the preparation that changes the five original shared ring values. Both obey every parity equation. The growth law passes cold groups and vetoes hot groups with two or more parents. [Law and bond reading](definitions.md)

Write \(I=[i+1,j]\) for the old path holders between two marks \(e_i,e_j\), with \(0\le i<j<N\). Define

\[
D_0(I)=1+2(j-i)+2\,|I\cap\{s-1,s,s+1\}|+2\,{\bf1}_{s\in I}.
\]

This is their common source bond distance. After one complete layer their bond distances are

\[
D_{\rm loaded}=D_0(I),\qquad
D_{\rm quiet}=D_0(I)-2\,{\bf1}_{\{s-1,s+1\}\subseteq I}.
\]

**Why padding preserves the round.** In the original source two ordinary knots eligible to join share one neighbor. A pairwise eligible triple with no common neighbor would require a cycle of six original knots, or a shorter cycle of three or four if some of the witnesses coincided with proposed parents. The only original cycle has length five. Thus the first multipart groups are precisely the pairs of distinct neighbors of an original knot. Each plain interior path knot away from the ends and the attachment requires two openings. The attachment and its two path neighbors require four each. At either end, the end knot and its immediate neighbor each require one opening and have one spare. The ring's original funding stays sufficient. Every vetoed input supplies a unary child instead. Both full first rounds are funded at all the stated lengths, with at least four children from the end spares alone.

**Cuts through two old marks.** For the parity calculation, represent a loose distinction by a connection from its holder to an auxiliary exterior. Adding the parity equations of any set of holders gives the cut of that set, and these sums exhaust all projected parity equations. The nonempty cuts minimal under inclusion are precisely those with both shores connected. Thus the least such cut size through the marks, minus one, is their bond distance. [Parity and connected cuts](../EDDY-WORLD-20261119-001/bending.md)

Let \(S\) be the old holders on the connected shore away from the exterior, and \(o_v\) their original loose counts. Keeping every new child outside gives a cut of size \(|\delta_{\rm old}S|+\sum_{v\in S}o_v\), where \(\delta_{\rm old}S\) counts the original shared distinctions crossing that shore. Inserting a child of \(p\) parents with \(a\) inside and \(f\) fresh distinctions adds \(p+f-2a\). A child with no inside parent cannot be part of this connected shore. A child with one inside parent can be removed as a leaf. A child of two inside parents can connect them at extra cost \(f-2\). In these sources \(f\ge2\) for every binary child. To see the costs directly, a surviving binary child on \(x,y\) has \(o_x+o_y-2\) other sibling incidences. For each other \(z\) with surviving children on \(x,z\) and \(y,z\), two sibling pairs become one unheld triple. Those are all the replacements because a pairwise-intersecting family of one- and two-member parent sets either shares one parent or consists of the three pairs on three parents. Such a replacement here occurs only among the four neighbors of \(s\), each of which has four opens. All other surviving binary children have \(f=o_x+o_y-2\ge2\). Each outside old holder can reach the exterior by a child outside when the only retained children have both parents inside. This gives an actual connected outer shore.

**Minimization.** An old ring holder away from \(s\) can help connect the retained path only through \(s-1,s,s+1\). If \(s\) is inside, its old meetings already connect all retained members of these three. Keeping \(s\) alone from the ring leaves two old ring crossings, no more than any nonempty proper ring part contributes. Removing a full ring except \(s\) saves more openings than the two crossings it creates. If \(s\) is outside and the ring is quiet, the direct child of \(s-1,s+1\) connects those neighbors at cost two, paid by the openings saved on any removed ring holder. Under load every mixed child with an off-path ring parent is vetoed. A minimum therefore exists with only path holders among its old knots. At the source the same argument omits all children. Its connected shore then occupies precisely \(I\), giving \(D_0(I)\).

After growth, the span of the retained path holders contains \(I\) since one endpoint of each mark must be inside. Connections along this span can skip only a single missing holder \(g\), using the child on \(g-1,g+1\). Starting with the whole span, such an omission creates two old path crossings, saves \(o_g+2{\bf1}_{g=s}\), and pays \(f-2\) for that child. At \(g=s\) the child has four fresh distinctions when quiet and six when loaded. Indeed its two parents have four opens each. The two other neighbors of \(s\) supply both sibling replacements when quiet and neither under load. Omitting \(s\) consequently saves two when quiet and zero when loaded. Every other available omission has nonnegative cost. At \(s\pm1\) the two parents include the four-open attachment and another knot with at least two opens, with no sibling replacement. Elsewhere \(o_g=2\) and \(f\ge2\).

Within \(I\) one can omit \(s\) exactly when both its neighbors are in \(I\). The direct binary child keeps the shore connected. Extending the span to bring a previously unavailable saving into reach adds at least one of these four-open interior neighbors. Extending to an original path end also costs at least two opens to save at most one end crossing. Thus no extended span gives a smaller value, whichever endpoints of the two marks are taken inside. This proves both formulas.

**Exact offsets and falloff.** For straddling marks \(i<s\le j\), their nearest old holders lie \(\ell=s-i-1\) and \(r=j-s\) original knot steps from the attachment. The shortest source incidence walks from the attachment to the two marks have \(2\ell+1\) and \(2r+1\) hops. If either offset is zero the load contrast vanishes. If \(\ell,r\ge1\), the quiet and loaded first-layer bond distances are exactly \(2(\ell+r)+9\) and \(2(\ell+r)+11\). Same-side pairs all have zero contrast. In total \((s-1)(N-s-1)\) of the \(\binom{N}{2}\) path pairs have positive contrast.

One choice of marks works at every length without inspecting the outcomes. Insert \(p,q\ge0\) ordinary two-open knots into the left and right portions of the original 17-knot path, keeping the four end spares in their end positions. Then \(N=16+p+q\) and \(s=4+p\). Always take the two outermost original shared distinctions \(e_0,e_{N-1}\). Their nearest-holder offsets are \(3+p,11+q\). Their first-layer bond distances are \(37+2p+2q\) quiet and \(39+2p+2q\) loaded. The absolute difference remains two as both offsets grow arbitrarily large. Its fraction of the quiet distance tends to zero. This is a statement about a single bond reading involving both original marks. A neighborhood measured separately at either endpoint is a different observable. [Original 17-knot comparison](distances_proof.md)
