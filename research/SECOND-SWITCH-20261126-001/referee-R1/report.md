# Can a far pattern change the switch receiver’s course after both exist?

**Confirmed with fixes.** **Block** on the general negative under a cap. The 43 selected far ends do come apart, and the recorded interventions give zero hearings. A four-knot far end from the same census stays linked under the cap of three. Two changes at its own members after round 3 change the receiver’s own birth count, in rounds 13 and 15.

## What holds

1. **Coming apart.** Rebuilt all 688 round readings for the selected 43 cap-three far ends. Both ends were present. They first lay in different classes in round 1 for 9 far ends, round 2 for 32 and round 3 for 2. Testing for one part, for one part or a direct link, and for a common class gives identical results on all 688 readings. Across **all 646** census entries after round 3, **419 were wholly apart, 205 were still linked and 22 lacked an end**. Twenty-eight stayed linked through round 16. [Structural replay](structure.json)
2. **Why different classes stay apart.** A part connects members through common knots. Parts link directly when a member of each holds the same distinction. A class includes all parts reached through those links. A waiting member keeps its places. A birth meets only its parents, all in one class. An older knot that could bring different classes together would already have met a member of each, or would itself have been a member linking them. Either contradicts the initial split. Groups, cancellations and fresh counts are local to those classes, and this law has no round-wide stall or closing rule. Adding one loose distinction to a current member creates no meeting. Barring one can remove a member. Neither joins classes. Future strictly own births descend only from current own members. The earlier induction therefore applies when the classes differ **at setting**, beyond the finite window. [Fold definitions §1.6](../../PARTS-MEET-AGAIN-20261124-001/definitions.md) · [Induction, items 1 and 4](../../PARTS-MEET-AGAIN-20261124-001A/preregistration.md)
3. **The negative.** Recount and independent regrowth agree on **0/416** first-panel capped changes, **0/80** ordinary-count changes and **0/236** cap-three waiting-member changes. All 18 far-end table rows and 167 populated horizon cells match. Of the 652 capped changes, **644** were set outside every receiver-own class (408 first-panel and all 236 at waiters). The remaining eight, both signs at the one-knot far end after round 3 under caps four to seven, were inside the receiver’s part. All eight reached the timing benchmark below. They supply finite negatives for linked placements. The interventions often changed growth elsewhere. Total births differed in 476/496 first-panel changes and 104/236 waiting changes. A stub supplied no own far member to change. [Raw recount](recount.json) · [Class at setting](structure.json)
4. **One fewer with waiting.** Rebuilt all 821 predecessor pair-and-law cases and all 3,985 second-change comparisons. Each one-fewer ration gave 360 second hearings in the same 120 of 376 pairs. In all positive pairs, the unmarked ends were wholly apart by round 4. With only the first change they stayed in one part through the second hearing. All 116 positive pairs reaching round 12 under the record’s ration and all 114 under the alternative remained together that long. The remaining 4 and 6 reached round 11. Birth counts alone reproduce all 720 first differences. The hearings were already recorded. This study adds the comparison of parts. [One-fewer replay](contrast.json) · [Earlier result](../../SHORT-AGAIN-20261126-001/AGAIN.md)
5. **Receding and waiting.** Seven of the original 18 runs supported even one consecutive far-member distance comparison from round 3. All 96 steps were +1, in six capped runs and one ordinary run. The nearest member was seven sharings away after round 1 and nine or ten after round 3. Under cap three the one-knot far end reached 30 at round 24 and the two-knot end reached 30 at round 23. Rebuilding all 646 census runs reproduced 10,330 rounds of nearest-own distances, nearest-waiting distances and own birth counts. There were **521** with every recorded step +1, **44** with no far member from round 4, **50** with a nonpositive step, and **31** with all steps positive and some greater than one. A nonpositive step can precede a renewed increase. [Census counts](recount.json) · [Member identities](structure.json)
6. **Horizon.** The archive and the independent growth both give **110/349** capped additions heard and **0/59** ordinary additions. All 110 first differences occurred at the same round using own birth counts alone, and all began in a receiver-own part. Another 193 capped additions inside such a part and 46 outside went unheard. Hearings fell at the setting round plus the number of sharings in 2 cases, then one, two and three rounds later in 65, 28 and 15. At one sharing, 51/102 were heard and an earlier hearing was impossible. At two or more sharings, 59/306 were heard. All 306 allowed at least one earlier round to be observed, and 299 covered the full earlier interval. [Timing and denominators](recount.json)

## What was not run

No exhaustive intervention search over the 205 linked census entries, all members, all initial changes, other join lengths, or unbounded growth. The four-knot example addresses a change set after both ends exist. A second change set after that receiver has already switched was not tested there. The full-length ordinary-bar swap exceeded its memory allowance. Only the next round is reported for that swap. The original three one-fewer pairs could not be identified as a particular subset. Neither predecessor received a separate referee decision here.

## How it was checked

Each fraction weights its listed placements or census entries equally. The original study was exploratory. Its proposed positive hearing was revised after advice and a 48-change trial. The census and the parts explanation followed the first table. The scripts here import no author reading or analysis code. [Growth](growth.py) rebuilds met sets, groups, shortness, births, demand, ancestry, parts and their classes from the definitions. A knot is strictly own when every starting ancestor belongs to that pattern. The receiver hears a change at the first difference in the parent-derived names, fresh counts or number of places of its own births. The slow way matches the stub in own births. Independent names use parentage and sibling rank. [Structural comparisons](structure.py) include a uniform sample of 16 census entries, seed 26112643, and then the entire 646-entry census. [Raw recount](recount.py) uses the compressed outputs. The archived count sequences permit direct paired comparison. Horizon rows save only the hearing and endpoint. A seed-20261126 stratified sample plus targeted cases covered 143 interventions before the full replay. All 1,140 archived interventions then matched, including every first hearing and common endpoint. Historical per-round name lists were unavailable. The full changed-run replay took 143 seconds and peaked at 71,264 KiB. [Changed growth](replay.py), [the one-fewer comparison](contrast.py), [scope swaps](swaps.py) and [the direct-link counterexample](argument_check.py) perform those separate checks. [Witness verification](verify_witness.py) independently reconstructs the four-knot start and both changes without importing the first implementation.

From the repository root, run these commands serially. The pilot flags use the same growth and bounds on fewer cases.
