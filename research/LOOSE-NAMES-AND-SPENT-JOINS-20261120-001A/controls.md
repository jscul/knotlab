## What the local tests eliminate

Write $C_{xy}=1-2r_{xy}$ for the signed pair mean. A true loose name $x$ locates its companions $S$ at its strictly negative row minimum $-q$. The original screen demands at least two companions. It checks that $q$ equals the measured constant mass for two or three companions, or lies strictly below that of each proper companion triple. The supplied preliminary checks then use positive proper projections, the funded arity and input requirements for its in-holder pair twins, the constant-input mass and the exterior conditional row identity for one or two inherited inputs. The tests require only the candidate and exact observed table. True roles from the reconstructed history enter later to score each exclusion.

At 29 names the historical screen leaves **six** false indices under uniform words and **seven** under recursively inherited weights. Uniform name 0 is a literal example. Its companions are 6 and 7, with signed minimum **−481/2403**. It passes the companion-pair constant test and fails the two-input conditional row identity. That identity removes 0 and 8 at each weighting. The preceding sequence leaves 2, 3, 4 and 5 as false indices under uniform weighting and adds name 1 to those four under inherited weighting. All twenty actual loose indices pass. The absolute exterior test independently rejects 0 and 8 under uniform weighting, and the companion-pair exterior test independently rejects both under each weighting. [Per-index rates and exact rejection witnesses](../../artifacts/LOOSE-NAMES-AND-SPENT-JOINS-20261120-001A/LOOSE-NAMES-AND-SPENT-JOINS-20261120-001A-E001_loose_roles/control_target.json)

For an exterior $y$, the loose-name conditional identity assigns the all-constant companion event masses $L_+=(q-C_{xy})/2$ and $L_-=(q+C_{xy})/2$ according to the sign of $y$ relative to the companions. Their positivity gives the absolute exterior test. Each $L_\pm$ is bounded by the corresponding pair-equal and exterior-sign mass for any pair from $S$. These upper bounds give the companion-pair exterior test. With two companions their upper bounds are attained. For the three-companion test, consider a triple $T\subseteq S$ together with $y$. Its two all-constant complement-pair masses $P_+,P_-$ sum to the observed constant-triple mass $M$. Each of the other six complement-pair masses equals one observed pair-equal and exterior-sign mass minus the corresponding $P_\pm$. Thus the four-name feasibility test asks whether $P_\pm\ge L_\pm$ can be assigned within the smallest such upper bounds $U_\pm$, with sum $M$. A realized table gives $U_++U_-\ge M$. The earlier checks give $L_\pm\ge0$, $L_++L_-\le M$, and $L_\pm\le U_\pm$, which suffice. So this particular four-name test gives no further exclusions after the pair and constant tests on a realized table. The separate four-name test on the alleged inputs uses their rescaled constant mass. It also gave zero additional exclusions here.
