# Two reports from one evaluator on five adjacent products

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

Let \(x_0,\ldots,x_5\) be independent Boolean inputs, \(p_i=x_ix_{i+1}\), \(Q=\sum_{i=0}^4p_i\), and \(P_A=\sum_{i\in A}p_i\), with sums in \(\mathbb F_2\). Eliminate the opposed public checksum \(t\) by \(t=1+s\). The first evaluator requires \((s,u,v)=(Q,P_A,P_B)\). The second requires \(s=1+Q\) and allows every \(u,v\). Starting from all visible assignments, replace an affine case by the intersection of the affine hulls of its two separately allowed subsets, repeating to a fixed point. An empty case is terminal. A live case may ask any affine question on all its visible coordinates. Charge one leaf per terminal case in the resulting tree.

**Every choice of the two masks requires exactly eight leaves. The minimum worst-case query depth is three.** This includes all 35 independent two-dimensional mask spaces, all single reports and the empty interface. Giving the first evaluator **each of the five individual product results** still costs eight. Fixing \(x_1,x_3,x_5\) attains both minima in every case. After these answers \(Q\) is affine, so the separate checksum equations disagree throughout the remaining case. [Explicit tree](certificate.json) · [Checked results](checks.json)

Report placement matters. If each evaluator separately reports the sum of the first two products in its own bit, ask whether those bits agree. On disagreement, fixing \(x_1\) makes the shared prefix affine and closes both answers. On agreement, fixing \(x_3,x_5\) makes the remaining three products' sum affine and closes four answers. This gives six leaves with one report on each side of the same original problem. ## Which inputs can close together

Suppose the first evaluator reports any list of functions privately against the same second evaluator. In a case let \(S\) be the original inputs still allowed by the first evaluator. Its points have one honest lift each, including \(s=Q(x)\) and all its reports. Let \(V\) be their affine hull and let \(\pi\) forget all reports, preserving \(x,s\). Thus \(H=\pi V=\operatorname{aff}\{(x,Q(x)):x\in S\}\). We may take the first hull before the second. Both alternating and simultaneous iteration descend to the greatest common fixed affine subcase, since every common fixed subcase survives each update.

The second evaluator is free on every forgotten coordinate. For any subset of \(H\), its whole inverse image in \(V\) has hull equal to the inverse image of its hull: every nonempty fibre has the same direction kernel, which the inverse image contains in differences. Thus the second hull fills \(V\) precisely when
\[
D=\{(x,s)\in H:Q(x)+s=1\}
\]
spans \(H\). If \(D\) spans, all first-evaluator points in the current case survive. If \(D\) fails to span, it is already affine or empty. To see this, choose affine coordinates on \(H\) so that \(D\) lies in \(y_1=1\). The squarefree polynomial \(Q+s\) has degree at most two and vanishes on \(y_1=0\). It therefore equals \(y_1\) times an affine function. Its one-set is the intersection of two affine equations. This second hull contains no honest lift of \(S\). The next first hull is empty. The same conclusion holds for empty \(S\) or \(D\). This proves an exact terminal test independent of the chosen report functions. A live nonconstant question splits its honest inputs faithfully, since these points span the current case.

For the present \(Q\) this test has a finite description. Write \(J(x,y)=Q(x+y)+Q(x)+Q(y)\). Its matrix is the adjacency matrix of the six-vertex path over \(\mathbb F_2\) and is nonsingular: \(J(x,e_i)=x_{i-1}+x_{i+1}\) with missing coordinates zero, and the six equations \(J(x,e_i)=0\) force all \(x_i=0\).

**The maximal terminal input sets have size twelve.** Choose a two-dimensional subspace \(E\) on which \(J\) vanishes, choose a coset of \(E^\perp\), and discard any one of its four \(E\)-cosets. Whenever the remaining honest inputs are a subset of those twelve, the case closes. Which subsets are reachable by the given questions is handled separately by the search.

To verify the last assertion, \(E^\perp\) has dimension four, with radical \(E\). On a translate, \(Q\) differs by an affine function from the product of two independent coordinates modulo \(E\). Their affine shifts allow the one-set of the product to be any one chosen \(E\)-coset. The other twelve honest inputs therefore share an affine checksum equation. The opposite inputs on that equation lie in the missing affine coset, so the case closes. Subsets inherit closure by monotonicity.

Conversely let \(T=\operatorname{aff}S\). If the checksum is free in \(H\) over \(T\), the graph of \(Q\) on \(S\) spans \(T\) times the free bit. Then \(Q|_T\) is nonaffine, and its opposite graph spans the same space, so this case is live. In a terminal case \(H\) must be a graph \(s=\ell(x)\). If the defect \(Q+\ell\) vanishes on \(T\), the direction of \(T\) is isotropic. Extend it to an isotropic three-dimensional space and choose a two-dimensional \(E\) inside that space. Its translate occupies at most two of the four \(E\)-cosets in the corresponding translate of \(E^\perp\).

Otherwise the nonempty opposite level is the common one-set of two affine literals by the factorization above. Their linear parts are independent, since a single opposite hyperplane would place all of \(S\) in the other hyperplane, contrary to its affine span. The polar form on the direction of \(T\) thus has rank two. Split its direction into a nondegenerate pair space \(P\) and radical \(N\). The opposite level is one \(N\)-coset. Extend \(N\) inside the nondegenerate four-dimensional \(P^\perp\) to an isotropic two-space \(E\). These isotropic extensions exist by repeatedly choosing a vector in the orthogonal complement outside the current space. Now \(E^\perp=P\oplus E\). Extending the omitted \(N\)-coset to its \(E\)-coset omits no element of \(S\), because \(E\) meets the original direction precisely in \(N\). This puts \(S\) inside one of the displayed sets.

There are \(63\cdot30/6=315\) choices of \(E\), four translates of \(E^\perp\) and four discarded cosets per translate. The twelve points span the translate of \(E^\perp\), from which its radical \(E\) and the missing coset can be recovered. This gives **5,040 distinct maximal sets**. An independent exhaustive calculation lists all 26,387 affine input domains \(T\) and the 361,600 distinct affine checksum graphs over their respective domains. For every spanning honest graph whose opposite level fails to span it directly verifies that the opposite level is already affine. All 75,064 such graphs have their honest inputs in the displayed family, and every one of the 5,040 maxima occurs. [Affine-graph and direct pair census](census.cpp)

## All questions and all 35 pairs

Encode masks by integers, with \(p_0\) in the low bit. On the honest graph an affine question is, up to swapping answers, a linear input form plus a product mask in \(\operatorname{span}(31,A,B)\). Conversely every such function is an allowed question. Adding the total to a mask is the invertible visible recoding \(u\mapsto u+s\) or \(v\mapsto v+s\). The opposite evaluator permits the recoded bits freely. Invertible changes of basis on the two reports also preserve the problem. Represent each class modulo 31 by its member with high bit zero, an integer in \(0,\ldots,15\).

There are \((15\cdot14)/6=35\) rank-two spaces. Each contributes six ordered bases and four lifts of each basis, hence 24 ordered raw choices. The following table gives every class once as its three increasing nonzero members \(a,b,c\), where \(c=a\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}b\). Each entry has optimum eight.

The 840 independent choices, the 180 choices of rank one and the four choices of rank zero exhaust \(32^2=1024\) ordered raw pairs. The checker enumerates the raw pairs and tests the affine lifts on all 64 original assignments. There are 511 effective nonconstant first questions for each independent pair. The six input monomials and the three independent product masks are linearly independent as Boolean polynomials.

For a nonempty live set \(S\), a budget \(k\) succeeds exactly when one of these questions divides \(S\) nontrivially into \(S_0,S_1\), and for some positive \(i<k\) both \(S_0\) at \(i\) and \(S_1\) at \(k-i\) succeed. A terminal set costs one leaf. A lower bound for any nonempty set is \(\lceil |S|/12\rceil\), increased to two when a live set would otherwise cost one. That point count alone gives six at the root and does not settle the threshold. The finite recursion rejects seven for all 35 pairs. It tests all 511 root partitions for each pair, permits arbitrary unbalanced subtree budgets and only identifies partitions with the same two actual children. ## Raw controls and a larger path

For \(A=1,B=2\), take \(x_1=x_3=0\), \(s=u=v=0\). The 16 raw points leave \(Q=x_4x_5\) free to vary. The first evaluator allows twelve inputs and the second allows the four with \(x_4=x_5=1\). The successive case sizes are **16, 4, 0**. Adding just the honest lift of the omitted input \(x=48\) to the twelve honest points and taking their affine hull instead yields a live case containing all sixteen honest inputs on this slice. Replacing the opposed charge by an equal charge leaves 64 common witnesses. The verifier rejects the original tree on this mutation and rejects a prematurely stopped branch on the opposed instance. It explicitly saturates both original relations at every node of the positive tree for every canonical pair, every singleton mask and the full product dictionary. [Raw relation, hull and tree verifier](check.py)

On the six-product path fix \(x_6=0\) and append a zero sixth product coordinate. This injects each five-product honest relation into its corresponding six-product relation. Affine queries pull back along the injection and common affine hull updates preserve inclusion, so a cheaper six-product tree would give a cheaper five-product tree. The inputs \(x_1,x_3,x_5\) also meet every product in the six-product path. The exact cost is again eight at depth three, even with all six one-sided products. The checker verifies the injection on all allowed points of both sides and replays the upper tree. This bounds deterministic trees with the stated inference rule.
