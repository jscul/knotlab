# Which feedback rules give one outcome?

**Exactly four of the sixteen tables give one coloured outcome on every allowed input, up to a renaming that preserves the old coloured stage. They are the four whose hot entry vetoes a joint birth.** Requiring the renaming to fix every old distinction individually leaves only V/V, which is insensitive to activity. Among the rules that retain ordinary growth on a quiet input, P/V is the unique state-sensitive rule passing the first test. On inputs with every loose distinction at its reference value, all sixteen pass the first test. On bare rings, P/V gives an exact future birth count for any prepared closed pulse and can change the cumulative growth from linear to quadratic. [Frozen definitions and comparisons](definitions.md)

## The state and the completed round

The old binary value at each distinction is its difference from the transported allowed reference. An assignment is allowed when every old parity holds. A loose distinction has one holder. All other distinctions have two. Assume that the old knots are connected through shared distinctions. Keep this incidence and assignment fixed while deciding a round. A group is hot if at least one parent has a nonzero distinction anywhere on its support. Determine the complete list of ordinary proposed groups first. Each group reads the same frozen input.

V deletes a multi-parent proposal. Each retained group takes one different loose distinction from each parent, and each remaining loose distinction receives its own unary child. Funding compares the **retained** demand at each parent with its supply. If every parent can pay, the resulting roster fixes each child's raw next demand $d$. A nonimplied child with $p$ inputs has floor $l=\max(1,3-p)$ and ordinary fresh count $h_0=\max(d,l)$. P, + and − give $h_0$, $h_0+1$ and $\max(l,h_0-1)$. V on a mandatory unary gives $h_0$. A sole implied closure keeps zero fresh distinctions under all actions. The new roster and its unchanged ordinary lookahead are both recomputed after veto. A later funding shortage is possible under −.

For a specified allocation of physical inputs and fresh values, all births of the plan can be listed in any order. They use disjoint old loose distinctions and disjoint new distinctions. Their incidence assignments agree even when they share an old parent. Completing the whole frozen plan gives identical incidence and values with identifiers transported. Any cap is a measurement stopping condition. Recomputing the roster or reading a changing old assignment halfway through is a different law. A complete round is the supplied unit of growth. Its serialization gives no elapsed-time unit. ## Parity and the minimum fresh assignment

On an ordinary-connected old stage, the possible vectors on all loose distinctions are exactly the even ones. Summing the old knot equations proves necessity. Conversely fix any even boundary vector and take a spanning tree of the old shared meetings. Set the other shared values to zero, then satisfy each nonroot equation by its last tree distinction, working inward. The even total satisfies the root. Thus every proper subset of all loose distinctions has a full projection.

Write $k_c$ for the number of active old distinctions consumed at child $c$, and $h_c$ for its fresh count. The new equation requires the parity of its fresh values to be $k_c\bmod 2$. With $h_c\ge1$ it has $2^{h_c-1}$ lifts for **every** old assignment. All child lifts can be chosen independently once the input ports are allocated. A child using all old loose distinctions already has even input for every old assignment. It closes at $h_c=0$. Appending fresh distinctions to this implied closure would make the old inputs a separately constrained proper subset of its proposed row. If there are several ordinary-connected components, the boundary sum is separately even in each. The single global closure criterion above uses the stated connectedness.

Consequently every fixed realised incidence extends the entire old linear parity space. For a nonimplied new child one can prescribe any even local word: its proper subset of old loose inputs is free, and each other child's own fresh distinctions complete that child's equation. Its support therefore projects to full even parity. This is constrained, nonempty, and forgetting any one place leaves every remaining word possible. The older knots' projections persist. The same full-even-boundary statement applies to a closing row. If it would have fewer than three places, no new knot of the admitted arity is formed. These are structural parity claims. When the law itself is required to produce an observed outcome, that outcome can convey information about its input. Requiring every possible outcome to be produced from every old assignment would itself make the set of structural outcomes state-independent.

Among the lifts of a specified old assignment, the fewest active fresh distinctions is exactly $\sum_c(k_c\bmod 2)$. Even input uses all zeros. Odd input uses one fresh distinction, with $h_c$ choices. The total number of named minimum lifts is the product of $h_c$ over odd-input children. These formulas compare relative values. If both a nonzero absolute reference and an absolute assignment are wanted, first extend the reference by any allowed lift and add the chosen relative lift. Selecting the absolute reference's fresh names introduces a separate convention.

A deterministic choice on fixed fresh names must be invariant under their permutations. The only invariant binary words in one fresh sibling set are constant. For odd input and even $h_c$, no invariant word has the right parity. With odd $h_c$ all ones is invariant, and is minimum only at $h_c=1$. The minimum-lift relation includes every permissible singleton. For example, on one ternary old knot with two active loose distinctions, every one of the sixteen tables gives each active unary child at least two equivalent fresh choices. An outcome modulo new names can be unique even in this example.

## Allocation and arbitrariness of names

Fix the old **coloured** incidence. A preserving renaming respects every old meeting and every old relative value. In particular it may permute loose distinctions of the same colour on the same holder. A stricter comparison fixes every old distinction separately and still allows fresh names to be transported.

*If the hot entry is V*, every parent participating in a retained multi-parent group is cold. Every loose distinction there is zero. Two allocations at a cold parent can be matched by permuting its old loose distinctions, holding all its shared distinctions fixed. Match each retained group to the child with the same old parent set, and match its selected old input accordingly. Match the old residual ports to unary children. At a hot parent all proposals containing it have been vetoed. Its unary child for each old port can therefore be matched literally by that old port, even when several different colours occur. These choices give one old-colour-preserving renaming of the whole plan. Corresponding children have the same fresh count and input parity. Permuting their fresh siblings then matches any two minimum lifts. The rule is invariant under renaming, so the argument iterates across every completed round, and matching runs encounter the same shortages or closure.

*If the hot entry retains a multi-parent group*, use three old knots on the path $A$–$B$–$C$. Give each outer knot two loose distinctions, one active and one at zero. Give $B$ one loose zero. Both shared distinctions are active, so all three parity equations hold and all three knots have arity three. The two outer knots have their joint birth through $B$, one unary each, and $B$ has a unary. Sending both active outer inputs to the joint child gives **zero** active fresh distinctions. Sending the two zeros to that same joint child instead gives **two** active fresh distinctions at the two outer unaries. Neither colouring requires a choice of an old knot name as an input to the rule. Their global active-fresh counts differ. These outcomes cannot be identified even by a renaming that forgets which outer knot was which. The fresh floor ensures the witness works for P, + and − as hot entry, independently of the cold entry. This accounts for all twelve failures.

*If every old name must stay fixed*, V/V succeeds because each child is unary and determined by its unique old loose distinction. The only freedom in its minimum lift consists of fresh sibling names. For hot V with any other cold entry, take the same three-knot source at zero. The two endpoints still have a joint child. An individual old loose distinction of $A$ can feed either the joint child or its unary. The old-names-fixed incidence distinguishes these choices. Together with the previous twelve failures this gives one success and fifteen failures. More generally, on a cold parent with two identical old ports and a fixed multi-parent role, the swap of those ports can forbid an equivariant selection of one literal input. The growth relation includes both allocations.

*If the old input is closed relative to the reference*, every loose distinction is zero even when shared distinctions elsewhere are active. At every parent the argument matching equal-colour old ports now works for any table. Every minimum fresh lift is zero. The sector is invariant, giving agreement up to old-colour-preserving renaming at every later completed size. If all old names are instead held fixed, even equal zero ports can distinguish the joint and unary roles. A programming order among equally coloured ports is sufficient for one displayed representative. It does not establish that the displayed named allocation was forced.

## Distinguishing the tables and the structural law

Exactly four tables are structurally state-blind, the four constant tables. This four-table set has a different definition from the four hot-V tables above. Only P/P gives the old structural policy on every stage.

Here is one uniform family distinguishing the four actions. Start with a bare ring of $n=5$ or any $n\ge7$ old knots, one distinction at each consecutive meeting, and two loose distinctions on each knot. Compare the all-zero assignment with the assignment supported on the entire original ring. The latter is closed, since each old knot has two active shared distinctions. Every parent is cold in the first preparation and hot in the second. Label the old knots by $i$ modulo $n$ only to describe the proof. The pair groups are exactly $(i-1,i+1)$. The candidate relation connects indices differing by two. It contains no three pairwise related indices at the specified lengths. There are $n$ groups and each parent belongs to two. On the kept roster, children with indices differing by two share a parent, and there are again exactly two next groups per child. Hence each binary child under P, +, − has respectively two, three, one fresh distinctions. V instead gives $2n$ unary births. The four structural profiles are pairwise different at the very first step. On a quiet input the cold action is consequently forced to P by agreement with ordinary growth on all stages. On a loaded input the hot action distinguishes every off-diagonal table. This argument counts one vote per rule only within the registered family.

The difference can be created by **one ordinary least four-distinction change** on a common physical handover. Grow the same source once using P/P. Each of the $n$ now-live binary knots has two loose distinctions. Its ordinary four-response uses its two private old inputs and the two shared source distinctions through the old common neighbour. This changes every affected knot in two places. A change through either marked shared source distinction needs at least four. Its endpoints are exhausted, there are no parallel meetings, and no ordinary third knot meets both endpoints to make a three-distinction change. This single four-response lights exactly one live knot, leaving all loose distinctions at zero. Exactly its two incident pair groups turn hot. Whenever exactly one of the table entries vetoes, the number of new knots changes by two compared with the quiet handover. When neither entry vetoes but their count actions differ, the roster stays fixed and those two binary children alone change their fresh counts, among one, two and three. Every off-diagonal table thus fails to commute with this state change already at the **uncoloured structural output**. A later parity lift after cold growth cannot change which knots or distinctions exist. The handover uses a common passive first round to test every table on the same incidence.

For P/− this gives an all-size fate difference. With the quiet handover, the two-input, two-fresh roster repeats by the previous incidence calculation and continues at every size. After the single four-response, precisely two new binary knots receive one fresh distinction each. All new knots are cold. The next P round again requires two input slots at each of those knots and is unavailable. A single four-response thus changes an endless continuation to a funding stop for every listed circumference. The remote part stops under the complete-round funding requirement. This does not measure a disturbance propagating around the ring.

For a diagonal table the structural roster is insensitive to that change. This does not identify a universal named lifting of all value changes. On a ternary source with three loose distinctions, the zero input has one minimum fresh assignment per fixed named plan, and changing two old loose values gives multiple named minima, even under P/P. No one preselected named translation bijects these two sets of minimum lifts.

## The selected veto rule has an exact activity-dependent growth rate

On the same bare-ring handover, **P/V gives exactly $n+sk$ births after $s$ completed updates**, where $k$ is the number of candidate pairs meeting the chosen initial set of hot live knots. For $s=0$ the boundary has $n$ knots. The result also applies if the entire raw ring is loaded at size one, in which case $k=n$. The quiet boundary has $k=0$. One ordinary four-response on the handover gives $k=2$. These are transient, shared-only states. This is a post-registration all-size result for the table that satisfies the arbitrary-assignment renaming requirement above.

To prove it, write the original candidate pairs as one or two disjoint cycles of indices differing by two. Each of the $n$ old live knots has two loose distinctions. V removes the $k$ pairs touching a hot knot. A cut cycle with $j>0$ deleted pairs becomes exactly $j$ finite paths, counting each isolated knot as a one-knot path. The number of vertices across all the paths and uncut cycles is still $n$.

A path on $q\ge2$ of these old knots gives $q-1$ binary children, one for each consecutive pair, and one surplus unary at each end. Their candidate pairs form a path on $q+1$ knots: two new children have a common ordinary neighbour exactly when they took inputs from the same old knot. Each old knot gave an input to two children. For an isolated old knot its two unary children give a two-knot path by the same test. An uncut cycle gives another cycle of the same length. These new components have no candidate pair crossing between them. The unary children at path ends have one next partner and a fresh floor of two. Internal binary children have two partners. A two-knot path consists of two unaries of fresh floor two. All children therefore receive exactly two fresh distinctions. Since all the old loose inputs were zero, all new live knots are cold.

The following P rounds repeat the same calculation, growing each of the $k$ paths by one per round and leaving each uncut cycle constant. The accumulated number from the handover, including its $n$ knots, is $n(s+1)+ks(s+1)/2$. On this family a single four-distinction change replaces linear cumulative growth with quadratic growth. Two hot sets have additive effects exactly when no candidate pair meets both of them. If their hot sets come from adding physical parity states, first account for the possibility of cancellation of shared coefficients. For the registered ordinary four-responses on this handover the two private distinctions at each selected live knot make its coefficient unambiguous.

As a direct control, six successive P/V counts agreed with this expression for the quiet, one-response, two consecutive-response, two distance-two-response, alternating and full-response loadings at each of the eight circumferences 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12 and 17. The raw source-ring loading also agreed at each length, giving 56 checked preparations. These are constructed readouts from the physical growth engine, in addition to the general argument.

## A transient state can change the asymptotic growth factor

This post-registration result uses the **P/+** table on the same common bare-ring handover. Every subset $H$ of its $n$ live knots can be made hot by taking the parity sum of their ordinary four-responses. At each selected knot its two private inputs are active, independently of cancellations among old shared distinctions. The next age still gives one binary child for each parent pair $(i-1,i+1)$. Its fresh count is three precisely at the indices
$$
S=(H-1)\cup(H+1),
$$
and two elsewhere. All loose values remain zero. Only cold P acts from the following age onward.

To compute the asymptotic rate we can restart the ordinary law from a bare source ring having one spare at each index in $S$, with no other spares. Here is why the restart is valid despite the different historical structures. At the modified handover each old holder that sees any new live children sees exactly the pair at $j-1,j+1$. The synthetic ring has exactly this list of live neighbour sets, and its loose counts are the same two or three at the corresponding holders. Thus its candidate groups, funded parents and unaries agree. After the first new births, all common witnesses for two newest children lie among their immediate parents. The identical rosters give identical next group demands, fresh counts and subsequent rosters by induction. At least two live ports are present, and the ring components persist under the ordinary identified growth. This comparison is between the sequences of new births. The old incidence and the bond-distance tables of the two handovers need not agree.

The synthetic ring is fully funded, has at most five places per knot and falls in the ordinary decorated-ring class. A walk that advances its index by two has $m=n$ places for odd $n$ and $m=n/2$ for even $n$. Write $k=|S\cap C|$ on such a walk $C$. The unrolled ordinary ring has one optional single spare at each of those $k$ locations per period. No spare carries a further chain because its holder has three loose distinctions at the restart. In the counting variable $z$, a full period of a connected interval contributes $W(z)=z^m(1+z)^k$. If $k=0$ it gives constant counts per size. Otherwise every further full period gives another factor of $W$ and the end choices give a positive numerator at the positive root of $W(z)=1$. The terms of degrees $m$ and $m+1$ make that root the unique smallest-modulus root, and it is simple. The factor per size is therefore the unique $x>1$ such that
$$
x^{m+k}=(x+1)^k,\qquad \log x=(k/m)\log(1+1/x).
$$
The overall factor is the largest of those for its one or two walks. It is strictly increasing in their **maximum covered fraction** $k/m$, from one at zero coverage to the golden ratio at full coverage. This compares distinct hot sets through their coverage. Different sets can have the same rate. One four-response gives two spares on the **same** walk, since its affected child indices differ by two. On the five-knot ring the modified factor is approximately 1.262775 instead of one. Switching on the **entire raw source ring at size one** gives the same full-coverage restart on the first birth, so P/+ changes its factor from one to the golden ratio for every stated circumference. The same raw ring loaded after its source holders are exhausted gives zero contrast. The local signal changes a finite boundary, and the subsequent ordinary structural growth supplies the new rate. This theorem establishes a rate for the isolated bare-ring family. Attached and multiple-ring rates would require their own handover comparison.

Every P/− test then stopped with shortages of exactly one each at two knots. For P/+ at all eight lengths, the next eight stages matched the independently specified synthetic decorated source in their multisets of parent counts, demands and fresh counts. The same production engine grew both arms of this check. The all-size and asymptotic conclusions follow from the proof above. ## How long activity lasts, and which minimum has been met

Let $a_t$ be the number of active loose distinctions just before a completed step. Each old loose distinction is consumed exactly once. With the minimum fresh assignment,
$$
a_{t+1}=\sum_c(k_c\bmod2)=a_t-2\sum_c\lfloor k_c/2\rfloor.
$$
Thus active loose distinctions cannot multiply, although the number of new physical distinctions can increase. A new child is hot exactly when at least one of its consumed old inputs was active. The number of newly hot live knots is at most $a_t$. Even positive $k_c$ can make a child hot without transmitting an active loose distinction to its children. On arbitrary coloured inputs the loss can depend on the port allocation, as in the three-knot witness.

For $a_t=0$, every newborn support is cold. Every future live knot is newborn at its own stage, since this plan exhausts the old ports. Without an intervening change of assignment, a shared-only pulse can therefore influence the whole-parent read for at most **one** generation. In all later generations the cold action acts on the structure inherited from that response. A raw stored ring placed for the first time after all its old holders are exhausted has no influence at any subsequent age. It changes no live-parent value and adds no active loose distinction. More generally, two assignments agreeing everywhere except on distinctions whose holders are all permanently exhausted give the same current proposals and the same parity at every consumed port. Couple their allocations and minimum lifts identically. The new live values agree, so induction gives identical possible structural continuations at every common size, even in the presence of a common further stimulus. This proves the null for every incidence-derived reading, including all bond radii. A reader of consumed loose inputs alone always returns zero in the closed sector, even where a live parent carries a marked shared distinction. A reader taking only the parity of an entire parent's support also always returns zero. The common cold action in either control may itself differ from P.

For cold P and hot V, let $\mathcal R$ be the set of refused groups at a stage where the ordinary comparison is funded. Refusing a group of $p$ old parents removes one child and frees exactly $p$ ports for unary children. Hence the exact increment in births is $\sum_{P\in\mathcal R}(|P|-1)$. As a function of the set of hot parents this is a weighted coverage count. It is nondecreasing, is additive when no candidate group meets both stimuli, and saturates when the same group is reached from several parents. The statement compares hot sets. Superposing two parity assignments can cancel an occupied distinction, so superposition need not take the union of those sets. For cold P and hot + the roster is fixed and the extra fresh width equals the number of hot nonimplied children. For hot − on the same fixed roster the loss is one per hot nonimplied child whose original count exceeded its floor. [Frozen action definitions](definitions.md)

One observable bit is sufficient and information-minimal for a deterministic structural response to distinguish two assignments at the same old incidence. A constant observable cannot distinguish them. One changed entry is the minimum in table Hamming distance from P/P, and one fresh distinction is the smallest positive change of an integer count on a retained child. The local OR can inspect many distinctions. These bounds select neither its query cost nor the OR predicate across all rules. The frozen stage read, expiry of loose activity under the minimum lift, actions, admissible old renamings and complete-round funding are material hypotheses. The calculations describe possible growth of parity knots under those hypotheses.
