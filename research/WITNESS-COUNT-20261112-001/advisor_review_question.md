Review cold, at most ~250 words: is the lower bound right, what is settled by it, are the predictions/thresholds sensible?

Setting: NAE nested families (a 3-port definition holds its own pattern inside each of its knots, SELF calls), alive only in the usual limit, dead at every finite depth t >= T. Restriction lemma: every living state of the limit has, at each dead depth t, a SELF call of depth t (made from a level t-1 copy) with all three ends equal ("witness"). A prior test found one witness per depth almost never suffices (797/800 branching families).
Bound: a copy made by a call at depth s of definition i is "dead on its own at depth t" when i's relation at depth t-s is empty (no state of its insides for any ends). Each such copy needs a witness among its own depth-t calls (the lemma applied to the copy), and disjoint copies cannot share, so w_t >= A_t = largest antichain of dead copies (tree DP). Typically A_t = number of calls at depth t-T (each heads a dead copy of depth T), a fixed share of calls if branching is regular.
Measure: w_t = least number of witnesses at depth t over living states (SAT + totalizer, exact) for t = T, T+1, T+2; whether one state attains all three; in it, share of depth t+1 witnesses inside a depth-t witness's copy.
Predictions: P1 w_t = A_t at every depth read in >= 90% of families (the least count is one witness per independent dead copy). P2 growth law: fixed share (w/N at T+2 within factor 2 of w/N at T) in >= 80% of branching families; alternatives constant, between.
Is P2 settled by P1 plus the branching? Anything else settled? What would make the result interesting beyond the bound?
