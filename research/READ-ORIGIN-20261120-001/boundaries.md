# Recovering the origin at a complete boundary

A complete ordinary layer spends every loose distinction that its parents held. Unless a sole parity birth closes it, every new knot has a loose distinction of its own. At a positive open boundary, the knots with loose distinctions are therefore precisely the last layer. Remove them all together, delete their private distinctions, and restore the shared inputs to their older holders. This gives the only possible preceding complete presentation. To admit that predecessor, test its own knot law and whether its entire ordinary layer builds the proposed present with the correct fresh counts. [All-size argument](information.md#complete-boundaries-separate-prediction-from-recovery)

Repeating this removal gives at most one open predecessor at a time. The possible stopping points along that chain are candidate original starts. For a spent parity stage, first try each knot as the unique zero-fresh closing birth, then apply the same procedure. There are at most $1+J(J-1)$ literal source subsets for $J$ displayed knots under the complete-boundary promise. The five-knot ring below shows that historical recovery can need a number of bits proportional to the logarithm of the number of knots. Predicting all future complete layers at a known boundary needs zero origin bits under either law. Equivalent loose inputs at a holder may be exchanged.

## Computed controls under the supplementary freeze

- An odd ring of five knots with two loose distinctions each reproduces five two-parent births with two fresh distinctions in each complete layer. For each age from zero through 20, and under each law, the checker found exactly the original subsets given by the successive boundaries. At 105 knots and 215 distinctions, 21 possible original starts have different cardinalities and give the same future. Distinguishing these sources takes five bits. Joint renaming was checked at ages 0, 1, 5 and 20.
- Starting with one three-place knot, three complete layers under parity give eight knots and twelve distinctions. There are exactly 25 literal source subsets. Source sizes 1, 4, 7 and 8 have respectively 8, 8, 8 and 1 choices. The corresponding not-all-equal run has eight knots, thirteen distinctions and four possible sources, one at each of those sizes. If the fully spent twelve-distinction holdings themselves are admitted as a not-all-equal start, the inverse gives only the whole present. The counts 25 and 4 refer to subsets of a fixed display. The maximum number of inequivalent subsets at a closed parity stage is a separate basket question.
- For each exact current knot count $J=2,\ldots,12$, the two interpretations of a connected path give fresh counts three and two on the same old loose input. Here the current has $4J-1$ distinctions. The same construction works for every $J\ge2$. Start with $J-1$ knots on a path, four loose distinctions at one endpoint and three at every other knot. Write just one unary at the endpoint. The path, including the new child, still has at most two neighbors at every knot. Initial candidate pairs are two steps apart, with at most two incidences per live holder, and there are no multipart groups. Both starts can fund their pair and unary rosters. Each unary has only siblings at its sole parent as next candidates. Every pair involving that unary is a separate demand since all its candidate siblings share that parent. It consequently gets three fresh distinctions with the original four-slot supply and two with the new three-slot supply. Both interpretations retain at least one identical pending unary at the endpoint. For the singleton initial path the same count follows directly from the three or four unaries. The attached tree has the full local relations under each knot law. This gives actual binary ambiguity at every nontrivial knot count.
