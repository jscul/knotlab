# Six-bit bonds through a surviving fourth output

In a live one-spent source with a single six-place junction, a new attachment on a matching junction must co-occur in a source six-bit bond with either surviving output. The exact all-depth formation search finds four marked records with a candidate loose distinction of the required color. All four have a binary third three-output birth, a ternary spending child and exactly two shrinking joins. In each the qualifying shore is the entire ordinary source. This reduces the possible exchange to one formation branch regardless of the number of attachments retained. ## Why a qualifying shore contains all of its parents

Let \(H\) be the fourth three-output site, \(F\) its pair of surviving loose bits, and \(z\) a source-loose bit held at a different site. In a live source the two bits of \(F\) have row minimum \(K=6\), where \(K=D+1\). A rival six-place star containing \(F\) and \(z\) requires \(K(f,z)=6\) in the source. Any source shore \(U\) of an attaining bond must contain both holders, since the three bits are loose. The known small-site recovery makes the four small holders of the true attachments all opposite in color to \(H\). A replacement holder must have that same color. [Source reduction and sibling readings](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001O/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001O-P001_one_spent_output/live_low_span.md#the-possible-four-attachments)

At most two joins shrink on any live source. A one-site shore cannot contain both holders. A two-site shore containing \(H\) has boundary at least seven, by the degree bound and at most one shared bit. Order the sites of a larger shore by birth. Its first three sites have boundary at least seven. Including \(H\) in those three would give at least nine, since the three degrees sum to at least thirteen and there are at most two mutual meetings. Later sites each save at most one, so \(H\) comes after the first three.

An added site with \(p\) parents and \(h\) outputs, of which \(j\) parents are already in the shore, changes the boundary by \(h-p+2(p-j)\). An entering bit after the first three would add two to the balance, taking the final size to at least \(7+2-2=7\). All later selected sites therefore take their parents inside, including \(H\) and its spending child if selected. There must also be a shrink after the first three, since those start at seven or more. Thus there are at least five selected sites.

Suppose the seed belongs to the shore. An entering bit must enter one of the first three sites. Follow parents back from the outside endpoint to the seed. Some export leaves from an earlier selected site. That site is among the first two and precedes the latest three selected sites. If the latest three include \(H\), their at least seven outputs and at most two mutual meetings give at least five exports. If they exclude \(H\), they give at least four exports in addition to the two loose bits at \(H\). In either case the earlier export and the entering bit make the boundary exceed six.

If the seed is outside, some bit enters at the earliest selected site. The same latest-three count allows only one entering bit, and \(H\) must be among the latest three. The earliest selected site is therefore unary with three outputs. Forgetting the entering input turns it into a local ternary seed. All other selected sites have their parents inside. Each required pair witness remains inside as well. Otherwise the first common witness of two selected parents would already have been present by the birth of the younger parent and would give another entering bit there. A witness born after both parents could be their first common neighbor only by using both before they had a common neighbor, which is forbidden. The outside sites can thus be forgotten while preserving the formation rule on the selected sites.

The local history needs two unary three-output births after its local seed, so all three global unary three-output births belong to it. The first three selected sites in the global history have boundary at least eight without the global seed. The total of two shrinks is therefore spent inside. The local history has two unary three-output births and the ternary three-output birth at \(H\), with five loose bits left. The exact closure for that family has no surviving output at \(H\) with five loose. This contradicts the two globally loose outputs. The seed must be inside and there are no entering bits. ## Exact closure of these shores

Start from both ways to form the first two unary sites. Retain their possible parent-closed shore selections with the seed inside. A later site can be selected only with all its parents inside. The fourth junction must be selected. The other source conditions are imposed by the arity of the third three-output birth, the single spend at \(H\), and the bound of two shrinking joins. The search keeps the spending arity and the child's side, and requires a legal current triple.

In addition to the usual loose counts, live adjacencies and projected retired witness rows, retain each live holder's shore side and bipartition color. Parent groups share a color, so the newborn has the other color. The canonical permutations respect both tags and the fourth holder. A retired site can never acquire a crossing or participate as a holder, and its projected witness row suffices for future formation.

The boundary count is the sum of loose counts on selected live sites and the number of already spent crossing bits. An outside birth leaves that sum unchanged. It replaces each selected parent's used loose bit by one spent crossing. An inside birth changes the sum by \(h-p\), because it has no outside parents. The already spent count never decreases. A qualifying final shore contains the two loose bits of \(H\) and a distinct loose \(z\), so it can have at most three spent crossings. The search prunes a prefix above size six plus its remaining shrink budget. It also prunes one that cannot reach six even after the only available increase, at most two at the third three-output birth. These bounds hold along each history to a qualifying final state. The frontier has at most nine loose bits, and all tags and counts have finite range.

The four initial records close to **244,740 states** and **1,422,453 transitions**. There are four positive records with a size-six shore and a loose \(z\) of the required color. Every one has zero spent crossings. A parent-closed shore containing the root and with zero spent crossings is the whole ordinary source, since the ordinary incidence is connected. There are no proper qualifying shores. Breadth-first replay certifies the earliest width of **31**, with shortest representatives of the four records at widths 31, 31, 33 and 33. Each literal history also has its ordinary subsets checked directly against the qualifying-cut condition.

## Candidate roles and named retained-pair controls

The normalized records distinguish whether a candidate is already at a true small holder. Two records permit a candidate at a different holder as well. In the other two, comparing the same active incidence with the child-tracking quotient identifies the sole candidate as the child's one remaining loose output. That candidate can replace only the attachment already at the child. The projection tests every holder correspondence between the two quotients.

In the first width-31 representative, \(F=\{27,28\}\), the true attachments are \(\{13,21,24,26\}\), and the complete boundary is \(\{17,25,27,28,29,30\}\). The present legal triples are \(\{17,25,29\}\) and \(\{17,25,30\}\). Distinction 17 is at a holder different from all four true small holders. Its four bond cardinalities to the true attachments are all seven.

The connected-cut and parity methods agree on the four literal tables. In every representative, each proposed \(z\) has zero compatible retained attachments through \(K=6\). Where there is a short reading to a true attachment, that bit and \(z\) belong to the same small holder, which cannot supply both to a new star. This retained-pair calculation is for the four shortest literal histories. The exact formation closure supplies the all-depth restriction on where an exchange can occur.
