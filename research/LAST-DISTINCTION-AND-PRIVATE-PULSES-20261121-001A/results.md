# An outside birth takes the clock's last loose distinction

**Yes.** At birth 10 of a fully paid eight-knot clock, two histories have exactly the same holdings and both can spend loose distinction 27 at original 4. The clock can use it with original 6 and make its marked turn at birth 21. The attached original can use it instead with 4. That permanently exhausts 4 before its required child with 6 exists. The turn is then impossible. This holds under both parity and not-all-equal. **Why a common preparatory expense matters.** Before the first outside birth the attached holder meets only original 3. Among the genuine clock donors only originals 2 and 4 can meet that witness. Each starts with three slots and owes only two clock children. After any prefix consisting entirely of distinct required roles, one pending child at either of those donors guarantees at least two remaining slots there. A first outside birth can take a slot, and cannot exhaust that donor while its clock child remains due. The ninth birth in the witness is the extra pure expenditure that goes beyond this class.
