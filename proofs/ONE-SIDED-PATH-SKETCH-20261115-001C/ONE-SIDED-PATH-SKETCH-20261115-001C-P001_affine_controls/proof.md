# One-sided sketch fibres and the transfer to 31 products

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

For a path of \(m\) products let \(Q(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}x_i x_{i+1}\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). In the one-sided problem the visible coordinates are \(x,s,b\). The left relation is \(s=Q(x)\), with \(b\) free. The right is \(s=1+Q(x)\), \(b=B(x)\). On an affine case repeatedly intersect the affine hulls of its exact separate left and right restrictions until no further change occurs. The empty hull is empty. A nonempty fixed point may be split by any affine question on the visible coordinates. Count terminal cases and worst query depth separately. Number path products from one when calling a sketch **EVEN**: it sums the second, fourth, and subsequent even-numbered products.

On the seven-product path the one-sided EVEN sketch costs **16 leaves, with minimum depth four**. If the first question is the value of the sketch, the two children need **exactly nine leaves for zero and seven for one**. This first question can attain the optimal leaf count but requires depth five. In particular, the seven-leaf conditional tree cannot be doubled to give a 14-leaf refutation. The EVEN costs at one through eight products are \(2,2,4,4,8,8,16,16\). [Witness trees](trees.json) · [Exact checks](checks.json)

On four original bits **every Boolean right sketch** has the full four-leaf cost of a nondegenerate quadratic total. On six original bits **every Boolean quadratic right sketch**, including quadratics outside any selected path-product dictionary, has the full eight-leaf cost. An explicit cubic sketch on these six bits has the same cost while its two values give exact costs seven and one.

The 31-product transfer is a proved implication. Suppose every one-sided problem on eighteen independent inputs with nondegenerate quadratic total of polar rank eighteen and *arbitrary quadratic* right sketch requires at least 512 leaves. Then every pair of path-product masks at 31 products requires at least 512 leaves, and optimizing over pairs gives exactly 512. The seven-edge finite computation supplies neither the rank-nine premise nor an unrestricted-3-SAT lower bound.

## What a closed sketch fibre means

After fixing \(b\), consider the surviving *right* original inputs \(X\) in an affine case. Its right honest points are \((x,1+Q(x))\). If \(X=\varnothing\) the case closes. Otherwise write \(T=\operatorname{aff}X\) and \(V=\operatorname{aff}\{(x,1+Q(x)):x\in X\}\). The latter has one of two forms.

- If the checksum is free over \(T\), the restriction \(Q|_T\) is nonaffine: otherwise the right points could not span the free checksum. The graph of a nonaffine Boolean function spans its domain times the output bit, so the left graph spans \(V\) as well.
- Otherwise \(V\) is a graph \(s=\ell(x)\) over \(T\), for an affine \(\ell\). The left points inside it project to \(H=\{x\in T:Q(x)=\ell(x)\}\); the right inputs all belong to its complement. If \(H\) spans \(T\) both sides span \(V\). If \(H\) fails to span, it is empty or an affine subspace disjoint from \(X\), and the next restricted right hull is empty.

For the last assertion let \(g=Q|_T+\ell\). At polar rank at least four, both levels of \(g\) span \(T\): a proper affine hyperplane containing one level would leave its complementary hyperplane constant, although restriction to a hyperplane loses at most two polar ranks. At rank two, isolate the single nondegenerate product pair. If a linear term on the polar radical survives, both levels are graphs of a nonaffine function and both span. Otherwise translation gives a pair product plus a constant. The rare level is a radical coset and the common level spans. For affine \(g\), its levels are affine or constant. This proves the characterization. Taking the right hull first gives \(V\); alternating the two hull operators has the same greatest stable subcase as simultaneous intersection, by inclusion monotonicity. A live fixed point here retains all of its current right points. The checker compares this criterion with direct iteration for every one of the 65,536 possible restricted right subsets on four inputs. [Point-set control](check.py)

## Exact small sketches

A change \(b\mapsto b+\ell(x)+\epsilon(1+s)\) changes the honest right sketch by \(\ell+\epsilon Q\) and preserves the free left bit. On four inputs, the 65,536 truth tables therefore fall into 1,024 disjoint classes under the 64 shifts generated by all affine functions and \(Q\). In the Boolean algebraic normal form one can set the constant, four linear coefficients and the coefficient of a chosen monomial occurring in \(Q\) to zero. For each representative the full affine-query minimax rejects a three-leaf budget. Fixing two alternating input bits gives four leaves.

With six inputs a Boolean quadratic has \(1+6+15\) independent coefficients. The affine terms and addition of \(Q\) reduce its polar data to 16,384 possibilities. To group them further, let \(J\) be the alternating polar matrix of \(Q\). Each \(x\mapsto x+(v^TJx)v\) is an invertible symplectic transvection. It changes \(Q\) by an affine term, removable from \(s\). It sends a sketch polar matrix \(A\) to
\[
 A+(Jv)(Av)^T+(Av)(Jv)^T.
\]
Any affine remainder in the sketch is removable from \(b\). These changes preserve each side's honest relation and the whole adaptive tree model. A traversal under these actual maps and complementing by \(Q\) covers all 16,384 possibilities, in class sizes \(1,336,315,5040,4032,3780,2880\). An independent truth-table substitution checks the displayed polar update on all seven seeds and all 64 input vectors indexing the displayed transvections.

For explicit seeds take the even and odd vertex lists \(e,o\), let \(u_i=e_i+e_{i+1}\) with final out-of-range \(e\) zero, and write \(Q=u^To\). Use \(u^TMo\) with the three columns of \(M\) packed low to high as three-bit integers; the seven codes are \(0,1,2,10,11,12,94\). The minimax excludes seven leaves on every representative using **all** ambient affine questions, without restricting them to input or sketch questions. The public eight-leaf tree attains the bound. Any nondegenerate quadratic total on six Boolean inputs is a linear recoding and an affine change of this total by ordinary symplectic elimination. The same argument applies to the four-input result. A control outside the quadratic class takes \(B=[u=0]\) on the six-input example. This is the product of three independent affine indicators and has Boolean degree three. A first question on \(b\) closes the \(b=1\) leaf immediately: the right points force \(u=0\) and \(s=1\), while the left total on these inputs is zero. On \(b=0\), split the three \(u\)-bits to distinguish their seven possible nonzero values, stopping once only one remains. The right hull fixes that value and both totals are then affine and opposite. Full minimax excludes seven leaves for this particular cubic, so the two branch costs are exactly one and seven. ## The seven-product EVEN calculation

Here \(B=x_1x_2+x_3x_4+x_5x_6\), with input indices from zero. First fix \(b=d\), leaving nine visible coordinates \(x,s\). In each fibre the full affine space and every affine hyperplane remain unchanged under saturation. There are 173,740 affine subspaces of codimension two. On \(b=0\) they all remain unchanged. On \(b=1\), seven close and 476 shrink to a nonempty subspace. This already forces at least eight leaves on the zero fibre, while the one fibre permits some shallower branches.

The exact minimax uses the following finite recurrence. An empty fixed point fits any budget at least one. A nonempty fixed point fits \(K\) if and only if some nonconstant affine question admits child budgets \(i,K-i\) with both saturated children fitting. A nonempty child needs at least two leaves. The enumeration uses each character of the current direction space once, expanded in the pivot coordinate functions. Constants interchange the answers. When all cells up to a given codimension remain unchanged, every tree before that boundary splits into two live children. The checker uses the shallow census to certify this budget pruning before invoking it. It rejects budget eight for \(b=0\) and six for \(b=1\). Direct repeated hulls on finite point sets validate the two winning trees of sizes nine and seven. [Threshold recurrence](minimax.cpp) · [Budget outcomes and codimension census](checks.json)

To read one difference in the trees, an integer is the query coefficient mask on \(x_0,\ldots,x_7,s\) in that bit order. Within either fibre first ask mask 66 (\(x_1+x_6\)). On answer zero ask mask 88 (\(x_3+x_4+x_6\)). On its answer one, the \(b=1\) case closes. The \(b=0\) case instead has a three-leaf continuation, with masks 116 and 465. Mask 465 includes the public checksum bit \(s\). The other continuations in the two trees agree. The nine-leaf tree has depth four and the seven-leaf tree depth three, each depth forced by its leaf count. [Readable binary trees](trees.json)

It remains to exclude cheaper *other first questions*. Use the affine restriction principle: a map that sends each smaller honest relation into the corresponding larger one pulls an arbitrary larger tree back to a smaller tree, pruning constant queries. The inclusions persist through hulls, intersections and the query answers.

1. A nonzero input-only question \(\ell(x)\) restricts each answer to a hyperplane of an eight-dimensional nondegenerate quadratic space. The restricted polar half-rank is three. Fix its remaining radical coordinate and remove the affine part of its total from \(s\). Each child contains a six-input rank-three problem with a quadratic sketch and thus costs at least eight.
2. Write a mixed form as \(\ell(x)+\alpha s+\beta b\). Assume \(\alpha=1\), or \(\alpha=0,\beta=1\) and \(\ell\) sees an endpoint \(x_0\) or \(x_7\). Reverse the path if required, so that an available endpoint is \(x_0\). Start with smaller \(y_0,\ldots,y_5,s',b'\) and set
   \[
    x=(y_0+z,\lambda,y_0,y_1,y_2,y_3,y_4,y_5),\qquad
    s=s'+\lambda z,\qquad b=b'+\lambda y_0 .
   \]
   The large total on this input is \(Q_5(y)+\lambda z\). Its EVEN sketch is \(y_1y_2+y_3y_4+\lambda y_0\). The coefficient of \(z\) in the pulled-back question is \(\ell_0+\alpha\lambda\). Choose \(\lambda\) to make this one; with \(\alpha=0\) the selected endpoint already has coefficient one. Then set \(z\) affinely from the remaining smaller coordinates to produce either requested answer. The same substitution works for both honest relations, including the free left sketch. Both children therefore contain an eight-leaf smaller instance.
3. The remaining forms are \(b+\ell(x)\) where \(\ell\) vanishes on the two endpoints. The polar matrix of EVEN interchanges the two coordinates of each of its three independent inner products; its image is precisely these \(\ell\). Translating \(x\), with the corresponding affine corrections to \(s,b\), therefore carries every such question to \(b\) up to its answer convention. The fibre result applies.

These classes have 255, 704 and 64 nonconstant forms, respectively. The checker verifies the ranks or affine transformations for all 1,023, including all 256 visible smaller points in each of the two absorption maps where applicable. This proves the global lower bound sixteen; the public four-input alternating split attains it at depth four. An even-length path restricts to the preceding odd length by setting its last vertex to zero, preserving the EVEN sketch, and has the same public polar half-rank. This yields the stated small-path sequence. [Checked embeddings](solver.py) · [Tree and rank controls](checks.json)

## What a local saving would lift

For \(2r-1\) path products let \(U_i=x_{2i}\), \(O_i=x_{2i+1}\), \(V_i=O_i+O_{i-1}\), with \(O_{-1}=0\). This linear change is invertible. The total and one-sided EVEN sketch are
\[
 Q_r=\sum_i U_iV_i,\qquad B_r=\sum_{j<i}U_i V_j .
\]
For \(r=9\), fixing the first five \(V_i\)'s leaves \(Q_4,B_4\) on the remaining four pairs plus affine functions of the original visible inputs. The five remaining earlier \(U_i\)'s contribute only affine terms and can also be fixed when embedding the smaller problem for a lower bound. Shift \(s,b\) by their corresponding affine corrections. Hence, after this particular sequence of five input questions, *each* of the 32 cases has exactly 16 leaves left to pay in its optimal continuation. This is a 512-leaf strategy for that 17-product problem. A genuine \(L_4<16\) tree would have given \(32L_4<512\) via this construction; the cheaper conditional \(b=1\) tree does not yield that improvement. Establishing an unconditional 17-product minimum requires a lower bound covering other initial queries. [Symbolic expansion for all 32 prefixes](solver.py)

On the specific 31-product pair, expose the first 14 products on the left and products 16,18,\(\ldots\),30 on the right, with one-based numbering. Set \(x_0,\ldots,x_{13}=0\) and retain \(x_{14},\ldots,x_{31}\). The total is the full 17-product path, the left sketch vanishes, and the right sketch is exactly its EVEN sketch. Set the large left sketch bit to zero on both sides of the embedding. The original half-ranks of \(Q,A,Q+A,B,Q+B\) are \(16,7,9,8,16\). An independently established 512 lower bound for this particular 17-product problem transfers to the named 31-product pair. A smaller upper bound proved only on a restriction would require a separate lift. Likewise the common midpoint upper bound for an optimized choice does not upper-bound this particular asymmetric pair. [Raw-variable restriction and polar ranks](checks.json)

## Why a general rank-nine theorem suffices at 31

Write \(\rho\) for half the polar rank. If all four of \(\rho(A),\rho(Q+A),\rho(B),\rho(Q+B)\) are at least eight, the predecessor's large-rank theorem already gives 512 leaves. Otherwise choose a form \(P\) among them with \(\rho(P)=p\le7\). Exchanging the sides sends \((s,a,b)\) to \((1+s,b,a)\). Complementing a sketch on the left is the visible change \(a\mapsto a+s\), and on the right \(b\mapsto b+1+s\). Use these bijections so that the small form is the left sketch.

In a polar basis for \(P\), keep its radical and one member of each of its \(p\) nondegenerate pairs. On this codimension-\(p\) linear subspace \(T\), the Boolean function \(P|_T\) is affine, possibly with a nonzero radical-linear part. Set the observed left sketch equal to that affine function. The original total has \(\rho(Q)=16\), so \(\rho(Q|_T)\ge16-p\ge9\): restriction by one equation deletes only one row and column of the polar matrix in adapted coordinates. Choose nine complete polar pairs of \(Q|_T\) and fix every other pair and radical direction. The resulting injection \(x=F(y)\) on eighteen independent bits satisfies
\[
 Q(F(y))=q(y)+e(y),\qquad q(y)=\sum_{j=1}^9 y_{2j-1}y_{2j},
\]
for an affine \(e\). Fixing the radical is legitimate even when the original quadratic varies linearly along it. Use the actual checksum correction \(s=s'+e(y)\) on both sides. On the right retain the genuine other sketch \(B(F(y))\), which is an arbitrary Boolean quadratic after substitution. It can contain linear terms, constants or products between variables of the same original parity class. On the left its visible bit remains free; on the right the installed left sketch is allowed. This is an affine embedding of the required one-sided problem, so its rank-nine lower bound transfers.

The checker constructs such embeddings using polar elimination on actual 32-position inputs with small half-ranks \(0,3,6,7\), trims residual half-ranks \(9,10,13,16\) to exactly nine, and verifies the low sketch, affine checksum correction and a remaining quadratic containing extra cross terms. A separate nonzero radical-linear example checks the pin-and-shift step. No general projection is inferred from identifying the specific EVEN pencil at 17.
