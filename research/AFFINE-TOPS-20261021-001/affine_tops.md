# Mal'tsev structure on the tops

Epic `AFFINE-TOPS-20261021-001`, closed 2026-10-21.

## The question

`THOMSEN-KNOTS-20261021-001` proved that, in a law that composes like cycles,
no circuit overlaps. So composing like cycles is exactly having Latin circuits
modulo the tops.

Conjecture 7 of the tops proof was still open. It asks for one ternary
polymorphism that is Mal'tsev modulo the tops of the knot ranges.

The owner approved a fresh epic on Mal'tsev structure on the tops, with this
plan:
- three-variable circuits as abelian p-groups;
- gluing across a range;
- larger circuits;
- whether every top image is closed under the operation.

  - Rado's theorem on representations over finite fields: [Bell, Funk, Kim
    and Mayhew](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10262).

## What was found

- **A shorter route than planned.**
  - Latin circuits make every variable outside a basis a function of the
    basis, modulo the tops. So all nonempty fibres of a top image over the
    same set of variables have the same size.
  - Uniform fibres make every top image rectangular for every split.
  - The classical rectangularity argument, run on the system of ternary
    polymorphisms, then gives a polymorphism F that is Mal'tsev modulo the top
    of the range generated by its two arguments.
- **Conjecture 7 is true.**
  - On a knot range, a subrange that meets two top classes meets them all and
    inherits the top.
  - So F is Mal'tsev modulo the top of every knot range.
  - The generated form is the right one: parity beside J has no polymorphism
    Mal'tsev modulo every range containing its arguments, but it has one modulo
    the generated ranges.
- **The characterization.** A law composes like cycles exactly when:
  - every range has a top;
  - every tie is a bijection modulo the tops; and
  - some ternary polymorphism is Mal'tsev modulo the top of every knot range.

  This answers the conditions epic's question: composing like cycles is a
  Mal'tsev condition, modulo the tops.
- **The tops are affine.**
  - Every knot's two-by-two parallelogram relation is the graph of F's
    operation on the top. So the operation is unique, and it commutes with
    itself and with every polymorphism.
  - It is x − y + z for an elementary abelian p-group.
  - Every top image on knot-range variables is a coset of a subgroup.
  - Three-variable circuits are addition tables of such groups after
    relabelling, the first step of the approved plan.
  - Gluing across a range turned out to be automatic.
- **Matroids.**
  - Every system's matroid is a direct sum of parallel classes and matroids
    representable over finite fields.
  - A component's field is the endomorphism field of its tops, as modules over
    the polymorphisms' linear parts.
  - This proves the weak form of the matroid proof's Conjecture 5. Every
    direct sum of matroids representable over finite fields occurs, which by
    Rado's theorem is all of 𝓡.
  - The field is not the size of the top. The 3-net of Z₂×Z₂ has tops of four
    elements and binary matroids. The GF(4) law also has four-element tops, and
    it carries U(2,4).
- **The checks agree everywhere.** They cover fourteen census laws and a GF(4)
  law, with 1,680 top images in A and 1,200 systems in B, and every control is
  rejected.

**Did the perspective do real work?** It did.
- **The program's own notions.** Range-relative circuits, the tops and the
  no-overlap theorem make fibres uniform. That is the step the classical theory
  does not supply.
- **Classical tools did the rest.** These were rectangularity, self-commuting
  Mal'tsev operations, and the density theorem.
- **Folklore risk: moderate.** The overall statement resembles the
  model-theoretic classification of locally modular geometries, which are
  affine or projective over finite fields in the ω-categorical case. It also
  resembles the counting-CSP theory of strong rectangularity. A literature
  check should precede any write-up.
