# Two shared junctions and three shared junctions

Use the classical wired graph of the complete cheapest-count parity law. A bond has two connected shores. Write K(X) for the least size of a bond through a nonempty set of names X, with infinity allowed. The static and changing parts have disjoint distinction sets. Their common vertices are the supplied attachment junctions. The permutation acts on names of the static part, fixes the changing part pointwise and extends the prescribed old naming. All failures below are on old names.

| Common junctions | Fixed future reading of order k≥1 |
| --- | --- |
| Two | Present readings through k+1 carry every future reading through k. At every k there is a reached counterexample to using only k. |
| Any prescribed b≥3 | For every fixed present order r, a reached example passes the entire present table through r and fails at the next round, even on a singleton. A pair of nonparallel old names also fails. |

A reading of the pair distance K−1 preserves exactly the same pair information. A missing current singleton entry can be obtained by minimizing over pairs at these admissible stages, since the graphs have no bridges. The three-junction result permits many distinctions at each attachment. Under the narrower hypothesis of exactly three named unit links to the changing part, present readings through k already preserve all later readings through k. The final section proves this separately.

## Two junctions give a sharp one-order margin

If H and Q meet exactly at A,B, a bond using names from both restricts in each part to a connected cut separating A and B. In the other direction these two cuts glue to a bond. Let L_H(X) denote the least cost of such a cut in H through X, B_H(X) the minimum bond through X after identifying A and B, and λ the minimum A–B cut in Q. Then an internal query in H has cost min(B_H(X),L_H(X)+λ). In a lawful continuation confined to Q, its terminal cut λ′ cannot exceed λ. Put new sites on the old outside shore to extend any old separating cut at unchanged cost. Together these facts give the later value min(K(X),L_H(X)+λ′). A mark on a minimum separating cut in Q reads L_H(X)+λ using just one more name. Mixed future readings add the two marked separating costs and pure Q readings are fixed. These arguments also cover infinity. [Two-junction proof and relative presentations](../LATER-BOND-READING-20261119-001/boundary-proof.md)

For sharpness the result below uses one finite weighted host and many independent links from its shield sites to a common vertex g. Their current weight is M and their next weight is one. The host permutation fixes every such link. At the high weights it preserves all readings through the chosen k. At the low weights a singleton fails. These two endpoint facts are proved in the next section for every k. Give each weighted link one queryable name, and count its weight when it crosses. Marking two parallel copies imposes the same condition as marking one.

Lower the link weights one after another and take the first step that ceases to preserve all readings through k. Let e be its single changing link, dropping by d>0. For every nonempty X avoiding e,

$$
K_{\rm after}(X)=\min\bigl(K_{\rm before}(X),\ K_{\rm before}(X\cup\{e\})-d\bigr).
$$

This follows by separating the actual bonds into those crossing e and the rest. Marked sets containing e lose d whenever finite and stay infinite otherwise. At this first step all sets of size below k therefore stay matched, and all matched sets containing e also stay matched. A discrepancy occurs on a set of exactly k names excluding e. This argument involves all the queried links, including those repriced earlier in this comparison.

For a block C of c independently repriced links on a fixed host let dₑ≥0 be the decrease on e. The exact expression is

$$K_{\rm after}(X)=\min_{S\subseteq C}\left(K_{\rm before}(X\cup S)-\sum_{e\in S}d_e\right).$$

The cost of every bond through X falls by the sum of the decreases at its crossed links. In the displayed minimum that bond reaches its new cost by choosing exactly those links for S, and is never charged less than its new cost. Thus k+c current marks suffice for k-mark queries when this independent-link representation is supplied. For disjoint two-terminal branches with the self-map fixing their names, a mark in a minimum terminal cut represents each required virtual link in a current mixed query. At the new age, marks actually lying in each branch add their known separating costs in excess of its new link price. Any query with a moved static name has a mark outside every such branch. This gives the same upper bound on literal transport. Counting independently repriced links and counting the common vertices of two general connected pieces give different bounds.

To realize the single step, make g an ordinary exhausted junction. Keep the other links permanently at their selected weights as ordinary parallel distinctions. Delete e=Pg. Between P and the actual outside install a lawful M-to-one branch. Between g and the actual outside install M independent unit branches. This changing part meets the ordinary host exactly at P,g. At age one its minimum separating cost is M and at age two it is one. A unit branch has its own closed neighbor of g met by both initial live parents. Different branches have no common parent. The M-to-one group at P has an independent supply of old distinctions and gives one fresh as a proper join. The host with the temporary Pg link survives single vertex deletions. The branch plus its auxiliary terminal link does likewise. Deleting the actual outside leaves the two flanks attached to the connected ordinary host. Deleting P or g leaves that flank attached to the actual outside and the other flank still connected to the host. Deleting any other site in a branch leaves every surviving branch component joined to a surviving endpoint. Thus all initial ordinary supports have full parity projection. The two flanks and their descendants make the only live junctions. After the first round a new site meets only its own parents, so no descendant can acquire a common parent across flanks. Each round has more than one independent branch, every join is proper, and lookahead supplies each group's next demand. All subsequent growth stays inside the same supplied part. [Lawful M-to-one and unit branches](../LATER-BOND-READING-20261119-001/boundary-proof.md)

For a current physical query that meets both parts, write X for its static names and Y for its changing names. The marked separating cost of X in the static part equals K_host(X∪{e})−M, so matches up to k−1 static marks. Add the same cost for Y on both sides. Pure static queries are the host minima at the measured price, and pure changing queries are fixed. This proves the complete literal current premise, including queries with no bond. At the next round the failed host k-set avoids e and has the same value on choosing one old direct distinction for each name. Fixing the fresh names throughout the two-junction upper bound is admissible. Every possible fresh naming fails at this lower witness.

All 60 singleton, 1,770 pair and 34,220 triple queries match at observation. After the one repricing, 130 triple queries and zero pair queries differ under the permutation. The two physical rounds agree with an independent parent and parity replay, and the growing end and pair-flip classes are fixed at the observed stage. ## Three ordinary attachments suffice for a future singleton and a nonparallel pair to split

Write h₀,h₁,t for the three shared ordinary junctions, and O for the actual outside. Use two private sets of middle sites and two private sets of anchors. Inside each part every middle meets every anchor, and every anchor meets t by one distinction. The two parts have no private site in common.

In the static part use n+5 middles (the special a,b,c,d, an extra u, and n ordinary dummies) and n+2 anchors (v,w and n ordinary weak anchors). Connect a,b to h₀ and c,d to h₁ at weight L, and connect each of these four sites to v at weight L. Put the four links h₁u, uw, wa and wc at weight D. Connect each dummy to h₁ at unit weight. All remaining middle–anchor weights are one. The candidate permutation interchanges a with c in every named incidence by type and copy, including their hub incidences. It fixes all other names. At the changing side let 2s private middles, the shields, meet n private anchors. Attach s shields to h₀ at weight M each and the other s to h₁ at weight M each. Install at each shield P a separate, initially simple M-to-one branch to O. The original ordinary neighbors of every shield outside its branch are exhausted, so the simultaneous rounds of the separate branches have exactly their independent parent sets. Each has terminal cost M after the first complete round, and one after the second.

Both ordinary private blocks stay connected on deleting any one vertex. A surviving hub has at least two available middle neighbors beforehand. Each surviving private anchor reaches surviving middles and t when present, and each surviving middle has several private anchors. The full ordinary core consequently survives vertex deletion. At the start the branch sites remain connected to their attachment or to O after one deletion, and other branches still join the core to O. Deleting O leaves all branches attached to their own ordinary pole. Every junction has at least three distinctions. Thus every starting support is a bond and has full parity projection. At both rounds each new junction takes a proper subset of the old loose distinctions, whose proper subsets have full projection in the connected ordinary graph. Its fresh distinctions achieve its complete parity projection and allow every old state to extend. All moved names belong to the exhausted static core. Their parallel classes map onto parallel classes and every live support is fixed pointwise.

### Literal marks before the cut comparison

At a given age replace each branch by a weighted virtual link PO of its terminal cut size λ. If the marked set contains a moved name, every branch with marks has another mark outside it. A bond through such a branch must separate P and O, and both restricted shores must connect to the respective endpoint. Write Y for its local marks and L(Y) for its least connected separating cost through Y. Mark the corresponding virtual link in the host, then add L(Y)−λ to the host reading. Impose this for every marked branch. Unmarked branches integrate at their minimum separating cost when their endpoints differ and can stay entirely on one side when they agree. This reduction follows in both directions by gluing connected shores. If any L(Y) is infinite, the original reading is infinite on both sides. A query containing only fixed names maps to itself. This accounts for all positive mark sets, including absences, and isolates every possibly large marked-branch surcharge before applying a bound to the host.

At age one identify h₀,h₁,O in the virtual host to a single site z. The proposed permutation is then an exact incidence symmetry, fixing t. Every shield has two named z-links of weight M, one ordinary and one virtual. For any at most r marked links choose an untouched weak anchor from each private block. This is possible for n>r. Put t, these two anchors and every middle incident to a marked link on the positive shore. Everything else is on the negative shore. A marked t–anchor link crosses because its anchor was touched and could not be selected. The other marks cross from a selected middle to z or a touched anchor. All positive middles reach the chosen anchor in their block and hence t. Each block has more than r middles, so an untouched middle in each block joins every negative anchor there to z. Both shores are connected, including when a block contains no marked middle.

At most r shield middles on that shore cross their two links to z. Write B for the total weight in the folded host except those two M-links at every shield. The constructed bond costs at most 2rM+B. Choose s>2r and M large enough that this is strictly less than sM. Before identification there are sM mutually distinction-disjoint routes from each true hub to O through its shields. A cut cheaper than that keeps all three sites together and an intact route from each hub to O. Thus contracting the three sites preserves a cheap bond, and lifting any cheap folded bond keeps the shore containing the three sites connected. Every reading through r in the actual virtual host consequently equals its folded reading. The previous surcharge formula proves equality on all physical marks through r.

### What changes in the next round

At price one put h₀ and all its s shields on one shore. It is connected through h₀ and the complement is connected through h₁, its shields, O and both blocks' anchor incidences. The corresponding local branch cuts preserve both connections. The resulting bond has size

$$2L+s(n+1).$$

It contains both h₀a and h₀b. Choose D=s(n+1)+1. The single image h₁c requires separating h₁ from c. The L direct paths, L paths through d,v and D paths through u,w are mutually distinction-disjoint. The image of the original singleton therefore costs strictly more. Any prescribed k≥1 different distinctions in the h₀a bundle, with L≥k, give the same failure at exact future order k. For a pair on two different ordinary meetings choose h₀a and h₀b and take 2L>s(n+1). Their image includes one crossing of each of h₀–a–v–b–h₀ and h₁–c–v–d–h₁. Each cycle requires at least two L-bundles to cross. Its reading is at least 4L, strictly greater than the exhibited cost. The failure in each case concerns only old distinctions.

More explicitly, let n=max(r,k,3)+2, s=4n and L=D=4n(n+1)+1. Counting the listed links gives B≤9n²+10n+12+8L+4D. The static middle–anchor block contributes its ordinary count and the extra weights on four v-links and three w-links. The hub–middle links on that side add the other four L-links and the fourth D-link. The changing middle–anchor block has 8n² unit links. With M=64n we have (s−2r)M>B. For fixed k this gives O(r²) ordinary junctions and distinctions at observation, including the literal branches. No fixed degree assumption is used.

### The minimum number of shared junctions

The static and changing parts share precisely h₀,h₁,t. Choose three distinct shields and paths in their private branches from the shields to three distinct live first-round junctions. To reach the first shield start from a through h₀. To reach the second start from c through h₁. To reach the third start from a static weak anchor (it holds a moved a-incidence), go through t and a private changing anchor, then that shield. These full paths are vertex-disjoint. Any other partition that puts all moved names in its static part and all live supports in its changing part must meet each path in at least one shared vertex. The attachment count three is therefore minimal for this comparison.

For exactly b≥3 replace t by b−2 distinct shared sites, each joined to every anchor in both blocks. Put all of them on the positive shore in the same argument. The extra ordinary cost is independent of M, and the later h₀ bond has its original cost. Choose n and the shield pools at least b. Distinct static weak anchors, shared sites, changing anchors and shields give b−2 disjoint paths in addition to those through h₀,h₁. Exactly b shared junctions are necessary for any admissible partition of this marked source. Taking n=max(r,k,b,3)+2 and M=128n suffices in the bound above, since the new links contribute at most (b−3)(2n+2).

One can also require all ordinary meetings to be single. Keep the first direct distinction of each parallel bundle. Replace each other unit by a private exhausted four-site piece, internally complete apart from PR and attached at P and R by one link each. Its minimum terminal cut is one. Carry the moved pieces by their type and copy. Marks in multiple pieces reduce to the same marked virtual crossings with equal separating surcharges. For marks confined to one moved piece, the local contracted-terminal bond cost is the same and the other alternative uses the complementary separating cost, which is the matched virtual singleton cost minus one. The controls h₀a,h₀b use retained direct copies. For an exact k-set using multiple copies, choose one first attachment link of each unit piece as its price-one mark. This link alone supplies a connected separating cut through the mark. The same k-set values follow. Each piece stays in the same private block as the distinction replaced, and all paths in the preceding separator proof can use retained direct copies. This adds only a constant number of sites and distinctions per replaced unit. ## Exactly three unit links give same-order transfer

Assume that the changing part Q is connected and that its three distinct shared vertices 1,2,3 are leaves *in Q*. Write qᵢ for the named unit distinction at its ith leaf. The interior H carries the permutation, which fixes Q and these three links. A connected future replacement Q′ keeps the same named links at the same three terminals. Both presentations use this replacement with the same naming. This includes the replacement of a single trivalent junction. The starting Q can be any connected piece having the three boundary leaves.

For a nonempty H-marking X choose distinct i,j,k from 1,2,3. Write aᵢ for the least cost in H of a cut through X having i alone among terminals of its shore. Every component of either shore must meet a terminal. Thus the i shore is connected and the j,k shore may split into two components. Write bᵢ for the cost when that latter shore must also be connected. Use infinity when the indicated cut is impossible. Denote the corresponding relaxed and strict costs for a nonempty marking Y of Q′ by vᵢ(Y) and uᵢ(Y). As in the ordinary terminal-partition formula, a mixed global bond exists precisely when it separates the terminals and the j,k shore connects in at least one part. Its minimum cost is

$$F=\min_i\bigl(a_i+u_i(Y),\ b_i+v_i(Y)\bigr).$$

For a pure X, the constant-terminal possibility contracts the terminals in H. In every other case Q must pay at least one. It can pay exactly one and connect j,k by putting every internal Q vertex on their shore and cutting only qᵢ. Thus the minimum on any purely interior X is unchanged by *every* such connected replacement, whatever the size of X.

The current table supplies two exact probes. If both qⱼ and qₖ are required to cross, j and k cannot lie on opposite shores. The lone terminal among them would be isolated in Q on a shore also containing the internal endpoint of the other crossed link. With j,k on the same shore they are both isolated there in Q, so that shore contains no internal Q vertex and H has to connect j to k. Placing the entire interior of Q with i attains cost two. If only qᵢ is required and i is alone, Q can connect j,k at cost one. If instead j is alone, H must connect i,k, because i is isolated in Q. A separate j–k path in Q avoids the leaf link qᵢ, forcing a second crossing. Putting all internal Q vertices with j attains two. Consequently the present readings give

$$b_i=K(X\cup\{q_j,q_k\})-2,\qquad
a_i^*=K(X\cup\{q_i\})-1=\min(a_i,b_j+1,b_k+1).$$

When minimizing F one may substitute the observed aᵢ* for aᵢ. To see why, take a strict cut for uᵢ(Y). A path between j and k on their shore keeps both leaf links qⱼ and qₖ uncut. Move the leaf k alone to i's side. The marking is preserved, the cost increases by one, and the cut is a feasible relaxed cut with j alone. Hence vⱼ(Y)≤uᵢ(Y)+1, and likewise vₖ(Y)≤uᵢ(Y)+1. Either newly introduced term bⱼ+1+uᵢ(Y) or bₖ+1+uᵢ(Y) is already bounded below by an available global candidate. This also covers an infinite b or u.

There is a further saving when Y consists of one distinction. Take a minimizing relaxed exterior cut for vᵢ(Y). If it is strict then aᵢ*+uᵢ(Y)≤bᵢ+vᵢ(Y). Otherwise the j,k shore has exactly two components. The single crossing mark touches only one of them. Move the entire other component to the connected i shore. At least one crossing disappears since Q′ is connected, and none is added since the two original components had no link between them. Both new shores are connected, the mark remains crossed, and one of j,k is now alone. For that index j (up to renaming), uⱼ(Y)≤vᵢ(Y)−1. Since aⱼ*≤bᵢ+1, the term bᵢ+vᵢ(Y) is again bounded below by an observable clipped term. When Y is a singleton it follows that

$$F=\min_i\bigl(a_i^*+u_i(Y)\bigr).$$

Now take a target order p. If |Y|=1 then |X|≤p−1 and a single current boundary probe suffices. If |Y|≥2 then |X|≤p−2 and both probes are available through total order p. Pure interior readings retain their prior values and pure exterior queries match identically. Thus current readings through p carry every future reading through p under this three-unit hypothesis, including absent bonds and marks on the boundary links themselves. A uniformly supplied set of only three *junctions* has the different result above because those junctions can each have many incidences on the changing side.

### Four fixed unit links can lose the current order

In the two-junction argument above lower each weight one integer at a time. At the first loss through p, the changing link falls from an integer ℓ≥2 to ℓ−1. A set of exactly p old names missing that link fails. Keep ℓ−2 fixed ordinary parallel distinctions at its endpoints P,g. Realize the remaining price as two changing to one. At P use the lawful two-parent branch with closed first neighbors U,V, which together have two unit incidences at P. At g place two unit branches behind two further unit incidences, also with closed first neighbors. The true outside connects both flanks. Together with the fixed copies the two effective prices are ℓ and ℓ−1. The earlier single-link reduction still gives the whole literal present premise, including queries mixing the fixed parallel copies with the changing names, since the copies and the variable branch together form a pointwise fixed two-terminal piece.

To give the four links distinct static terminals, replace each of these four connector distinctions by a separate unit piece of the kind used above. Assign the exhausted internal sites and their connections on the P or g side to the static piece, leaving one unit link from its last site R to the original closed first neighbor on the changing side. There are now four distinct terminal leaves in the connected changing piece, one per connector. All other junctions there connect after removing these four leaves. These replacements preserve the two-flank minimum and preserve the ordinary witnesses for all live parents, since the original endpoints were closed and the inserted sites are exhausted. The proper fresh counts and complete rounds remain as before. Although some fixed auxiliary names now belong to the supplied static part, their readings are included in the preceding pointwise-fixed two-terminal-piece comparison.

The reduction is sharp for the count of distinct unit terminal leaves. With two leaves the changing part always has terminal cut one. One marked boundary link measures a mixed query's single required separating price, within the original order. With one separating link the parts have independent bonds apart from that link itself. Three leaves satisfy the theorem above, and four permit loss at every prescribed order p. This four-link conclusion concerns a supplied unit interface. The physical examples of this subsection also have a separate decomposition at the two ordinary junctions P,g.
