# The common low-site equations and the one remaining coset

Write \(S\) for the three-parent, three-output site, \(n\) for the number of ordinary sites, and \(L\) for the span of the source stars other than \(S\). Put \(K=D+1\), the minimum cardinality of a bond containing the two named bits. An equation is a cut in the classical graph obtained by wiring loose bits to the exterior. Each star is a bond, and the ordinary stars are an independent basis. A shrinking site has three parents and two outputs. The finite frontier closure proves that at most two shrinking sites occur, and at most one precedes \(S\). ## Every source star of size at most five is forced

This statement uses only \(K\le5\) and permits every reachable rival. Any ordinary shore containing at least three sites has boundary at least five. The first three selected sites have total arity at least eleven and at most two internal meetings. Each subsequent selected site adds \(p+h-2j\), where \(j\) is its number of earlier selected neighbors. Only a shrinking site can reduce the boundary, and by only one. A five-boundary on such a shore therefore uses both shrinking sites after the initial three, all three input parents of each being inside. No input bit of either shrinking site belongs to any such boundary. All source equations through size four transfer by the short-equation theorem. Every rival four-place site is physically one of the source four-place sites, since the unique ternary site rules out a four-bit sum of adjacent ternary stars. [Short-equation theorem](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F-P001_finite_interval/structural.md#recovering-every-equation-through-size-four)

Fix a shrinking site \(v\). All bonds through size five on any of its three inputs are single stars or adjacent-star sums. The only possible adjacent-star sum through five uses the seed and a four-place neighbor. For each input whose old parent is not the seed, any bit outside the star of \(v\) within this cutoff must consequently meet the corresponding old parent, or the seed if that parent is four-place and meets it. Two inputs at \(v\) have common minimum exactly five, since their old parents are distinct, of the same color, and cannot supply a smaller bond through both. If the seed is one of the parents, choose the other two. They have the seed's color and avoid it. Otherwise the three parents cannot all meet the seed, as such a common witness would forbid the join at \(v\). Choose one avoiding the seed and any other. No outside bit fits both closed five-neighborhoods, since the two old parents cannot meet, and the first also avoids the seed. The rival bond realizing the pair minimum is exactly the five-place star. This proves \(L\subseteq C_R\), where \(C_R\) denotes the rival equation space on the matched names.

## All nine high input-output pairs require the extra rank

Take any input \(e=aS\) and any output \(f\) of \(S\). There is no word of \(L\) of size at most six containing both. A word of \(L\) is the cut of an ordinary shore omitting \(S\), by independence of the ordinary stars. If \(f\) is loose, that cut cannot contain it. Otherwise let \(b\) be the child on \(f\). The supposed shore includes \(a,b\).

At the birth of \(S\) there are three edge-disjoint ordinary paths from \(a\) to \(S\). Use \(e\) and, for each of the other two parents, the path through its earlier pair witness with \(a\) followed by its input to \(S\). The two witnesses are different, as a shared witness would have forbidden the join. Each path contributes a crossing. The bit \(f\) contributes a fourth. The latest selected site after \(S\) contributes two further crossings. Two selected sites after \(S\) would export at least three outputs between them, giving at least seven crossings in all. Thus \(b\) is the only selected site after \(S\), and its two outputs exhaust the available boundary.

Every other input to \(b\) therefore comes from a selected site before \(S\). Rewind to the prefix immediately before \(S\). The three old path crossings remain, with any input to \(S\) now loose. The other \(p_b-1\) inputs to \(b\) are reopened loose bits at their pre-\(S\) holders. These are all the crossings, because every previously loose bit leading from a selected site to a later unselected site had already been counted. The resulting old cut has exactly \(3+(p_b-1)\) bits and contains \(p_b\) loose bits at different holders, including \(e\) at \(a\). The other holders have the color of \(S\), opposite to \(a\).

Before \(S\), at most one shrinking site existed. Hence any shore on three or more sites cost at least six. If \(p_b=2\), the alleged four-bit cut would have to be one four-place star. Two nonadjacent sites give at least six bits, and an adjacent pair gives four only if both are ternary, whereas the seed is unique. A single star cannot contain loose bits at two distinct holders. If \(p_b=3\), the five-cut would have to contain three different holders although it has at most two sites. Both cases contradict the claimed word of \(L\).

The star of \(S\) gives \(K(e,f)=6\) for each such pair by the input-output theorem. Thus any matching rival through six strictly contains \(L\). Its rank is at least \(n\), since \(L\) has rank \(n-1\). The width is \(2n+4\). In any rival with \(r\) sites it equals \(3+2(r-1)+m\), where \(m\) is the number of three-output additions beyond the seed. The same single ternary star is forced, and the first two additions must be unary with three outputs. The width parity makes \(m\) odd, hence at least three and \(r\le n\). Therefore \(r=n\) and \(m=3\). There is exactly one coset of \(L\) outside \(L\) in the rival space, and that coset must supply a six-bit bond for each of the nine high input-output pairs. [Input-output readings](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J-P001_seven_to_six/structural.md#an-input-and-an-output-read-the-whole-arity)

The source high star is in fact the **only** source word of size at most six containing any fixed input-output pair \(e,f\). It remains to handle a shore that includes \(S\). Then \(e\) enters it and \(f\) leaves it. If exactly two selected sites follow \(S\), those two and \(S\) make seven outputs in all, of which at most two can be used internally among these three latest selected sites. At least five outputs leave, in addition to \(e\). If at least three selected sites follow \(S\), the latest three make at least four outgoing crossings, in addition to the earlier crossings \(e,f\). In both cases six is possible only if \(e\) is the sole entering bit and no crossing is strictly older than \(S\). The other two parents of \(S\) then belong to the shore, whereas \(a\) does not. An old two-step path from \(a\) to either of these parents crosses on a bit strictly older than \(S\). This contradiction leaves at most one selected site after \(S\).

Let \(A\) be the selected shore before \(S\) and \(j\) the number of its selected parents. We have \(j\le2\) because \(e\) enters. Immediately after \(S\) its boundary has size \(|\partial A|+6-2j\), and one subsequent selected site can save at most one. If \(j=2\), the old shore contains two distinct same-color holders. Its cut costs at least six, since a smaller shore would have at most two sites and two nonadjacent sites already cost six. If \(j=1\), a final boundary through six requires \(|\partial A|=3\) and a saving of one. Thus \(A\) consists only of the seed, and the possible next site has just two earlier selected sites available for its three required internal parents. If \(j=0\) and \(A\) is nonempty, the final boundary has size at least eight. Finally, if \(A\) is empty, a later selected site with at most one earlier selected parent strictly raises the boundary. The only qualifying shore is the singleton \(\{S\}\). Different reachable spaces can nevertheless supply different six-bit witnesses under the very same full table. ## Which competitors remain

If the source has at most one shrinking site, there is no five-bit boundary on three or more ordinary sites. The five-bit theorem therefore gives equality of all equations through five and makes every rival site of size at most five a physical source site. They can supply only the \(n-1\) generators of \(L\). A rival of rank \(n\) must contain a six-place site. It is the sole such site, since it uses the only three-output allocation beyond the two unary additions. Counting the other \(n-1\) sites gives physical equality of **all** the low stars under the given bijection. Thus the rival belongs to the same growth branch with the same number of shrinking sites. Deleting the one six-place site on each side leaves exactly the same named ordinary low sites and the same incidences between them. [Five-bit transfer](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J-P001_seven_to_six/inverse.md#five-bit-equations-at-the-first-shrink)

With two shrinking sites the source has \(n-4\) four-place sites, two five-place sites, the six-place site and the seed. Every rival four-place site is again a source four-place site. If the rival has a six-place site, it is in the stated branch, has at least two shrinking sites by the four-place count, and therefore has exactly two. The frontier closure gives empty triple menus in both presentations. If the rival has no six-place site, it must have at least three five-place sites. Its third three-output addition was then unary or binary. In the unary case at least three other sites shrink, and in the binary case at least two do. Both such rival branches can have live triples at these counts. The following proof uses the common four-bit equations and the structure of every large five-bit boundary to settle their marked verdicts. [Alternate branches](alternate.py)

## Every two-shrink source has its empty menu forced

Assume the source has two shrinking sites, denoted by \(u,v\), and suppose the rival has no six-place site. A rival with at most four loose bits has no legal triple, since all its loose bits lie on the exterior bond and any pair of a legal triple requires \(K\ge5\). We can therefore suppose the rival has at least five loose bits. [Necessary minimum for a legal pair](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001E/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001E-P001_marked_symmetry/scalar_inverse.md#a-necessary-distance-within-every-legal-triple)

**The four-place sites are common physically.** The rival cannot have a four-cut on a shore of three or more sites. If it did, that shore could be lawfully built first as an ancestor-closed prefix with exactly four loose bits. Its seed is the unique ternary site and its first two additions use one parent and make three outputs. If it had used just those two three-output additions, all its remaining sites would have used two or three parents and made two outputs. The minimum-width closure gives at least five loose bits for every such prefix. Thus it must already have used all three allowed three-output additions. Every site still to be added makes two outputs and takes at least two, so the final loose count could not exceed four. This contradicts the assumed five. Now every common four-bit word is an actual rival four-place site, since two adjacent ternary sites are also excluded. Consequently all source four-place sites and the seed are physically present. There are exactly three remaining rival sites, all five-place. [Four-cut reordering](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001E/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001E-P001_marked_symmetry/frontier_exchange.md#the-shore-can-be-built-first) · [Minimum-width closure](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J-P001_seven_to_six/family.md#all-histories-have-at-most-two-shrinking-joins)

**Every rival five-place star belongs to the source space.** A rival star \(W\) already in \(L\) needs no further argument. Suppose \(W\notin L\). It has at most two bits at each of \(u,v\), since otherwise adding that cap's forced five-bit word would produce a rival word through four, and all such words belong to \(L\). It has at most one bit at any common physical site by ordinary simplicity. Moreover \(W\) cannot use distinct bits from opposite sites of any adjacent common pair. The two adjacencies to \(W\) would complete an ordinary triangle, or two bits would meet at the same common site.

No input to \(u\) or \(v\) can lie in \(W\). Fix such an input \(e\). A source bond through five on \(e\) has a shore of at most two sites, as shown above. Thus any \(x\in W\setminus\{e\}\) can only share a true ordinary star of size at most five with \(e\), or come from the sum of the seed and an adjacent four-place site. The common physical sites and their adjacent sums have just been excluded. This includes the case in which the seed itself held \(e\). The only possible remaining stars are \(u\) and \(v\). At most one more bit from each can join \(e\), short of the four required in \(W\).

Let \(\mathcal M\) be the family of minimum ordinary shores containing the seed, \(u\) and \(v\), with the exterior outside. Their boundary size is exactly five. Any such shore costs at least five by the three-site lower bound, and the whole ordinary graph costs five. Conversely every five-bit cut on three or more sites comes from \(\mathcal M\). Equality in the chronological estimate requires the seed among the first three selected sites and both shrinking sites later.

Every pair of bits of \(W\) shares a boundary through five in the source. The only possibilities off the boundaries of \(\mathcal M\) are the short stars and seed-plus-four sums. By the physical exclusions and the input exclusion, any pair of \(W\) still lacking a common boundary in \(\mathcal M\) must consist of the two outputs of one of \(u,v\). Each of those outputs has at most one such exceptional partner in \(W\), so it occurs individually on a boundary from \(\mathcal M\). Both outputs then occur together on the boundary of the intersection of two witnessing shores. Their common holder is pinned inside each shore and their other endpoints are excluded by at least one of them. Intersection and union preserve membership in \(\mathcal M\) by cut submodularity.

For completeness, whenever a boundary bit occurs in \(\mathcal M\), its inside and outside endpoints are consistent throughout its occurrences. Opposed orientations in two minimum shores would give an edge between their two differences, making cut submodularity strict. For each pair \(i,j\in W\), choose a witnessing minimum shore \(U_{ij}\). For fixed \(i\), the intersection of these shores over \(j\ne i\) contains the inside endpoint of \(i\) and excludes the outside endpoints of **all** members of \(W\). The union of these intersections over \(i\) therefore has every bit of \(W\) on its boundary. Its capacity is five, so its boundary is \(W\). In particular \(W\) is a source equation.

Thus every rival star belongs to the source space. Their equal ranks give the same space and the same three-parent menu. Combining this case with the six-place rival and the rival with at most four loose bits proves the empty menu in every reachable rival at this source cutoff. In fact a no-six rival with at least five loose bits is impossible here. Its three five-place stars, the common four-place stars and the seed give exactly six loose bits by degree sum. The source ordinary graph starts with a four-cycle and subsequently adds sites with at least two different earlier neighbors, so remains connected after one ordinary deletion. Its wired graph remains connected after any two vertex deletions, by this observation when one is the exterior and by the two-ordinary-site deletion theorem otherwise. In the source each augmented star is therefore a bond whose deletion leaves all surviving bits linked by surviving cycles. In any representation a bond with at least two sites on each side would leave an edge on each side and no surviving cycle linking them, so each of these labeled bonds is again an augmented star. There are \(n+1\) such stars in both representations. The six-bit rival exterior must be the unique six-bit source star, at \(S\). But under either possible shrink order all three outputs of \(S\) have been used, by the high-holder column of the frontier closure. Its six incident bits meet six different ordinary neighbors, so none are parallel. The rival exterior must have a parallel pair from the final site. Parallel pairs are two-bit cycles and are preserved by equal equation spaces. ## A necessary condition for different one-shrink equations

When there is just one shrinking site \(v\), all six-bit cuts on at least three sites minimize the boundary of shores containing the seed and \(v\). This follows by equality in the chronological lower bound, since the initial three need the seed and \(v\) must save one. The minimum for the pinned pair is exactly six. The whole ordinary graph attains it and the two-site shore of the seed and \(v\) costs at least six. Write \(\mathcal M_6\) for these minimum shores, including that two-site shore if it attains six.

All ordinary sites except the high site physically agree. Let \(W\) be the rival six-place star, different from the source high star. Any pair in \(W\) that fails to occur jointly on a boundary of \(\mathcal M_6\) must lie on the source high star. All other short witnesses have at most two sites. The possible single low stars are common physically. The possible adjacent pairs have total arity at most eight and both are low, excluding them for \(W\) by bipartiteness as in the five-bit proof. In particular every bit in \(W\) occurs individually in \(\mathcal M_6\), by pairing it with a bit of \(W\) outside the high star, or with any other bit if it is itself outside. If every pair occurred together, the same union-of-intersections argument would give \(W\) as a true boundary and force equal spaces. Thus unequal spaces require two **shared** high bits whose boundary orientations at the source high site are opposite. Two incident bits ever occurring with that site inside occur jointly by intersection, and two ever occurring with it outside occur jointly by union. After a shrink preceding the high birth, each input occurs with \(S\) outside on the old prefix immediately before birth, and each output with \(S\) inside on the prefix immediately afterwards. These orientations persist whenever those bits occur in a minimum cut. An unequal competing star must then retain at least one true input and at least one true output.

## Testing the possible last equation through all observed pairs

There is an exact finite test at any size once the common space \(L\) has been proved. The test distinguishes binary extensions before imposing reachability. A bit's occurrence in a forced bond, or its finite observed pair reading with another bit, rules out an invisible singleton word. Here this holds at every bit, including a loose bit of \(S\) because of a six-valued cross pair. Two-bit words are excluded by the pair table. There is only one ternary bond in any reachable rival, the common seed in \(L\). Hence in every admissible one-coset extension every nonzero word of weight at most six is a bond. For a candidate binary extension, discard a coset immediately if it contains any word of weight one, two or three.

For any pair whose observed \(K(x,y)=k\le6\) is strictly smaller than its minimum inside \(L\), form the list of cosets \(W+L\) from all \(k\)-sets \(W\) containing \(x,y\) and having every pair reading at most \(k\). Exclude \(L\) and intersect these lists over **all** such pairs. Each list includes the true coset, and all nine input-output pairs contribute lists. For each remaining coset reject it if a word \(W\) of size \(4,5,6\) in it contains any pair with observed \(K>|W|\). No larger words need testing.

The remaining cosets give exactly the binary extensions of \(L\) having no extra singleton or ternary words and agreeing with the observed clipped readings. For necessity use a bond attaining each newly shorter pair. For sufficiency every word through six in the resulting linear space is a bond, because there are no small words that could form a disjoint decomposition through six. The rejection test prevents any smaller reading than observed. Pairs already attained in \(L\) retain a bond there, and list membership provides every remaining required witness. A single surviving coset therefore fixes the full equation space and all marked verdicts by allowed-state invariance. Multiple cosets in this binary test require a separate check of reachable realizations and their marked triples. In the one-shrink source family the augmented frontier calculation determines the actual high star in every state admitting a legal triple. Applying the same statement to a hypothetical positive rival excludes a disagreement at an empty source menu.
