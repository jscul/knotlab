# The four attachments of the live six-place junction

**Standard statement.** In the finite serial parity rule, take a source whose seed is its only three-place junction. Exactly four later junctions introduce three outputs each. The fourth has three parents and exactly one of its outputs has been used. If a three-parent extension is currently legal, every isometry of the pair minimum bond sizes through six to any reachable presentation preserves every ordinary vertex star on the transported edge names. Consequently it preserves the current allowed states and all three-parent extensions. Bond distance is minimum bond size minus one, so this is a cutoff of five.

**Nearest known result.** O recovers all smaller junctions and the two loose outputs of this junction. O leaves open the other four members of its support. Here we rule out exchanging one of those four and, by the same argument, exchanging any overlapping subset. The all-size step uses a proved finite formation quotient closed under every legal transition. ## The candidate that a rival must supply

A legal triple uses three loose distinctions at distinct ordinary holders. Each pair of holders must have a common ordinary neighbor, and no one neighbor may serve all three. These witnesses are junctions of the actual presentation. An input to the reader consists of the current table and an unordered triple. No high-site mark, names for actual parents, or past events are supplied.

Transport a rival's names along any proposed table isometry. With two six-place sites O already recovers every support on a live source. Otherwise write \(H\) for the sole six-place site. Its two loose outputs \(F=\{a,b\}\) and all the smaller physical junctions agree in the two presentations. Write \(I\) for its three true input names, \(s\) for its output used at the spending child \(C\), and \(A=I\cup\{s\}\). A rival support must be \(F\cup A'\), where \(A'\) has four members and retains at least one member of \(A\), by O. In the connected system of smaller sites its four holders must be distinct and of one bipartition color. The retained attachment fixes this color even when three of the four attachments are replaced.

Choose any added name \(z\in A'\setminus A\). In the source it is loose at a smaller site of that same color. Put \(K=D+1\). Its occurrence on the rival six-place star gives \(K(a,z)\le6\). Every source cut through \(a\) costs at least six, so equality holds. An attaining bond has both \(H\) and the low holder of \(z\) on its ordinary shore, since \(a,b,z\) are loose there. This includes the case where \(z\) and the removed bit have the same small holder. No common growth order has been assumed.

## When a new attachment can read this close

Every qualifying six-bit shore contains the seed and all parents of every site it contains. To see the constraint on births, its first three sites already cost at least seven. At most two later joins can each save one. A missing parent after those first three costs two extra, and including the six-place site among them would already cost at least nine. Counting what the latest three sites must send out rules out an entering bit near the beginning as well. The case with the original seed outside reduces to a previously classified five-loose prefix in which the six-place site cannot have its two surviving outputs. [Full parent-closure proof](shore.md#why-a-qualifying-shore-contains-all-of-its-parents)

Follow such a shore through the formation law. It suffices to retain the multiplicities and adjacencies of the loose holders, the sets of them witnessed by retired sites, their shore sides and colors, the fourth holder, and the number of already spent crossings. An outside join cannot reduce that last count. Every future test and transition is defined on this finite record. The closure has 244,740 records and 1,422,453 transitions. Exactly four records have a live triple and a qualifying loose \(z\). In each case the six-boundary is the entire set of loose bits, the spending child took three parents, and there were exactly two shrinking joins. The result is independent of the width or the length of the history. [Transition rules and coverage](shore.md#exact-closure-of-these-shores) · [Fixed-point certificate](shore.json)

We also need to know that the child still has a loose output. In O's child-marked quotient there are just two positive records with these parameters. In one, both child outputs are loose. In the other, one is loose. Following every predecessor back to the fourth birth gives precisely a three-parent spend, or that spend followed by one three-parent join using the child. ## Why none of those candidates can fit the table

Take one still-loose output \(f\) of \(C\). Each true input \(i\in I\) satisfies
\[
                     K(s,i)\le6<K(f,i).
\]
The first reading is witnessed by \(H\). For the strict second reading, any shore through \(i\) crosses three already existing disjoint paths between its two holders. A shore through \(f\) includes \(C\). If it also includes \(H\), the two loose outputs there and the exports from the latest selected site at or after \(C\) give at least four more crossings. If it omits \(H\), the spent bit \(s\) crosses. Including any later site then gives at least three further exports. The only case left is a shore already present at the birth of \(C\), which is excluded by the old legal-parent cut bound. The proof includes a direct birth-time argument for both child arities. [Strict reading at every depth](siblings.md#the-strict-reading-persists)

If the rival attaches \(f\) to \(H\), it must remove \(s\), since both meet \(C\). At least one true input \(i\) must remain by overlap. Then \(f,i\) lie together on the rival six-place junction, contradicting the strict reading.

Otherwise \(f\) stays loose. Removing any \(i\in I\) would leave it loose as well. There are six loose distinctions in the rival, since all support sizes and the width agree. The entire set of loose distinctions is a bond: the ordinary sites form a connected shore, with just the exterior on the other side. This would give \(K(f,i)\le6\), the same contradiction. Therefore the rival keeps all three inputs. Finally, removing \(s\) leaves \(s,f\) loose at the same small site \(C\). Every cut through one contains the other, so both have identical pair readings to \(i\). Their source readings lie on opposite sides of six. The rival must keep \(s\) too. This proves \(A'=A\).

## What the conclusion permits

- At **every finite size**, a source in this one-spent family with at least one legal triple is uniquely determined as a collection of physical supports on matched names by \(D\le5\), against every reachable mixed-law rival. The common supports give all allowed states and all current triple verdicts. They also give corresponding continuations for the ordinary history-independent serial join rule, by induction on subsequent joins and a corresponding naming of each new output group.
- If the unknown source is promised to come from this one-spent family, a table-only verdict works even when its menu is empty. Enumerate finite reachable presentations in the promised family at that width with the observed clipped table. If any presentation has a legal triple, the theorem forces all matching presentations to be the same on the given names. Otherwise return an empty menu. This existence argument puts no bound on the reader's running time and supplies no past history.
- Whether an **empty-menu** source in this family can match a positive source **outside** the family remains open, as does unrestricted mixed-law menu invariance. Two presentations elsewhere with the same pair readings and different allowed states would establish loss of state information. A change of three-parent permission requires a disagreement on an unordered triple under the same table correspondence. [Bounds on a possible live rival](exchange.md#what-changes-if-the-given-source-has-an-empty-menu)
- Five is sharp. At width 31 a lawful exchange between distinct holders preserves all 465 pair entries through four, yet erases both legal triples. Three retained attachments each read bond distance six to the candidate in the source and five after it is attached. Eleven entries differ through five and 47 in full. At 23 there is already a sharpness witness at the same small holder.
