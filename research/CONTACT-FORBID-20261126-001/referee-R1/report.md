## Fixes

1. **Block — the two addressed starts do not give bidirectional certainty.** The decision says, “In all 232 contact placements of the forbidding study (a newborn holding both readers' distinctions) and at the two starts where one edit changes the other reader's birth, each reader's own births change with the far edit, so the far setting can be read off the near reader's structure with certainty; single values stay fair.” In the 24 addressed combinations of placement, law, grant and weighting, the **second** reader’s named birth description changes in 24. The **first** reader’s changes in zero. Its record is unchanged in 21 and moves only in its values in three, all under not-all-equal and U. At the seven-knot start these are the ordinary and one-fewer grants. At the six-knot start it is the ordinary grant. For example, under ordinary parity at either start, the first reader’s total variation is exactly 0 at both own settings. The earlier flag answers whether *some* reader changes. The study’s tally likewise counts a shape change for a row if *any* reader has one. [Decision, finding 1](../decision.md) · [Tally](../tally.py) · [Exact comparisons](addressed.json)

2. **Block — a reading can use the changing birth and still give fair outputs above the bound.** The note says, “Readings other than the study's (any function of a reader's complete record): with the record moving, a reading that uses it would signal; a reading that hides it is a coarser record, not the reader's own.” Take the five-knot census placement numbered 5280, drawn in the referee sample, with holders 3 and 4, under not-all-equal and one fewer. The same three-parent birth receives inputs 9, 10 and 11 at all four settings. It has one fresh distinction at 11 and zero at 00, 01 and 10. The following rules use only the specified reader’s observations. [Exact sentence](../README.md#what-this-does-not-say) · [Births and original marked readings](shared.json)

   | Reading at that placement | Correlations in setting order 00, 01, 10, 11 | CHSH |
   | --- | --- | ---: |
   | From stored fields, each reader reports its own marked old value when the common birth has no fresh distinction and its one fresh value otherwise. | −29/86, −29/86, −29/86, 1 | **173/86** |
   | From A289’s declared fields, both readers take the parity of the three common old inputs. The second reader flips it when the common birth has a fresh distinction. | 1, 1, 1, −1 | **4** |

   Both weightings give exactly 1/2 for each output at each setting. Complementing all values preserves the not-all-equal states and exchanges both outputs. The fresh count still tells a reader at own setting 1 which far edit was made. Thus the complete-record signalling test detects this change even though the reported bits pass the single-reader no-signalling test. The frozen score bound stands for the study’s original readings. 3. **Narrowing — identify what each reader can name and what values it receives.** The note says, “The forbidding study's own growth, edits, weightings (U, S), readings and complete records (A289), unchanged.” The stored record lists all input names but omits the values of inputs contributed by outside parents, as the predecessor referee recorded. Including those values changes six of the 96 addressed per-setting total variations, with both own settings counted separately. All six belong to the first reader in the three cases in fix 1. For example, at the seven-knot start, ordinary not-all-equal under U, the variation changes from 35344/645136107 to 141376/1751083719. The named-record verdict of zero versus positive survives in every case. [Study note](../README.md) · [Stored fields](../../FORBID-EDITS-20261126-001/forbid.py) · [Predecessor report, fix 5](../../FORBID-EDITS-20261126-001/referee-R1/report.md)

   For the second reader the **only** changed part of the addressed birth description is the name of a loose distinction at one outside parent. In the seven-knot start it is 16 or 17 from holder 5, joined to the same own input 14. In the six-knot start it is 12 or 13 from holder 4, joined to the same own input 10. Counting these names as observable gives total variation 1. As a control, keep the outside parent, its contributed value, every near input and each fresh count, and omit only the outside input name. No addressed birth then changes shape. The second reader’s variation is 1/2 in the eight shortened-parity combinations and 0 in the other 16. The first reader retains its three changes in value. Under this reading both records are invariant in 13 of 24 combinations. Swapping the two loose distinctions fixes every old knot’s unordered set of distinctions and every marked reading. Recognizing which one the outside holder supplied requires access to its name or its allocation order. At a fixed prepared state the conditional probabilities can still change. At the seven-knot start, fixing the second reader’s setting at 0 with just distinctions 0, 2, 8 and 17 equal to one and both additions zero, changing the first edit makes the second reader’s all-zero fresh triple go from probability 0 to 1/8 under the ordinary not-all-equal grant. Averaging over the preparation gives identical second-reader distributions once the outside name is removed. [Declared and stored comparisons](addressed.json)

4. **Narrowing — far-setting invariance does not establish fairness.** The note says, “The single values stay fair throughout, so a reading of one value hides the signal; the complete record does not.” The marked old values are fair. In all 24 addressed parity setting tuples, both added values are fixed at zero under U and S. The original even-parity equation and its edited version differ only by the added distinction, forcing its value. The four added-value correlations are therefore 1, giving CHSH 2. Under not-all-equal the added values are fair. The joint old-and-added marginal of each reader is invariant under the far edit in all addressed cases. 5. **Narrowing — specify which far settings can be distinguished with three readers.** The note says, “For each reader: the largest total variation of its complete record between the two far settings (over its own setting), and whether the shape of its births (which births, their parents, inputs and fresh counts) changes, which it can read off its own structure with certainty.” For the five-knot placement numbered 4821 with holders 2, 3 and 4, each reader has **four** far-setting pairs at each own setting. At own setting 0, the full record gives identical distributions for far 00, 01 and 10, and distinguishes 11 with certainty. At own setting 1, it distinguishes 00, either of 01 or 10, and 11. The two middle settings give exactly the same full distribution under U and S. Every reader has maximum variation 1, but none can reconstruct both far bits in all cases. [Study note](../README.md) · [All 36 comparisons](readout.json)

6. **Narrowing — the construction establishes a shared birth.** The note says, “The joint response at a contact is a far edit reaching the near reader's own births: a contact carries the setting across, which in Bell's terms is communication, not a correlation without it.” The selection uses a newborn with parents from multiple readers. The calculation establishes changes in those readers’ records on the selected placements. A native contact between readers additionally requires independent recurrence witnesses and a joint allowed-state relation passing the canonical knot and persistence tests. The study supplies no such test for the parent-list criterion. Its broader inference applies only to the measured starts, laws and observation convention. ## How it was checked and what was not run

I read the handoff, note, decision, the predecessor’s decision and referee report, A289 and the repository’s foundation, discipline and guardrails. I matched the selected rows in the complete shared-birth archive against the predecessor’s flagged rows. Replays use new growth and enumeration code. They import no producer or predecessor referee code. For each setting they keep both the original and edited constraints, enumerate allowed old assignments, and enumerate each birth’s allowed fresh assignments directly. Fresh distinctions occur in just one birth, so multiplying the completion counts for a fixed old assignment sums exactly over all combinations. For the alternative readings I also enumerated every full completion at the example placement. The addressed check regenerated the two specified starts from their draw seeds. [Shared-birth replay](shared.py) · [Addressed replay](addressed.py) · [Readings and three-reader comparisons](readout.py)

The initial sample used seed **26112641**, drawing uniformly without replacement within the three reported groups, in sorted order of start and holders. It comprised 12 of 163 not-all-equal two-reader rows, 8 of 68 parity rows and the sole three-reader row. I included both not-all-equal score maxima, adding one distinct row. The three-row pilot took 0.63 seconds. The 22-row replay took 1.10 seconds. A two-row pilot for the remainder took 0.49 seconds, and checking the other 210 took 5.61 seconds. The additional reading and three-reader checks took 0.25 seconds after a 0.13-second pilot. Each of those processes had a 1 GiB address-space cap and used under 25 MiB resident. The addressed replay used one process. Its same-configuration pilot on the seven-knot ordinary not-all-equal row took 4.44 CPU seconds. The full addressed check took 9.28 CPU seconds and peaked at 551 MiB resident (rounded up). [Replay evidence](shared.json) · [Remainder](remainder.json) · [Addressed run](addressed.json)
