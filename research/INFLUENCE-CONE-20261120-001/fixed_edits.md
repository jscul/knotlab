# First changes measured from the actual edited cut

Count parent steps from the two physical presentations being compared. Suppose both complete ordinary plans stay funded and nonclosing through the observation, and fix a common receiver \(R\) among their live knots. Count a receiver birth if **at least one** of its ancestors at the comparison cut lies in \(R\). A fresh count belongs to the newly formed knot. A change in its number of immediate children is a roster change at the following step. A roster comparison retains complete parent sets and counts repeated unary births separately.

| Edit at the comparison cut | Earliest possible changed fresh count on a receiver birth | Earliest possible unmatched receiver birth |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Loose counts change only at an already-live set \(S\), with the same witnesses and live knots, \(d=\operatorname{dist}(R,S)>0\) | \(d\) | \(d+1\) |
| Erasing \(S\) gives the same witnessed end and counts, \(d\ge2\) measured in the union of the two ends | \(d-1\) | \(d\) |

Both rows are sharp for every indicated finite \(d\), under parity and not-all-equal. Both statements allow arbitrary sizes of groups and unaries. The second row is attained even when both arms keep every live knot and the distances in the two arms agree. Infinity protects all funded nonclosing steps. The primary step joins two live knots that some current knot witnesses together. Equivalently it joins all co-input pairs of actual births in a funded plan, because every witnessed pair receives group coverage.
