# Can a receiver be forced to miss signals, and can one successor hold its count?

The [structural study](../SOLE-PAYMENTS-AND-SUCCESSOR-COUNTS-20261122-001A/preregistration.md) and the [successor study](../SOLE-PAYMENTS-AND-SUCCESSOR-COUNTS-20261122-001B/preregistration.md) began separately after independent Astra Max design review of each exact freeze. The [revised first design](../SOLE-PAYMENTS-AND-SUCCESSOR-COUNTS-20261122-001A/advisor_receipt.json) distinguishes finitely or sparsely constrained receipts from an actual limiting bound. The second advisor challenged the implicit identification of the predecessor’s old inputs after all its marks have been paid. The [revised second design](../SOLE-PAYMENTS-AND-SUCCESSOR-COUNTS-20261122-001B/advisor_receipt.json) specifies the raw physical view and tests what a supplied pointer adds.

The two results have different scopes. The [sole-payment proof](../../proofs/SOLE-PAYMENTS-AND-SUCCESSOR-COUNTS-20261122-001A/SOLE-PAYMENTS-AND-SUCCESSOR-COUNTS-20261122-001A-P001_sole_payments/proof.md) covers any fixed material course in its two-parent, one-receiver class. Changed older values in its continuing example force an unequal pair under parity and allow both equal and unequal pairs under not-all-equal. The single chosen designation at the two interchangeable unary children is supplied. Hiding it ends strict singleton enrollment there. This leaves intact the result for wholly intrinsic indefinitely returning enrollments, including the [earlier five-founder clock](../QUIET-RETURNS-AND-SIGNAL-FRACTIONS-20261122-001/returns.md).

The [successor proof](../../proofs/SOLE-PAYMENTS-AND-SUCCESSOR-COUNTS-20261122-001B/SOLE-PAYMENTS-AND-SUCCESSOR-COUNTS-20261122-001B-P001_successor_tally/proof.md) fixes that marked clock. From the same start, the birth displayed as knot 50 receives distinctions 98 and 100 with values 11 and writes 108 and 109 with values 00. It pays 108 to knot 55 along with a zero payment, 110, from knot 51. This happens both with and without one early signal. In both histories the newborn’s full word is 0011. The earlier paid names still retain different values. Repeating the local change on separate returns permits arbitrarily many earlier totals to have identical readings through a fixed number of coholdings. An added conditional copy can deliver the cumulative parity of the signal count without input from an external tally. This requires a return flag, the incoming roles, the chosen outgoing slot and a specified filling rule. Ordinary growth itself admits contrary completions.

For an independent check I applied the ordinary complete-cut operator to every abstract witnessed arrangement through four live holders with one or two loose distinctions each. Of 1,906 inputs per law, 842 were funded. Every one of the 55 binary births with two sole payers was terminal or had only a one-parent child next. Separately a full physical clause encoding reproduces two different preceding signals with identical own bits and anonymously matching one-contact readings. Two coholding steps recover both older inputs at every checked handoff after a return. These are checks of the proof, with no weighting. ## Final report as sent

**1. A receipt can be forced, but this cannot force a long-run signal frequency.** In one reached case, two payers each received **00**. Parity required both to send **0**. Not-all-equal required both to send **1**. The receiver was silent in both runs. Once those parents spend their only marks on the same child, neither can pay a sibling. That condition can happen at most once on the same succession. At every other receipt from grown parents, at least one payer can make the two arrivals agree or differ. Every limiting fraction from **0 to 1** is therefore possible on a fixed, value-independent course that keeps returning. A closure or a split into indistinguishable children can end enrollment. Only actual returns enter the count. [Structural proof and payment trace](https://github.com/jscul/knotlab/blob/main/proofs/SOLE-PAYMENTS-AND-SUCCESSOR-COUNTS-20261122-001A/SOLE-PAYMENTS-AND-SUCCESSOR-COUNTS-20261122-001A-P001_sole_payments/proof.md)

**2. The successor on the five-founder clock does not carry its running count.** In two histories, the first return received **00** or **01**, giving totals of zero and one. At the second return, the same knot received **11** and wrote **00** in both histories. It then paid the same distinction, mark 108 with value **0**, to the same successor. That successor’s complete word was **0011** in both. Even knowing the age and the parent roles cannot distinguish the two totals from that word. [Matched histories](https://github.com/jscul/knotlab/blob/main/proofs/SOLE-PAYMENTS-AND-SUCCESSOR-COUNTS-20261122-001B/SOLE-PAYMENTS-AND-SUCCESSOR-COUNTS-20261122-001B-P001_successor_tally/proof.md)

| What the receiver retains | Parity | Not-all-equal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The latest signal in the parent’s two fresh marks | The marks differ exactly when the incoming pair differed. | The same fresh pair can follow either signal. |
| The latest signal in the single mark paid onward | Either value is possible. | Either value is possible. |
| The cumulative count in any fixed number of contacts around the successor | Histories with totals zero and one can match. | Histories with totals zero and one can match. |

The older payments remain physically present. With its predecessor identified, the immediate child can recover the **latest** signal by visiting that parent and following its contacts one step further. Visiting the parent alone can leave its old inputs ambiguous. Following the full retained history recovers the **total** under both laws. A supplied writer can carry whether the count is **odd or even** under either law. It needs to recognize the return, distinguish the two payers and know which mark will go to the enrolled child.
