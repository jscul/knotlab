**The state after the middle reads.** Let the allowed words be the kernel of the binary matrix whose rows are the knot laws. Write a word as `(y,t)`, with the two readers holding `y` and the middle holding `t`. For middle record `m`, the coefficient at `y` is proportional to the sum of `(−1)^(m·t)` over its allowed completions. Cancellation leaves exactly the records of the form `m = h_middle`, where `h` is a sum of knot laws. All such records have the same probability. On the allowed projections `y` the amplitude sign is `(−1)^(h_readers·y)`. Thus a shared change `x` with middle completion `tₓ` contributes the correlation sign `(−1)^(m·tₓ)`. The fixed middle measurement can precede the independently chosen settings of the two readers.

**The game.** The checker solves for two changes and two value laws whose local pairings are the identity on both readers. Each local read uses the convention `XˣZᶻ`, with an extra minus sign at the bottom right on both sides. Direct multiplication gives commuting contexts, row products `+,+,+` and column products `+,+,−`. Omitting that extra sign puts the minus in the last row. The study’s checker records membership of the unsigned reads and commutation on the first side. Its docstring also promises “the row and column product signs.” [Study checker](../css.py#L132)

Let `s₁,s₂` be the two signs from the completed changes. In the first and third rows the intersection products are `s₁,s₂,s₁s₂`. In the second row all are positive. One reader can multiply their three answers by the indicated signs using only their own local setting and the two-bit record. This preserves the required local parity. Alternatively, herald the `(+,+)` class before the settings and play normally on its quarter of trials. An analyst can also recover the corrected conditional score after the trials. Without the record, the fixed reads give 2/3 under uniform settings, verified directly on the full initial state. A herald disclosed to a reader carries information from the middle and conveys neither remote setting. [Study’s event-ready claim](../README.md#L100)

**Rebuilt.** All three families, three growth rounds and two reader choices give the four sign classes. For example, on the five-knot rings joined by chains, at the start and using all places, the two change completions are single middle distinctions 75 and 49 in the supplied indexing. The possible nonzero record with minus signs on distinctions 75, 82 and 83 has `(−,+)` and wins 5/9 before correction. Exact setting-by-setting probabilities give eight equally weighted joint answers at every one of the 648 combinations of arrangement, sign class and setting pair. Each reader’s four local answers have probability 1/4 for every remote setting. Enumerating all 4,096 parity-legal pairs of deterministic answer lists gives optimum 8/9, attained by 144 pairs. This uses equal weighting of the nine independent setting pairs. [Input arrangements](joined_square_inputs.json) · [Counts and sign witnesses](game_results.json)

The program eliminates the middle columns first, solves for the two local pairs and derives the joint answer tables from signed products. On every contributing product the dependence on the record lies in the two calculated signs. Their map from the possible records has rank two on every arrangement. This covers all 100,786,200 possible records summed over the 18 arrangements by exact linear algebra. The individual records were not enumerated. A six-distinction control also evaluated literal amplitudes by summing the allowed words. The one-arrangement pilot took 0.008 CPU seconds and the full check 0.048 CPU seconds, each with one process and peak resident memory below 19 MiB.
