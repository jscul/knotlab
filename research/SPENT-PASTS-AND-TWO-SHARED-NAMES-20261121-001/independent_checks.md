Its weight after coherently adding predecessor alternatives is the squared norm of that row times B. All ordinary probabilities of all such retained labels remain invariant under normalization, for every preparation, exactly when BBᵀ is a positive scalar times the identity. Indeed, compare any two preparations carried on different orthogonal labels. Invariance of their relative weights forces the same factor for every squared norm. Testing individual basis rows and the sum of two basis rows gives the diagonal and off-diagonal conditions. For a binary incidence matrix this says that every input has equally many successors and different inputs have disjoint sets of successors. ## Why a second shared distinction changes the sign measurement

Write a,c for the first reader's names, b for the second reader's and d for the companion's inaccessible name. With two shared names there are four named bits. All 16 possible words were tested in each of eight placement and law cases. The ordered law pairs parity/parity, parity/not-all-equal, not-all-equal/parity and not-all-equal/not-all-equal give 4, 6, 6 and 10 lawful four-bit words respectively at either placement. Both projected holder triples pass the canonical test in all eight cases. A coherent source and the corresponding diagonal control are different preparations on those same words. With a primary parity knot, a reader acting only on b must preserve it because a,c determine it. Reading the final b then leaves one original input value at that reader. This excludes generating the entangled state required by either exact signed return on the independent test input 00. With a not-all-equal primary and a parity companion sharing a and b, parity with the unchanged unread d likewise prevents either individual from changing its shared bit. If the parity companion instead shares both a,c, the equal-a,c component has b fixed by a and cannot be released. In the unequal-a,c component the source at the b reader already factors.

For two not-all-equal laws with a,c shared, write the initial amplitudes in two four-by-two matrices, one per unread d. The b=1 column at d=0 equals the b=0 column at d=1. An exact positive signed transcript with local residual work resolved on product input 00 must map any surviving slice to a multiple of the same entangled target. The operation at the b reader must have rank two. After undoing that invertible matrix on the two logical columns, the common source column would have to be a multiple of two linearly independent target columns. Both multiples vanish. This obstruction holds even for unrestricted local offers with d unread.

When a,b are shared and unequal, every original c value is compatible with both d values. Any individual offer legal on each reached basis word keeps that pair a,b fixed on those inputs. Resolve a putative successful transcript by the final physical pair and by the remaining local work. Keeping both b columns for an entangled target forces annihilating the other original a sector. In the surviving a sector, one unread slice has a single nonzero column and that very column occurs in the other slice. Forcing both output slices to be zero or proportional to a common entangled target kills the column in both. This excludes a positive transcript even if a reader couples its private data before declaring success.
