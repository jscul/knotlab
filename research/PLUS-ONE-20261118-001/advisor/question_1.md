Colleague review before a freeze; terse.

Context (TOUCH-CENSUS, refereed): under the fewest-count parity growth (complete-round joining rule), a "birth" is an offshoot of
demand 1 whose parent is not itself an offshoot of demand 1; its "partner" is its unique candidate (the other live child of its
parent); births of one round with a common partner form a class (two sibling births, each the other's partner, are one class of
two). The rule "dimension (junctions by round) = largest class" held in 11,476 of 11,621 growing arrangements of up to five knots;
in all 147 exceptions the dimension was the largest class plus one (classes 1+1 giving 2, 1+1+1 giving 2, 2+1 giving 3).

Pilot on 12 exceptions and 8 matches: in every exception, two classes born in the same round have partners that are candidates of
each other (share a neighbour); in the matches, no two classes' partners are candidates.

Proposed corrected rule to freeze: link two classes of one round when their partners share a neighbour; the dimension is the
largest, over rounds and linked components, of the SUM of the class sizes in a component (0 if no birth; birthless growth handled
separately). Note 1+1+1 giving 2 in the pilot came as components [[1,1],[1]], i.e. the third class unlinked.

Questions: (1) Is "sum over a linked component" the right form, or should it be largest + (number of linked classes), or
something else, and why mechanically (does linking partners make the two chains' directions commute into one product)? (2) Predict
where the sum rule could fail (a component of three linked classes? classes linked across rounds?). (3) One or two sharp
predictions to freeze beyond "the rule matches every settled growing arrangement with a birth in the census", e.g. on a six-knot
sample.
