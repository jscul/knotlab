# Referee report on reading ordinary growth from the finite core
**Refuted — Block on chained twins as the sole exception and on their least rank as a general first-difference rule.**

The published comparisons on pieces with a cycle hold on their sampled starts. The general explanation fails on a **nine-knot start with a finished core, two chains and no chained twins**. Under both knot laws, two births receive the wrong number of fresh distinctions in round 9. The total first differs in round 10. The same fault occurs on a start whose only piece the late-views count calls a tree. Part 3 measures limit hits under its weightings. Its recorded rates do not measure the frequency of infinite cores.

## Where the reading fails

The nine knots have 4, 5, 7, 7, 5, 4, 3, 4 and 3 places. The four round-one groups of live knots are 012, 123, 234 and 345. Their loose counts, in knot order 0 through 5, are 2, 2, 3, 3, 2 and 2. Each end supplies one chain. The cycle-class core finishes after **43 births**, in successive counts of 6, 7, 8, 8, 7, 4, 2 and 1. An exhaustive check of birth pairs found five pairs sharing at least two parents, three in round 1 and one each in rounds 4 and 7. None has the required chain-bearing common grandparent. The two core births in round 7 both hold both chain bases. Of their two shared parents, one holds only the first base and the other only the second. Their common grandparent holds neither. Extend each birth once on each chain. The two resulting round-9 births have **disjoint parent sets**. The node reading puts them together in one four-parent join in round 10. Lemma R gives two three-parent joins instead. Two other round-9 births each belong to both of these joins, raising each demand by one. Direct enumeration gives 52 groups and two offshoots where the reading gives 51 groups and two offshoots. The error is in the assumed parents of a chain extension, before the binomial counts are summed. Removing either chain restores both readings through round 24 under both laws. The pure core still obeys the predecessor's conditional theorem. - **Tree-class start.** Take two ordinary rounds of the nine-knot witness and use the resulting 24 knots as the start. The late-views count gives one tree piece. Its 134-birth core finishes, with two chains and no chained twins. Both implementations first miss both readings in round 13 under both laws. The round-14 total is 116 against 115 predicted. The twin flag can correctly detect a later change after the reading has already failed. No tested positive start agreed through its first twin round under both laws. ## Fixes to the record

1. **Block — the necessary condition for failure.** The [README, Part 2, lines 74–76](../README.md#part-2-the-twin-exception-fresh-frozen) states the explanation under test as “the node reading fails exactly on starts with **chained twins**, two core births of one round that share two parents C and D with a chain base in the points of a common parent of C and D.” Part 1's result says “So on pieces with a cycle the node reading is exact wherever it reads, and its only misses are the chained twins: the late-views study's six-knot cycle starts it could not predict are now read, except 11 with chained twins.” Retain the finite-sample results. Withdraw the general exception in both map classes. The nine- and 24-knot witnesses pass the read gates and fail under both laws. They also rule out a proof of the current chain reading from the absence of chained twins alone.
2. **Block — timing as a rule about the whole start.** The [README, T3, line 104](../README.md#part-2-the-twin-exception-fresh-frozen) says “On each chained-twin start where it differs, the first difference is at the round equal to the least rank of its chained twins, under each law where it differs”. Keep this as a result for the sampled starts. The connected 34-knot example gives first difference 9 and least twin rank 10. Whether every chained twin forces an observable difference when the earlier rounds agree remains open.
3. **Narrowing — what the cap counts.** The [README, lines 185–186](../README.md#part-3-how-often-a-core-is-unending-1) says “A core is unending here when the round rule lists more than 5,000 families (core births) before it finishes; a 120-second backstop marks a start *not run*.” The earlier [pre-freeze change, lines 25–26](../README.md#the-reading) still says “A core whose round rule lists more than 20,000 families (core births) is *unending* and not predicted; no time limit decides a start.” Give the actual settings for each saved run. The code defaults to 200,000 unless configured. With the study settings, one piece can stop when (a) accumulated nodes, including live starting knots, exceed 5,000, (b) *all* maximal candidate families in one rank exceed the remaining node budget, even families discarded before a birth, or (c) the sum over parents of the number of child pairs exceeds 250,000. A pair counts once per shared parent. Draw and growth timeouts are separate. An eight-knot tree+s50 sample (1990) has **963 births, 971 nodes** and 5,728,977 pair incidences in one round. Dropping that guard gives a finished core and a round-5 miss under both laws. [Guard implementation, lines 28–55 and 118–136](../../GRADED-ORDER-20261126-001/order.py) · [Case replay](recover_missing.json)
4. **Block — infinity or explosive growth inferred from a limit hit.** The [README, lines 202–206](../README.md#part-3-how-often-a-core-is-unending-1) says “Every unending core seen is explosive: its rounds' births multiply (9, 11, 13, 17, 22, 38, 127, 720 on one `tree+` start), as in the explosive starts of `WALL-READ-20261119-001`.” Two nine-knot tree+ samples from the 20,000-sample run, 4490 and 16615, provably finish with 67,108,985 and 65,735 nodes. In each, all births of one rank share a parent. The next births are all pairs, then all triples, continuing through subsets of increasing size to one final birth. The stored witnesses have 26 and 16 members at that rank. At most one of these two distinct samples can be the historical timeout. A third sample, 8915, has isomorphic consecutive-rank parent incidences three rounds apart, with a recorded renaming. Lemma R therefore repeats **7, 7, 6** forever. It crosses the deterministic guard with bounded births per round. Its individual historical stop reason is unavailable. [Finite and periodic certificates](weightings.json)
5. **Narrowing — relay and horizon.** The [README, lines 20–21](../README.md#the-reading) says “a core that does not finish by rank 25 never relays.” Suppress the relay within the scored horizon. A 57-knot cycle-class example still has two core births in round 25. Its 351st and last birth occurs in round 26, takes one fresh distinction under not-all-equal and relays. Single-piece parity closes at round 27. For reproduction, “Here the core is built to rank 25 (enough for 24 rounds)” (line 14) uses **birth-round rank**, with the starting knots at zero. The code stores size equal to rank plus one. For round 24 it processes size 25 and retains size 26 to measure demand. For round 9 it retains size 11. Omitting that last rank changes seven signatures in the 24-round chainless control. The implementation did retain it. [Delayed relay](delayed.json) · [Lookahead controls](checks45_audit.json)
6. **Narrowing — fresh starts and twin buckets.** The [decision, line 35](../decision.md#position-and-next-directions) says “The late view of every read start without chained twins is read off the start, on 385 fresh starts”. Those are 385 agreeing sample entries and 384 distinct starts. The seven-knot grow sample 47631 renames to tree+ sample 52. The 30 agreeing six-knot starts come from the earlier uniform sample, and seven-knot grow sample 107 also repeats the earlier growth sample. That leaves 353 distinct agreements new relative to the checked predecessor files. The two Part 2 retained samples are disjoint from each other and from those earlier samples up to renaming. The study's “no twins” bucket skips twins born in round 1. Counting them changes that name on 36 uncapped Part 1 entries. Starting knots cannot supply a chain-bearing grandparent in that round, so every chained-twin flag and score stands. The historical “417 earlier sample starts” (README, line 29) likewise counts 417 completed entries, 416 distinct. [Exact identity and sampling checks](exclusion.json) · [Rebuilt flags](replay.py)
7. **Not a block — missing Part 2 comparisons.** The [README, lines 176–179](../README.md#part-2-the-twin-exception-fresh) says “Twelve were not scored”. Use **13** in the original run, comprising twelve growth timeouts and one further pair-limit exclusion. The sampled verdict extends to all 55 flagged starts with the short replays above.

## Checks, limits and stopping point

The independent core uses parent incidence and maximal groups from Lemma R. The reader groups named chains by base, chooses which lengths equal one, and counts the remaining length assignments by binomial coefficients. Both birth multisets and the total were compared round by round against ordinary growth. Stored parity closures were checked for zero predicted births from closure through the requested horizon. A separate subset enumeration certified the counterexample's core and mixed parent groups. Only after the blind comparison did the audit inspect the study's code, review notes, strata, score files and growth records. The unchanged study scorer was also run as a separate executable on each decisive witness. [Core](core.py) · [Chain reader](reader.py) · [Stored-row replay](replay.py) · [Targeted certificate](targeted.py)

The four-start pilot took 0.84 s and the remaining 396 took 115 s. Five stored-row pilots took 0.80 s including file loading, and the other 472 replays took 12.24 s. A higher-limit check of 33 cycle-class entries used a 20,000-birth limit and 50 birth rounds, with a three-second per-start backstop. Six of the seven previously read unfinished entries still agreed; one crossed the larger birth limit at this longer horizon. Two runs reached the backstop. [Changed limits](vary_cycles.py) · [Results](vary.jsonl)

**Not run.** Full 24-round regrowth of every archived start, a repeat of the historical 120-second timeouts, and explicit enumeration of the tens of millions of births covered by the finite certificates. The 13 recovered starts were grown only through the decisive round. The untested ten-knot grow row mentioned in the design has no Part 3 tally. The independent samples and controls support no population-wide success rate for the node reading.
