# Supplementary referee report on factorization over knots
**Refuted — Block** on the universal claim and the stated reason for stopping. Where defined, the two registered countings factor over the **listed rules** of a fixed value-blind growth. Some listed rules fail the knot test in the whole structure. An earlier transfer law also gives a weighting that fails to factor even when all four listed rules are knots. Both findings concern factorization. A scoring advantage would require a separate test.

## 1. Block — the listed rules and the knots in the whole give different answers

“Every recorded weighting the model makes factors over knots” is the verdict in the decision, line 26. To select reader pairs, the code treats every listed rule as a knot. The counting definitions instead test the relation induced on those distinctions by all allowed states. A knot binds at least two open distinctions and a contact. It must pass K1–K3 in the whole. [Decision](../decision.md) · [Pair selection, lines 65–69](../countings.py) · [Counting definitions, §10](../../TWO-COUNTINGS-20261125-001/definitions.md#10-the-knot-test)

**Growth itself can cause the failure.** Begin with five not-all-equal rules, all knots in the start. One arity-floor round adds two births, both knots in the finish. The additional restrictions make an original eight-place rule fail K2. Across the 14 distinctions, exactly 2,898 states remain, and every one- and two-distinction projection is full. The 18 knots in the whole together admit 2,910 states. Counting from the finish and the renormalized start counting agree. For readers 0 and 9, an assignment of the rest gives the table `[[1,1],[1,0]]/2898`. This was the smallest verified-start example of this kind in the not-all-equal census through two funded rounds and 14 distinctions. [Full holdings, fixed values and canonical verdict](whole_nae_3108.json) · [Census](whole_nae_prefix.json)

**Smallest funded census case.** Take three even-parity rules on `014, 023, 123`, with digits naming distinctions. The last two imply that 0 and 1 agree. The first then fixes 4 at zero. One ordinary round adds the implied rule on 4 alone. Of the 32 assignments, four are allowed. Every knot in the whole misses 4, since any scope containing 4 and another distinction has a constrained proper part. The two knots on `023, 123` together admit eight assignments. Flipping 4 in the all-zero state therefore preserves every knot’s local pattern and produces a forbidden state. No product of nonnegative factors on those knots can assign it zero. Five distinctions is minimal in this census, since three starting rules have at least nine places and each distinction has at most two starting holders. A one-distinction factor can enforce this particular fixed value. [Parity census and minimality check](whole_census_small.json)

**A case where even two-distinction factors cannot help.** Start with not-all-equal on `012, 034, 12345`. One ordinary birth adds `567`. The eight distinctions allow 102 states. Every one- and two-distinction projection is full. Among all 255 nonempty scopes, exactly four are knots in the whole, on `012, 034, 1234, 567`. Their joint local patterns admit 108 states. For example, forbidden state `01111100` has a positive-weight local pattern on every knot. The five-place rule already fails K2 in the start. Eight is the smallest such not-all-equal finish among the first two funded rounds of the 7,075 census starts. [Exact states, local witnesses and canonical check](whole_nae_34.json) · [Census minimum](whole_nae_small.json)

The finish weights on 1 and 5 are proportional to `[[0,1],[1,1]]`. The raw five-place rule holds both, so the study’s code skips precisely this pair. Here the unary-parent birth has three completions for either old input, so the start counting gives the same table. ## 2. Block — an earlier transfer law already supplies a counterexample on fixed holdings

The [README, line 72](../README.md) says “That no growth law could make a non-factoring weighting; only that the recorded ones do not.” The recorded law for transferring an active parent’s state changes which fresh assignments are reachable. Its smallest founder is one three-place even knot. Start uniformly on its four states and grow one round. All four starts give the same listed knots `012, 034, 156, 278`. Each newborn takes one old distinction and gets two fresh ones. On a 1 input it occupies one of the pair. On a 0 input it occupies both if **another distinction of the parent is 1**, and neither if the parent is quiet. The additional condition reads outside the newborn’s own distinctions. [Transfer definition, lines 7–10 and 20](../../RING-TO-END-20261119-001/definitions.md) · [Original one-founder control and implementation](../../../experiments/RING-TO-END-20261119-001/RING-TO-END-20261119-001-E001_transfer/transfer.py)

I rebuilt every permitted lift from each start. **13 states are reached; their four local projections admit 32.** With uniform choice among each birth’s eligible lifts, one reached state has weight 1/4 and twelve have weight 1/16. The state with ones exactly at `0,1,3,5` is unreachable. Its first three local patterns occur when `7,8` are also 1, and its last local pattern occurs in the all-zero state. This obstruction holds for every weighting positive on the four starts and their permitted lifts. All four scopes have the complete even relation in the parity structure and in the reached support. The ordinary finish and start countings of the *same parity structure* both give 1/32 to each of its 32 allowed states. [Reached words and minimum-lift control](transfer_check.json) · [Canonical checks](listed_checks.json)

For a direct reader check, the first reader holds `0,1,3,4,5,6` and the second `7,8`. Fix the remaining distinction 2 at zero. Reading 0 and 7 gives the joint mass table `[[1/4,0],[0,1/4]]` before conditional normalization. No listed knot meets both readers. This is a failure of conditional independence. The older law supplies the transfer rule and the reference. Uniform starting weights and positive lift choices are declared here. The law does not prescribe their probabilities. [Transfer replay](transfer_check.py)

Two further scope checks separate this effect from other recorded choices. On the historical three-founder veto, 15 of the 16 even starting states gave the same five-birth history at the fixed allocation and lift. Conditioning on that history gave two separated readers a table proportional to `[[0,1],[1,1]]`. The one-founder transfer example needs no such selection. In the older fair-reveal counting, a fair choice among extendible values on `NAE(a,c,d), NAE(b,c,d)` fails a missing-pair determinant in 12 of 24 orders. Revealing `a,b,c,d` gives determinant −1/128 at `c=1,d=0`. These are two distinct three-place knots on the minimum four distinctions. The order and fair choices are supplied. ## 3. Narrowing — rank one tests the specified readers after the rest is fixed

“Otherwise every recorded weighting the model makes factors over knots, and the line stops.” [Preregistration, line 43](../preregistration.md)

A nonzero two-by-two determinant disproves a product over the declared scopes. Vanishing is equivalent to conditional independence of those **two individual distinctions** at each positive-mass assignment of the rest. For two complete readers, use their complete local states as row and column labels and test every two-by-two minor with the complement fixed.

A supplied control shows why the converse fails. Give two disjoint three-place even knots weight 1 on each of their 16 allowed joint states, and double the all-zero state. Every one of the 144 single-distinction tables passes, because fixing the other two distinctions in each knot fixes the third. The two complete readers have a minor `[[2,1],[1,1]]` before normalization. Passing can conceal a three-way dependence even when some tables are fully positive. On `NAE(a,b,p), NAE(b,c,q), NAE(c,a,r)`, double the weight at `abc=111`. All 78 tested tables pass, including 12 fully positive tables. Summing out the private distinctions and cancelling their completion counts would make the doubling factor a product on the three old pairs. Such a product has identical products on even and odd old triples, while this one gives 1 and 2. Conversely, a product on even knots `(a,m,n), (b,m,n)` makes `a=b` with certainty after the two shared distinctions are forgotten. [Rebuilt controls](controls.py) · [Counts](controls.json)

I **read** 4,303,444 tables per weighting in the original output, zero failed for either registered counting, and 1,476 failed in seven arm-histories for whole-growth division. The implementation counts assignments of the rest that occur in at least one positive state. Omitted all-zero tables have zero determinant. I did not regenerate the four-million-table tally. ## What survives at later rounds

On a fixed finite history whose growth choices ignore assigned values, the finish weighting is proportional to the product of all listed rule indicators. For a birth let $L_b$ be its rule indicator and $c_b$ the number of fresh assignments its own old inputs permit. Each fresh distinction belongs to one birth in its round and all inputs come from the preceding structure. Thus the round count is the product of the $c_b$, even when several births take the same **old** shared distinction. Starting uniformly on allowed states gives the start-rule indicators times one factor $L_b/c_b$ per birth. Give the factor value zero where $c_b=0$ and normalize surviving mass if positive. A round spent waiting contributes 1. The argument covers any finite number of such rounds, including counting from a fixed common stage, even or odd parity, not-all-equal, implied closures, cuts, and value-blind takes. Pooling different histories changes the question. ## Reproduction and limits

Run the Python files in this directory from the repository root using `.venv/bin/python -B`, one numerical thread, and `prlimit --as=2147483648 --` before the interpreter. For the later-round sample use `growth_replay.py --pilot`, then `growth_replay.py`. For the original sample use `whole_parity.py`, `whole_nae.py` and `cut_loss.py --starts 300`. For the eight-distinction determinant use `whole_nae.py --case 34 --rounds 1 --canonical`. The other exact checks are `transfer_check.py --old 4`, `feedback.py --states 16`, `controls.py --histories 4` and `listed_checks.py`. Each batch had a timed prefix at its own configuration. The 30-start loss pilot took 0.08 s, the one-start transfer pilot 0.02 s, and the six-round growth pilot less than a second. The full later-round replay took under two seconds. [Pilot](growth_pilot.json) · [Replay code](growth_replay.py)

To reproduce the support census through two funded rounds, add `--census --count 7075 --prefixes --rounds 2 --cap 14` to either whole-structure script. The narrower census files retain their exact caps. The canonical certificates record the checker arguments. [Smallest funded case](whole_parity_21.json) · [Verified-start case](whole_nae_3108.json)

**Not run.** Exhaustive state enumeration on the large histories, every census history at rounds 3–6, every allocation or take, and rounds beyond six for the later-round sample. Joint completion enumeration stopped at ten fresh distinctions per round and separate birth enumeration at seven. The bound-fact and event-ready-square studies and any test of a scoring advantage were outside this supplementary review.
