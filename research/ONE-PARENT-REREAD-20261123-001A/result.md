**With one law for every newborn, a stated growth law lifts eight parts with at most two holders per distinction from
exactly 2 to a hidden excess in one round. It does so only when the two births of one parent take different shared
distinctions, which the rule leaves open and neither fixed convention does.** Rule Q: as ordinary growth, except that a
one-parent birth takes, with its parent's loose distinction, one distinction its parent shares. A hidden excess is a
four-correlation score above 2 with both complete records unchanged by the far setting, under the supplied setting
births and with every allowed word of the finished structure counted equally. A part is a connected piece of one of
the 292 structures with a hidden excess.

- **One law for every newborn.** The eight parts gain at round 1 and keep the gain through round 3. In each, the one
  live knot makes two one-parent births, and the two births take different shared distinctions of it. When both take
  the same distinction, as under either convention, the score stays at exactly 2.
- **Laws handed out.** With one sibling not-all-equal and the other parity, all 27 parts that one added knot lifts
  gain at round 1, under any choice. More gains appear at rounds 2 and 3 in the same 27 parts.
- **Which parts.** Every part that gains sits at exactly 2 before the round. The 24 other parts at exactly 2 never
  pass 2, and the remaining 5,710 never pass 1.692.
- **The control.** With the shared input removed from the re-reading births and the laws kept, all 680 round-1 gains
  are lost.
- **Fresh distinctions.** Every re-reading birth inside a gain has exactly one fresh distinction. Elsewhere 64,947
  round-1 re-reading births got two to four, because the new meetings raise what the next round asks.

- **Growth is damped.** A re-reading birth gets one fresh distinction where an ordinary one-parent birth gets two, so
  most draws that grew without bound now make a bounded number of births each round.
- **Oldest-first builds one much-held distinction.** A child's oldest shared distinction is the one its parent re-read,
  so along a chain of one-parent births one distinction gains a holder every round: a common hub, in the foundation's
  word. Newest-first stops at six holders, and no draw's largest number grows after round 3.

## What this is about

About rule Q as worded, on these draws and parts. Supplied: the parts, which already hold the ties; the observers and
the setting births; the equal weighting of finished words; and the choice of which shared distinction each birth
takes, on which the one-law result depends. In the second column the law of each newborn is supplied as well. For
1,377 parts the mixed-arm null rests on 4,096 drawn assignments. Later rounds of a choice history use the oldest-first
convention.

**Verdict.** A law that re-reads where nothing is short does what rule R could not: with every newborn under one law
it grows a hidden excess from a two-holder part. It needs the two sibling births to take different distinctions, and
it reaches only parts that already sit at 2. As a growth law it is a strong change: it damps growth, and under one of
its conventions it makes a common hub.
