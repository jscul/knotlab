## The answer

The owner asked: "Is time itself a fold like intelligence/life through DNA?" The thread read that as a test a program
can decide: does the growth make a clock (a part that returns) and a second part whose own knots follow the clock's
ticks at a fixed number for each tick, and stop when the clock is barred?

**No. Under the laws run, no made part counts another's returns, and the study does not show that none can.**

1. **No count.** In 88,299 tests of a made clock and a second part wholly apart from it, the claim of a count fails,
   every test decided, under every law run.
2. **Under the two cheapest laws that let parts meet, no clock gives parts off at four ticks.** Under the first this
   is argued for every start, given the supplied rule that a birth's fresh distinctions are counted by its distinct
   inputs: a birth that takes uses one loose distinction and receives one, so a part's loose distinctions do not rise
   once the start knots have acted, and a part that gives parts off must gain one each time. Under the second it is
   seen only. With no taking rule all 227 census starts of the ring study keep such a clock; under the second law
   none does.
3. **A second part's course does depend on a clock at each of three returns, but only under supplied names.** That
   weaker reading holds in 78 tests, all under rules that take from a recorded ancestor and choose by name. On the
   two-knot start under the first law it holds in 80 of the 240 numberings of the start, exactly those in which the
   start's shared distinction is the least-named place of one knot. Every such case is lost under some renumbering,
   and in every one the second part's own knots grow from period to period, so there is no fixed number for a tick.
4. **The number of ticks a ring keeps under a rule that takes from an ancestor is the law's own delay.** Taking from
   k parent steps above cannot touch the ring's own births for 2k rounds; in no run did the ring return after those.
5. **Every period is measured in rounds, which are supplied.** Nothing here measures a period by anything the growth
   makes.

Sixteen predictions and the gates were judged. Fourteen hold, one is reported, one hand prediction fails (25 knots
fail the knot test where 2 were predicted; all are joins under parity, none in the run of a case). One conditional
run did not have its condition met.

## The three things kept apart, after the run

- **An order** (a knot comes after its parents) is held by the record of births. The sister study closed the same day
  shows it can be written into a knot's own places by a ration, and not read off the structure without one.
- **A clock** (a part that returns) is made under some laws and not under the cheapest laws that let parts meet.
- **A made count of returns** was not found. So, on this record, time in the sense of one pattern counting another's
  returns is not a fold of emergence that the growth has made; whether a law exists under which it is, is open.

## Limits

- Everything is finite: twelve named starts and 227 census starts, 18 rounds, the registered family of rules.
- **About half of the main run was not run**: 5,116 of 10,116 runs, in the registered order's tail (the fresh count by
  parents, demand before the takes, and two further groups in part). The run was cut at the hour by a rule recorded
  before it; what was not run is not a negative. The primary laws and the whole of the first group were run.
- 148 tests of the weaker reading are undecided (the masked run does not reproduce the difference).
- One program; its gates are against two earlier engines, and the thread recounted the main run's verdicts from the
  raw records. No second program.
- The count was asked in one decidable form (a fixed number of own knots for each tick, over four periods). A count
  kept in another form (for instance in which distinctions are shared) is not tested.
