**The bench** (three patterns, 2,441 cases with the later passes).

- Joined so that each can afford the join (a meeting), under the ordinary count, no knot is ever short, and so a
  pattern's own knots cannot feel the other at all: they are made exactly as with a stub there. Everything the two
  have to do with each other is in the knots that descend from both.
- Joined by a bare shared distinction, knots are short at once and each pattern's own births do depend on the
  other; every such effect is first seen by round 3.
- Under a cap of three, "just enough for three" and the eight rations that hand a parent's owing on, the joins
  between the two stop within five rounds (two for the last eight).

**The census** (990 pairs of starts of one to three knots, 17,809 cases). Of 101,994 marks, a start's own knots
first hear 6,706 in round 1, 3,988 in round 2, 1,550 in round 3, 246 in rounds 4 to 6, and none later. The reason
under the ordinary count is simple: a change reaches a pattern's own knots only through a cancelled join, a
cancelled join needs a short knot, and the ordinary count gives every new knot what it is owed.

**One thing the toy showed me.** Two two-knot parts sharing a distinction make 2, 4, 6, … knots of both a round,
n(n + 1) after n rounds, each at a pair of places along the two parts' own lines, with its parents one place
back: a table of the two lines. That is the record's own mechanism (two touching knots grow a pattern of dimension
two), not a new result, but it says what "making something together" is here: each lends the other a line to
count along. Three parts give 1, 3, 6, 10, … a round. A mark on one part does not stay at a place of the table; it
gives another table.
