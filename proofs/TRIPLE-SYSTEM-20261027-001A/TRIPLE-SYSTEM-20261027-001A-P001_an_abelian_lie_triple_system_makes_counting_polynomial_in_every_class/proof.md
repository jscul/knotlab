# An abelian Lie triple system makes counting polynomial in every class

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

**Setting.**
- p is an odd prime and N is a finite p-group of exponent p and nilpotency class c < p.
- θ is an automorphism of N of order 2, and G = N ⋊ ⟨θ⟩.
- **The Lie algebra.** 𝔫 = log N is the Lie algebra of N over F_p, by the Lazard correspondence. θ acts on it as an involution,
  so 𝔫 = 𝔫⁺ ⊕ 𝔫⁻ with [𝔫^±, 𝔫^±] ⊆ 𝔫⁺ and [𝔫⁺, 𝔫⁻] ⊆ 𝔫⁻.
- **The triple system.** The −1 part 𝔫⁻ with the product [[a, b], c] is a Lie triple system. It is **abelian** if
  [[𝔫⁻, 𝔫⁻], 𝔫⁻] = 0.
- **The cochains.** For a group π and ψ: π → C₂, F_p^ψ is F_p with g acting by (−1)^{ψ(g)}, and Z¹(π; F_p^ψ) denotes its twisted
  1-cocycles.

**Lemma 1 (odd words).** The following are equivalent:
- (i) [[𝔫⁻, 𝔫⁻], 𝔫⁻] = 0;
- (ii) every Lie word of odd degree at least 3 in elements of 𝔫⁻ vanishes;
- (iii) the subalgebra 𝔤 generated by 𝔫⁻ is 𝔫⁻ ⊕ Z with Z = [𝔫⁻, 𝔫⁻] ⊆ 𝔫⁺ central in 𝔤.

**Theorem 2 (rigid count).**
- **Hypotheses.** π has H¹(π; F_p) = 0 and H²(π; F_p) = 0, ψ: π → C₂ is any homomorphism, and the triple system of 𝔫⁻ is abelian.
- **The count.**

  |Hom_ψ(π, G)| = |Z¹(π; F_p^ψ)|^{dim 𝔫⁻}.

- **Rigidity.** Every homomorphism over ψ lands in the class-2 subgroup exp(𝔤) ⋊ ⟨θ⟩. It is determined by its image in
  𝔫⁻ ⋊ ⟨−1⟩, which is an 𝔫⁻-valued twisted cocycle.

**Corollary 3 (polynomial counting).** Let M be a closed oriented 3-manifold with H₁(M; F_p) = 0. Then

  |Hom(π₁M, G)| = Σ_{ψ ∈ Hom(H₁(M), Z/2)} p^{(dim Z¹(π₁M; F_p^ψ)) · dim 𝔫⁻},

and dim Z¹ is linear algebra over F_p. So for an abelian triple system, counting maps to G is polynomial on closed 3-manifolds with
H₁(M; F_p) = 0 and 2-rank of H₁ bounded, in every class c < p. With H₁ = Z/2 the count is 1 + p^{(N+1) dim 𝔫⁻}, where
N = dim H¹(M; F_p^ψ).

**Examples.**
- Every class-2 algebra: [[𝔫⁻, 𝔫⁻], 𝔫⁻] ⊆ γ₃ = 0. This recovers the r = 2 case of `CLASSK-DICHOTOMY-20261026-001C`.
- The class-3 cross-layer algebra of `BEYOND-CLASS2-20261027-001A` at r = 2. Here x is trivial and y is −1. Its triple system is
  abelian, so it is polynomial at r = 2, although at r > 2 it is hard.
- The class-4 algebra on x (+) and y (−) modulo [y, [y, [x, y]]].
- **Non-abelian.** The four-way algebra u₄ with D = diag(1, −1, 1, −1), where [[E₁₂, E₂₃], E₃₄] = E₁₄; free class 3 on two −1
  generators; free class 4 on x (+) and y (−). Ticket C treats these.

**What it says.**
- At r = 2, with no F_p-homology, the +1 part is rigid (no classes, no obstructions).
- A homomorphism is then built from −1 classes by brackets. If no bracket of three −1 elements survives, nothing can obstruct, and
  the count is a pure dimension formula in every nilpotency class.
- This is the polynomial half of the conjectured dichotomy, now proved in full generality.

## Nearest known results

- **This program.** The class-(K) dichotomy (`CLASSK-DICHOTOMY-20261026-001C`) is the class-2 case, at every r.
- **Samperton–Weiss (arXiv:2602.02885).**
  - Their positive result: class-2 nilpotent targets are polynomial with bounded H².
  - The reason in both is obstruction theory on a 3-manifold whose relevant cohomology is controlled.
  - Here the argument is shorter: the +1 part is rigid and the −1 part lifts freely through a central extension.
- **Lie triple systems** (Jacobson; symmetric pairs). Standard.
- **Novelty.**
  - The counting statement for nilpotent-by-involution targets of every class, with the triple system as criterion, was not found.
  - Folklore risk: medium. The proof is a few lines of nonabelian cohomology once the subalgebra 𝔤 is noticed.

## Proof

### 1. Lemma 1

- **(i) ⇒ (ii).** Prove two statements together, by induction on d ≥ 2:
  - (a_d): every odd word of degree d + 1 vanishes;
  - (b_d): [e, c] = 0 for every even word e of degree d and every c ∈ 𝔫⁻.
  - **Base.** For d = 2, e = [a, b], and (b₂) is (i). (a₂) follows: a word of degree 3 is ±[[·, ·], ·].
  - **Step for (b_d), d even and at least 4.** Write e = [u, v].
    - If u and v are both odd, one has degree at least 3 and vanishes by (a_{d′}), d′ < d.
    - If both are even, then by Jacobi [[u, v], c] = [u, [v, c]] − [v, [u, c]] = 0 by (b) in lower degree.
  - **Step for (a_d).** An odd word of degree d + 1 is [u, v] with one part odd and one even.
    - An odd part of degree at least 3 vanishes by induction.
    - Otherwise the word is ±[e, c] with e even of degree d, and (b_d) applies.
- **(ii) ⇒ (iii).**
  - 𝔤 is spanned by Lie words in 𝔫⁻.
  - The odd ones of degree at least 3 vanish. The even ones of degree at least 4 also vanish: they are [u, v] with u, v odd (one
    of degree at least 3) or both even, and [e₁, [a, b]] = [[e₁, a], b] + [a, [e₁, b]] = 0 by (b).
  - So 𝔤 = 𝔫⁻ ⊕ Z, the sum being direct because the θ-signs differ. Z is central in 𝔤 by (b) and since [Z, Z] = 0.
- **(iii) ⇒ (i)** is immediate. ∎

### 2. Theorem 2

Write Z¹_± := Z¹(π; F_p^{±ψ}). The hypotheses give Z¹(π; F_p) = Hom(π, F_p) = 0.

- **Lower bound.**
  - 𝔤 is a θ-stable subalgebra, so H = exp(𝔤) is a θ-stable subgroup of N. Z is fixed pointwise by θ and central in 𝔤, so exp(Z)
    is central in H ⋊ ⟨θ⟩.
  - The quotient (H ⋊ ⟨θ⟩)/exp(Z) is 𝔫⁻ ⋊ ⟨−1⟩: an abelian Lie algebra exponentiates to a vector group, with θ acting by −1.
  - Homomorphisms π → 𝔫⁻ ⋊ ⟨−1⟩ over ψ are exactly the twisted cocycles Z¹(π; F_p^ψ) ⊗ 𝔫⁻.
  - Each one lifts through the central extension by exp(Z), on which π acts trivially: the obstruction lies in H²(π; Z) = 0. The
    lift is unique: lifts form a torsor under H¹(π; Z) = 0.
  - So |Hom_ψ(π, H ⋊ ⟨θ⟩)| = |Z¹(π; F_p^ψ)|^{dim 𝔫⁻}, and these are homomorphisms into G.
- **Upper bound.**
  - The lower central series γ_k of N is θ-stable, and L_k = γ_k/γ_{k+1} is central in N/γ_{k+1}.
  - The fibres of Hom_ψ(π, (N/γ_{k+1}) ⋊ ⟨θ⟩) → Hom_ψ(π, (N/γ_k) ⋊ ⟨θ⟩) are empty or torsors under Z¹(π; L_k), where π acts
    through ψ and θ.
  - Split L_k = L_k⁺ ⊕ L_k⁻ (p odd). Then |Z¹(π; L_k)| = |Z¹(π; F_p)|^{dim L_k⁺} |Z¹(π; F_p^ψ)|^{dim L_k⁻}
    = |Z¹(π; F_p^ψ)|^{dim L_k⁻}.
  - The filtration is θ-stable, so Σ_k dim L_k⁻ = dim 𝔫⁻. Hence |Hom_ψ(π, G)| ≤ |Z¹(π; F_p^ψ)|^{dim 𝔫⁻}.
- **Conclusion.** The bounds agree. So every homomorphism over ψ lies in H ⋊ ⟨θ⟩, and the count is as stated. ∎

### 3. Corollary 3

- **The hypotheses hold.** For M closed and oriented with H₁(M; F_p) = 0:
  - H¹(π₁M; F_p) = Hom(H₁(M), F_p) = 0;
  - H²(π₁M; F_p) injects into H²(M; F_p) ≅ H₁(M; F_p) = 0 (Hopf).
- **Sum over sectors.** Every homomorphism has a C₂-part ψ ∈ Hom(H₁, Z/2). Theorem 2 counts each sector; ψ = 0 gives the single
  trivial homomorphism into the p-group N.
- **Polynomial time.** dim Z¹(π; F_p^ψ) is the corank of a twisted Fox matrix, computable in polynomial time from a presentation,
  and there are 2^{rank₂ H₁} sectors.
- **H₁ = Z/2.** |Z¹(π; F_p^ψ)| = p^{N+1}. ∎

## What `check.py` verifies

1. **Lemma 1** on free nilpotent algebras with prescribed generator signs (classes 3 to 5) and on the examples. Where the triple
   system vanishes, every odd word up to degree 5 vanishes too, and 𝔤 = 𝔫⁻ ⊕ Z has Z central.
2. **Independent counts.** Ticket A's direct layered count (BCH group law, Wirtinger presentations) for the class-3 cross-layer
   algebra at r = 2 equals |Z¹|^{dim 𝔫⁻} = p^{3(N+1)} in sector 1, and 1 in sector 0. This holds on 2-surgeries of 4_1, T(2,5) and
   their connected sums at p = 5. It also holds on the surgery with a cabled [[A₁₂, A₂₃], A₃₄] insertion, where the four-way group
   is obstructed.
3. **Class-4 example.** On gadget surgeries the class-4 algebra x (+), y (−) modulo [y, [y, [x, y]]] never obstructs: random free
   data at all layers lift, using the general braid model. The full class-4 algebra does obstruct on the same surgeries.
