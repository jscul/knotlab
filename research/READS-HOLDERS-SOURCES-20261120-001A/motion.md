With the supplied signs on real weightings, every allowed parity change together with the knot reads fixes one line of weightings. After the first growth of either bare ring, its archived shortest changes allow two independently variable values, one on each class of assignments. With the supplied probability rule these weightings can give different answers to a change read, although their ordinary assignment probabilities and motion records agree. The fixed physical stages and the motion guards below come from the two earlier motion studies. Each is a built control. ## Two meanings of least

With the supplied read on a set $z$ and the change of a set $t$, the ordinary basis word $x$ goes to the same word $x+t$ in either order. Reading first gives sign $(-1)^{z\cdot x}$. Reading second gives $(-1)^{z\cdot x}(-1)^{z\cdot t}$. Thus every odd overlap reverses the sign, independently of the assignment. Arithmetic in the exponent is modulo two.

With even parity at every knot, let $S$ be the legal assignments. A weighting fixed by all knot reads vanishes off $S$, since a violated read reverses its value there. Within $S$, a weighting fixed by a group $H$ of changes is exactly a constant on each set $x+H$. Its dimension is the number of those sets. Taking every legal translation gives $H=S$ and a one-dimensional space, including zero. The positive unit weighting is unique. The other unit representative has the opposite overall sign. An allowed nonempty change contains an inclusion-minimal one. Removing its distinctions leaves an allowed smaller change, so induction shows all inclusion-minimal changes generate $S$. This proof also covers any selection of distinctions pinned at zero by additional reads.

With two loose distinctions at each source knot, their pair is a shortest change. Each of the $n$ original shared distinctions has a three-distinction change using one loose distinction at each holder. Those $2n$ choices are independent and span the $2n$ available bits. Pinning the loose distinctions leaves just the change of the entire ring, which is then shortest through each shared distinction.

After one ordinary growth the $n$ four-distinction changes of the earlier study each use two consecutive old shared distinctions and two different parent-to-child distinctions. With loose values pinned they are independent. The old odd ring supplies the missing bit. With loose values free, the shortest changes also include the $n$ pairs of loose distinctions within new knots and the changes that use two parent-to-child distinctions at one old knot and a loose distinction at each of the two children. The $n$ latter choices, one per old knot, are independent on the parent-to-child distinctions. The $n$ loose pairs are independent of them. Projecting the ordinary four-changes onto the original shared distinctions supplies $n-1$ further independent changes, all even there. Every shortest change has even overlap with the old ring. The full odd ring is itself allowed, giving rank $3n$ altogether and rank $3n-1$ for the shortest changes. In particular the positive indicator of $S$ and that indicator multiplied by the sign of the old ring read are independent fixed weightings for the shortest changes. ## One-to-one events and a restored mediator

With the **unconditional allowed change** of a specified set, applying it twice returns each state. For an event enabled on the set $D$, the firing $x\mapsto x+t$ is one-to-one from $D$ onto $D+t$. Counting disabled attempts as idle gives a permutation of all allowed words precisely when $D+t=D$ for nonzero $t$. If an enabled input reaches a disabled input, both have the same image under this total rule. The literal 31-knot traveler supplies just such a collision on its first four-distinction event. Restricting the unique next firing to either of its two oriented 124-state cycles does give a permutation of that cycle. The physical assignments and event names in the collision are in the receipt. With the **archived swap** of the two loose values on the three-place knot meeting a four-place knot, 8 of 16 parity words moved and all 16 images were distinct. Under not-all-equal, 28 of 42 moved and all 42 images were distinct. For the other archived rule, which toggles two loose values in a four-place knot when an occupied shared distinction meets a five-place neighbor, 384 of 512 parity assignments moved and all images were allowed. Applying that rule to the same three holders under not-all-equal sends 225 of 3,150 allowed words outside the relation. In each parity case the rule undoes itself. With the **uniform positive weighting on all allowed assignments**, every permutation of them leaves the weighting itself unchanged. On the two-knot swap source, take physical holdings $\{0,1,2\}$ and $\{0,3,4,5\}$. The split parity readers $\{1\}$ and $\{0,2\}$ have a joint fixed read on $0,1,2$ and joint fixed change on $1,2$, which anticommute locally. For readers $\{1,2\}$ and $\{3,4,5\}$, both values of the unread distinction 0 give product supports under both laws. Exhausting the supported reads and changes found zero anticommuting fixed pairs there, before and after the swap. In general, with no holder meeting both readers, fixing all unread ordinary distinctions gives a product of allowed sets at the two readers. Their positive weighting factors in each case. Forgetting the unread values gives a mixture of products. Thus a permutation of the full allowed set cannot deliver a split tie to separated readers in this preparation, including when every intervening distinction is restored.

## What response probabilities motion determines

With **two supplied weightings of opposite relative sign**, multiply the uniform allowed weighting by $(-1)^{x_1}$ on the same two-knot source. Every ordinary input and every input/output pair of the archived swap retains exactly the same squared mass on all 16 parity assignments and on all 42 not-all-equal assignments. Reading the change of $1,2$ under parity gives $+1$ with certainty on the original weighting and $-1$ with certainty on the altered weighting. Under not-all-equal the global change of all six distinctions gives the same certainty reversal. Reading the change of $1,2$ there yields $5/6$ versus $1/6$ for $+1$, even though this change can leave the allowed set. The values follow from the supplied squared norms of the two outcome parts. The ordinary motion response cannot determine their probabilities because it contains no relative sign between distinct allowed assignments.
