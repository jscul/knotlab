# What an earlier receipt and a second send can change in a successor

Under the ordinary complete funding rule, two histories that first differ on already-paid original sender marks admit the same continuations at every later knot disjoint from those marks. In the recorded preparation each immediate successor nevertheless holds one real bit of the earlier receipt. A different source with a still-private pair can send another bit. What one later knot can read depends on the preparation and on whether it can tell its two inputs apart.

## Earlier result, new claim and observations

In ordinary mathematical terms, each funded extension of a finite family of sets appends new sets containing previously singly held elements and fresh elements. All existing assignments extend. The previous [fixed-holder proof](../../REPEATED-SENDS-20261122-001B/REPEATED-SENDS-20261122-001B-P001_fixed_holder/proof.md) already identifies the two original spent coordinates and proves constancy of a fixed set after payment. Here the new objects of observation are *future* sets and the fields they can simultaneously preserve. The [frozen successor definition](../../../research/SUCCESSOR-RECEIPT-20261122-001A/preregistration.md) and [reviewed probability supplement](../../../research/SUCCESSOR-RECEIPT-20261122-001B/preregistration.md) specify the receiver and weighting.

At each new holder the value and sharing histories of the two incoming distinctions can be followed, but the roles of the two parents are anonymous. A single simultaneous permutation of the two incoming tracks preserves the observation. Own new distinctions retain their physical slots until their own local payment. A stronger read has a supplied association of incoming tracks to parent roles. A numeral identifying a holder or a parent step occurs only in the audit.

## Coupling of complete continuations of the original sender

Fix the entire reached history through the first receiver’s finishing payment. Its three-bit word is W. Compare holding the source marks in place with transposing their present unequal values while fixing every other value and every holding. Before the first source payment, either mark is private to the same source. At the later request each is held by the original source and by a different first child. On the recorded ordinary source this original private transposition is unavailable. On parity, a proposed late swap would flip one bit at each of the two separate children and violate both constraints. On not-all-equal, an extra whole-word guard admits the swap exactly if the other entries at each affected child already contain both values. When that fails, the supplied control continues unchanged with an unannounced refusal. These source facts were already established in the [repeated-sends study](../../../research/REPEATED-SENDS-20261122-001/decision.md).

On a complete pre-offer word that admits the transposition, compare the two actual interventions. After them, all physical holdings and all values except the two spent marks agree. The ordinary group and grant rules inspect holdings. A funded cut can take only a loose distinction and so cannot take either spent mark. It follows that the identical next births have identical paid inputs. For every newborn, choose the same fresh word on both sides. Exactly the same words are allowed on each side, even at an implied closing birth with no grant. This pairs all complete next states in both directions. A stall preserves the same agreement. Induction gives a correspondence on every finite continuation in which every newborn’s entire word and its ordinary payments agree. Earlier marks remain physically present where their initial children test them. The argument covers deeper successors and reunions of ordinary payments. It excludes reads of the original marks or their aliases, acceptance announcements and additional writers.

For the B307 weighting, every pair of corresponding fresh completions has the same conditional probability given the actual paid inputs. Hence the correspondence preserves probability, including any dependence due to shared parents. A matched unannounced refusal also leaves the continuation unchanged. With the second request chosen at equal odds independently after the fixed prefix, its best identification from any of these successor records is 1/2. On parity there is only one physical post-payment endpoint for the two requests. On not-all-equal two lawful physical endpoints can really exist on the **same** prefix. Whether the word happens to admit a swap may be correlated with W, which by itself cannot establish reception of the second intervention. Equality of support uses no weighting. The probability claim uses the specified local birth kernel. ## Exactly what the immediate successor retains

The three slots 1754, 1755 and 1756 of the first receiver 294 are paid to **three different** children, 631, 633 and 634. Each inherits one specified bit x of W and an ordinary bit u from a different parent. Its own bank contains respectively three, four and three new marks. Its later local finish is a real payment of that bank. No inherited mark is repaid by this event. The two incoming tracks have distinct partners and their values and sharing stay fixed. The new marks acquire other holders distinct from those parents. Thus the pattern of partners does not name the origin of an incoming bit.

For each child, under either early source action on the same complete original word, **all sixteen** pairs (W,u) have positive mass. This is verified by exact elimination of the actual contributing birth words, on the intact course and on the diversion and its naming control. Given x,u, the entire new bank of this child depends only on those two values. The two orders of a mixed pair admit the same new words with the same weights. Consequently, even the strongest local oriented read narrows W to precisely the four words with its paid bit x. For the anonymous read an equal pair certifies x, leaving four earlier words. A mixed pair leaves all eight. The local finish and full bank do not discriminate among them further. The collector of all three children could physically combine the three bits, but is a different instrument. Both early sender choices allow every individual complete read. ## A genuinely later independent action on a different live source

The frozen rule selects a fourth-cut child G=1595 paid by H=631 and K=632. H transmits its own fresh distinction h=4381. The original W bit x=1754 is already shared and cannot be paid again. The other source K acts only after R has finished, before the fourth cut. Its q=4384 and spare 4383 are then private to K. On the supplied ready word 01, keeping the pair pays q=0 and exchanging it pays q=1. Both are lawful on the *same* complete state since their holder sets agree. A new source choice and the two distinguishable interventions are supplied. The fourth-cut birth, its two physical payments and G's following local finish arise under ordinary growth. G comes into existence with both h and q. It can test them in its own state without observing the older receipt as an earlier event in its own life.

With only K ready, either value of h is possible at each x. The two claims “both are possible” and “both receive equal weight” have different content. For parity, H has further free fresh bits and h is fair at either x. Under not-all-equal the chance of h depends on the actual incoming values. Exact weights are given below.

Thus a role-addressed G reading h and q knows (x,second action) on every prepared parity word. Under not-all-equal h=0 certifies x=1, while h=1 allows either x. Under the anonymous read, G sees the two incoming values with exchangeable roles. In the parity control two zeros certify (0,keep) and two ones certify (1,exchange). In the not-all-equal control only two zeros certify the whole pair (1,keep). All globally possible opposite explanations of the other cases remain. G's fresh word has the same law when its two inputs are interchanged. The native local funding event depends on the holdings alone. Neither supplies an incoming role or the missing two bits of W.

Here is a check on why the ready pair matters. If K's unprepared pair is instead drawn from its ordinary conditional weighting, interchanging its two assignments preserves K's full fresh-word law at every fixed incoming word. H is a different newborn. Its values and W can stay fixed under this bijection. This remains true if the only information about K is that the two bits differ and their orientation is unavailable. Consequently all later observation probabilities for keep and exchange agree in this comparison. A particular exchange can still change an individual run. This symmetry is a deductive consequence of the weighting, independent of the numerical results for the two frozen ready cohorts. ## Exact probabilities and readiness

For a birth of k fresh bits, suppose that m specified slots have been assigned. Under even parity the marginal for any partial fresh word with m<k is 2 to the power −m, since half of its completions meet the constraint. If all slots are specified, use the actual parity. Under not-all-equal a mixed paid word admits all complete fresh words. Homogeneous paid input of value b excludes the all-b completion. Thus the numerator for the specified assignment is the number of all completions minus one exactly when every assigned slot equals b. The denominator is the total number of admissible fresh words for the given paid inputs. For each target, sum these factors over every relevant parent’s word **once**, marginalizing values after their final use. Readiness is a constraint on that same original process before the second action is assigned equal odds.
