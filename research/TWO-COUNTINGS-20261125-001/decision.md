## The answer

The direction asked which weighting "it's random" means. The owner answered that his "random" means not chosen, and
that between the two countings the thread may take whichever is more interesting and record the choice as reversible.
The study then asked what each counting gives and whether anything in the growth decides between them.

1. **Both countings give weight to the same configurations; they differ in how much.** Counting from the finish
   counts every allowed state of the finished structure once. Counting from the start counts every allowed state of
   the start once and, at each birth, shares an old state's weight equally among its completions. The weight is our
   rule; the growth makes none.
2. **Under parity they are one counting; under not-all-equal they part at once.** Along histories in which every
   parity birth brings a fresh distinction the two agree at every stage (the record's theorem). Under not-all-equal,
   on 100 census starts under six laws, they parted by round 3 on every growth that makes a birth except two under
   the ring law.
3. **On the settings line the excess belongs to counting from the finish.** Counted from any common stage before the
   setting knots, every registered setting test scores exactly 2 with both records hidden: 1,816 verdicts on the
   hand-built structures and the 894 grown records of two earlier censuses, and all 512 sign variants of the 11-rule
   structure. Counted from the finish every recorded score above 2 is reproduced (20/9, 19/9, 37/18, 61/30, 55/27,
   109/54 and the others). A lemma says why, under five stated hypotheses (argued, not refereed); seven built
   examples each break it and score above 2 or change a record, though they do not isolate two of the hypotheses.
4. **Supplied orders of construction can put an excess back under counting from the start.** Building a finished
   structure from nothing in a supplied order, the score stays at 2 in every order in which each setting knot brings
   only its own fresh distinction (104, 92 and 133 orders of three lists of 400), and passes 2 in some of the others
   (up to 104/45). The orders are ours; no law made them.
5. **No run decides between the countings.** Every law here uses no value, so it makes the same structures under
   either counting; nothing a growth makes can depend on which one we use. The excess is what the thread's working
   choice produces. It is not evidence for the choice.
6. **Some rules of the settings line's structures are not knots.** Tested in the whole structure, two rules of the
   11-rule structure, one of the 8-rule structure, and one setting knot of the registered smallest gain at one of its
   two settings fail the knot test; each passes on its own. Two programs and the checker agree on all 33 registered
   structures. The scores stand as arithmetic. An excess also stands on structures whose every rule is a knot: the
   7-knot, the pencil 9-knot and the 18-knot structures, and, new here, two seven-rule parts of the rule-Q census that
   give the same 61/30 as the registered smallest gain with every rule a knot in the whole (checked by the canonical
   command on all 176 rules of their finished structures).

Eight predictions were judged, one of them in two halves. All hold; one is vacuous as registered (no two of 194 types
