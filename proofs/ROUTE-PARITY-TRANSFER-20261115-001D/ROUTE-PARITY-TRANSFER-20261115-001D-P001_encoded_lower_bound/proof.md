# Proof length on listed and fixed two-use routes

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

Let \(F_n\) be a list of \(8n\) independent uniformly sampled strict three-literal clauses, with three distinct variables per clause and signs chosen uniformly. A clause may occur more than once. The decision input specifies every private guard. Hidden middle and padding names each occur in at most two positions of their own kind. We count the nodes of a directed acyclic parity-resolution refutation, allowing disjunctions of arbitrary affine Boolean equations, complementary resolution and one-premise semantic weakening. Each earlier node may be used repeatedly. The empty clause is the conclusion.

**Theorem.** With probability tending to one as \(n\) grows, every such refutation of the pinned route formula for \(F_n\) has \(\exp(\Omega(n))\) nodes. If \(r\) counts stages, the bound is \(\exp(\Omega(r))\) for the schedule compiled from the list and \(\exp(\Omega(r^{1/3}))\) for the fixed schedule per \(n\). The latter probability uses the switches induced by drawing the original list.

## Nearest known result

Pang gives the random strict-clause lower bound. The predecessor route construction gives ordinary SAT equivalence. Combining them requires transporting proofs through the newly introduced hidden variables and pins. ## Proof

The axiom substitution for the actual guarded formula and its pin units sends each axiom either to a tautology or to a selected source clause. A parity cut may remain a cut, and if one premise turns tautological, the remaining premise can imply the projected conclusion. The full proof transformation handles affine cancellation and reuse of earlier nodes. Applied to any \(S\)-node target refutation it yields a source refutation in the local calculus on at most \(S\) nodes with no greater resolution depth. This holds without knowing how the proof was found. [Axiom projection](../../ROUTE-PARITY-TRANSFER-20261115-001A/ROUTE-PARITY-TRANSFER-20261115-001A-P001_literal_projection/proof.md) · [DAG transformation](../../ROUTE-PARITY-TRANSFER-20261115-001B/ROUTE-PARITY-TRANSFER-20261115-001B-P001_dag_transport/proof.md)

The admitted external input is Pang v2, Theorem 7.2(a), at characteristic two, degree one, width three and density eight. Its lower bound, with probability tending to one, is exponential in the number of source variables. The local convention permitting semantic weakening has a polynomial simulation in the syntactic system in **nodes** even for arbitrarily long equation lists. An \(S\)-node source proof costs at most \(O((n+1)^3 S)\) syntactic nodes by that simulation. Thus a source lower bound \(e^{c n}\) forces \(S\ge e^{c n}/O((n+1)^3)=e^{\Omega(n)}\). This is a corollary of a preprint and the local transport arguments. The source lower bound has not been independently re-proved here. The chance of satisfiability is also at most \(2^n(7/8)^{8n}\), by counting satisfying assignments in expectation. [Admission and convention proof](../../ROUTE-PARITY-TRANSFER-20261115-001C/ROUTE-PARITY-TRANSFER-20261115-001C-P001_source_admission/proof.md) · [Pang v2, §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00837v2#S7)

For a physical catalogue of \(m\) clause occurrences, including unswitched slots, the compiler creates \(h=m+2n\) disjunctions on \(v=2n+2\) signed signals including the terminals. It uses \(r=4h+2v+4=4m+12n+8\) stages, \(32(r+1)\) strict ternary clauses and \(24(r+1)\) specified guard values. Pins are supplied with the instance and are unit axioms in the refutation. Listed wiring has \(m=8n\) and \(r=44n+8\). For fixed wiring, prepare the catalogue of all \(8\binom n3\) signed clauses and turn on precisely the slots present in the sampled list. Repeated axioms can cite the same line: replacing every citation to a duplicate by a citation to its representative preserves the proof node for node in either direction. Consequently the lower bound transfers through this induced selector distribution. Here \(r=32\binom n3+12n+8\), giving the two stated powers of \(r\). In either form the number of declared names is \(\Theta(r+1)\) for explicit guarded inputs. ∎

For a literal listing with all clauses and pins, and name fields of \(\Theta(\log(r+2))\) bits, the input has \(L=\Theta((r+1)\log(r+2))\) bits. The listed-wiring lower bound then reads \(\exp(\Omega(L/\log L))\), and the fixed-wiring bound reads \(\exp(\Omega((L/\log L)^{1/3}))\). If the fixed wiring is implicit and the input is its raw guard vector, that vector has \(\Theta(r+1)\) bits. Charging a more succinct specification requires its own conversion. The result bounds the output length of an explicit contradiction proof in this system, hence also the work to output one node at a time. It establishes no lower bound on parallel depth or on an algorithm that may decide satisfiability by other means.

**End-to-end checks.** The checker obtains a learned-clause trace from Glucose on the raw formula plus every pin. For each added clause it reconstructs a conflict by unit propagation under the negated clause, resolves backward along its reasons and keeps only ancestors of the empty clause. It incorporates a cut on the parity of two distinct signal variables at an intermediate clause where both parity values still occur under falsification. It verifies the full target proof, translates it, and checks every source inference again by exhaustive evaluation on source assignments. A three-variable listed contradiction and a four-variable fixed catalogue exercise both modes. These small examples test the transport mechanism.
