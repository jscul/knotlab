# Five- and six-place junctions under the mixed parity rule

**Standard statement.** For a finite reachable binary graphic cut space, a coordinate is a distinction. The observation is the pairwise minimum cocircuit cardinality \(K\) minus one, its bond distance \(D\). A single coordinate bijection preserves a threshold if every value below or at that threshold and the pooled symbol above it agree. We ask whether it preserves eligibility of an unordered marked triple. We prove exact recovery against an unrestricted rival for the source classes below. We also obtain an exact uniform bound on the separation between members of any eligible group. The general mixed-source comparison remains open.

**Nearest known result.** The earlier arbitrary-rival theorem covered sources of maximum ordinary arity four. A separate theorem for three fresh distinctions at each join recovered high junctions when the rival also followed that fixed-output law. Here both possible fresh counts are permitted in the rival throughout. A competing presentation can use a different number of junctions or a different growth history. ## What the reader can recover

Start with ternary even parity. An addition uses one loose distinction at each of one, two or three distinct parents and supplies two or three new distinctions. Every pair of parents shares an ordinary neighbor. For three parents no one ordinary neighbor may meet them all. The reader receives only the pair table on all current distinctions and the marked group. Every statement about input history below is a hypothesis on a possible source. It is never data given to the reader.

| Hypothesis on one compatible source | Conclusion for every compatible rival |
| --- | --- |
| Only the seed has three places, all four-place sites are unary, and every bond of size at most six surrounds at most two ordinary sites | Distances through five determine all junctions, the states and every next group. This includes every all-three-output source. [Proof and reader](high_inverse.md) |
| All additions make three fresh distinctions except at most five three-parent joins that make two, anywhere in the presentation | The same recovery holds. It also holds with arbitrarily many such exceptions if no two meet or have an ordinary neighbor in common. [Five without spacing](five_exceptions.md) · [Arbitrarily many separated sites](high_inverse.md) |
| A finite three-dimensional arrangement contains all the coordinate predecessors of each of its sites. Any three-parent join may omit one still-unused positive direction | The same recovery holds, with arbitrary numbers of adjacent two-output joins. The competitor may belong to any reachable family. [Proof for every finite shape of this kind](coordinate_growth.md) |
| The only two-output join uses two parents both born unary on the same seed or on the same five- or six-place junction | The same recovery holds even though pairs of outputs alone can fail to identify a large junction. [Proof using an input and an output](high_inverse.md#a-binary-exception-whose-two-parents-started-at-the-same-high-site) |
| A prefix grows with only one- or two-parent additions, each making two fresh distinctions, followed by a single five- or six-place junction | Distances through five determine every three-parent verdict, including fresh marks, against all rivals. The first theorem works by forcing a short parity equation at a time. In each large star, two appropriately chosen distinctions have their first common bond at exactly that star's size. No outside distinction is close enough to both to replace a member of the bond. A rival must then contain that entire equation. The proof goes in the other direction too. A shortest hypothetical extra five- or six-bit equation would have too many mutually close pairs to be supported at the small sites of the source. Equality of equation spaces and the short-bond classification identify the actual junctions. The distance algorithm removes cross-pairs already explained by two adjoining smaller sites, then recovers same-junction groups from the remaining pair links. All steps commute with arbitrary renaming. [Two-way proof](high_inverse.md)

In the coordinate family, seven is the minimum size of a boundary enclosing at least three sites. For a large set, count incoming and outgoing distinctions separately in successive layers. Three occupied layers and a crossing between layers already require seven distinctions. If there is no such crossing the horizontal count rises. A set occupying at most two layers in each direction has at most eight sites, where a degree sum gives the same bound. Thus all bonds visible through distance five are actual junctions or sums from two adjacent junctions. This remains true when two-output choices are made next to one another as often as the finite source permits. [Layer count, attainment and checks](coordinate_growth.md)

## Why the cutoff is sharp

The sharp generic horizon for the first three classes in the table is **five in bond-distance units**, even when every addition makes three outputs. A 51-distinction source has a legal triple with two terminal six-place holders. Swapping a marked loose distinction at the first with a spare at the second makes the triple use the same holder twice. All 1,275 pair entries agree through distance four. Distance five detects the exchange. The two genuine operands themselves are at bond distance 13. We prove 13 is the largest possible separation between two members of any legal triple under the unrestricted rule. A reader may recover a long-distance join from short entries throughout the table. [History, upper bound and sharp witness](marked_distances.md)

For the distinct problem of matching two choices appended to an already identified common prefix, one matched fresh row through \(D=h+2\) determines the entire triple menu, where \(h\) is the common fresh count. The correspondence must fix the old incidence and match fresh outputs. Two branch constructions of widths 35 and 36 give opposite verdicts under such a matching at the next lower cutoff, even when the entire clipped table is available. This proves sharpness for that information model. A separate result excludes a transposition of one marked terminal output with one physically loose old bit outside the mark, fixing every other label. ## The remaining unrestricted comparison

For a collision the scalar bijection must preserve every entry while carrying a legal triple to a forbidden one. The earlier finite lower bound is 27 distinctions. Both retained parts containing the marks must themselves contain a five- or six-place site. Large sites in discarded branches alone are insufficient. With only three-parent two-output exceptions, failure of the short-shore test requires at least six such exceptional sites in the same shore. Other unresolved sources can instead have several ternary sites or four-place sites with two parents. One explicit four-place cycle permits a locally plausible six-bit support that is no parity equation. What remains is to force or exclude such a support under **all** other pair entries, or to realize the opposite verdict on a matching full table. The previous width-32 symmetry changes an equation and preserves the eight actual choices.
