## What the calculation checks

The classical flag complex takes each live junction as a vertex, each compatible pair as an edge, and every pairwise-compatible triple as a filled triangle. For the direct fundamental-group certificate, contract a spanning forest of the candidate graph and assign an oriented generator to each remaining compatible pair. A triple supplies the product of its three oriented pair generators, with the forest pairs set to the identity. A generator occurring exactly once in a relator can be solved for and substituted in every other relator. The initial certificates list the forest, pair generators, triple relators and substitutions. Each of the sixteen lists ends with no generators.

For the sparse homology calculation, build the classical bipartite incidence graph between junctions and blocks. Its mod-2 cycle space modulo alternating four- and six-cycles computes the filled first homology on either side. A candidate pair lifts to a two-step incidence walk through any holding block. Changing the block gives a four-cycle, and the boundary of a compatible triple gives a six-cycle or a shorter closed walk of the same quotient. Conversely, pair the incident links of an even incidence chain at each block. Any two such pairings differ by cycles within that block, which are filled by its compatible triples. This gives inverse maps. Applying the same argument with the two sides transposed proves the block-intersection calculation. In particular all triples of pairwise-intersecting blocks are filled. They need no common junction.

The sparse algorithm gives each component of the block-intersection graph a connected order. At a new block it chooses a spanning forest of the graph on earlier intersecting blocks. Each forest pair there uses one compatible triple to express a new cochain coordinate in terms of earlier coordinates. The first root coordinate is gauged away, and disconnected parts of this earlier graph contribute one free coordinate each. Exact bitset tests check any remaining cochain against all compatible triples. A failed test supplies a new independent relation. A fully checked cochain paired with a forest-and-pair cycle gives a lower certificate, and the remaining coordinate count gives an upper bound even if the scan stops at its stated budget. Positive controls include the block cocycle and the closed walk on literal junctions that evaluates to one.

The sparse and literal calculations agree on all 1,099 labelled simple graphs through five vertices and on 480 sampled block families with six to ten junctions. Named controls include the partnered pairs and triples, a single block, a four-cycle with and without a fill, disconnected four-cycles with an isolated junction, and a compatible triple held only pairwise. A separate control forces a residual rank pivot. With zero residual-scan budget it returns the correct unresolved range from zero to one.
