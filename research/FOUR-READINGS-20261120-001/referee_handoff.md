## Closure assessment

With supplied operator values and a shared entangled state, two readers can win all nine trials on six parity rules that no single assignment satisfies.

Under either knot law, identical allowed states on just three distinctions can give three births or none.

With settings taken from the state, the not-all-equal pair-pattern score is at most 2 under flip-invariant weights, and no single-bit score exceeded 2 in the complete search through ten distinctions.

Randomly drawn starts released lasting families under both laws, and the smallest source found had four knots and eight distinctions.

All four independent referee reviews are pending. The six-rule reading gives up **one definite assignment for the whole**. Every proper subset of rules still admits a state, both readers obey their own rule, and each complete local record has the same distribution for every distant choice. One assignment satisfies at most five rules. Separate classical responses win at most eight of nine trials. Conversely, the eight-knot closure grown from three leaves admits a non-signalling agreement table that no quantum state realizes. The known operator test asks whether there are disjoint, internally connected groups of knots with either five groups all meeting each other, or two sets of three with every cross-meeting. In the leaf construction, three leaves fail the operator test and every size from four leaves onward passes. The growth minima and the comparison of the two tables are the additions here. [Arkhipov (2012)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.3819) · [Explicit tables](https://github.com/jscul/knotlab/blob/main/research/FOUR-READINGS-20261120-001A/results.md)

With settings inside the state, the supplied 16-word example scores 4. Using free settings on those same words gives 2. A Bell comparison assumes free settings. For ordinary not-all-equal, a score strictly between 2 and 4 with both full records invariant remains open beyond the proved class. [Setting comparison](https://github.com/jscul/knotlab/blob/main/research/FOUR-READINGS-20261120-001C/results.md)

Sizes four through eight had equal weight, each labelled tree of a given size had equal weight, and each knot independently received an extra loose distinction with probability ¼. The two readers agreed on counted releases and meetings. The tree release rates had 95% intervals of 23.4–34.9% under parity and 28.6–40.5% under not-all-equal. The frozen donor withdrawal put indirect meetings at 21–30 of 256 trees and 5–8 of 256 augmented trees under each law. A later lawful three-knot comparison resolved the counts to 30 and 8. No tree in this weighting can emit separate lasting families indefinitely. In the augmented sample, zero of 256 did so, giving a one-sided 95% upper bound of 1.16%. Inside the partnered runaway, every birth inherits the entire cohort two parent steps earlier, which rules out a separately lasting release there.
