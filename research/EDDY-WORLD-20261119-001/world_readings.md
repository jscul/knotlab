# What the historical parity states say about a change

**On all ten starts, every original shared distinction has the same shortest-change profile at every positive age.** Three further distinctions change, and the farthest is exactly two physical distinction steps away for every minimizing choice. Pinning all current loose distinctions gives the same positive-age answer. At the source, with the loose distinctions free, the corresponding numbers are two further changes and one step. These statements continue beyond the measured sizes for every open complete round. All original shared distinctions are covered, including source distinctions outside the frozen probe list.

For general starts, the minimum number of further changes can rise by at most two per round. Both the least and greatest physical reach among cardinality minimizers can rise by at most one step. They can *fall* by arbitrarily many steps in a single open round. These are comparisons between pairs of allowed states at successive configurations. ## The parity calculation

Use an ordinary vertex for every historical junction. A distinction with two holders is an ordinary multigraph edge. Connect each one-holder distinction to a common auxiliary exterior \(\Omega\). Call the ordinary graph \(O\), its currently loose holders \(L\), and the wired graph \(G\). All costs below count physical distinction names, including multiplicity. The exterior serves only the parity equations. Two distinctions are a physical step apart exactly when they share an ordinary holder.

The difference between any two allowed assignments is an even edge set of \(G\). Conversely any even edge set is a permissible difference from every allowed assignment. Indeed the given parity equations impose evenness at every ordinary vertex, and summing them imposes evenness also at \(\Omega\). Inclusion-minimal nonempty flip supports are precisely the simple circuits, including a parallel pair. The parity equations are cuts, since summing the vertex rows of one shore gives its boundary. In a connected wired graph their minimal nonempty supports, the bonds, are exactly cuts with both shores connected. If a shore disconnects, separating one of its components gives a proper subcut. Conversely any stricter subcut of a connected-shore cut would have to split a connected shore without cutting one of its internal edges, which is impossible. [Earlier finite parity formulation](../../proofs/GROWN-END-READING-20261119-001A/GROWN-END-READING-20261119-001A-P001_transport/proof.md)

For distinct \(e,f\), bond distance is the least size of such a bond containing both, less one. It is infinite when there is no common bond and zero on the diagonal by convention. Enumerating connected shores is exact. Another exact method tries both assignments of the two marked edges' ends to opposite shores. Enumerate a pair of disjoint connecting paths inside the proposed shores, one linking the two positive ends and one linking the two negative ends, allowing a singleton for coincident ends. Contract these two connected witnesses separately and take a minimum cut between them, with unit cost on each original distinction. A minimum cut separates two connected witnesses and may be taken with both shores connected. Every desired bond supplies such a pair of paths. Merely contracting disconnected sets of marked ends can give a cut with a disconnected shore.

For \(e=uv\) in \(G\), the minimum number of *further* changes is \(d_{G-e}(u,v)\). A shortest alternate path and \(e\) give an even change. Any even change through \(e\) contains a circuit through it and hence such a path. An infinite result means that \(e\) is pinned by the parity system. For shared \(e\), holding all loose distinctions fixed replaces \(G\) by \(O\). For loose \(e\), such pinning of every other loose coordinate makes its flip impossible. With all loose bits free, a loose \(e\) at \(u\) needs one further change plus the ordinary distance from \(u\) to the nearest holder of a *different* loose distinction. Its physical reach is the same number for every shortest change, since that second loose distinction is at the far end of the chosen path.

There is also an exact test for necessary crossing of a named region. Given a flippable \(e\notin S\), **every change of \(e\) touches \(S\)** exactly when deleting \(S\) makes \(e\) a bridge of \(G-S\). Equivalently there is a bond \(B\) with \(e\in B\subseteq S\cup\{e\}\). An alternate path after deletion is a crossing-free witness. If no path survives, take a cut separating the endpoints of \(e\) inside \(S\cup\{e\}\) and reduce it to a bond containing \(e\). One way to do the reduction is to choose an inclusion-minimal subset that still separates the endpoints. This tests a universal requirement, which one displayed minimizing flip does not establish. In applying it to a seam, include the exterior in the interface whenever both pieces have loose distinctions. A single shared source junction can therefore give two wired connections between the pieces.

## Exact numbers, balls and reach

For a shared \(e=uv\) put

\[
a=d_{O-e}(u,v),\qquad h_u=d_{O-e}(u,L),\qquad h_v=d_{O-e}(v,L).
\]

With infinity allowed, the number of further changes is \(c=\min(a,2+h_u+h_v)\). An alternate path avoids \(\Omega\) or uses two loose distinctions. Independently chosen shortest paths to loose holders give an alternate walk of the second length. If their concatenation repeats a vertex, simplification gives an ordinary bypass of at most that length.

Minimizing reach alone gives a different statistic from minimizing cardinality first, as specified in the registration. The smallest possible farthest physical distinction step among **all** valid changes of \(e\) is

\[
R_{\rm all}(e)=\min\{\lceil a/2\rceil,\ 1+\max(h_u,h_v)\}.
\]

For distinct ordinary edges \(e,f\), their physical step distance is one plus the minimum ordinary distance between their holders. The same holds for two distinction names with any nonempty sets of ordinary holders. In computing distance to the set \(\{u,v\}\), deleting \(e\) makes no difference. At physical radius \(r\ge1\), the allowable ordinary edges are those incident with the radius-\((r-1)\) holder neighborhoods of \(u\) or \(v\), with \(e\) omitted from the bypass. These two collections meet or have a common endpoint exactly when \(a\le2r\). Otherwise an eligible bypass must use the exterior, which requires a loose holder within distance \(r-1\) in each neighborhood. These possibilities prove the formula. More generally, for any specified nonnegative cost \(w_e(f)\) on the other coordinates, the minimum, over all flips of \(e\), of the maximum changed cost satisfies

\[
\min_{\text{circuit }C\ni e}\max_{f\in C-\{e\}}w_e(f)
=\max_{\text{bond }B\ni e}\min_{f\in B-\{e\}}w_e(f).
\]

At each threshold the region-crossing test either produces a permitted circuit or a bond certifying failure. This applies also when \(w_e(f)\) is bond distance. Its numerical radius can differ arbitrarily from the Hamming size. In a triangle of wired bundles of sizes \(1,2,k\), \(k\ge2\), mark the unique member of the first. A minimum flip has size three, whereas every flip uses a member of the \(k\)-bundle at bond distance \(k\) from the mark. The exterior can be the holder between bundles 1 and 2. The two ordinary vertices then have at least three places and the even assignments at each extend.

The frozen least and greatest reaches among cardinality minimizers can be evaluated without enumerating all flip supports. Direct the shortest \(u\)-to-\(v\) paths in \(G-e\) by increasing distance from \(u\). For each admissible final step, take the maximum of its physical distinction distance from \(e\) and the preceding path's largest distance. Minimize or maximize this quantity over admissible preceding steps. These two recurrences cover all minimum-cardinality ties. The source-to-stage reads implement this method. ## Changes between complete rounds

Projection of the new allowed assignments onto all old distinctions gives exactly the old allowed assignments. Each nonimplied new junction can satisfy its one parity equation using a fresh distinction of its own. At a closing junction without fresh distinctions its equation is already implied. Therefore an old bond remains an inclusion-minimal bond on its unchanged support. A smaller support *contained in it* could use only old distinctions, where the constraints have stayed the same. A new bond through a marked old pair may use fresh distinctions and need not be contained in any previous bond. Thus every fixed old pair's bond distance is nonincreasing and eventually constant on any infinite continuation. It can start infinite, in which case it stays infinite or first becomes finite. This argument also applies to the least bond size through any fixed finite marked set. Whole balls can change as new distinctions enter the domain.

Strict decrease already occurs on a canonical start within five places. On ordinary sites \(A,B,C,W_1,W_2,D\) take one distinction each on \(AB,BC\) and two each on \(AC,BW_1,W_1W_2,W_2D\). Give \(A,B,C\) one loose bit each, \(e_A,e_B,e_C\), and \(D\) two. The intermediate \(W\) sites are dead. Every ordinary parity projection is full, as deleting any one site from the wired graph leaves it connected. The only group is \(ABC\), and \(D\) makes two unary children. Initially a cut containing \(e_A,e_B\) puts \(A,B\) opposite the exterior. If \(C\) is on the exterior side, three additional ordinary distinctions cross. If \(C\) is with \(A,B\), all three loose bits cross and the twice-shared route to \(D\) or its two loose bits supplies two further crossings. The exterior cut attains five. In the next round the \(ABC\) child has no candidate and receives one fresh distinction. Its four-place star is a bond. Any cut through the two marks still needs at least four distinctions. Placing \(C\) apart from \(A,B\) already gives five, while leaving it with them crosses all three old inputs but does not cut the alternate route from the new child through the exterior and \(D\) back to \(B\). Thus true pair distance drops from four to three. Its former minimizing bond still exists. For exact flip size, fix any old flip \(z\). For every new child, count how many changed old loose distinctions it consumes. The minimum number of fresh changes needed to extend \(z\) is precisely the number of children with an odd count. Give each odd child one of its fresh bits. An implied closing child consuming all loose distinctions sees an even count for every old flip. Projection gives the reverse lower bound. Thus the full minimum flip size \(s=c+1\) for an old mark never decreases. A shortest old flip is a circuit, which uses either zero or two loose bits. Consequently \(s\le s'\le s+2\).

The physical least and greatest reach **among shortest flips** each increase by at most one. Distances between old distinction names cannot increase because each earlier common-holder step remains. A new changed fresh bit in a minimum extension lies one step from a changed consumed bit. If \(s'=s+2\), every old minimizer admits an optimum extension, bounding the new least reach. Every new minimizing circuit using the exterior projects to an old minimizer in that case: deleting its two fresh bits leaves an old flip of size \(s\). Each fresh bit has an incident changed old bit of the same child, since a circuit through the old mark cannot consist of a single child's two fresh bits. This bounds the new greatest reach for those circuits. If \(s'<s+2\), no new minimizer can use the exterior, since projection would leave a flip smaller than \(s\). Any ordinary minimizing circuit at the new stage has reach \(\lfloor s'/2\rfloor\). Its alternate path is an ordinary geodesic, so its middle is farthest. In every old minimizing circuit at least one changed bit has *wired* step distance \(\lfloor s/2\rfloor\) from the mark, by the same geodesic argument in the wired graph; physical steps give at least that distance. Hence the ordinary cases also satisfy both signed upper bounds.

The two tie reaches can fall without a universal one-round bound. For \(q\ge2\), use a marked ordinary edge \(u v\), another ordinary \(u\)-to-\(v\) path of length \(2q+5\), and a stem of length \(2q\) from \(u\) to a new site \(w\). Double every ordinary link except \(uv\). Give just \(v,w\) two loose bits each. The wired graph stays connected under deletion of any site; all junctions have at least three places. The two separated live sites produce unary children independently in round one. In round two each pair of siblings gives its pair child and two offshoots. At age one the unique cheaper route type through the exterior has alternate length \(2q+4\). The furthest changed bit is loose at a child of \(w\) at physical reach \(2q+2\). At age two the exterior route has length \(2q+6\), so all minima use the old ordinary alternative of length \(2q+5\) and have reach \(q+3\). Both tie reaches drop by \(q-1\), while the minimum number of changes rises by one. [Four literal controls](world_theory_controls.json)

## A fixed old seam and the actual all-size reading

Every new detour between two old ordinary holders through a common new child has an old replacement of the same length through one of their common ordinary neighbors. A new unary holder provides no shortcut. Thus inclusion into each next stage preserves all ordinary holder-to-holder distances exactly. The same applies after deleting an old shared distinction \(e=uv\) once neither endpoint is live. Any old two-step replacement between current live parents avoids \(e\) in that case. At every further open round all new loose holders are children and every old live holder has a child. It follows that ordinary distance from a fixed old holder to the new loose-holder set, after deleting \(e\), increases by one, with infinity preserved.

For exhausted endpoints at a chosen reference age, let \(a,h_u,h_v\) be its three original readings defined above. Exactly \(n\) later open rounds give

\[
c_n=\min\{a,\;2+h_u+h_v+2n\},\qquad
R_{{\rm all},n}=\min\{\lceil a/2\rceil,\;1+\max(h_u,h_v)+n\}.
\]

In particular an ordinary bridge that survives until both its endpoints are exhausted remains an ordinary bridge. If it is flippable, its minimum Hamming size grows by exactly two per round and its minimum possible physical reach grows by exactly one. If \(a\) is finite, both quantities eventually remain at their ordinary-cycle values. When \(h_u=h_v\), all Hamming minimizers through the exterior use equal-length arms. Whenever that exterior route minimizes, each arm has length \(h_u+n\); no point on it can be closer to the opposite endpoint than its distance along the arm, since \(a\ge 2+2(h_u+n)\). Their loose tips therefore attain physical reach \(1+h_u+n\). In an ordinary tie \(\lceil a/2\rceil\) has the same value. For this balanced case \(R_{{\rm all},n}\) also equals both of the frozen tie reaches. The preceding unbalanced example explains the additional hypothesis.

On each frozen source all ordinary junctions are live, the connected ordinary meeting graph is simple, has at least three vertices, and has no triangle. The additional loose-bit comparisons at the source therefore give a three-distinction circuit for each shared \(e=uv\), one loose bit at each endpoint and \(e\). No parallel edge shortens it and every minimizing choice has physical reach one. Some endpoint of \(e\) has a further neighbor \(w\); if that endpoint is \(v\), the live pair \(u,w\) are candidates through \(v\). A real joint child of this pair gives an ordinary four-cycle through \(e\) at age one. No new triangle through \(e\) appears because any new child joined to both \(u,v\) would require that they already had a common neighbor. Both endpoints are now exhausted and the ordinary alternative length stays exactly three. Each original live endpoint reaches the current loose holders in precisely \(t\) ordinary steps at age \(t\), by following successive children. No shorter path is possible because every ordinary step increases age by at most one, and the construction from either endpoint avoids \(e\). Thus the exterior route has length \(2+2t>3\) at every positive age. The shortest ordinary flip uses four distinctions and its reach is two, with every other loose bit pinned or free. This proves the stated all-size null on the path-versus-ring comparison. The literal archive check covers 162 source observations and 1,737 positive-age original shared observations. ## What source-erased recognition can establish

In a complete open history every ordinary contact joins adjacent generations, except for contacts internal to the source. Every initially live source holder and every positive-age holder has a chain of children to the current loose holders. An age-\(j\) holder is therefore at ordinary physical distance exactly \(t-j\) from them. For an initially dead source holder, add to \(t\) its original ordinary distance to the initially live set. Indeed a route from it to a positive-age holder must first pass an initially live one. In this study all source holders were live. Consequently the maximal distance to any nearest loose holder is \(t\), and its maximizing holders form exactly the source. With anonymous full supports one can recover its original contacts and former loose bits from their current old-holder incidences. This supplies an exact all-size control for primary recognition. Independent continuation of wholly internal births must be checked from the cluster identification and the actual map inclusion. A stationary recovered source feature by itself provides no change of position relative to landmarks.
