# Transporting a parity-resolution DAG through a literal substitution

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

Let \(G\) be a target CNF and \(F\) a source CNF. Give one simultaneous substitution \(\sigma\) assigning each target variable a source literal or a Boolean constant. Suppose the image of every axiom of \(G\), including any pin unit, is either a tautology or exactly a clause of \(F\) after simplification. Every explicit \(S\)-node parity-resolution DAG refutation of \(G\) yields a refutation of \(F\) with **at most \(S\) nodes and no greater resolution depth**. This holds for every width. The factor one is optimal, including for minimum proof sizes. Constructing and verifying the proof takes polynomial time in the explicit bit length of the formulas, map, variable rosters and supplied proof.

Both proof systems use complementary affine-pivot resolution and arbitrary **one-premise** semantic weakening over \(\mathbb F_2\). An axiom line cites one of the formula's actual clauses. Earlier lines may be used repeatedly. Node count includes axioms and weakenings. Resolution depth counts binary resolution steps on a path to the final empty clause. The target may retain a designated pivot literal within a side disjunction. Every binary step emitted in the source uses exact deletion of the two designated complementary literals.

## Nearest known result

Braun gives the basic rules and node measure in §1.2. His Lemma 7.6 observes that affine substitution preserves implication and separately treats unrestricted sound two-premise inference. Deducing a bound for a syntactic rule list with only literal-adding weakening requires an additional simulation. The theorem here uses the basic two rules above. [Braun, *An exponential lower bound for the bit pigeonhole principle in resolution over parities*, §1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23015v1#S1.SS2)

The local common-restriction result for a majority lift already transports complete shared DAGs, discards tautologies and preserves resolution depth with at most \(2S\) nodes. Its converter resolves first and can then weaken to repair a pivot lost through identification. The proof below replaces that pair by one weakening of an original premise. It also provides an executable adapter for two arbitrary supplied CNFs and a single literal map, including the raw guarded routing format. The transformer reuses the predecessor's equation substitution and Gaussian tautology test. ## Substitution and the initial clauses

Write an equation as \((m,b)\), meaning \(m\cdot x=b\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). Store each linear clause as a finite disjunction of equations. To substitute into an equation, XOR the coefficient vectors of the selected images and XOR their constant offsets into \(b\). Thus two identified equal summands cancel, and a variable plus its complement contributes one. In a Boolean polynomial presentation Frobenius gives \(u^2=u\). Cancellation \(u+u=0\) is the separate characteristic-two law used here. All equations in the proof are affine, so substituting them requires no product expansion.

Remove duplicate disjuncts and the false equation \(0=1\). A clause is tautological exactly when the simultaneous falsification of all its equations is inconsistent. This test catches \(0=0\), complementary disjuncts, and dependencies involving more than two equations. For example \((x=1)\lor(y=1)\lor(x+y=0)\) is tautological. A non-tautological image includes the possibility of the empty clause.

Normalize each supplied target CNF axiom in this way. Record a tautology marker or the index of an *identical* source CNF clause. Test all target axioms, including unused ones. A clause merely implied by the whole source formula does not satisfy this hypothesis. If a supplied proof mentions extra unconstrained names absent from all input clauses, include them in the finite target roster and extend the map by zero before translating. This preserves every axiom image. Given a source assignment \(s\), evaluating any target formula under the target assignment induced by \(\sigma(s)\) gives its substituted value at \(s\). This identity applies simultaneously to every node.

## One output node per input node

Process target nodes in topological order. Associate each with a tautology marker or one output reference whose clause is its normalized image. For a tautological image, record the marker. For a non-tautological axiom image, use the checked source axiom index. For a weakening \(C\models D\) with non-tautological image \(D'\), substitution preserves \(C'\models D'\). Its premise \(C'\) is therefore non-tautological. Emit one weakening of its retained reference. This includes weakenings justified by linear dependence even when the clauses have no included disjuncts in common.

For a resolution step write its images as \(L=A\lor p\), \(H=B\lor\bar p\) and \(V=A\lor B\), where bars mean complementary equations. The notation describes the logical side disjunctions before duplicates are removed. Assume \(V\) is non-tautological.

1. **One tautological parent.** If \(L\) is tautological, any assignment falsifying \(V\) falsifies \(A,B\) and must satisfy \(p\). It therefore falsifies \(H\). Thus \(H\models V\), and one weakening from \(H\) is legal. If \(H\) is tautological the symmetric argument uses \(L\). This covers a constant image pivot. If both parents are tautological the same assignment would have to satisfy \(p\) and \(\bar p\), which is impossible. A step with two tautological premises can only have a tautological conclusion and contributes no initial or derived output node.
2. **Two non-tautological parents.** Now \(p\) is nonconstant. Both designated disjuncts survive in their respective normalized parents. Form the canonical resolvent \(R=(L\setminus\{p\})\cup(H\setminus\{\bar p\})\). Then \(R\subseteq V\subseteq R\cup\{p,\bar p\}\), since only removed pivot occurrences can have surviving aliases. If \(R=V\), emit that exact resolution. If \(p\in V\setminus R\), **the entire first parent \(L\) is contained in \(V\)**. Use the single weakening \(L\models V\). If \(\bar p\in V\setminus R\), use \(H\models V\). This covers identification with another target equation and a pivot already retained in the original side context.

For a concrete alias take target parents \((x=0)\lor(z=0)\) and \(x=1\), resolving on \(x\) to \(z=0\). Identify \(x,z\) with \(t\). The source parents are \(t=0\) and \(t=1\). Exact resolution would produce the empty clause. The needed image \(t=0\) follows from the first parent in one unary step.

Each non-tautological target node has now emitted exactly one source node. Every use of the same surviving input node refers to the same output node whenever that premise is required. Separate input occurrences keep their own references even when their images agree, since an older occurrence with the same value may have greater derivation depth. A binary output step raises the maximum of its own two parent depths by one. A unary output step inherits one actual parent's depth. Induction bounds every surviving node's resolution depth by its original depth. The empty final target clause survives and is last. This proves both bounds without unfolding the DAG.

**Sharpness.** For \(n\ge1\), apply the identity map to \(x_1,\ (\neg x_i\lor x_{i+1})_{1\le i<n},\ \neg x_n\). All \(n+1\) clauses are necessary. Deleting any one admits an assignment whose initial run of ones has length \(0,\ldots,n\), respectively. Every refutation must use all these axioms in the ancestry of empty. If this connected ancestry contains \(a\) initial occurrences, \(u\) unary steps and \(r\) binary steps, it has at most \(u+2r\) parent references and needs at least \(a+u+r-1\) to connect all its nodes. Hence \(r\ge a-1\ge n\). Ordinary unit resolution attains \(2n+1\) total nodes, so the source minimum can equal the entire target minimum for arbitrarily large \(n\).

## Verification and bit cost

The independent proof verifier checks actual axiom indices, earlier parent references and the selected complementary pivot plus its side contexts. It permits a general target resolution child between exact deletion and deletion with the designated disjuncts retained. For weakening from \(C\) to \(D\), for each equation \(e\in C\) it tests the inconsistency of \(\{\neg d:d\in D\}\cup\{e\}\). Every such system must be inconsistent. This Gaussian elimination uses the lowest available coefficient as pivot. The producer uses the predecessor's highest-coefficient implementation for tautologies. For the finite output controls, a third check evaluates every inference on source assignments.

Each equation visits at most its original number of variable occurrences during substitution. Each new row has at most the original number of disjuncts. Gaussian elimination, index lookup, explicit masks and parent-reference encoding all cost polynomially many bit operations in the supplied input size, counting the source roster or indexed mask length. The transformer makes one pass over the DAG after verification and retains shared references. A bound on the *number of nodes* by itself gives no bound on the bytes per line. For example the disjunction of \(m\cdot x=1\) over all \(2^n-1\) nonzero masks describes the predicate \(x\ne0\). The identity controls put that line on the actual ancestry of the empty clause, with \(2n+3\) nodes in the whole proof. The statement about formula-specific lower bounds is conditional: a lower bound on source nodes transfers to target nodes in the two-rule system through a verified map. Matching a different proof system or supplying an external lower-bound family requires its own evidence.

## Executable controls

The real-axiom fixture compiles two guarded corridors for the opposite units \(p\) and \(\neg p\). The inputs are the literal raw 3-clauses and their switch units. The target proof resolves guards, witness bits, endpoint pins, the unconditional and conditional implications in each corridor, and finally the pad. Its 280 axioms have 278 tautological images and two exact source axiom images. A 253-node target DAG with two shared parents and resolution depth 10 produces an 11-node source DAG of depth 1. A distinct eight-variable proof uses nontrivial affine pivots, an inferred three-equation tautology, a constant pivot, both tautological parents, a retained pivot and reused source references. Its 25 nodes and depth 8 become 19 nodes and depth 4. [Terminal guarded corridor construction](../../TWO-RELAY-PADDING-20261115-001A/TWO-RELAY-PADDING-20261115-001A-P001_track_regrouping/proof.md)

The local census takes all 64 clauses on the six nonconstant affine equations in two variables, all 36 signed-literal/constant substitutions into two variables, and all legal choices of retained pivot contexts. It tests 497,664 resolution contexts, 99,540 valid unary weakenings, and 864 equation values evaluated directly on assignments. In 4,512 proper cases the exact-deletion image differs and a single weakening of a parent suffices. Six negative controls cover a spurious empty conclusion from two tautological images, an unused axiom that is only a consequence, a missing variable image, a bad target weakening masked by an alias, a forward parent and incompatible maps for two opposite units. The genuine DAG fixtures, sharp chains and width controls complement the uniform argument. [Exact finite outputs](checks.json)
