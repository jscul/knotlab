## A lower bound after training one affine conclusion

For two sets of source rows, let \(T(R,P)\) be the minimum ordinary cost under the shared charges: one per binary question and one per call to one evaluator. Write \(T^\circ(\emptyset,\emptyset)=0\) for an empty sector and \(T^\circ=T\) elsewhere. Suppose the only named learned conclusion is \(G\Rightarrow L\), with forbidden affine cell \(F=G\cap\neg L\). If the proof contains \(k\) citations of that conclusion, its total work is at least

\[
 T^\circ(R\cap F,P\cap F)+T^\circ(R\setminus F,P\setminus F)+k. 
\]

The training term is the ordinary proof at \(F\). For the second term, restrict both source relations globally to the complement of \(F\) and simulate the main tree. At every citation, all surviving rows lie in its certified guard and therefore already satisfy its conclusion. A nonclosing citation can be deleted without changing the surviving trace sets. Maintain the invariant that a processed node has at least one surviving row in the union of the two sources. Suppress a split whenever one answer has empty union. A source call either retains its tested nonempty rows or closes immediately if its tested rows are empty. A citation whose continuation is affinely inconsistent is therefore encountered on no processed branch: both filtered trace sets would have to be empty there. This produces a valid ordinary tree with at most the main cost minus all \(k\) citations, including citations on branches that were pruned. The argument uses surviving rows to simulate an ordinary no-citation proof. It does not infer a guard for free from a union of source traces in the original proof.

In particular, if the least common ancestor of all \(k\) uses already entails \(G\), one can also split once on \(L\) at that ancestor, use the training proof under the stronger false-answer assumptions, and restrict the old subtree to the true answer while erasing the \(k\) uses. The resulting ordinary tree costs at most the learned proof minus \(k-1\). The sector lower applies even when consumers have no common ancestor satisfying the guard. For several independently stored affine conclusions in topological order, the same simulation gives a lower bound by summing the ordinary costs on the disjoint new forbidden regions left after deleting earlier ones, plus the final residue and the number of citations. A covered or empty sector costs zero in that inequality.

### The literal \(v=0\Rightarrow x_0=0\)

Use the invertible regrouping into three triples \((x_1,x_0,u),(x_2,x_1+x_3,v),(x_4,x_3+x_5,s+u+v)\). In each triple \((y,w,p)\), write \(e=p+yw\). Rich rows have all errors zero. Plain rows have odd total error. The forbidden cell \(F\) fixes \(w_1=1,p_2=0\). It contains 24 rich and 64 plain rows. Its complement contains 40 rich and 192 plain rows.

Training costs exactly **7**. Once \(w_1=1,p_2=0\) are imposed, replace \(p_1\) by \(g=p_1+y_1\). The now irrelevant \(y_1\) can be fixed for a lower bound, or ignored for the matching upper bound. On six remaining bits \((g,a,b,c,d,p)\), the rich tests are \(g=0,ab=0,p=cd\) and the plain test is \(g+ab+p+cd=1\). An exact search compares each individual hull call and all 63 affine questions up to complement, testing every interleaving of those calls.

The complementary sector costs at least **15**. Here are the details of a smaller relaxation used to verify this number without exhaustive search on nine bits. Let \(z\) consist of the first two raw triples, let \(D\) be a surviving set of core rows, let \(R_0\subseteq D\) be the surviving rich core inputs and let \(P_{\rm odd}\) be the core rows of odd error. Before the first query involving the third triple, a rich side call acts on \(D\) by intersecting it with \(\operatorname{aff}(R_0)\). The four true local triple states span all three of their coordinates. For each \(z\in D\) the plain side permits either the four true local states or their complementary translate, both affinely spanning. A plain call therefore leaves both trace sets unchanged. A core-only query splits \(R_0,D\) in the indicated two answers. If \(R_0\) is empty a single rich call closes. If it is nonempty, fixing any one point of \(R_0\) embeds the one-triple problem of weighted cost five.

For a first question involving that third triple, its nonconstant local affine term takes both values on true local rows, since those four rows span. Pick one such row for each value. Interpolating between the two with the residual question bit embeds the entire ordinary core problem \((R_0,D\cap P_{\rm odd})\) in **each** answer while preserving the rich and plain relations. Under an affine restriction a formerly paid local check is simulated by at most one check and questions can become constant. Each answer also contains a half of the one-triple problem obtained by fixing any rich \(z\); this half has both source types, requiring at least two more paid operations. Thus the first-question cost is bounded below by \(1+2\max(2,T(R_0,D\cap P_{\rm odd}))\). Minimizing this lower estimate over first touches of the third triple, together with the exact rich and core-query alternatives, initially gives 13 for the complement. At the twelve encountered states with five rich core rows, six or eight allowed core rows and an eight-point affine hull, replace the estimate by ordinary exact search. That affine hull contributes three coordinates and the untouched triple contributes three more. All twelve compressed problems cost seven. The sharpened recursion gives the lower bound 15. Consequently, a proof which trains *only* this affine literal and cites it twice costs at least **24**, regardless of how the two consumers are situated. The 23-unit direct tree is cheaper under the stated charge. This is a lower bound for this named conclusion and its affine symmetry images. It does not assert a global DAG optimum.
