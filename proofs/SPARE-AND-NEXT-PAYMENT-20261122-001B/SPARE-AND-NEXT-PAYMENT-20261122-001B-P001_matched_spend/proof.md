# The common newborn pays each of its next children

In the two enrolled histories, two or three private distinctions at the first common newborn become two or three actual payments, one to every common child of the next size. The extra child takes a real input from this newborn. Which individual mark it takes can change while every child still forms. The number of marks and the eligible partner groups, by contrast, are already tied together by the preceding funded step. ## Why two or three payments are already called for

The ordinary operator first forms the complete parent list of the new cohort. Before assigning fresh names, this list determines which members of that cohort a single older holder witnesses together. A group of three or more must be maximal among pairwise witnessed groups and lack a single common witness. Uncovered witnessed pairs then form the remaining multiple-parent groups. Let d(H) be the number of these next groups that include H. Away from the sole-child parity closure, a newborn with s paid inputs receives

```text
max(1, 3 − s, d(H))
```

fresh marks. Both reached cuts are fully funded and have multiple children. Here s=2. In particular, which partner groups are proposed can be checked from the size-eight parent lists before giving H a single fresh name. [Complete-cut rule](../../../experiments/ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001A/ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001A-E001_ordinary/end_operator.py) · [Literal individual payments](../../../experiments/GENERATORS-20261120-001A/GENERATORS-20261120-001A-E001_generators/fixtures.py)

* In the original preparation H=71 has just two witnessed partners, 69 through 58 and 73 through 60. The partners have no common size-seven parent with each other. The required groups involving H are the two separate pairs. Thus d(H)=2 and the grant is two.
* With the spare, H=98 has precisely four witnessed partners. The older holder 77 witnesses H with 92 and 95; holder 80 witnesses H with 90 and 100. Among these four partners the witnessed pairs are (90,92) through 73, (90,100) through 80 and (92,95) through 77. Thus (90,92,H) is maximal and its three pairs are witnessed by three *different* old holders. The incoming marks from 73 to 90 and 92 are 172 and 173; those from 77 to 92 and H are 181 and 183; those from 80 to 90 and H are 188 and 189. No fourth partner meets all three. A triple lying wholly in one of the older witness groups does not qualify. The remaining uncovered pairs at H are (95,H) and (H,100). Thus d(H)=3 and the grant is three.

In the spare history the additional first-cut unary 17 is an ancestor of both 90 and 92, but it is absent from the parent ancestry of H=98. H’s size-two ancestors are 11 through 16 and 18. Its larger grant reflects meetings involving the additional branch in the forecast. No mark from unary 17 had to pass along an ancestral chain to H to change this forecast. The ancestor comparison follows actual paid parents at each step. It agrees with the archived independent characterization of the parent roles. The upstream edit already changes a matched A-descendant grant at size six and changes other partner roles and grants, so it does not isolate H's grant as an independent intervention.

**Counting lemma.** In any complete funded ordinary cut, a holder with n private distinctions pays exactly n **distinct children**. Each occurrence in a multiple-parent group spends one distinct private mark and the group contains the holder only once. Every remaining mark pays its own separate unary child. Newborns persist as distinct members even if two unary parent lists agree. If the holder already descends from both launches, every one of its children inherits both ancestries.

For actual H there is no unary remainder in either arm: its two or three private marks fund exactly its two or three required multiple-parent groups. Conversely, on any funded continuation that retains joint H with three private marks, there are **at least three** common children whatever their partner identities. A further birth bypassing H could increase this count. In these reached rosters none does, so the number of H's private marks immediately before funding equals the *entire* number of common children at size nine. This is the sense in which the newborn’s stock is necessary material and predicts the next count. It gives no independent choice of partner or of grant.

## What can be interrupted while the opportunity stays open?

**Divert a particular distinction.** At the identical size-eight prefix keep every older holding and value fixed, preserve all other payers' assignments, and exchange two assignments from H to its actual size-nine children. There are 2!=2 complete assignments in the original and 3!=6 with the spare, per law. These exhaust the bijections to this fixed roster. Each H end mark acquires one new coholder, and H's two inherited marks retain their old coholders. For example, with the spare interchange 231 and 232 at 111 and 118. Then 111 really receives 232 from H and 118 receives 231. The third H mark 233 still goes to 120. In the ordinary recorded assignment it was 231, the first mark in H's fresh list, that paid the extra group. No position in that list carries an intrinsic “extra-child” meaning. These new coholdings differ when the whole prefix is fixed pointwise. In contrast, a coherent renaming of the *whole* history, including the ordered allocation convention, preserves which physical mark went to which child after inverse transport. Sorting named marks picks the recorded representative in the literal implementation. The other bijections are alternative coherent allocations to exactly the same proposed groups. They do not amount to an action selected by H. Every assignment keeps both launches, their pure continuations, both original common opportunities, the extra common child when present, every partner list and every grant. The alternatives also preserve the full allowed-state tests. Any permutation of H's fresh labels, applied to the whole *relation*, maps the original family of holdings to the reassigned one: before the next cut these labels occur only in H's single bank. The local K1–K3 projections are therefore transported. In the fixed-prefix intervention the labels themselves stay put and the future coholdings change. Even then, every lawful old value assignment extends each alternative allocation. Each size-nine newborn has at least one independent fresh mark. For parity choose an unused fresh value to complete its own relation. For not-all-equal an incoming mixture already suffices; for homogeneous inputs give a fresh mark the opposite value. Same-cut children have disjoint fresh banks. There are also matched witnesses keeping *all* values the same across a diversion. Under parity take the all-zero word. Under not-all-equal arrange one of H’s incoming marks to be one by prescribing it at its size-seven parent, and give a different private mark of that parent value zero. Such a second mark exists at 58 and 77. Keep H's own fresh bank all zero. H is lawful, other same-cut births can extend, and every recipient still sees the same input values under each H reassignment. The same admissible size-nine fresh values therefore work across those arms. More generally, all four original and all eight spare H fresh words are attainable within their respective widths. For parity choose the two paid inputs to match the parity of any requested fresh word. These are payments from distinct parents, 58 and 60 or 77 and 80, each with at least two fresh positions and therefore a remaining position to complete its own constraint. For not-all-equal a mixed H fresh word needs no particular paid input; a constant fresh word needs just one opposite paid value, which the parent can supply on the same reasoning. **Interrupt H’s whole contribution.** On the spare roster, every lawful birth with the exact parent group (90,92,98) takes a previously private mark from every listed parent. Replacing the name of H's payment still supplies H. Omitting its parent contribution while claiming to keep that birth violates the parent-set definition. At fixed witnessed groups, lowering H's supply from three to two leaves three required group seats and creates a shortage of one. The ordinary operator then stalls the entire cut, withholding both pure continuations and the other common opportunities. This is a diagnostic calculation on a deficient end. The actual preceding step deterministically granted three and did not produce that end. Increases beyond its forecast are equally unavailable as independent choices at H's formation. Posting more or fewer marks by hand, changing a witness to change several partner groups, or replanning after individual writes would change the tested conditions. For an already fixed ordinary plan, writing a child later in a parent-respecting order still includes it in the completed cut. There are consequently two lawful comparisons with different conclusions. A fixed-prefix diversion breaks the specific payment 231→111 while H still pays 111 and every other opportunity survives. The original X₄ preparation removes the extra recipient itself while both product families and the two common opportunities survive, but it also changes the groups funding H and its neighbors well before this payment. At fixed reached parentage, witnesses, availability and complete funding, there is no third comparison that suppresses just H's required contribution to 111. The demonstrated record is structural: H’s physically countable private holdings reflect an earlier difference and participate in later production. No numerical value, source-selected signal or independent decision by H is required or established.
