# The question to the advisor (26 November 2026), before the two-receptions study was run

A short check of two designs and their predictions; be skeptical. Same growth system as before (rounds; members are
knots with usable loose distinctions; groups from met sets; short when owe > h; a short member waits; a member that
is not short spends all and leaves; each new knot gets f fresh distinctions, ordinary count f = max(D, 3 - p, 1) with
D its demand next round read on the structure after the births, and after any distinction a birth takes from a common
knot; a cap of k gives min(f, k)). A receiver and a far pattern joined by a meeting through two link knots.

Known: under the cap of three, 28 of 646 far ends stay linked to a receiver kind; a change (one more loose distinction)
at the far end's nearest linked member after round 3 is heard by the receiver's own knots at round 13. A scheduled
second change after round 7 was unheard; a referee found a second change after round 4 at another linked member that
cancels the first's response (17, 29, 29, 17 own knots in round 15 with neither, either, both).

Design A: in those 28 pairs (caps three and four), a first change at the nearest or second-nearest linked far member
after round 3; then, in the once-changed growth, a second change at each of the 8 nearest linked far members after
each round 4 to (first hearing - 1); read whether the receiver keeps the first response (its own births at the first
hearing round equal the once-changed growth's) and later differs again from both the unchanged and the once-changed
growth, persisting to the end of the read (32 rounds). Prediction: no such pair (second changes either cancel or merge
into the first response), because the two changes travel along the same linked class and arrive at the same boundary.

Design B: does any law of the record with no number set by hand make a knot short after the start? My argument: under
any ration with f >= D, next round's groups are exactly the demand roster's (fresh distinctions create no sharing,
takes are read before D), so owe = D <= f = h for every new knot: no knot made after the start is ever short; only
start knots can be. Rations with f < D are the caps, "just enough for three places" (FLOOR1, f = max(3 - p, 1)), the
"one fewer" rations and the parent-owing rations (an offshoot receives what its parent was owed). Test: the census of
receivers against five senders under the ordinary count with each recorded take, and under FLOOR1 and the parent-owing
rations: short knots made after round 1, linked pairs, far changes after round 3 heard. Prediction: none under any
f >= D law; under FLOOR1 and the parent-owing rations, shortages but no linked pair hears a later far change.

Questions: is the argument in B sound (any gap: the demand roster's placeholder holdings, a take after D, waiting
members joining new groups, parity's implied births with f = 0)? Which of FLOOR1 and the parent-owing rations count as
"no number set by hand" in a fair reading? Which prediction fails? Under 400 words.
