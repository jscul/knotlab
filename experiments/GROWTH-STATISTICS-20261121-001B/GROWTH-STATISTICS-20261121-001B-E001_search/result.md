# Independent starts found more late growth signatures

Across 48 paired runs per law, independent starts reproduced more distinct late signatures than diversity-seeking edits at the same CPU allowance. The result held at equal completed proposal counts. Every arm found at least one signature absent from the reference dictionary and verified that the first two birth counts could miss a later difference. The smallest such pair gives first counts of five and five in both starts, then three births from eight loose distinctions and four from seven. The proposed loose-count predictor agreed on all three fresh pairs with unequal third counts.

## Smallest verified examples in this sample

The smallest verified count collision under both laws uses two four-knot starts with meetings `01, 02, 03`, each with eleven distinctions.

| Loose counts at the four founders | First two births | Loose after round two | Third-round births | Births saved by sharing parents |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `(0,2,3,3)` | 5, 5 | 8 | 3 | 5 |
| `(1,2,2,3)` | 5, 5 | 7 | 4 | 3 |

The first start's third round has one two-parent and two three-parent births. The second has two one-parent, one two-parent and one three-parent birth. Every loose distinction is spent in a funded round, so subtracting the sum of each birth's parent count minus one from the loose supply gives the birth count. In this pair the extra joins outweigh the extra loose distinction, reversing the proposed predictor. The compact and literal engines agree in both laws, with parity closing after its fourth-round single birth on the first start. All source descriptions and parent/fresh count histograms are recorded in the [search summary](../../../artifacts/GROWTH-STATISTICS-20261121-001B/GROWTH-STATISTICS-20261121-001B-E001_search/search-summary.json).
