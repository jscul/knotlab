# Referee report · Settling after the last fall in births

**Confirmed with fixes. No Block.** Both frozen predictions hold on the checkable sample. The point law had 16 distinct late starts on which it could fail. A nonlate start contradicts the unrestricted claim that the view always settles within three rounds of the last fall.

## What holds

1. **Sample and population.** I recovered the two samples in their recorded order. The first seed, 20261126, selects 1,000 of the 82,656 six-knot starts. The second, 20261127, selects 2,000 of the remaining 81,656 without replacement. The weighting gives each census start up to renaming equal weight. Under both laws the same 1,223 starts stall and a further eight are labelled unread. Under not-all-equal 769 receive a label. Under parity 198 of those close, leaving 571. [Census, seeds and law comparison](sample_check.json)
2. **Point law.** The reading counts births by kind, number of parents, fresh distinctions and places. The second reading adds the sorted descriptions of the parents. For a completed-run dimension label $D$, the settling cut $K$ is the earliest permitted round whose last $D$ counts for each kind predict every later saved count by a polynomial of degree at most $D-1$. The search begins at $\max(3,D)$ and needs two later rounds. $T$ is the round that receives the last strict decrease in total births, or 1 if there is none. $M$ is the larger of the most live start knots sharing a neighbour and the largest fresh count at a first-round birth. Among the checkable starts, **16/16 under not-all-equal and 2/2 under parity** have $K=T+3$ when $K>M+5$. The very same starts have $K=T+5$ when $K>M+7$ in the reading with parent descriptions. All have dimension label two. Fourteen fall to one birth and close under parity. The other two fall to four and eight births and give identical readings under the two laws. [Exact recount](recount.json) · [Last falls and joins](overlap.json)
3. **Combined bound and tightness.** All **751/751** checkable not-all-equal starts and **553/553** checkable parity starts meet both bounds, $\max(M+5,T+3)$ without parent descriptions and $\max(M+7,T+5)$ with them. Equality holds on 44/751 and 8/553 in each reading. All four slack distributions and both empty lists of breaks reproduce. Every accepted fit has nonnegative counts throughout the rounds checked. Invalid forecasts at a premature cut cannot earn a pass here because settling requires equality with the observed count of every kind. [All scores, slack and signed forecast checks](recount.json)
4. **Earlier fitting evidence.** The four fitting archives contain 3,304 dimension-labelled start–law records. All meet the combined bound through their last saved round. All 47 late not-all-equal starts meet both point values, and 34 reach one birth at their last fall. Across all earlier not-all-equal starts with a strict fall, 794/815 have the two offsets. On the 47 late starts, subtracting the number of live **start** knots plus first-round offshoots from $T$ gives −7 to +1. This tests the proposed total member count. No static prediction of $T$ was frozen. [Fitting recount](fitting.json)

## How it was checked

The archive recount uses exact integer differences and checks every field in both result files against 4,000 start–law records, 33,302 saved rounds and 6,522,079 births. The independent growth implementation determines joins from the knots that share a neighbour, spends their loose distinctions, assigns fresh distinctions under each law and carries the actual parents into the next round. It reproduces all 4,000 first rounds or stalls and all values of $M$. Across the timed pilot, stratified sample and extensions, direct replay reached the saved end of 2,655 distinct records, including all 2,446 immediate stalls and all 18 late outcomes. The 209 positive-length records account for 3,982 saved rounds and 2,395,688 births. No study growth engine or analysis module was imported. The census check uses the shared atlas for its original order and independently checks its coverage against all connected numbered meeting patterns.
