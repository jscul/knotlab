# Which private distinction will a fixed child receive?

A newly grown sender can recognize a lone zero among two ones, or a lone one among two zeros. On this sender, that role does not identify which mark the fixed child will receive. With a completely anonymous bank of three, a deterministic rule cannot even single out an unequal pair for exchange. With a bank of exactly two, the unequal pair itself is recognizable and a real exchange reverses every ensuing payment. The value at an unmarked destination remains uncertain. A supplied unordered pair and a supplied destination mark have different, demonstrable powers.

## Why reading values can coexist with identical received distributions

**Finite-bank lemma.** Let a newborn have n simultaneously private fresh bits. Conditional on its actual paid inputs, the law of its fresh word is invariant under permutations of those n positions. Allow any request-specific, possibly randomized update that (i) respects such permutations on the unpointed bank and (ii) preserves the number of ones. Random choices have no hidden correlation with allocation or future completions. Keep all other holdings and values fixed at the offer. Under the fresh-word weighting, every downstream observation formed by the ordinary growth and read rules has the same distribution under either request, provided it receives no source decision, admission flag or pre-action address correlation.

To prove this, condition on the *entire* predecessor state, the fixed holdings and payment allocation, and the fresh assignments of the other newborns at K's cut. This includes H’s payment h and preserves shared ancestry. The fresh assignments for siblings are independent given the past. At each possible count k, every arrangement of the k ones has the same conditional mass. Permutations act transitively on those arrangements. Applying a permutation-respecting kernel to their uniform distribution gives another permutation-invariant distribution, supported on the same arrangements by count preservation. The only such probability distribution on this finite set is uniform. This holds separately at every possible k, for either request. Consequently the conditional law of the *whole* post-offer K word is the same, with its positions again attached to the fixed physical allocation. The remaining ordinary steps use the same rules and fresh-word kernels on these identical joint laws. Their induced distributions, including local finishes, agree. Averaging over the conditioned causes proves the lemma.

Local refusal can be represented by leaving the entire word in place. A policy may condition on occupancy, the older inputs and comparisons with their values. As long as the resulting kernel has the two stated properties, the proof applies even within a common pre-offer count cohort. Filtering on a request-dependent announcement or on an action having occurred changes the experiment. Separate local tie-breaking by an independent symmetric draw can give a genuine physical action on a particular trial. Equal observation laws do not assert that two interventions have the same consequences on that trial. They give equal posterior odds for the independent requests at *every* positive-probability received history. To establish particular-payee role ambiguity requires the preceding fixed-allocation construction or another realizable pair, in addition to equality of probabilities.

For example, on a mixed three-bank a uniformly selected unlike pair contains the singleton with certainty and each majority member with probability 1/2. On the *same* prefix every selected exchange differs physically from hold. Depending on q, the exchange can always reverse G's payment or can reverse it on only one of the two draws. The sender always knows that two of the three actual future payments have reversed. The finite-bank lemma still applies. It also applies when q belongs to a supplied unordered pair and the action only exchanges that pair or leaves it. In this case use permutations of the two inside marks, conditioning separately on the outsider and the pair's number of ones. If the pair differs, its exchange reverses q on every such prefix. The role reaching G and the requested choice cannot be inferred with certainty from this blind reversal.

## Spending an outsider or addressing the payment

Denote the supplied inside pair by P, its outsider by e, and the majority value across the three marks by b. K is told that G receives one of P. Let d be the chance that P is mixed and p the chance that the three-bank is mixed. At a mixed full bank two of its three pairs are discordant, so d=2p/3. The [published readiness calculation](../../../research/SUCCESSOR-RECEIPT-20261122-001/later_source_joint.json) gives the mass of one ordered orientation, d/2. Thus d is 1/2 under parity and 61741/115248 under not-all-equal. The corresponding p is 3/4 or 61741/76832. The counts here are per original launch, under the common intact preparation.

* **Literal exchange crossing the marked pair.** If P is mixed, exactly one member differs from e. Exchanging that member with e makes P equal to (b,b). If P is equal, it already contains (b,b) and the protocol silently refuses. Therefore the exchange request pays b on *every* prefix, independently of the orientation of P. An actual exchange occurs with probability d and the physical q changes with probability d/2. On a mixed pair the sender knows in advance what the final value will be, though it does not know whether q is the member it must change. In contrast, hold leaves q in its original position. On a homogeneous pair the value is known even before the request.
* **Conditional writer using that same subset.** For desired m, hold when P=(m,m), exchange the outsider on a mixed P if its value equals m, and otherwise refuse. This certifies the desired payment precisely for m=b. For any fixed three-bit inventory there are at least two copies of only one value. No count-preserving action can put two copies of the other value in P. Conversely, at m=b the prescribed legal action does so or holds the pair already equal. Thus no single prefix lets this writer *guarantee both desired values* while either inside member could be the payee. When q actually is the minority in a mixed pair, one desire happens to be paid even under refusal and the other by a genuine correction. This accident occurs with probability d/2 across prefixes. At independent equal desire odds, certification and refusal each have mass 1/2, an actual exchange has mass d/2, and a change at q has mass d/4. The actual payment matches the desire with probability 1/2+d/4. The original full-bank lemma does not apply across this pointed boundary: the procedure changes the number of ones *in P*. Merely observing the singleton value in an unmarked bank supplies no physical subset known to contain q.
* **Addressed writer.** When shown q in advance, hold if q=m. Otherwise swap q with an opposite mark when one exists, using the declared independent tie rule, and refuse on a uniform bank of the wrong value. On every mixed prefix, the two desires are both made physical at q on the same starting state, regardless of the ordering of the other marks. On a homogeneous prefix the single available value is forced by the inventory. At equal desire odds hold has mass 1/2, exchange and changed q each have mass p/2, and refusal has mass (1−p)/2. The desired payment occurs with probability (1+p)/2. For a fixed desire m the corresponding quantities are Pr(q=m), Pr(q≠m and the bank is mixed), and Pr(the bank consists entirely of 1−m). This controller gains a genuine address and a value-dependent action; it need not start from a prepared 01 pair.

## The actual two-private-mark comparison

For a two-mark newly born sender the unique unordered pair is its whole private bank. When the bits differ, the one agreeing with an inherited reference and the one disagreeing are individually recognizable. Hold and exchange are both genuine choices from the very same complete word. Exchange reverses the initial value of **each** subsequent payment, including the one going to the already fixed child, without predicting which relative role the child initially receives. Swapping the two assignments at this birth while keeping all previous values and the allocation fixed exchanges those explanations.

In the one-founder fixture, three first children each get one real paid input and exactly two private marks. The enrolled sender pays its two marks to two distinct real second children. For parity the inherited bit is fair, and its fresh pair differs exactly when the inherited bit is 1. The exchange therefore moves on 1/2 of original launches. For not-all-equal each inherited value permits three fresh pairs, two of them unequal, giving 2/3. Every actual exchange changes the fixed child's paid value. This smaller receiver has one fresh bit. At the same complete prefix under parity, exchanging an unequal pair flips that bit for certain. Under not-all-equal, a common fresh bit opposite the companion input always permits both source choices. The remaining exchange requests refuse on the equal pair, which still remains in the population. In both laws, exchanging an unprepared whole two-mark bank preserves the conditional distribution given the founder, its inherited reference and all other first children. Therefore the new receiver's best independent-request guess is 1/2 under either law, even conditional on a genuinely unequal pair and through its actual local finish.
