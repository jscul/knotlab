# The complete finite classification from allowed words

In a connected finite not-all-equal presentation, let every distinction occur at one or two different holders, let every holder contain at least three distinct names, require the full local not-all-equal projection on each, and require each individual rule to be essential. Both presentations under comparison satisfy these hypotheses. There is exactly one type of named ambiguity up to renaming. It has four three-place holders on six names, and both choices are closed. Every other allowed relation in this class has exactly one named physical presentation. Consequently every allowed relation in the class determines every later birth under the given ordinary law.

## Two observations on the states

For distinct x and y, look for three allowed words with one common assignment on every other name and respective values 01, 10 and 11, while 00 is forbidden in that same assignment. This is a test on the *whole named relation*. Its positive pairs are precisely the pairs of names held together. Its easy direction follows because any rule violated at 00 must contain each name capable of repairing it. The converse uses arity at least three, two holders at most, local surjectivity on every nonconstant word, and a private erasure witness for each rule. It works across repeated meetings. Write N[x] for x together with all its partners in this test. [Proof of the three-word test and direct controls](recovery_argument.md)

Here is a direct proof of the converse. Pick two names x,y of H, pin the other places of H to 0 and remove H. All four patterns on x,y now extend: 01,10,11 by the full local projection on H, and 00 by a word violating H alone, complemented if necessary. If some other holder also contains both names, start from any extension of 11 with H's other places 0. Changing either of those two 1s to 0 now gives that other holder and H a mixed pair. Nothing else containing either name changes. Thus these three words have one common exterior. If there is no such other holder, remove x and y from their respective other holders, if present. An assignment that satisfies these shortened holders also satisfies their originals for every choice of x,y. Each shortened rule retains at least two original places. At most two full two-place rules have been introduced.

To show the shortened rules admit a common completion, repeatedly enforce a remaining unassigned place whenever all other places of its holder are fixed to one common colour. Force the opposite colour there. A rule that fires is permanently satisfied. If this process reaches a monochromatic rule, every shortened row used in the deduction has supplied at most one forced value or the final conflict. For each such row reinstate its missing x or y with the colour of the fixed places that justified its deduction. This keeps that deduction valid in the original row. The two choices of x,y can be made independently because their remaining holders are different. The same conflict would occur for one of the four patterns already known to extend with H removed. Thus forcing stops without conflict.

After forcing, unresolved holders have at least two free places. Those already containing a fixed value require only its opposite; the others require both colours. Temporarily give a free name used only once its own unrestricted second endpoint. In every connected part, alternation along a walk using each remaining name once colours all holders in both colours apart from a possible closed odd sequence of two-place holders. For details, if some counts are odd add a temporary holder meeting every odd-count holder or end once, start there and alternate. At each formerly odd physical holder of count at least three, deleting the added name leaves an intact pair of opposite values. If all counts are even, start at a holder of count at least four when present; it keeps an intact pair even if the first and last colours agree. An even two-place sequence alternates. In an odd two-place sequence choose a holder with an already fixed value and give it the required opposite colour twice, alternating elsewhere. Such a holder must exist: a wholly unpinned two-place holder was one of at most two shortened rows, while a closed odd sequence requires at least three different holders because a name cannot touch one holder twice. Free names held nowhere may be filled arbitrarily. This proves a common exterior for 01,10,11 in every case. Restoring H makes 00 forbidden. The independent proof and its colouring source are recorded with the literal checks. [Detailed argument](recovery_argument.md)

There is another short state-only test. Two distinct names x and y have **exactly the same physical holders** if and only if overwriting their values in any allowed word by 01, or by 10, always leaves an allowed word. If their holders agree, those holders all see both values after the overwrite. In the other direction, suppose a holder contains x and omits y. Its full local projection extends the word on that holder with x its only 1. Overwriting x by 0 and y by 1 destroys the rule. Interchange x and y if only y has an extra holder. Write T(x) for x together with the names passing this test with x.

Distinct physical supports are incomparable. A larger support containing a smaller one could not project onto its full law, since the smaller support can be constant in a mixed word on the larger. Repeated identical supports would both be implied. Therefore a loose x is recognized by the exact not-all-equal projection on N[x], which is its one holder. For a shared x between H and K, the set N[x] is their union and strictly contains H. Its projection omits some mixed words because H must see both values.

This result also proves the suggested reading by individual changes. Fix a name x and collect all forbidden words that become allowed when x alone is flipped. The union of all their possible one-name repairs is N[x]. A positive three-word test supplies both x and each actual partner as repairs of the same forbidden word. Conversely, every pair of distinct repairs must occur together in each rule violated there. A full-law candidate containing x and ruling out **all** these forbidden words must contain every repair, since otherwise the corresponding one-name change would leave that candidate violated. For a loose x the only such candidate is its true holder. For a shared x there is none, because a candidate cannot properly contain one of the two true holders. The frozen test succeeded on all 5,981 loose occurrences and correctly rejected all 16,502 shared occurrences through ten, counting each presentation once up to renaming. ## One recognized holder suffices

Once a holder H is known, take any shared x in it. The only other holder K of x is read exactly as

$$
K=(N[x]\setminus H)\cup T(x).
$$

The first set is K outside H. Every distinction of their intersection is held by the same two holders as x, and every distinction with those two holders lies in the intersection. To recognize a shared x of H one may simply test whether N[x] has a name outside H. Apply the formula at every newly read holder. Connectedness yields the entire presentation. In particular, **one loose distinction determines all named holders** and an open presentation cannot share its states with a closed one.

## A repeated meeting supplies a starting holder even when everything is shared

Suppose every distinction is shared and x and y belong to the same two holders. The overwrite test recognizes this physical fact in every rival presentation. Set x=0 and y=1. This discharges exactly their two incident rules from the conjunction. All other supports are unchanged. They retain the full local projection because one can overwrite these two names in every original word. An old witness for the essentiality of any surviving row still violates only that surviving row after the overwrite, since it does not contain x or y. In the fixed slice, discard any names left completely free. Such names are read from the states because in every physical holder its full projection includes a word with each specified name its only 1, whose individual flip is forbidden.

There must be at least one surviving holder. A connected closed one-holder presentation is impossible, and two closed holders would have identical supports and be redundant. Along an original sequence of meetings out of the removed pair, the first surviving holder gains a loose name formerly shared with one of those removed. In the reduced state relation the loose-name test recovers this surviving holder, even when other surviving parts have become disconnected. The same test gives the same actual support in every rival. It survives with its full original names, so it is a common holder in the original relation. The propagation formula above yields the entire original presentation. Thus **every closed presentation with a repeated meeting is unique**. Absence of repeated meetings in a closed presentation is equally detectable by the overwrite test.

## The closed presentation with all meetings single

Every name now belongs to a distinct pair of holders. Any set of names that pairwise occur together therefore describes distinct two-element sets whose intersections are pairwise nonempty. Such sets either have a member in common or there are just three, one for each pair among three objects. Indeed two sets may be written ab and ac. A set missing a must then be bc. No fourth distinct pair can meet all three unless the original collection had a member in common after all.

If a holder in one presentation has four or more names, those names in a rival must share a common holder. Its support cannot properly contain the original, so the two holders are equal. Propagation again gives full equality. The same argument applies starting from either presentation. Thus in a genuine ambiguity *all* holders in both are ternary. No holder can occur in both presentations. In the rival, each triple must consist of the three different pair names among three actual mutually meeting original holders.

Take a name shared by original holders A and B. Its two rival triples must come from two different common neighbours C and D of A and B. Both A and B now have all three of their meetings accounted for. Apply the same argument to the name joining A and C. Its common neighbours must be B and D, so C also meets D. Each of these four holders now has three meetings inside this set, exhausting its entire support. Connectedness rules out any other holders. The original four triples are the three pair names incident to each one of the four holders. The only possible rival consists of the four triples avoiding them.

Conversely, in the original every one of four holders sees both values. Look at the pairs on which the value is 1. Each holder occurs in one or two of those pairs. Thus the ones are either two disjoint pairs, the complement of two disjoint pairs, or a three-pair sequence that meets every holder and has no holder in all three. There are 3, 3 and 12 such words. Each of those 18 words also gives both values in each avoiding triple, and the complement pairing that exchanges the two presentations gives equality. Each physical triple has its six nonconstant words locally. Removing any rule gives 26 global words, as checked directly in the earlier census. Every name is held twice, so neither presentation can give a birth. There are 15 relabelled versions of the state set on six fixed names, and exactly two presentations for each. [Direct six-name words and erasures](../READS-HOLDERS-SOURCES-20261120-001B/census.md)

This last step is related to the literature's line-graph reconstruction theorem. The proof here uses its elementary pair-intersection argument directly with named holdings. The two preceding steps explain how the full state relation gives the needed co-holding information and handles repeated meetings. [Whitney, *Congruent graphs and the connectivity of graphs* (1932)](https://doi.org/10.2307/2371086)

## Growth and scope

These are results for the finite, connected, locally full, irredundant two-holder model. A loose name can be read even in a connected part of a larger source. If arbitrary disconnected starts were admitted, taking the closed ambiguity beside a live part would give different holdings in a presentation with loose names. Essentiality and full local projection are also substantive restrictions. The prior duplicate-holder comparison and the seven-name five-place alternative test them separately. [Previous controls](../READS-HOLDERS-SOURCES-20261120-001B/results.md)

Given the supplied complete-step rule, a uniquely recovered presentation determines all future parent lists and fresh counts, and both exceptional presentations already end. A reached ordinary not-all-equal stage can also be presented afresh. Every old word extends through the new rules at their own fresh names, old erasure witnesses survive, and the new rules are essential. Each new physical holder's full mixed words also extend. To see the last point, assign any desired pattern to its old inputs. They are loose, at most one per old parent, so at least two places remain in each parent. Colour remaining distinctions to satisfy all old parents in both colours wherever possible. In an exceptional odd closed component of two-place remainders each holder has lost one input; choose one holder and let its two remaining places both oppose its assigned input, while every other holder alternates. The specified inputs therefore extend. The fresh places of the chosen holder can take any values consistent with its own rule, and the other newborns can each be satisfied at a fresh place. All structural bounds persist. Every nonempty funded step gives each newborn at least one fresh, loose distinction. Consequently its reached stage has uniquely recoverable physical holdings even in isolation: the closed six-name exception cannot be the product of such a step. [Earlier complete-step and essentiality proofs](../READS-HOLDERS-SOURCES-20261120-001B/recovery.md)

The primary all-size conclusion concerns **predicting growth after the ordinary law is given**. The reconstruction uses logical properties of the allowed words. Those words by themselves supply neither a command to grow nor the ordinary prices for fresh distinctions. The argument does not derive the law from logic or choose among admissible rival growth laws.
