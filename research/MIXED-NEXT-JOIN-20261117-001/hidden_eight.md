# The same states can allow different next joins

With the original eight betweenness distinctions attached, bond distances alone cannot tell whether a complete next round can run. Two legal ways of growing the same attachment give exactly the same 524,288 states on the same 36 distinctions. One permits eleven simultaneous joins. The other runs short of loose distinctions at two junctions. This is a proved finite counterexample. The ambiguity involves four of the original distinctions. Call them a, b, c and d. In each of the eight allowed original states, a and d differ exactly when b and c differ. The growth rule permits joining the same three fresh distinctions to either pair. The parity constraint on those fresh distinctions is identical in every old state. Choosing a pair uses up its two distinctions for later joins. Both systems preserve every original state, and revealing which eight are original still leaves the ambiguity.
