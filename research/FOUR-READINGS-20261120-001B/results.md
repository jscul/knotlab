## What the states determine

A *holding* is a physical knot together with the distinctions it holds. Its local law must be precisely its projection of the allowed states. At any specified set of distinctions the allowed states determine the projected relation, and therefore decide K1–K3 and whether it is exactly the given knot law. They can leave open whether a physical holder uses that set, how many holders use it, and which holdings participate as parents. In the three-place example the individual projections allow the four even words under parity or the six mixed words under not-all-equal. Each passes K1–K3. The one-holder start has three loose distinctions, while in the two-holder start all three have a second holder. The named disagreement counts on the three pairs are all 2 for parity or all 4 for not-all-equal in both preparations. Even supplying the entire state set leaves this first birth question unresolved. ## All parity changes that preserve the states

Temporarily give every loose distinction a second holder, the bookkeeping exterior. If there are no loose distinctions, the exterior has no holdings. A choice of ordinary holders gives the set of distinctions it holds an odd number of times. These sets form exactly the binary sums of the written parity equations. The even allowed words are their common nullspace. Thus two named presentations have identical states exactly when they have the same sets of parity checks, equivalently the same inclusion-minimal nonempty check supports (bonds). Every nonzero check contains a minimal such check, and subtracting it strictly reduces support, so the bonds recover every check. The distinction names are fixed throughout this criterion.

In the literature this is the cycle matroid of the completed graph, characterized by Whitney's 2-isomorphism theorem. For the lawful connected starts here the occupied completed system stays connected after removal of any holder. If removal of an ordinary holder separated two other nonempty parts, the distinctions attaching either part to that holder would give a proper parity check inside its support, violating its full projection. Removing the exterior recovers the connected ordinary start. Apart from the two-holder case, this is the usual hypothesis for Whitney's two-terminal switches. One may detach a part attached at exactly two holders and reverse which of the two it meets. If both terminals have the same side in a proposed parity cut, keep the old assignment of sides. If they differ, complement all internal holders of the switched part. This preserves each named crossing distinction. Such switches and incidence isomorphisms characterize the occupied completions of one named relation, with the isomorphism required to induce the given distinction correspondence. One must also specify which holder serves as exterior, including the possibility of leaving the occupied completion closed. Retain only lawful, connected physical outputs after this choice. In the general theorem that allows one-holder separations as well, parts may also be cleaved and reattached there. Such a separation is excluded here by the projection and connectedness gates. The established theorem classifies equal states. The contribution below identifies the extra information needed for ordinary growth. **Which changes preserve growth?** Compute the first parent roster in each presentation under the matching being tested. On a funded source this lists each witnessed multi-parent birth with its actual parents and has one one-parent entry for each remaining loose distinction. Retain multiplicities of repeated parent sets. Each parent appears exactly as often as it had loose distinctions. All future witnesses are now determined: two first births occur together at an old parent precisely when their two parent lists contain it. These lists supply the entire witness family for the next birth rule. Its demand gives each first birth its fresh count, with the ordinary exception of a sole closing parity birth. At the next step the new parent lists supply the same information again. Induction gives the complete history. Consequently a parity-preserving change with matching terminal outcomes preserves growth exactly when its funded first parent roster admits the required matching. Differences between seed witnesses that generate the same roster can be forgotten. The two zero-birth cases (already closed and initial funding shortage) carry their respective terminal observation. Old inputs used by a particular allocation are interchangeable siblings within their actual holder. Literal named input choices would require an additional rule. **No bounded number of bits suffices for the whole first roster.** Fix \(r\ge3\) leaves and one ordinary common holder S. Each leaf \(i=0,\ldots,r-1\) holds two disjoint sets of named distinctions of sizes \(r+2i\) and \(r+2i+1\). At each leaf freely choose which set also belongs to S. Its other distinctions are loose. These are \(2^r\) physically connected preparations on the same \(4r^2-r\) distinctions. At any leaf, its parity equation says that the parity on its two sets is equal. Switching either set into S therefore preserves *every* named allowed state. Every local projection is the full even relation: after removing a leaf the other leaves still link S to the exterior, and after removing S each leaf remains linked to the exterior. Every ordinary holder support is therefore a bond.

The common holder S witnesses all leaves together. They form all two-parent births and each owes \(r-1\) loose distinctions. Every leaf has enough, and its total number of children is exactly its chosen loose count. The possible counts for different leaves are disjoint. Merely listing the numbers of children of all original parents, with their names erased, recovers all \(r\) binary choices. Thus one named state set has at least \(2^r\) different growth records **even up to renaming**. The lower bound is \(r\) bits at these widths, at least of order the square root of the width along this family. The independent computation found 8, 16, 32 and 64 different records for 3 through 6 leaves. For any specified finite state set with \(k\) distinct admissible whole growth records, its exact fixed-length conditional minimum is \(\lceil\log_2 k\rceil\) bits. A roster is constructive sufficient information. Its index could be encoded as one integer of unrestricted size. The question about bounded information concerns its bit length. This argument concerns all parents and births together. The price of one specified birth has the separate conditional bounds of the fresh-demand study.
