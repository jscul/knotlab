## 1. The law, fixed first

The law is chosen before any pattern and is never changed to make a pattern work.

- **The taking rule: MET** (FD 3.3), the fold study's cheapest record-free rule under which parts meet.
- **Two base laws, both primary under MET.** The fold result's P11 gives, for parts that came apart, STEPS-1 under FLOOR1
  and MET under CAP-3; MET under FLOOR1 has a confirmed case only for the two-knot start's own two parts, which were never
  one part. So MET is registered under both, neither as a swap of the other: **COPY-LAW(t)**, the fold study's name for
  (FLOOR1, t, RELEASE) (not a law of copying), and **CAP-3(t)** = (CAP-3, t, RELEASE), for t = a short knot retires,
  makes offshoots, or waits.
- **STEPS-1** under the same six base laws as the second taking rule; **STEPS-2** for the arm of parts that came apart
  (its cost: three holder steps and two parent steps of the record).
- **The form arm's laws:** MET and STEPS-1 under CAP-3(t) and under **FBP(t)**, (FLOOR1, t, RELEASE) with the fresh count
  by parents: the base laws under which a part with a member holding two can be made after the start's own offshoots.
- **The choice**: each of OLDEST, NEWEST, IN-TURN, BY-COUNT, fixed before the runs, the same rule text for every birth,
  on the registered naming (FD 2.1, the shared distinction e named last). All four use names, so the naming is supplied too.
- Knot laws: parity with IMPLIED, and not-all-equal; every run under both.

The copier is then a **pattern** grown under that law. Nothing here adds a rule for copying.

## 2. The patterns

- **The first pattern X**, the candidate copier; in the owner's first test, the two-knot part (two members holding two
  usable loose distinctions each, joinable through one knot).
- **The second pattern Y**, a part of the live roster after round s, wholly apart from X, not the same as X.
- **Y's places**: the distinctions its members hold after round s. **Y's knots**: its members and every knot a member
  meets.
- Two families of designs (D 2.2): **made** designs, in which X and Y are made in round 1 by two start knots that share
  one distinction (X made, never one part with Y before they meet); and designs in which **Y is present with X from round
  0**: X is then the three-knot start's two founders with their dead knot, a two-knot part present at round 0 (a start
  pattern), and Y's members are met by a dead knot that shares one distinction, e, with X's dead knot.

## 3. Three readings of "two of it", each decided by a program (D 3)

"Form", "built structure" and "values" label the three readings.

- **Form: two parts the same as Y.** At least two parts of the live roster after a round t > s, each the same as Y was
  after round s (SD lines 29–30), each containing a member made after round s.
- **Built structure: two renamings of Y's knots among the made knots.** Two sets of knots made after round s with no
  common knot, each carried onto Y's knots by a renaming that keeps every place, holders inside the set, whether a holder
  lies outside, loose and barred; the strong form also keeps the allowed states on the places.
- **Values: two usable loose made distinctions tied to one place of Y.** A place v of Y, free and tied to no place of X
  after round s, and two distinctions made after round s, each tied to v (equal in every allowed state, or unequal in
  every one) and each usable and loose at the same round read.

A tie is a two-place relation of the allowed states and never a knot (lesson 3.2); a passing knot of three or more places
has no two of its own places tied.

## 4. What makes it a copier in the owner's sense (D 5.1)

For one law (a base law, a taking rule and a knot law) and one stated rule, fixed before the runs and the same for every
member of a stated class, and one first pattern X:
1. **Made by X**: in values, at least one of the two descended from X only or from both; in form and built structure,
   every member, or every knot, of each of the two descended from X only.
2. **Not of the others**: in form and built structure, run A with Y has, after every round read, fewer than two of Y′
   (any descent) for every other member Y′ of the class, and the run with Y′ has two of Y′ made by X at some round read.
   In values the first half holds by construction and the second half is condition 1 for Y′, so condition 2 adds nothing
   in values; X alone is reported.
3. **What appears twice is the second pattern's**: Y is not the same as X; Y's knots are not a renaming of X's; in values
   v is tied to no place of X.
4. **Not the numbering's**: the case survives every registered renaming of the start (D 4.5).
5. **For the whole class**: every member of the class meets 1 to 4 under the same X, law and rule.

Reported for every case, never a condition: the second pattern barred (whether two of Y "need Y's births"), the first
pattern barred (the second pattern handing on its own value, or returning), X alone (Y never there). The copying on
record (`research/COPYING-PARTS-20261123-001/decision.md` lines 13–15) fails conditions 2 and 3: it takes no input, and
what it leaves two of is itself.

## 5. What is claimed, and for which class

**Settled before any test (arguments; the runs check them; P, "Settled by construction"):**

- **The ration's doing.** Under MET or STEPS-1 with COPY-LAW(t) (FLOOR1 with the fresh count by distinct inputs), every
  birth other than an offshoot of a start knot receives exactly one fresh distinction (none under parity when it is
  implied), and FLOOR1 never gives more than two. So every knot made by such a birth holds **at most one** loose
  distinction, and **no part with a member holding two is made at all, copied or not**, except by offshoots of start
  knots; the part that copies itself stops copying. That is the ration's doing and says nothing about leaving two parts the same as a second pattern:
  the form readings are therefore an arm of their own under CAP-3(t) and FBP(t).
- **Not-all-equal: tied.** From a start with no tie, **no made distinction is ever tied to another**, under any taking rule
  and choice and whether or not knots pass. The registered designs have at most two holders per distinction and so no
  tie; the starts with a third pattern, in which e has three holders, have no tie at round 0 either (computed: P, run G).
  So the values reading cannot hold under not-all-equal on them. (From a start with a tie it can: the Fano pattern less
  one knot, with two further knots, gives a made distinction tied to a start distinction.)

**The claims tested, each predicted to fail, each stated before the runs.** For each stated rule r in OLDEST, NEWEST,
IN-TURN, BY-COUNT and each base law b, one b fixed for the whole class:

> **C(r, b), values, X made.** Under MET with b in COPY-LAW(t) or CAP-3(t) and parity, the made two-knot part X of the
> made designs leaves, for every second pattern Y of 𝒴_made, two usable loose made distinctions tied to one place of Y,
> made by X, meeting condition 2, and surviving every registered renaming.

> **C(r, b), values, X a start pattern.** The same for the two-knot part X of the designs with Y present from round 0,
> for every Y of 𝒴_start.

> **CF(r, b), form.** Under MET with b in CAP-3(t) or FBP(t), the same X leaves, for every Y of the form class of its
> family, two parts the same as Y, every member descended from X only, meeting condition 2, and surviving every
> renaming. CF is claimed under MET only; the same reading under STEPS-1 is reported.

The classes:
- **𝒴_made**: the h offshoots, made in round 1, of a second start knot holding h loose distinctions, for h = 3, 4, 5, 6
  (four second patterns, none the same as X; h = 2 is the control, Y the same as X).
- **𝒴_start**: one member holding two; one holding three; two holding two and three; two holding three and three; three
  holding two each, each met by a dead knot that shares e with X's dead knot (five; two members holding two each is the
  control).
- **The form classes, one per family**: the members of 𝒴_made, and of 𝒴_start, of which the form arm's laws make at least
  one part the same, after round s, in some registered run of that family (a census by the study's engine; members it
  never makes are dropped and listed). Seen: all four of 𝒴_made; one-2, one-3 and three-222 of 𝒴_start (two-23 and
  two-33 dropped). made-3's Y and three-222's Y are the same pattern (three members holding two, in one met set), as are
  made-2's and two-22's.

All of these are predicted to fail, from the disclosed runs: under a stated rule, two of a second pattern are seen for one
member of a class at most, and no such case survives every renaming. A failure of every C(r, b) is about one first
pattern, four stated rules and the registered classes; it is no reason that no made pattern can, and for values under
parity the question stays undecided (P, "What a result would and would not show").

**Controls, not claims:**
- **F1, every choice** ("the knot law allows it; the choice decides it"): for each Y, whether some history of takes
  leaves two usable loose made distinctions tied to one place of Y, made by X; registered with its take lists to replay.
  It cannot earn the word copier, since the history is picked.
- **The second pattern handing on its own value** (values) and **Y returning** (form), with X present and with X barred.

- What a second pattern can change, under the registered laws (argued, owner_arm.md B1): **nothing changes a place, or a
  relation among distinctions that exist; what changes is the holders of shared distinctions and, through the course,
  which loose distinctions are taken or barred.** The words of his second sentence stay inside his quotation.

## 7. Verdicts, as three questions (D 5.2)

Who made the two (descended from X only, from both, from Y only, from neither); what fixed the takes (a rule whose case
survives every renaming, a rule whose case does not, a history picked); where X and the linking knot come from (the
growth or the start). A case is **made** only when all three answers are the growth's.

## 8. Law and pattern, kept apart

  second pattern.
- The copier is X, a pattern; what it leaves are knots and distinctions made by births under that law.
- A history picked from every choice is supplied, never made.
- No claim is made about living things, minds, physics or time.
