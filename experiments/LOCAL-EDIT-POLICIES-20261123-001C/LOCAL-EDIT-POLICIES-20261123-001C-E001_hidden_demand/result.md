# Two equally supplied addresses can conceal which needs a spare

**The address that needs the edit is independent of the controller's entire pre-edit record. A message still gets through.** The 62-knot parity preparation gives CHSH $9/4$ on constant addresses, with zero change in either complete endpoint distribution. Have the receiver always add at address 1. The sender encodes zero by adding at the address named by its own old terminal bit and one by adding at the opposite address. The receiver's old terminal and its gain flag then distinguish the two messages with total variation (TV) $1/4$. Both parties use compulsory one-spare edits. This works in either direction.

## Ordinary source and local access

Each side has two editable knots $A_0,A_1$, each with one loose distinction and two shared with its own spent witness $W_i$. Each $W_i$ has one further distinction held with detector $V$ and one, $c_i$, held with $H_i$. The two $H_i$ share two distinctions with each other. Exactly $H_u$ has two private distinctions on Alice's side, and $H_v$ on Bob's. The detector has three private distinctions, including its designated mark, and three data terminals. A setting adds one private distinction to the named address. The binary output is one exactly when the old terminal 0 was one and the persistent mark gained a holder.

The four preparations are equally weighted before either free message. Their three terminal correspondences have eight successive pairs of ternary knots per index. Two fixed correspondences at each end enclose four interchange choices $P^u,Q^v,P^u,Q^v$, where $P$ interchanges 0 and 1 and $Q$ interchanges 1 and 2. The resulting correspondence preserves all three indices in preparations 00, 01 and 10. Preparation 11 sends $0,1,2$ to $1,2,0$. The construction links each hidden type to two interchange choices before the edits.

The complete observed old record includes both $A_i$, both $W_i$, $V$, the nearest entire fixed correspondence, all their held distinctions, their values and every co-holder name. In particular, both $c_i$ and both $H_i$ names are visible. Seeing a co-holder's name does not give its other holdings under the declared access. The second fixed correspondence keeps even the outside guard-holder names fixed. After the round the observer also sees its action, all incident births and their complete holdings, including the newly received $H_u$ inputs and all fresh values. The two detectors are 17 shared-holder steps apart, their marks 18 steps between co-held distinctions, and the closest edit holders 21 shared-holder steps apart. Their live parts have no path through witnessed pairs. An $H_i$ is two shared-holder steps from its own address through $W_i$. Extending access to its support lets the controller see the shortage.

The two witnesses produce four pairs on each side with parents $A_0,V$ and $A_1,V$, and also $A_u,H_u$ and $V,H_u$ on Alice's side. Bob's pairs use $v$. The richer witness meets its three live neighbors together. Their three pair proposals each take one use from each parent. Thus $A_u$ owes two while $A_{1-u}$ owes one, although both supply one before the edit. $V$ owes three and $H_u$ owes two. A mismatch or a withheld spare leaves one shortage at its own address. The ordinary whole round funds exactly when both correct addresses are chosen.

The old source has 109 distinctions. After two matching edits, eight knots are born. On each side the $A_u,H_u$ pair receives one fresh distinction and the three other pairs each receive two. Every birth has two parents. The funded stage has 70 knots and 125 distinctions. Each old, edited and grown support passed K1–K3 on its whole-presentation allowed relation. The literal replay evaluates the idle and compulsory actions, the commuting correspondences, transported names and a swap of two detector offers. It also independently contracts the endpoint relations and checks the parity count by row rank. ## Exact concealment, including values received on a funded trial

First fix every bit in the observer's old record. The two unobserved $H$ holders communicate with that record only through $c_0,c_1$. Enumerating their two shared bits and the active holder's two private bits gives 4 completions for every parity port pair. Not-all-equal gives 12 for equal ports and 11 for unequal ports. Both tables are identical under interchanging the active and spent $H$ holders. The full visible incidence, including each outside name, is also identical.

For the rest of the source, one pair of ternary knots has terminal completion matrix $K=2I$ under parity. Not-all-equal has 3 on the diagonal and 2 off it. Summing the local device at fixed detector triple gives $M(a)=1024$ in parity. Not-all-equal has $M(a)=43136$ for a constant triple and 44528 for the other six. These weights are invariant under permutations and independent of the active address. With the three outer guard bits fixed at $z$, the seven remaining pairs per index and the far device give exactly the same number of outside completions for each hidden-type pair. The only effect of the prepared interchanges on this sum is to permute the summed far triple. Multiplying this outside count by the local $H$ completion count gives the exact old multiplicity of **every particular lawful complete visible record**. Consequently the posterior chance that either one named address is required stays $1/2$ for every old record, under both laws.

More generally the pairs of old records have a joint distribution obtained by weighting their two outer guard triples, then filling in each full near record independently from its local conditional law. The conditional laws do not depend on the hidden type. The separate terminal–terminal, terminal–guard, guard–terminal and guard–guard counts use eight, seven, seven and six pairs per index, respectively. This distinction changes the observable not-all-equal signal. If the round stalls, the later record consists of the local edit and the unchanged holdings. On success the active $H$ index on that side equals the observer's own chosen address. Conditional on its observed $c_0,c_1$ and that index, all compatible middle and private $H$ assignments have their original uniform conditional weights. This includes both private inputs to the newly seen births. Conditional on those values and the local old holdings, each birth chooses among its own legal fresh extensions. Every fresh distinction is private to that birth. The normalized choices thus multiply across births. In either outcome the rest of a receiver's record, conditional on its old record, own action and gain, is generated independently of the far policy. The local checking code also compares the two constant far settings atom by atom over both $c$ values, each guard triple, the gain, and each pair of hidden middle and private bits on success. This proves sufficiency of the guard triple and gain for complete-record TV at a fixed receiver address and specified sender policy. ## Sending a message without knowing readiness

With receiver address 1 fixed, the receiver reads its old marker $b$ and observed gain $F$. Order these two-bit outcomes as $(b,F)=00,01,10,11$. Under parity the sender's instructions “address named by my old bit” and “opposite address” give $(5,3,7,1)/16$ and $(7,1,5,3)/16$. The event $b\ne F$ has probabilities $5/8$ and $3/8$. The gain alone remains one with probability $1/4$ for either message. The published answer $bF$ changes from $1/16$ to $3/16$. At receiver address 0 every one of these comparisons gives zero variation.

These are exact for this pair of instructions, in both directions. The not-all-equal complete variation is approximately $0.00000322622$. The sender's decision uses only its own old terminal 0. The joint table of the two parties' old records can depend on the prepared types even though each complete local marginal is type independent. The edit turns that joint dependence into an observable gain correlated with the receiver's old value. The companion policy experiment optimizes over the full menu. With commuting interchanges, the four complete joint pre-record distributions agree, so every compulsory local sender policy gives zero variation in the full receiver's record. The fixed-address CHSH becomes 2 in parity and $2726736570435298431202118/D$ in not-all-equal. With no old values available to choose an address, the constant compulsory policies also give zero on the primary source. Allowing the sender to withhold its spare produces full TV $1/4$ and published-output TV $1/8$ against a fixed addition, at either fixed receiver address under either law. Revealing the hidden supports permits the sender to repair or miss its actual shortage. The full TV is then $1/2$ at a fixed receiver address and 1 when the receiver also repairs. Biasing Bob's hidden preparation to probability $3/4$ for type one instead gives Alice full TV $1/4$ and output TV $1/8$ under the two constant Bob addresses. Bob's variations remain zero.
