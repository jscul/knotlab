## Fixes

1. **Block — the line-wide necessity claim.** The decision says “On every history read in it, a far change made after both patterns exist reaches the receiver late only where a join owed four is given three.” Under the earlier cap of four, three mutually sharing receiver knots each held two loose distinctions. I used the far pattern from the first linked-pair draw and added one loose distinction after round 3, nine sharings away. The first join owed five appeared in round 7 and received four. Before the hearing, 109 changed-growth births were owed at least five, and all 266 changed-growth joins owed four received four. The receiver made **27 own births unchanged and 46 changed in round 13**. Both growths continued. Giving every join owed five its full five removed the hearing through round 14. Giving those joins three preserved the round-13 hearing. These controls changed only that allocation and marked the same descendant. HALF itself had no hearing through round 14 on this cap-four preparation. [Exact sentence in the decision](../decision.md#what-stands) · [Earlier cap-four finding](../../LINKED-UNDER-CAP-20261126-001/decision.md#what-stands) · [Counterexample and controls](reading.json)

   A second counterexample uses one of this study's own census preparations. Its four-knot receiver meets a one-knot sender through the same two link knots. Under the earlier rule giving only the minimum fresh count, every join receives one. All three far additions after round 3 were seven sharings away. Each changed the receiver's own births from **82 to 83 in round 9**, with further births in both growths. In the no-take, not-all-equal setting the ordinary count still funds every join's demand. The Block concerns the asserted necessity of one particular underfunding. 2. **Narrowing — equal hearing histories do not imply equal pairs.** The decision says “On every pair where HALF hears, its whole record equals the cap's: there every birth it holds back is owed exactly four, where HALF gives three. Where HALF departs from the cap (145 census pairs, births owed five or more) neither hears.” **173 of 505** pairs differed at an allocation. The 145 already differed in unchanged growth. Another 28 differed only on changed growths with equal saved summaries. Two of those 28 pairs also heard a *different* addition. In the three-knot switch receiver against the mutually sharing three-knot sender, an addition after round 1 was heard in round 9. An addition after round 3 was silent but reached a join owed five in round 8, given four by HALF and three by the cap. The archive stores no changed birth counts for a silent addition. Equality of its recorded outcomes on the four hearing pairs stands. [Exact sentences in the decision](../decision.md#what-stands) · [Per-pair witnesses](allocation_census.json)

3. **Narrowing — where PLACES first differs, and which difference matters.** The assumptions register says “The statement's last sentence was wrong: joins are given two to four distinctions, and PLACES differs from the cap at joins given three and owed four, not at offshoots (logged in `docs/CORRECTIONS.md`).” The join describes the first difference on all 124 linked-pair hearing histories. In the census, **933 first-round offshoots** differed, 913 owed three and 20 owed four. Each received two under PLACES and three under the cap. In two of the six census hearing histories the first difference was such an offshoot. On all six matched additions, changing just the cap's offshoots to PLACES preserved the first hearing. Giving four instead of three to each join given three and owed four removed all six through round 12. The original join diagnosis survives as a class of allocations that decides this comparison. On one linked preparation, giving three **only** to that class while fully funding every other birth also lost the hearing. Giving three to *all* joins owed four restored it in round 13. This isolates the contrast between laws while leaving open which individual join carries an addition. 5. **Not a block — the direction and size of the HALF difference.** The decision says “That a self-read threshold carries a change by allocations the cap does not make: no history was found where HALF, or the hold-back referee's binary-count ceiling, gives less than the cap and a far change is still heard.” The README says “HALF departs from the cap in 145 census pairs (births owed five or more, where it gives four and the cap three), and in those pairs neither law hears anything.” Both demand-based ceilings give **more** than three whenever demand is at least five. HALF gives four at demand five or six and five at demand seven or eight. The rebuilt first census round included 30 births owed seven and five owed eight. The meaningful open test is whether HALF can give **more** than the cap on a changed history before a first hearing and preserve that hearing. ## How it was checked

The new simulator reconstructs the starts and meeting through two link knots, forms each round's groups, lets short members wait, assigns the fresh distinctions and follows descent. It selects the far members again and compares changed growth to its own unchanged growth. The numbered start catalogue and the library procedure for enumerating maximal cliques are shared conventions. The study's growth, tally and parity-recheck routines were not imported. The census runs use 12 rounds and a 1,500-knot bound. The linked runs use 24 rounds and a 6,000-knot bound. Both use the original fourfold guard. The demand-five controls used a 14-round prefix, one round beyond their predicted first hearing. [Starts](starts.py) · [Growth](growth.py) · [Paired replay](replay.py) · [Matched high-demand controls](reading.py)

All computation used the repository interpreter with bytecode disabled. The first complete three-law pair took 0.49 seconds in the census and 2.01 seconds in the linked pairs. The two full allocation checks then used 18.69 and 20.44 CPU seconds. Parity pilots used the same 12 rounds, knot bound, guard and equation treatments as their sample. The eight-record sample used 14.11 CPU seconds and the diagnostic used 6.55. Each computation used one process with an address-space limit of 1–3 GiB. At most six such processes were active together. From this folder, `../../../.venv/bin/python -B verify.py` checks the saved threshold results, draws, controls and parity comparisons. [Verification](verify.py)

Parity regrowth was sampled. The 75 stale-only additions have no renewed archive comparison, and the 279 settings absent from both archives remain not run.
