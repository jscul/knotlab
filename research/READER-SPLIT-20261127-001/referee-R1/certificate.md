# What a reader split certifies

For a connected closed holding, every legal odd split gives a perfect strategy when each reader receives only its own setting. A common state, the signs outside the readers, and this restriction on questions are supplied. Any one knot can be chosen to have full check sign `−1`, with all other full check signs `+1`.

## A realization on the readers

Give **each failing pair** one qubit belonging to its reader. Its first distinction acts by X there and its second by Z. A distinction acts by the product over its pairs. Give each outside distinction its own auxiliary qubit and let it act there by Z. Two distinction operators anticommute exactly when they form a failing pair. All settings in a knot act on disjoint qubits, and the split asks each reader at most one setting. Reversing the choice of X and Z at any pair preserves these properties.

Every nonempty subset of distinctions has a nonscalar product. On a qubit belonging to any chosen distinction, its X or Z occurs once, and its partner can only contribute the other operator. Outside qubits work independently. A product of knot checks can consequently be scalar only if it includes both holders or neither for every distinction. Connectedness leaves the empty selection and the whole holding. The whole product has sign `−1` by the odd choice. Commuting the knot checks preserves that sign under every order.

Omit any one check. The product of the projectors `(I + Q)/2` for the remaining checks has positive trace. In its expansion only the identity has nonzero trace, by the preceding independence. Every outside Z commutes with this projector. At least one joint choice of outside signs therefore leaves a nonzero vector on the readers' qubits. On this vector the free full check has sign `−1`. The target for the readers at a knot is its full sign times the signs of the outside distinctions it holds. Measuring the individual settings gives that target product with certainty, since their product has the stated eigenvalue.

The reader targets multiply to `−1`, since every outside sign appears twice. Every fixed list of local answers gives product `+1`, since every reader setting also appears twice. Shared randomness cannot give certainty on a question distribution that gives each knot positive weight. Announcing the whole knot to a reader would change this comparison. Under the third-reader study's definition no listed knot may join the readers. Here an odd split requires a knot asking at least three distinct readers, by the cancellation argument below. [Earlier definition](../../THIRD-READER-20261126-001/README.md)

The original checker performs exact signed elimination for the chosen strict, general and minimum-reader witnesses. A no-split row has no witness to check. Its saved general results give sizes and a pass flag. The proof above applies to every legal split. [Original witness selection](../readers.py#L661)

## The hand claims

**At least three readers.** Delete distinctions in no failing pair. If every knot contains at most two remaining distinctions, the resulting components are chains or rings, with a doubled meeting allowed. List each component in succession along its chain or ring. Within each knot the values commute, so adjacent copies cancel, including the closing pair on a ring. The product is `+I`. This proves the bound for two readers and rules out the claim that allowing a single reader *up to two* commuting values per knot would suffice. Allowing the reader every value of a whole knot removes that bound. Two failing pairs cannot have odd sign either. For their contributions to the knot-pair checks to cancel, they must have the same four cross-pairs of holders. These determine the two sets of holders up to exchange, so the two signs agree.

**Knots holding at most three distinctions.** For a strict choice, let each active distinction's partner be the one with which it fails. At each knot the list of its partners must have even incidence at every knot, by commutation. Its nonempty list is either a doubled meeting or a ring of three. Thus taking partners preserves which active distinctions meet, in both directions. It pairs or fixes the connected components that remain after inactive distinctions are deleted.

Every active meeting is in a double or a ring of three. With at most three places per knot, the connected possibilities are a double, a triple, a ring of three, a ring of three with one meeting doubled, four mutually meeting knots with one meeting missing, and four mutually meeting knots. To see completeness, a double can only acquire a third copy or a single meeting from both of its knots to one new knot. A three-knot ring can acquire one repeated meeting or two meetings from its knots to a fourth knot. In the latter case the only further meeting possible joins the two knots with a spare place. A connection beyond these lists would require an extra place to complete its own double or ring of three.

The five-meeting case needs a double in its partner component and is the only five-meeting possibility, which contains no double. Components paired with distinct components give even sign. List one before its partner and no failing pair interleaves. Of the possible fixed components, only four mutually meeting knots admit a disjoint partner for each distinction. Each must pair with its opposite, giving odd sign and using every place. In a connected holding with the size bound this is the whole holding. Even units elsewhere can be joined by inactive distinctions if spare places remain. For exactly three strict readers in any holding, deleting inactive distinctions already leaves at most three per knot. The odd four-knot component uses all six active distinctions. This proves the converse for three strict readers at any knot size.

The prediction rationale in the README uses a six-knot even unit as an example requiring larger knots. Take two rings of three, double one meeting in each, and join the two knots opposite the doubles by an inactive distinction. Pair the two distinctions of each double with the two single meetings of the other ring. Each knot holds exactly three. Listing one ring before the other gives even sign. [Prediction rationale](../README.md#L165)

**Odd rings in larger holdings.** Write the length as `n = 2m + 1`. Pair the common knot's meeting at ring knot `j` with the ring meeting whose holders are `j+m` and `j−m` (indices modulo `n`). Every affected pair of knot checks receives two failures. In the order common knot first, then ring order, only the meeting from last to first interleaves its partner. Extra meetings do not alter this construction when all selected pairs are decidable. Here `2m ≡ −1`, so `m` is **minus** the inverse of two. A fixed shift on an even ring fails because its two offsets must be closed under negation. Neither offset may be zero, and consecutive offsets cannot be negatives of each other for even length. An even ring with the additional meeting from knot 0 to knot 2 already admits a strict split on knots 0, 1, 2 and the common knot. The bare-ring case has a stronger conclusion, proved below. [Claim and frozen scope](../README.md#L103)

**Other fixtures.** On two joined rings of three, the three meetings of the first ring require three different colours in a proper three-colouring. Each joining distinction must take the colour of its opposite meeting, as must that meeting's counterpart in the other ring. There is one partition up to colour names. The two odd choices use that same partition. In each triple the value failing with the other two comes from the first ring throughout or the second ring throughout. The literature's Latin squares describe the separate three-by-three holding, where its nonempty choices permitting splits use all three triples from one of two partitions, three even choices per partition. None mixes the partitions. On the five-knot fixture formed by joining two loose distinctions, each of its four odd choices has two members of one failing group at one knot. ## Bare rings, including general splits

Let the common knot meet knot `j` in `s_j`, and let `e_i` join `i` to `i+1`. There are no further meetings. A reader can contain at most one `s`. Commutation of each ring knot with the common knot says that its two ring meetings both fail with an `s`, or neither does. This propagates around the ring. In the latter case only ring meetings can fail and the chain cancellation above gives even sign. In the former case colour each `e_i` by the unique index `f(i)` whose `s` it fails with. Adjacent meetings have different colours, and neither holder of `e_i` is its colour. Possible additional failures join ring meetings of one colour. Call the knots whose index is used **marked**. There are at least three.

Take two ring meetings of colour `j` and multiply the ring checks between them. All interior ring meetings cancel up to sign. The two remaining meetings both fail with `s_j`, so this product commutes with every `s`. It also commutes with each ring check. Its commutation sign with successive ring meetings is therefore constant. For a meeting of colour `k ≠ j`, this sign is negative exactly when the interval contains knot `k`. Thus all other marks lie on one side. All meetings of colour `j` lie in one interval between consecutive marks after removing mark `j`. They can touch at most its two end marks, once each, and cannot touch their own mark. There are two incidences at each mark and one colour for each mark. Equality forces every colour to occupy both end meetings of its interval.

Consider an interval between consecutive marks `p,q` before removing any mark. If a colour other than `p,q` occurs, that colour occupies both ends. Neither endpoint colour can appear, since it would require the meeting at the opposite end, and a second outside colour would also require both ends. Adjacent meetings have different colours, so the interval consists of one meeting. Otherwise its colours alternate `q,p`, beginning with `q` at `p` and ending with `p` at `q`. In this case colour `p` must also reach the preceding mark and colour `q` the following mark. The same alternative propagates to every interval.

The latter alternative is impossible. For a mark `j` with neighbouring marks `p,q`, consider the commutation sign between its check and successive ring meetings. The sign can switch only at `p,q`, the colours of the meetings touching `j`, because its check commutes with every ring check. It is positive next to `j`, hence throughout the interval from `p` to `q` through `j`. Yet all meetings of colour `j` lie there and each fails with exactly `s_j` in that check.

Thus every interval has length one, every knot is marked, and the colours are bijective. No additional ring failures are possible. Join each knot `j` to the two holders of its unique ring meeting of colour `j`. Ring-check commutation makes this relation symmetric, so it consists of disjoint rings. The two neighbours of each knot in this relation are precisely the two holders of one original ring meeting. Taking steps of two must therefore produce the single original ring. An even ring would break into two sets under steps of two. On an odd ring the two-step order determines the one-step order up to reversal, giving exactly the construction above. Every bare odd ring has this single odd split and every bare even ring has none, when all unheld pairs are available. The direct check at lengths 3 through 8 found 1, 0, 1, 0, 1, 0.
