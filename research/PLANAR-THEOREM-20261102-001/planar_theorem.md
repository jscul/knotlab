## Conclusions

| 1 | **Potentials.** On a plane knot space minus a line, integrating a matching against a reference from the removed line's hole gives integer potentials, one per matching. They are exactly the solutions of difference constraints, so they form an L-natural convex set (closed under rounded midpoints). A point's value relative to the hub is the parity of its potential. | proof; 325 removals | A |
| 2 | **The staircase.** At a gluing line, the two non-odd ports of each state differ in potential by one value on that state and by the neighbouring value off it. A port tied to the hub makes the three states a chain with that port's state in the middle. | proof; 4464 states, 877 chains | A |
| 3 | **The access lemma is a theorem.** By rounded midpoints, a pinning by the hub never selects the state of a hub-tied port. With no port tied, the hub and the non-odd ports form a near knot one step from the pinned pattern. So every pinned-and-separated three-point knot through the hub is generated on a plane near side, whatever the far side. | proof; 9020 pinnings on 42 plane spaces | A |
| 4 | **Knots separated by one port are 2-sums.** In any splice, a crossing knot whose excluded pattern one port separates is the elimination, through that port, of its near part plus the port and its far part plus the port. **Scouting after ticket C (not a claim; `handle_locality_scout.py`).**
- **The construction.** The Fano plane is spliced into two 10-point plane spaces, both ways round, giving four non-planar knot spaces of 16
  points with one handle.
- **Gluing line in the plane part.** All 6,056 pinnings by two non-port points have access, even when the removed line lies in the Fano
  part.
- **Gluing line in the Fano part.** 11,376 of 12,368 lose access, even when the removed line lies in the plane part. All 992 that keep it
  have the removed line in the Fano part too.
- **So** access is decided by where the gluing line sits relative to the handle, not where the removed line is.

## Answer

- **The access lemma is proved, for every pinning.** The proof uses one idea: line–face matchings of a plane space carry potentials, and
  their convexity forbids a pinning from reaching past a port.
  - Re-centring at any point gives the same convexity. So on a plane side, the hub is not special.
- **The planar theorem is proved for two classes of crossing knot**, on a plane near side at a six-pattern removal, for any far side:
  - knots separated by one port;
  - knots pinned by two near points and separated by one far point.
- **The full theorem is one lemma short.** The lemma would reduce the residual knots to these two classes. It holds on every splice
  tested, but their routes are heterogeneous: some are generated without access along their pinned pair. So access alone will not finish
  it.
- **The premise holds on all data, in its original form.** All 564 new knots are pinned three-point knots through the hub.
- **Correction.** PLANAR-COMPOSE gave the plane pinning total as 8020. It is 9020: 4560 in its ticket A plus 4460 in its scout.

## Reading

- **Why a plane side composes.** Every point of a plane side reaches the gluing ports when it pins them, because matchings on a plane
  side have convex heights. A pin can therefore always be taken apart one port at a time.
- **What a handle does.** It destroys this for every pinning on the small census spaces. On larger spaces (scout), the damage is local to
  the gluing line: a gluing line in the plane part keeps full access even when the removed line sits in the handle.
- **Doctrine.** Hubs remain equality fan-out. No native path or distance is claimed, and the potentials are a proof device, not a
  magnitude.
