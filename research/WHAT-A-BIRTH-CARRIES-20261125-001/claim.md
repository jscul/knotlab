Epic `WHAT-A-BIRTH-CARRIES-20261125-001` (sub-goal B331; assumptions A223, A241, A244, A245, A248; goals G15 with G18),
ticket `WHAT-A-BIRTH-CARRIES-20261125-001A`.

## The owner's words, and how they are read

The owner asked the thread to work through its four top-valued directions (25 November 2026, by the record's dates).
This epic takes the first, in the thread's words: "What is the least a law must let a birth carry for a made pattern to
read *what* it meets, not only that something is there." Here "carry" is the thread's word for what a newborn holds,
and it is used in no other sense.

His test (A246): "A part reads another when its course differs between the two runs." The guide pairs two questions
under it: **Whether** the other is there, and **Which** it is.

**The fold of emergence.** The owner's phrase (A223): a pattern that the rules make, where what it makes depends on
another pattern, and that makes a pattern of its own. The third property is the last of these.

This ticket asks Which, with the recorded laws only: under the recorded ordinary count and the recorded taking rule MET,
with a choice by names (take the oldest; take the newest as its swap), does the course from X only differ with which
second pattern it meets, whatever the numbering of the start?
- **Which second pattern** means, here, how many of the second pattern's members a hand-placed bar left usable (RD 2.3).
- **Whatever the numbering** means set disjointness over every numbering (RD 4.2).
- The law uses names, so every result is stated as "under take-the-oldest (or take-the-newest), whatever the
  numbering".

## The law, fixed first

ORD, CANCEL-RETIRE, RELEASE, and MET with OLDEST (its swap NEWEST), in FD's round, under both knot laws and the bounds
of RD 1.6. Nothing is changed for a pattern.

**A reversible choice, by the owner's rule for forks** ("we can pursue whichever is most interesting and document the
choice as reversible", 25 November 2026). The rule that takes every candidate is not a member of the search: it gives a
newborn every eligible place of a met knot. It appears only as the planted control (RD 5.2). If the owner wants it in
the search, it enters as a law like any other.

## The claim, per take and per knot law

> **R9-Which(take, knot law).** For h = 3 and for h = 4, there are a ≠ b in 1 … h such that:
> - C(h, a) and C(h, b) are disjoint on the compared prefix (RD 4.2);
> - on every pair (ν, A, B) of the two cells (RD 4.1), both runs have a meeting round (RD 3.4), the same round, and the
>   attribution is "through the meeting" (RD 4.3);
> - every knot of every run of both cells passes the knot test under that knot law.

Reported with it, never conditions:
- follows a, and follows h (RD 4.5);
- handed in one value, composed over several births, or no difference (RD 4.4), and where a count hands it over, that
  the law did;
- needs the second pattern's members usable (RD 4.6);
- the number of distinct courses over the class (RD 4.7);
- FD's trace and the joint births;
- the linking knots and their class (RD 3.5), and whether it is the same meeting (RD 3.6), counted over the pairs;
- run 2's comparison class.

## Settled by argument (argued, not refereed; each can be contradicted by a run)

- **S1.** Under ORD with RELEASE, no member made after round 1 is short, except after a round in which some birth
  received nothing (an implied birth, parity only). Two reasons:
  - RELEASE leaves no old member holding a usable loose distinction;
  - the next round's groups are the demand roster's, and f ≥ D.
  An implied birth that holds nothing can raise another member's owing. So under not-all-equal the treatment never acts
  after round 1. Check: run 1b.
- **S2.** Numberings related by a symmetry of the start give identical runs. The round depends on place sets, holders,
  the record and names, and a symmetry maps the named start onto itself. Check: run G, nearly empty (RD 5.3).
- **S3, round 2 under the registered numbering.**
  - Y_h's members hold h − 1 after round 1 and owe a − 1 in round 2. None is short, and each makes a − 1 joins and
    h − a offshoots.
  - Every round-2 birth takes e: X's join because e is its sole candidate, the others because e is the oldest.
  - So all round-2 newborns meet one another in the demand step, and every round-2 birth from X only has D = f = 2 + M.
- **S4 (the advisor's argument, checked by hand against RD and the gate listing).** Write J for X's round-2 join.
  1. J's sole candidate is e, in every numbering and under both takes. J's parents meet only X's start knot, whose other
     places they hold.
  2. Every round-2 birth of Y's usable members takes one place of Y's start knot, since those places are its only
     candidates.
  3. So Y's start knot meets J and all M = a(h − 1) − a(a − 1)/2 of those births.
  4. Every clique of the demand roster that holds J lies within the met set of one start knot, so all of J's demand
     groups are pairs.
  5. J's partners are X's two offshoots and those M births. So J has D = f = 2 + M and 5 + M places, under both knot
     laws. J is not implied: each of its loose inputs has only its parent as another holder.

  The drafter's earlier rule, 2 + a(a − 1)/2 + a(h − a), is the same number: both equal 2 + ah − a/2 − a²/2.

  M is 0, 2, 3, 3 for h = 3, a = 0 … 3, and 0, 3, 5, 6, 6 for h = 4, a = 0 … 4. It is equal only for a = h − 1 and a = h.
- **S5 (the advisor's hand reading, checked against the gate listing).**
  - Under OLDEST on the registered numbering, a = h − 1 and a = h give the same number of round-2 newborns (3 + M).
  - Every round-3 join takes distinction 1, and the demand is the same.
  - So the courses from X only are identical through round 3.

## Verdicts, as questions with exclusive answers

1. Are C(h, a) and C(h, b) disjoint? Disjoint, not disjoint, or undecided; on the compared prefix, stated.
2. Under each pair (ν, A, B): the same meeting; different meeting rounds (the gap is reported); or no meeting in one
   run.
3. Attribution: through the meeting, a third part, round-wide, or unclassified. Each is counted over the pairs.
4. Over the class at fixed h: handed in one value, composed over several births, or no difference.
5. Follows a, follows h, both, or neither (descriptions only).
6. Where the link comes from:
   - S4 predicts that the linking knots are Y's start knot holding e, and X's start knot where a birth of Y's took e;
   - in FD 5.7's classes that is **through a start knot**;
   - on D 5.2's third question ("where X and the linking knot come from") the case is therefore **supplied**.
   - The law, its choice by names, the start, the bars and the bounds are supplied. X, the births of Y_h^a and the
     takes that make the link are made by births.

## What a result would and would not show

It would show:
- whether, under a listed law, the course from X only differs with which of a small class it meets;
- whether one value the law uses already separates them.

It would not show a fold of emergence. The third property is untested, and where one value separates them the law,
not the pattern, did it.
