# What grows in the ten frozen starts

The background makes exactly $17+2t$ junctions at round $t$. Joining even the bare five-knot ring makes the total grow exponentially. In every joined case the part whose entire ancestry belongs to the ring keeps its isolated count at every size. With two decorated rings, the fraction of births with an exclusive ancestor in **both** copies tends to one. Their separation changes both the first such size and the total growth rate.

Here $m=t+1$ is the number of points in a connected cluster of the unrolled map of joins. Each cluster counted contains an original knot. Uppercase $N_t$ counts new junctions at round $t$, including the source junctions at $t=0$. A polynomial dimension is the degree of the **cumulative** junction count. For an exponential case the rate in this note is $\lim_{t\to\infty}N_t^{1/t}$. The single-cycle cases also have successive-count ratios tending to their stated rates. These quantities describe the complete cheapest-parity rule under A104. Measurements of a ball in bond distance and the reach of a least change in allowed states have different definitions. ## Which starts qualify

Every listed meeting receives one shared distinction and each loose distinction belongs to precisely one knot. In the wired graph of junctions and the exterior, deleting any one source knot leaves every other source knot directly connected to the exterior by at least one loose distinction. The deleted knot's star is therefore a bond. Its projected relation is exactly even parity on its places. It is nonempty, constrained, and surjects onto every subset missing a place, giving K1–K3. This argument applies also at the funded eight-place seams.

Each ordinary source graph is connected and has neither a three-, four-, nor six-meeting simple cycle. A pair of source knots with two common neighbors would give a four-cycle. A pairwise candidate triple with no common neighbor would give distinct witnesses for its three pairs. If a witness belonged to the triple it would produce an ordinary triangle. Otherwise those six knots would form an ordinary six-cycle. Thus each candidate pair has one witness, every candidate triple is held, and all first groups are pairs. For larger pairwise sets, unique pair witnesses imply that if all their triples are held, the same witness holds the full set. Demand at $v$ is consequently $\sum_{w\sim v}(\deg w-1)$. Every source funds that demand and has at least two loose distinctions in total. C1 preserves J1's opens while reducing some demands.

These are DOUBLE-IDENT's S0/S3/S4/S6′ conditions. They impose no five-place ceiling. Its map starts with one point for each live knot and a block of all live neighbors of each source knot with at least two. A leftover makes a pendant point carrying 0, 1 or 2 endless chains when the owner's open count is respectively at least 3, 2 or 1. Every piece here already has a cycle or a chain. The theorem therefore identifies every birth and its parents with the clusters of the unrolled map and their connected one-point deletions that keep an original knot. RING-CORES applies directly to the isolated within-cap rings. For the larger joined sources the counts below are derived on their identified maps. Before adding the pendants, the point–block incidence graph is the bipartite double cover of the ordinary graph with degree-one block vertices trimmed. An odd ring makes this cover connected. For $V$ ordinary knots and $E$ meetings its connected cycle rank is $2E-2V+1$. The isolated and singly attached odd rings have rank one, the doubles rank three. For a tree the two parts correspond to the two ordinary parities. The four path leftovers give one chain at each of $b_0,b_1,b_{15},b_{16}$. No attachment changes those four counts.

## What a source restriction counts

For a physical birth let its sources be the union of the round-zero ancestors obtained by following every parent link. Under the identification these are precisely the original knot labels appearing in its cluster, including a label once if multiple lifts occur. Deleting points cannot introduce an original label. Conversely, fix any original knot in a cluster. A spanning tree of the cluster's point adjacency allows successive deletion of leaves other than that chosen knot. Each such deletion preserves connectedness and a knot, so it is a parent step. The chosen label is reached.

To restrict the sources to a set $A$, keep each original block on its surviving points, discard blocks of size below two, and keep the original pendants and rays of each surviving knot. The witness knot of a retained block can lie outside $A$. Recomputing open counts after erasing the other sources would change the experiment. In the universal cover the preimage of this incidence subgraph is a forest. A cluster using only allowed labels connects its selected points through their original blocks. A used pendant or ray requires the lift of its owner on the route back to a genuine knot. Consequently this forest contains every such cluster. Each component covers its downstairs component universally. Two clusters in one component that are identified by a full deck transformation are already identified by a transformation preserving that component, since their images meet it. Every lift of a downstairs rooted point is related by a deck transformation. This gives exactly the restricted cluster orbits, including their multiplicities.

On a path restriction, the neighbors of a seam block that remain are its two path neighbors. Other ring blocks introduce no additional path pair. The four original path chains remain. On a ring restriction, the seam's two ring neighbors remain in its block and all additional path blocks meet the ring in at most one point. Each ring keeps precisely its designated spares. Thus every pure ring count equals its Rk count, and every pure path count equals its B count. This applies to both rings of either double. The shared source knot is in both regions at $t=0$. It has no pendant, and no block joins two different lifts with only that source label. At positive rounds the pure background and pure ring counts are disjoint. These equalities use source information. Recovery from an anonymous presentation is a separate reading.

## The path, one ring and the cut

In B each parity piece extends to a line, with a consecutive run of respectively nine or eight original knots. A length-$m$ interval meeting that run has $m+8$ or $m+7$ possible starts. Their sum gives $2m+15=2t+17$, including round zero.

In Rk, the five-point periodic line has $k$ optional spares per turn. Its turn weight is $W_R(z)=z^5(1+z)^k$. Without spares there are five interval starts at every size. With spares the rate $x>1$ satisfies $x^{5+k}=(x+1)^k$. In Jk the same cycle appears with four additional independent chains. At the point copy of the seam an arm leaves in each path direction. At the block copy, which joins the two neighboring ring points, its two additional path points each begin an arm. Successive path points on a given arm are two ordinary meetings apart. Where the original path ends, its leftover and endless chain continue the same sequence. Each arm has initial-segment series $A(z)=1/(1-z)$, including the empty segment. The turn weight and rate are therefore

$$W_J(z)=z^5(1+z)^k A(z)^4,\qquad W_J(1/x)=1.$$

Every cluster hitting the line has a leftmost line point modulo five and a finite interval. Each complete turn multiplies its series by $W_J$. The boundary block of an interval also permits its attached arms when just one adjacent line point belongs to the interval. This changes the numerator and leaves the turn weight intact. Clusters avoiding the line give a linear count. The two arms belonging to its point copy contribute eight altogether, one possible starting position for each original even path knot other than the seam. The arms at its block copy still meet through that block and form a single line with eight original odd knots, contributing $m+7$. The no-line term is $m+15$. In particular the entire single-joint count is unchanged if its seam is moved from $b_4$ to $b_8$ or $b_{12}$ in this experiment.

The geometric sum over turns has denominator $1-W_J$. Its numerator is positive at the positive zero. The support of $W_J$ includes sizes five and six, so the triangle inequality excludes other zeros of that modulus. This gives successive-count ratio $x$ and $N_t\sim c x^t$ with $c>0$. The same argument applies to decorated Rk. For every Jk its rate exceeds the isolated Rk rate because $A(z)^4>1$ at $0<z<1$. Equivalently the proportion of births carrying both background-exclusive and ring-exclusive sources tends to one. The intrinsic ring count remains what it was in isolation.

For C1, the opens at $c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4$ remain $4,3,2,4$ while their demands become $3,1,1,3$. Thus $c_2$ has two finite pendant points, $c_1,c_4$ have one each, and $c_3$ acquires a pendant with one endless chain. The latter joins the parity containing $b_0,b_{16}$. In that three-chain piece a finite core cluster containing all three chain starts must take the whole even path and $c_3$. It can omit $c_2$ or take it with any subset of its two finite spares, giving five possibilities. CHAIN-IDENT gives leading birth coefficient $5/2$ and cumulative coefficient $5/6$. The two finite core sizes, including pendants, are 16 and 14, so the theorem makes the birth count polynomial of degree two from $t=16$. The exact series below fixes this polynomial at three consecutive ages and verifies it back through $t=3$, giving the formula in the table. ## Two copies at different separations

Let $d$ be the number of original ordinary meetings between the two seams. A knot exclusive to one ring is at least $d+2$ ordinary meetings from any knot exclusive to the other. Each step between two map points uses a common ordinary neighbor and can account for at most two ordinary meetings. A connected $m$-point cluster can span at most $m-1$ such steps. For even $d$, the nearest exclusive knots attain this bound along the unique ordinary shortest route. Each ring has two choices for that exclusive knot. Equality requires the whole cluster to be the resulting shortest path, with no extra point. Thus both-copy births first appear at $t=d/2+1$, sizes 4 and 6, exactly four at either first size. This proof is independent of left–right naming and the endpoints of the path.

Write $T_d(t)$ for total births and $J_1(t),B(t)$ for the matched counts. Avoiding either exclusive ring leaves precisely the singly joined restriction just proved. Avoiding both leaves the path. Hence the exact number using exclusive sources from both copies, including the round-zero convention, is

$$T_d(t)-2J_1(t)+B(t).$$

One can prove the limiting share without knowing the full double-ring rate. From ring 2 retain only $c_1$ in addition to the full first ring and path. This genuine point, without a spare, joins the second seam's block. On the arm from the first seam's cycle block, this is the block after the $d/2$-th path point. A chosen prefix may take the extra point as soon as it contains that many path points. Its series becomes $(1+z^{d/2+1})/(1-z)$. The restricted count has a single-cycle rate $\beta_d$ determined by

$$W_J(1/\beta_d)\bigl(1+\beta_d^{-(d/2+1)}\bigr)=1\quad(k=1).$$

It exceeds J1's rate $\alpha$ for both placements. The double therefore has at least $c\beta_d^t$ births at every sufficiently large age for some $c>0$. The union of births avoiding either ring has at most $2J_1(t)=O(\alpha^t)$ members. Its share decreases as $O((\alpha/\beta_d)^t)$, so the share using both exclusive sources tends to one. The one-extra-point lower rates are 1.965441739693 and 1.947230087530. At the last jointly materialized age $t=5$, the actual both-copy counts are 89 of 520 for T4 and 4 of 435 for T8.

The fixed seams remain $d$ ordinary junction steps apart. Between the two pure growing parts, with an exclusive source required on each side, the minimum is $d+2$ at the source and $d+2t$ at each positive round. Direct searches of the complete accumulated stars verify both ordinary distances at ages zero through five for each placement. ## Exact series and the two numerical rate certificates

For a point $p$ belonging to a block $B$, let $U_{pB}$ count the option of omitting $p$ or starting at $p$ and growing away from $B$. Write $e_p$ for the product of its pendant factors. A pendant with $r$ rays contributes $1+z/(1-z)^r$. The branches of the unrolled incidence graph meet only at the chosen point or block, so

$$U_{pB}=1+ze_p\prod_{C\ni p,\ C\ne B}\ \prod_{q\in C-\{p\}} U_{qC}.$$

The constant term is one. Each coefficient depends only on lower-degree coefficients. Omitting the exclusion $C\ne B$ and the initial 1 gives the generating series $R_p$ with $p$ selected. To count unrooted orbits, sum the $R_p$. For each block $B$ and subset $D\subseteq B$ of size at least two, subtract $(|D|-1)\prod_{p\in D}(U_{pB}-1)$. Indeed an original-point cluster with $s$ original knots is counted $s$ times in the pointed sum. Its connecting incidence subtree has $\sum_B(|D_B|-1)=s-1$. A finite cluster has trivial deck stabilizer, since any automorphism of a finite tree fixes a vertex or an edge and these deck transformations preserve point and block types and act freely. This proves the correction, including for a block of four and an original knot with eight places. The same argument counts each source restriction by intersecting its blocks as specified above.

For the two doubles the reciprocal radius gives a genuine root growth limit. On the finite incidence subgraph that remains after repeatedly removing degree-one vertices, the nonbacktracking directed branches communicate. To see this, each oriented incidence edge can be reversed after taking a detour round a cycle on its far side with that edge deleted. For a bridge each resulting side contains a cycle, since in that side at most one vertex has degree below two. For an edge on a cycle, deletion leaves the graph connected with at least one cycle. Connectivity then gives all transitions. The branch equations combine two steps, one at a point and one at a block. Between two oriented block-to-point incidences the number of steps always has even parity, so this combination preserves communication. The off-cycle branches are finite expressions in these series and the four chain factors, which are finite at the critical radius below one.

A branching site has a term containing two continuing branches in the same communicating set. Returning along fixed dependency paths gives a coefficientwise bound $U\ \ge z^h U^2$ for one continuing branch, with some finite $h$. The unrolled infinite path makes every coefficient $u_i$ of this branch positive. Thus $u_{i+j+h}\ge u_i u_j$. Applying supermultiplicativity to $u_{n-h}$ gives an ordinary root limit equal to its reciprocal radius. All continuing branches have this radius by communication. Unrooting a positive-size branch can identify at most its size many possible marks, and the pointed sum bounds the unrooted count above. These facts give the same root limit for total births.

The two stated rate intervals follow from exact inequalities on those equations at rational $z=1/x$. For the upper rate endpoint the artifact gives a finite positive vector on the ports with $F_z(U)\le U$. Iteration then bounds every finite-depth branch sum. For the lower endpoint choose port $i$ with two different dependencies $j,k$ returning to $i$. Every dependency step contributes a factor at least $z$. If their shortest return lengths give $h=1+d(j,i)+d(k,i)$, a finite solution must satisfy $U_i\ge z^hU_i^2$ and hence $U_i\le z^{-h}$. A lower iteration rounded down on the recorded integer grid exceeds that bound after 644 iterations for T4 and 964 for T8, with $h=13,17$. Capping iterates from above only weakens these lower inequalities. The upper vectors are checked with upward rounding, below the cap. The intervals are disjoint. At the level of the rooted series, the outer chains in both cases have the same factor $1/(1-z)$. The two core connections between the rings each acquire two additional ordinary map points when the source separation increases from four to eight. The finite extent assigned to original knots on the four outer arms affects bounded-source contributions, while those arms have the same factor when a core knot is already present. This identifies the change responsible for this pair's distinct exponential rates.
