## Setting

A growth starts from a finite set of "knots" (each a set of distinctions; a distinction lies in one or two knots). A knot is live when it holds loose distinctions (open count >= 1). Proved earlier (Lemma R): from round 1 on, the growth reads a start only through its live knots, their open counts and the maximal sets N(w) & live ("blocks"). One round: two live knots are candidates when some block holds both; the round's groups are the maximal cliques of three or more candidates that no block holds, and the candidate pairs inside none of them; a knot in more groups than its open count is short, and then the whole growth stops ("stall": only possible in round 1, since later each new knot gets fresh = max(d, 3 - p, 1) >= d, d its next-round groups); each group is a new knot with its members as parents, and each knot v also makes open(v) - use(v) one-parent births; the new blocks are the sets of new knots sharing a parent. Under the parity knot law, a round that makes one new knot holding every live knot, each with one loose distinction, closes the growth (the birth is implied); under not-all-equal it never does (it relays). Earlier studies showed a local reader's view (per round, the multiset of births by parents and fresh distinctions, and with the parents' kinds) is a function of the start's finite map, and that births can be made one at a time in any order from a record of parents and still build what the rounds build.

The owner's intuition: "we only need to compute what is discoverable/discovered ... we're not actually simulating the full environment from the start, just the parts we need", with the far parts settled only when needed. The coordinator asked: can a local reader's view be grown on demand, computing only the part the reader needs, and does it match the full growth whatever order the parts are filled in? And to say plainly what it does not show (earlier studies found nothing in the model exceeds the classical bound with free settings, and no far change is heard late).
