## How the original roles are read

Each admitted original pair uses a distinct spent witness. The witness shares one distinction with one live holder and two with the other in the ordinary case. This asymmetry orders the pair. Each live original has one loose distinction per admitted pair. Each original live holder takes part in a repeated meeting, as does each spent witness. Every later holder uses one distinct loose distinction from each of its actual parents. Repeated original meetings therefore stay identifiable in the complete later holdings. ## Funding and persistence at arbitrary size

The original holder incidence is connected and every shared distinction has exactly two holders. Each admitted pair has one witness, every witness serves at most two initial live knots, and the admitted pairs have no three mutually adjacent live knots. Initial demand at each live knot is its admitted degree, paid exactly by the loose count. There is no surplus at that cut. These are the repeated-meeting identification hypotheses. Under the identification, a current cluster is an orbit of a finite connected set in the unrolled candidate tree. Actual parents delete one occurrence while retaining connectedness, and actual fresh distinctions count the one-occurrence extensions. The degree of every original live type is at least two. A cluster of size m has m−1 internal adjacencies. Therefore the extension count is $2+\sum_u(\deg(u)-2)$ over its occurrences, at least two. Distinct continuous neutral windows in R and S give at least two births at every positive cut. Parity never enters the sole-birth closure and both laws have the same demand prices. The state projection argument then applies at every finite cut.
