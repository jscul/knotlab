# Distances through four decide every triple after the third one-parent, three-output join

**Theorem.** Start at a single three-place even-parity junction. Exactly three additions each take one parent and introduce three fresh distinctions. They can occur wherever the serial law permits. Every other addition takes two or three parents and introduces two distinctions. Under **any** bijection preserving the current whole bond-distance table through four between such a source and any reachable mixed-law rival, the complete allowed-state relations agree on the corresponding names. In particular every allowed or forbidden three-parent move agrees. When at least one triple may join, the same correspondence also takes the whole set of actual junction supports onto the rival's. The sharp cutoff for permissions in this family is four. [Frozen predictions](../../../research/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001M/preregistration.md) · [Width-22 lower bound](census.md)

A pair entry is the least size of an inclusion-minimal nonzero parity equation containing the pair, minus one. Let \(K=D+1\). Supplying readings through \(D=4\) means replacing all \(D>4\) by the same value. At a proposed move, the three distinctions must each be loose, at different ordinary junctions. Each pair of those junctions must have an ordinary common neighbor, and none may be common to all three. The reader is given the growth law, the table and the unordered triple. It can enumerate all reachable presentations at that width and all table isometries and evaluate the transported triple. Width increases by at least two at every addition, making this a finite procedure. The proof shows that every surviving explanation returns the same answer for these sources. The procedure need not know the source branch, parent count, or birth order, and no running-time bound is asserted.

## Why a five-place junction is visible

In the ordinary graph of meetings, wire the loose bits to an exterior vertex to compute parity equations. A set of sites has a boundary given by the bits that occur an odd number of times among its site equations. All equations arise in this way. Individual site equations are independent. A shrinking join takes three bits and makes two. With \(t\) shrinking joins the completed source has \(9-t\) loose distinctions. The exact finite transition system remembers each loose holder's count, the meetings between these holders and the projected neighborhoods of exhausted holders. No exhausted site can gain another neighbor. From the two possible starting configurations, 74,546 records close under all 484,418 transitions. At most four shrinking joins can occur, and four requires that the third unary birth came before the first shrink. This proves the five-loose lower bound at every depth, including any number of two-parent, two-output steps. The lower bound is attained. [Transition proof and replay](frontier.md)

In a set of at least three ordinary sites the latest three export at least four distinctions. A boundary of four would therefore have no entering input and could be constructed first in a lawful order. Such a prefix would contradict the preceding loose-count bound, or the at-least-five bound in the two-three-output prefix family. There is no such boundary. Every four-bit equation is consequently a four-place site, and the earlier short-equation transfer shows exactly these words, together with the seed, must be present in any matching rival. [Boundary and transfer proof](structural.md)

Every five-bit boundary enclosing at least three sites can also be put first. If one input entered, the latest three sites would supply all four exits. The seed cannot be absent, since deleting that single entering bit would then give an ordinary two-three-output prefix with only four loose outputs. With the seed inside, the net output count forces four shrinking sites inside and only two of the three unary sites. To reach the omitted unary site at all, a directed path from the seed must pass through one of the latest three inside sites. Consequently at most two inside sites could follow the omitted unary site. But in any four-shrink history all four shrinks follow that site. This eliminates the sole possible entering input. [Full proof and alternate chronological argument](structural.md#four-bit-and-five-bit-boundaries)

Any two of these large five-bit boundaries enclose the seed and at least one common unary site. Their intersection and union therefore each cost at least five. Cut submodularity makes their union another five-bit boundary. Now take a shrinking junction. If each of its three input bits appeared on such a boundary, take the union of the three witnessing sets. It would contain all three older holders and omit the junction itself. Adding the junction would replace three boundary bits by two, producing the four-bit boundary just excluded. Thus at least one input avoids *every* competing large five-bit boundary. A nonseed input can always be chosen. Its older holder's equation and the short pair readings force the entire five-place equation, by the protected-input argument. Induction in the source birth order forces every site equation under the supplied bijection. This uses chronology to prove that an explanation is forced, without handing that chronology to the reader. [Union and equation argument](structural.md#a-protected-input-at-every-five-place-site)

There are \(n\) independent source equations at width \(2n+4\). A rival with \(r\) sites and \(m\) nonseed three-output additions has width \(2r+1+m\). The shared short entries detect the same unique three-place site. Its first two additions must consequently be unary and make three outputs. The width requires \(m\) to be odd, so \(m\ge3\) and \(r\le n\). Inclusion of the source space gives the reverse inequality. This proves equality of the full parity spaces and equality of all three-parent permissions. Before the third unary birth the prefix is covered by 001J. ## When the table can hide a physical junction

A source with a cycle has one two-connected ordinary block and at most one leaf. To start each such block requires two of the three unary attachments. Removing and reconstructing the leaf, when present, reduces the retained history to the family with two three-output additions. Acyclic sources are similarly determined by their single-distinction separations. In the two-connected case the full equation space identifies all ordinary junctions and the exterior as one collection. Choosing a different member as exterior requires two complementary last moves on an identical prefix. With at least five loose distinctions, the sole possibility is two complementary legal triples on six loose distinctions, both making the same two outputs. The completed triple menus are then empty. The exact frontier has just one record with these complementary choices. This classifies every exception under a fixed table bijection, including arbitrary reachable rivals. [Incidence and exterior proof](incidence.md)

The smallest exception is at width 32. At a common width-30 prefix use \(\{24,26,28\}\) or \(\{25,27,29\}\), and introduce 30 and 31 in either case. These possible last equations differ precisely by the sum of all prefix equations. Both histories have rank 14, all 496 exact entries agree with every name fixed, and both menus are empty. The two histories can also be matched as physical arrangements by exchanging the two loose bits at each of the three old holders. As a control, another legal last choice that changes only one of the three selected inputs gives *eight different* entries through distance four.
