# Fixed two-relay switches encode strict 3-SAT with three pad placements per copy

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

For each $n\ge3$, one explicit guarded strict 3-CNF prefix, including all variable identities, pad wiring and literal signs, is computable from $n$ alone. Only two middle names $J,K$ occupy multiple middle positions. Every hidden Boolean name used to pad binary implications is used on at most three distinct implication occurrences. A later input selects some of the $M=8\binom n3$ possible signed clauses on three distinct public source-variable indices. There exists an assignment extending the resulting raw switch vector if and only if the selected source 3-CNF is satisfiable. Indeed, the input need only vary one specified raw switch per possible clause. All other switches on this menu are fixed.

The uniform decision problem is NP-complete, both on that selector menu and when arbitrary full switch vectors on the same templates are admitted. The template at any one fixed $n$ is finite. The NP-completeness statement ranges over $n$ and charges the varying input and the uniform construction.

## How a path tests a clause

At a two-route stage the distinct boundary names are $x_{i-1},x_i$. A middle occurrence $a$ gives the two separately guarded implications $x_{i-1}\to a$ and $a\to x_i$. The source units are $x_0,\neg x_r$. A binary clause $D$ for an implication is padded into two slots $D\lor p,D\lor\neg p$. Each slot has three private switches and three private witnesses. The witness disjunction and the three guards require its padded clause exactly when all three switches are on. Otherwise a witness attached to an off switch suffices. The endpoint unit has four padded slots, using all sign patterns on two private bits, so it is enforced by its twelve on switches. [Strict switch and pad semantics](../../ONE-RELAY-PADDING-20261115-001Z/ONE-RELAY-PADDING-20261115-001Z-P001_synthesis/proof.md)

With a pad assignment fixed, activating only the slot $D\lor L(p)$ enforces the implication when the signed literal $L(p)$ is **false**. Activating both slots forces the implication irrespective of the pad. Activating neither permits it to fail for either pad value.

Use one initial stage to enforce only $x_0\to J$, and one final stage to enforce only $K\to x_r$. Turn on both endpoint units. Hence $J=1,K=0$. Between these stages concatenate disjoint three-stage segments. In a segment the first middle on its three successive stages is, respectively, $J$, a fresh $m$, and $K$. All second middles are fresh. Use only the four occurrence positions

$$J\ \longrightarrow\ x_i\ \longrightarrow\ m\ \longrightarrow\ x_{i+1}\ \longrightarrow\ K.$$

The first arc is the outgoing half of the first stage, the next two are the two halves of the intervening ordinary branch, and the last is the incoming half of the third stage. Disable every other occurrence. At a boundary between consecutive segments, the preceding segment has no enabled incoming arc to that boundary and the next has no enabled outgoing arc from it. This also isolates the initial and final stages. Every internal name on the displayed path belongs only to its own segment. Thus a path from true $J$ to false $K$ exists exactly when some displayed path is intact. In the absence of an intact path, set a source or boundary variable true exactly when reachable from $x_0$. This assignment satisfies all enforced implications and both units.

For a data clause put its three signed literals on the first three arcs by the singly active padded slots, and use the fourth edge to select the clause. When selected, this last edge is unconditional. The whole path is then intact precisely if all three clause literals are false. A binary test $A\lor B$ uses $A,B$ on the first two arcs and two permanently unconditional arcs. It similarly contradicts exactly when both literals are false.

The simplest unselected fourth edge has all six switches off. The following fixed encoding varies **one bit** instead. Give that edge a private pad $h$. Activate $D\lor h$ permanently. Fix two switches of $D\lor\neg h$ on and use the third as the selector $y$. For $y=1$ the edge is forced. For $y=0$ the assignment $h=1$ makes the sole active padded clause true even if the implication fails. This private choice can be made independently for every omitted clause. Consequently, after existentially forgetting the private selector pads, unselected clauses impose no condition on the data copies. The physical live-edge assertion for $y=0$ uses this extension choice.

## Fixed pad identities and equality on occurrences

List all $M$ signed source clauses in a fixed order. For each of the three positions of each possible clause, allocate one distinct hidden Boolean pad $p$. Use it with the listed sign on the corresponding data arc. These are occurrence copies even in the slots not selected by the later input. For each source index $j$, order its $d=8\binom{n-1}{2}$ copies $p_{j,1},\ldots,p_{j,d}$ according to that fixed list. Add a mandatory binary-test segment for every cyclic implication

$$p_{j,1}\to p_{j,2},\ \ldots,\ p_{j,d-1}\to p_{j,d},\ p_{j,d}\to p_{j,1}.$$

Such a segment is the effective clause $\neg p_{j,a}\lor p_{j,a+1}$, with cyclic indexing. Since $d\ge8$, the two copies in a segment are distinct. Every common assignment satisfying all these segments makes the ring constant. If one copy is one it forces each successor around the ring, and if none is one they are uniformly zero. Conversely both uniform assignments satisfy all the implications.

Each copy pad occurs in exactly three binary-edge padding pairs: its own data test, its outgoing equality implication and its incoming equality implication. It therefore occurs in six raw literal positions, as complementary literals occur in the two padded clauses of every such pair. The bound of three counts **physical padding pairs**, including all possible slots before selection. All other binary edges, including forced, unused and selector positions, have their own private pad. Endpoint pads, public switches and the witnesses are separately typed and private to their groups. The equality cycles repeat logical truth values through distinct physical copies without increasing the number of placements of any one copy.

There are $M$ data segments and $3M$ equality segments. Thus the prefix has $r=12M+2$ stages, $4r$ binary-edge padding positions, $24r+24=288M+72$ raw switches, and $32r+32$ strict clauses. The $3M$ copy names occupy $9M$ of the $4r$ binary positions. Each remaining position has a private pad. $J$ and $K$ each occupy $4M+1$ middle positions. Every other middle occurs in one position. The fixed list can be generated in polynomial time and listed in $O(r\log(r+1))$ bits with binary names. The $M$ selector bits, or the expanded linear-size raw vector, are charged to the later input.

## Reduction and its converse

Take the admitted strict-3-CNF SAT input, whose clauses have three different underlying variables. Compact the indices of the variables actually occurring, order the variables in each clause, and turn on exactly the selectors of its signed clauses. Duplicating a clause changes no decision. With $c$ source clauses the compacted width is at most $3c$, so generating even the full fixed list and raw vector is polynomial in the input length. The empty formula maps to no selected clauses at $n=3$. If malformed strings require a rejecting output, select all eight signed clauses at $n=3$. They forbid the eight public assignments one by one. [Admitted uniform strict-3-CNF SAT theorem](../../EVIDENCE-001D/EVIDENCE-001D-P001_uniform_strict_3_cnf_sat_completeness_admission/proof.md)

Given a satisfying source assignment, give every copy its source value. Each equality path has a missing conditional implication. Each selected data path also has one, because some literal holds. Set the private selector pads of omitted clauses to one. The reachability assignment above completes the implications. Choose arbitrary remaining private pads where the source clause is already true. For each strict slot choose a single witness for an off switch or a true literal. This yields a full raw strict model. Conversely, in any full model an intact path from $J=1$ to $K=0$ is impossible. The mandatory paths therefore force every ring constant, and every selected data clause is true at the recovered common values. This equivalence is ordinary existential satisfaction, with the hidden padding chosen in the same assignment as the source variables and witnesses.

A polynomial-size full assignment verifies all clauses and pins, so membership in NP also holds for arbitrary raw switch vectors, including those outside the construction's selector menu. Their answers need not describe a selected source formula. The one-switch-per-clause reduction proves NP-hardness on the restricted menu and hence on the unrestricted language as well. An alternative instance-dependent template can allocate only the actually occurring positions and omit a tautological one-copy ring. It would use at most $4c$ segments. The cubic-size universal template is what permits fixing the named wiring before the input.

## Nearest known result

Groote and Ploeger use shared switches to encode literals on clause paths. Their Theorem 3.6 checks a list of terminal pairs under a common switch setting, and Theorem 3.7 treats disconnection. Boros, Elbassioni, Gurvich and Makino give the equivalent SAT problem of two disjoint cuts in networks on the same edge labels, also for directed networks. Here the signed-literal paths represent false clauses and their obstructions share two named Boolean relays in a fixed guarded stage template. [Groote and Ploeger, Theorems 3.6–3.7](https://pure.tue.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/3142478/639900.pdf) · [Boros et al., Proposition 14 and Remark 3](https://www.keisu.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/data/2007/METR07-35.pdf)

The cyclic occurrence-copy construction is Tovey's. His Theorem 2.1 gives NP-completeness for clause widths two and three with at most three occurrences per variable. Theorem 2.4 gives the essential Hall check for exact width three. In a formula of three distinct variables per clause and at most three clauses per variable, any set of $h$ clauses has $3h$ incidences and therefore at least $h$ distinct neighboring variables. Hall's theorem assigns every clause a different variable, each of which can be set to satisfy its matched literal. Such formulas are always satisfiable. Our *effective* constraints include binary equality clauses. The emitted raw clauses are strictly ternary, but their variables do not obey a three-literal-occurrence restriction. This is a transfer of known SAT and switching constructions to fixed strict switches and physically counted shared pads. NP-completeness supplies no unconditional superpolynomial time lower bound. [Tovey, Theorems 2.1 and 2.4](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-218X(84)90081-7)

The one-repeated-middle predecessor allows arbitrary pad reuse and constructs a model in linear time. Here two repeated middles already suffice for NP-completeness even under the stated pad-placement cap.
