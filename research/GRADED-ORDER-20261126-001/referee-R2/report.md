## Why the conditional core theorem holds

Define a *pure* birth to mean a core birth. All initial groups and initial offshoots are pure. Thereafter a pure birth is a join whose parents are all pure. A later unary offshoot, including the start of a relay, lies outside that definition. A core birth carries each initial chain base in its support, propagated by union. The theorem concerns the parent groups that define the core. Its conclusion alone gives neither their fresh counts nor the positive-length chain census.

Induct through the first possible discrepancy. Maintain equality of the pure rosters with the core-only iteration and these two properties of actual births.

1. Every birth outside the core has at most one pure parent.
2. If that pure parent F also has a pure child, the support of F contains an initial chain base.

Suppose a valid mixed group contains two pure members A and B. Every noncore member must share a pure parent with each of them, since all parents of A and B are pure. By property 1 that member has just one such parent. If all noncore members use the same parent, every member of the group shares it. A valid group therefore needs two noncore members with different pure parents C and D. A and B each have C and D as parents. The two noncore members must themselves share a parent W, necessarily noncore. One birth group contained {C, W}, the other {D, W}. Thus W shares a parent with each of C and D. By property 1 for W, this is a single pure parent F common to C and D. Property 2 for W applies because C is a pure child of F. This is the forbidden pair of twins.

Consequently no valid mixed group contains two pure members. Every pure maximal valid group stays maximal, and every pure candidate pair left uncovered stays uncovered. Pure candidate relations are unchanged. This proves the induction step for the pure roster and property 1. To propagate property 2, consider a noncore join with pure parent F. Another member of its birth group shares a pure parent with F. Property 2 one round earlier puts a chain base in that parent's support, hence in F's.

It remains to check unary births. If F has a pure child, its group demand *d* is positive. For a pure join with at least two parents the fresh rule gives exactly *d*, so it leaves no unary offshoot. The remaining possibility is a round-one offshoot of an initial knot with *o* loose distinctions. It has *o* − 1 sibling births, all sharing that initial knot. Each forms an uncovered pair with the offshoot because every birth compatible with the offshoot shares that same parent. Its demand is *o* − 1. When that demand is positive, an excess can occur only at *o* = 2, precisely an initial chain base. Zero demand gives no pure child and needs no mark. At round one every birth is pure, which starts the induction. This proves the conditional result for either law without assuming the proposed chain count.

There is also a converse at the level of labelled parent groups. At the first marked pair, follow an ancestral core path from the chain's initial offshoot to the common grandparent F. The initial offshoot has a noncore unary child from its spare. If a core birth U has a noncore child and a pure child V, those two children are candidates through U and occur together in some group. Before the first marked pair, that group has V as its only pure member, so the property passes to V. Applying this at F produces noncore births with distinct pure parents C and D and a shared noncore parent. They and the twins form a pairwise-compatible family with no common parent. A full maximal group contains all four. The length-counting proposal cannot represent that mixed parent roster. Which pure pair disappears, if any, and whether the anonymous counts detect it are separate questions.

**Relay and reduction to the prior identification.** In a chain-free piece, the initial births are connected by parent-sharing. Each candidate pair belongs to a selected group. Thus, while there are at least two births in a connected round, the groups cover every birth and stay connected through shared members in the next round. Every birth then has positive demand. A round-one offshoot in a chain-free piece comes from *o* ≥ 3 and has demand *o* − 1 ≥ 2. Every later pure birth has at least two parents. The fresh floor produces no surplus at these stages. If the piece finishes, the sole terminal birth carries the whole support by repeated coverage and has at least two parents. At zero demand it retains one fresh distinction under not-all-equal. Under parity it closes if the piece is alone and relays if another piece is present. Indeed, while two pieces are active neither can take all loose distinctions, so both stay active. The unary birth following the terminal birth starts the usual two-chain relay. This argument justifies the terminal choice for finishing chain-free pieces. The general identification of mixed births in a piece with chains remains open. [Fresh-count rule](../../../proofs/REDUCE-BLOCKS-20261119-001A/REDUCE-BLOCKS-20261119-001A-P001_reduce/proof.md) · [Relay law under not-all-equal](../../../proofs/NAE-GROWTH-20261119-001Z/NAE-GROWTH-20261119-001Z-P001_epic_closure/proof.md)

In the common acyclic domain of the prior identification, its connected sets give the same core births by the same initialization and parent rule. Two distinct sets of the same size cannot share two different one-deletion parents, since their union would recover both sets. The chain lengths count their extended sets, and deletion gives both parent readings. The prior result assumes connectedness, distinctions held at most twice, at least two loose distinctions and no shortage. Its class excludes a round-one group of three or more and a live pair with two common neighbours. Its parity theorem includes cyclic pieces by using the unrolled map. The submitted default declines such pieces. Five knots in cyclic succession, each with two shared distinctions and two loose distinctions, satisfy the prior hypotheses and witness that distinction. A lone finite piece also distinguishes the laws, as the not-all-equal extension requires. Separate controls checked one finite piece, two finite pieces and the cyclic example.
