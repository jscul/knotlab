# Arbitrarily many shallow product names and the unrestricted case

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

Consider characteristic-two syntactic resolution over degree-two formal polynomials, augmented with fresh acyclic proof-local declarations \(t=fg\) for affine forms in base variables and earlier names. Let \(d\) be the maximum nesting depth of these declarations, \(k\) their number, \(n\) the base-variable count, and \(S\) the entire proof-node count including declarations. There is an unnamed proof of the base CNF in degree at most \(D=2^{d+1}\) with \(O(S+(n+k+1)k)\) nodes (allowing the input variable axioms used in the construction). Consequently every fixed \(d\) preserves an exponential-in-\(n\) proof-node lower bound with high probability for the guarded encodings of the density-eight uniform strict ternary source. Separately, unrestricted names can simulate ordinary extended resolution with polynomial overhead in explicit proof size.

## Nearest known result

Pang's v2 Theorem 7.2(a) is for each fixed unnamed degree. Its Fact 7.4 uses a related Boolean-choice derivation for monomials. The earlier guarded transfer admitted only degree two. Here both affine factors may involve arbitrarily many earlier names, a name may be used repeatedly, and charged Boolean defaults on names must have actual unnamed derivations after expansion. Extended resolution's use of fresh circuit gates is classical. The local contribution is the explicit interaction of these facts with this syntactic calculus and the two-use guarded input map. ## Expansion and Boolean defaults

Inputs have depth zero. Assign each name one plus the largest depth among the variables occurring in its two factors, taking zero for an empty list. Expand definitions by a homomorphism to the *formal* polynomial ring on the \(n\) inputs. A depth-\(j\) name expands to degree at most \(2^j\), by induction and the degree inequality for products. Every original proof equation and displayed multiplier has degree at most two before expansion and therefore degree at most \(D\) afterward. Repeated factors are retained. A definition becomes the true equation \(0=0\). Ordinary deductions commute with this substitution. The formal product in a multiplication step equals the image of its stated result, so the cap applies even when the written inputs cancel.

For a polynomial \(f\), write \(E_f=[f=0]\lor[f+1=0]\). Construct each needed \(E\) by the arithmetic circuit that the declarations already describe. Apart from the \(E_x\) input axioms and the true axiom for constants we need two primitive constructions. Given \(E_f,E_g\), fix \(i,j\) in \(\{0,1\}\) and select \([f+i=0]\) and \([g+j=0]\), retaining their opposite choices as disjuncts. Adding the selected equations derives \([f+g+i+j=0]\). For multiplication, first multiply \([f+i=0]\) by \(g\). Add \(i\) times \([g+j=0]\) to derive \([fg+ij=0]\). In both constructions add the other disjunct of the intended Boolean choice by weakening. Cut the four cases in pairs, retaining the two desired disjuncts and contracting repeated context occurrences. Each gate takes a constant number of primitive steps; its product calculation reaches at most the permitted sum of factor degrees. A constant term in an affine sum exchanges its two choices. The argument works if children repeat, are constant, are complements, or expand to the same polynomial.

Each declaration contains at most \(2(n+k+1)\) affine terms across its two factors. Build \(E\) for each such sum from available Boolean disjunctions, then for the product, storing the result for repeated uses. A variable Boolean default for a name can now cite this derived disjunction. The other original nodes each cost at most a constant number of proof nodes. This proves the node estimate. Unnamed Boolean inputs can be cached and charged to their use in a factor or an original node. For fixed \(d\), a degree-\(D\) formal polynomial on \(n\) variables has at most \(\binom{n+D}{D}\) distinct monomials, so explicit expansion and verification also cost polynomial work in the explicit input proof. For growing \(d\), the node estimate still describes derivations with larger polynomial inscriptions; the bit-cost and the source constant can then depend on \(d\). ∎

## Proof length on the guarded family

First translate a named guarded refutation to a named proof on the \(n\) original variables, preserving the declarations and bounding their depth by the original depth. This uses a sparse signed substitution and increases the node count by at most a factor of two on strict source clauses. Expand that source proof as just shown. Since \(k\le S\), the unnamed proof-node count is polynomial in \(n+S\) with an absolute fixed exponent. For each fixed \(d\) the new scoped admission gives an unnamed degree-\(D\) lower bound \(\exp(c_D n)\) with high probability, for some \(c_D>0\). Hence \(S=\exp(\Omega_d(n))\). There is no restriction on the number of names beyond charging every declaration. Separately the bounded-count theorem gives \(S=\exp(\Omega(n))\) for \(k=o(n)\) at any depth. The two guarantees apply to different naming patterns. The distribution has \(8n\) independent uniform signed strict ternary clauses with replacement. The source theorem is Shuo Pang, arXiv:2610.00837v2, Theorem 7.2(a), with \(p=2,w=3,\Delta=8\); the PDF SHA-256 is \(\texttt{9f7eb894da07496b4cba14cbf7a4a109d64e994837fc0478be541cc4aaa57306}\). The listing has \(r=44n+8\) stages. The catalogue fixed per \(n\) has \(r=32\binom n3+12n+8\) stages, with switches induced by the same source draw. Thus the estimates are exponential in \(r\) and \(r^{1/3}\), respectively. In an explicit literal listing of \(L=\Theta(r\log r)\) bits they are exponential in \(L/\log L\) and \((L/\log L)^{1/3}\). The source is a preprint and is admitted as stated. The result neither supplies a uniform exponent for arbitrarily growing \(d\) nor bounds unrestricted algorithms for deciding SAT. ## Reusing the same original factor at arbitrary depth

There is also a useful case with no bound on either the number or depth of names. Suppose one affine form \(f\) on the base variables has the following property: in every declaration at least one factor is \(f\) or \(1+f\). The other can be any affine combination of old variables and earlier names. Branch just once on \(f=c\). Replace each new name in order by zero or by its other factor's affine image, according to the value of the common factor or its complement. The image of every defining equation is \((f+c)g'\), where \(g'\) is the current affine image of the other factor. Derive this under the single failure disjunct from \(E_f\) by one multiplication. The bounded-count replay of the ordinary rules now carries only that one failure disjunct. One cut merges the two branches. The two unnamed proofs together use \(O((n+1)(S+1))\) nodes in degree two, independently of depth and reuse. A proof may supply either orientation of each factor pair. In this special case a new exponential lower bound follows already from the degree-two source theorem, by the same polynomial simulation argument. A signed input projection sends a common original factor to another affine form or a constant, so it preserves this promise. Our checker exercises an eight-level chain, alternates factor order and polarity across it, and rejects an unrelated proposed factor.

## What unrestricted naming permits

In the ordinary AND basis for extended resolution a fresh \(t\) for two prior signed literal truth values \(u,v\) adds the three clauses \(t\to u,\ t\to v,\ (u\land v)\to t\). Introduce \(t+uv=0\) once in our system. From \(E_u\), multiply the atom \(u=0\) by \(v\), then add the defining equation to obtain \([u+1=0]\lor[t=0]\), the first clause. Exchange the factors for the second. For the third, multiply \(u+1=0\) in \(E_u\) by \(v\), add \(v+1=0\) from \(E_v\), then add the defining equation. The retained disjuncts give \([u=0]\lor[v=0]\lor[t+1=0]\). Signed inputs use the same variable axioms with their roles exchanged. Ordinary resolution now uses linear combination on complementary literal equations, deletion of the false constant, and contraction of duplicated literals. Gate clauses cost constant many nodes. Contractions in ordinary steps cost at most the explicit premise widths. This is a polynomial simulation of standard AND/negation extended-resolution refutations. It applies to precisely the same input CNF.

It follows that a short extended-resolution proof is among the shortcuts unrestricted product names can express. An unrestricted lower bound for this calculus would imply an extended-resolution proof-size lower bound on the same formulas, accounting for the polynomial simulation. The unnamed preprint theorem and the transfer into a system that retains the names provide no such bound. Neither the balanced examples nor the cost of our elimination algorithm demonstrates the existence of short proofs on the random sources.

## Checks

The named ancestry reader is based on reduced formal factors. Before serializing that ancestry with consecutive IDs, cancel repeated terms in every retained factor, then renumber; otherwise a pair of canceled mentions could reference a dropped name. The `prune` function implements that order and verifies the resulting certificate. The degree reporter also counts the explicit multiplier when the chosen atom is zero and its product disappears from the conclusion. The independence audit exercised a depth-32 common-factor proof as well as these two certificate-format details.

Signed one-gate and balanced multi-gate extended-resolution proofs are derived from actual clauses, expanded, and independently checked in the unnamed formal rules. The root assertion is recovered through a square before it contributes to the contradiction. Balanced cases reach formal degrees \(4,8,16\); lowering each permitted degree by one rejects the displayed proof. Separate replay tests dense factors and aliases. The common elimination entry point also handles a source with a previously cited empty input axiom: that axiom alone refutes the CNF in one node. This corrects the cached-last-node corner of the first count-only replay helper; all strict ternary cases and both mathematical bounds are unaffected.
