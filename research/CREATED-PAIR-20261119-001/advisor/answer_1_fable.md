Not checked against the repo; this is reasoning from your description only.

**Before freezing: check the loose counts.** The created pair's isometries take a distinction that is shared on one side and loose on the other. If the two stages therefore have different numbers of loose distinctions, "no isometry keeps the loose distinctions" is a counting fact, true of every bijection, and says nothing about isometries. Record the loose count per stage (and per twin class) as E0. If counts alone separate created from carried, that is the cheaper discriminator and the isometry enumeration adds little.

**(1) Neither notion is quite right.**
- **The twin-class version adds almost nothing.** Loose distinctions of one junction are twins in the table, and any isometry maps twin classes to twin classes. It can only differ from the setwise version through false twins (same row, different junctions). Record both, but expect them to agree.
- **What the account needs is the growing end kept.** That means loose onto loose *and* the junctions holding them onto junctions, out to whatever radius the law reads. The stages have other allowed states, so no isometry keeps every junction. The question is whether the moved junctions can sit away from the loose ones.
- **Small stages may not have an "away".** At 7–9 junctions most junctions hold a loose distinction. Make "moved junctions hold no loose distinction" the recorded prediction, not a side report.

**(2) Only one direction can be a lemma.**
- **Sufficiency (provable):** if the next table is a function of the table, the marked loose set and the loose-bearing junctions, then an isometry keeping those extends over the new junctions. This would explain "carried stays", and the referee's extra round is the same lemma applied again. It requires that growth reads nothing else; state that as the hypothesis.
- **Necessity (not a "must"):** the created pair refutes it. Its two starts had different tables, so no isometry at all, yet they grew into one table. Growth is not injective on marked tables.
- **Best available partial converse:** if the old distinctions are recognisable in the grown table and growth leaves old distances unchanged, any isometry of the grown stages restricts to a loose-keeping isometry of the old ones. So no loose-keeping isometry implies no age-respecting isometry next round. The four-way join may shorten old distances, so check that hypothesis first.
