# Can the higher-arity births carry a separate cycle?

**On both start-03 and start-14, every parent of every large age-nine birth also belongs to a different joining group.** The exact check uses the age-eight candidate relationships. This rules out a local way for higher-arity births to create a recurring cycle away from the tip. The same mechanism succeeds on a small funded control with a recurring unary tip and a contractible candidate complex. A second exact test rules out a cycle of entire true-twin classes on both reached stages. ## A local higher-arity certificate

Suppose actual children $P_0,\ldots,P_{m-1}$, with indices read modulo $m\ge4$, satisfy the following conditions in their literal parent sets. Consecutive sets meet in a single old site $v$. Sets two places apart are disjoint. Every site in $P_i\setminus\{v\}$ is incompatible with every site in $P_{i+1}\setminus\{v\}$. The $P_i$ may have arbitrarily many parents. Any other child meeting both $P_i$ and $P_{i+1}$ must use $v$, since a lawful child cannot contain two incompatible parents. Consequently every mutually compatible family containing this pair is held at $v$. Their pair is residual on the whole new stage. The disjointness two places apart supplies an allowed turn at every point. These turns propagate indefinitely, including with new groups outside the displayed family. [All-round walk proof](pure.md)

A way to recognize an entire arm is an old site $p$ used only by that larger group. Write $N[p]$ for $p$ and its candidates. Every candidate pair occurs in a joining group, so if only one group uses $p$ it equals $N[p]$. Conversely, if $N[p]$ has at least three sites, is mutually compatible, and is unheld, it is the unique maximal group through $p$. It covers every incident candidate pair, so no residual pair uses $p$. A possible extra one-parent offshoot affects neither direction about joining groups. This gives an exact test for such a private parent.

At age eight the test considered all 28,065 sites on 03 and 162,336 on 14. A site with fewer than two candidates cannot qualify. An incident holding block of size $|N[p]|$ holds the whole neighborhood. Finally, if a neighbor has fewer candidates than $p$, it cannot meet all of $N[p]$. After these exclusions, eight sites remain on 03 and sixteen on 14. For every one, the certificate supplies two actual neighbors that do not meet. The unique simplicial site on each stage is the tip with its single candidate. Thus neither stage has even one higher-arity arm with a private old site. This answers the private-parent question across all future births of this particular round, without enumerating them. In the positive control, take four sites $r_i$ in cyclic order, a site $w$ adjacent to all four, a tip adjacent only to $w$, and four sites $p_i$, each adjacent only to $r_i,r_{i+1}$. Hold each triangle $\{w,r_i,r_{i+1}\}$ and the pairs $\{p_i,r_i\}$, $\{p_i,r_{i+1}\}$ and $\{w,\mathrm{tip}\}$. Give every site its exact group demand, with one additional loose distinction at the tip. The four unheld ternary groups are $\{p_i,r_i,r_{i+1}\}$. Each $p_i$ is private. The residual pairs all go through $w$, so they have no cyclic walk. The four ternary offspring satisfy the local criterion and produce a permanent residual cycle outside the unary tip's ancestry. Deleting the four private sites and their attached triangular flaps, then the pendant tip, retracts the initial filled candidate complex to the cone on the original cycle. This verifies that a local higher-arity mechanism can succeed even when the full stage has trivial fundamental group. The checker enumerates both rounds, including the tip's offshoot. These holding blocks and open counts admit the connected dead-hub realization. The actual start-13 demonstrates the limitation of the private-parent test. Each parent of sites 85, 105, 178, 179 and 824 in its age-six exponential certificate takes part in at least 15, 12, 13, 13 and 15 births, respectively. Higher arity with multiply shared parents is sufficient there. The finite 781-site single-block tail on 04 likewise has no private parents at its complete subset layers of size $2$ through $779$, since each such subset has at least two one-site extensions. On 03 and 14 it remains open whether another set of such births has enough separated differences or forced common parents to survive every extension.

## Two restrictions on a projection

A global projection to a cycle that fixes a once-around loop is impossible on either complete age-eight stage. In fact, assign any image in the cycle to each old site and any consistently oriented path to each candidate pair. If on every filled old triangle the three paths fit inside a contractible proper arc, their concatenation is null. Consequently the winding of every edge loop factors through the old fundamental group. Both complete reached stages have trivial fundamental group, so none of these loops can wind once. A proposal defined only on selected legal groups need not extend across the held triangles. Its persistence needs a local argument such as the one above. The weaker proposal that requires proper arcs only on *legal enlargements* of the rim pairs also fails on these two explicit candidates.

Each diagonal is incompatible. On every rim pair the cone meets both ends, but the three sites have no common holding block. Each of the four triangles thus extends to an actual unheld maximal age-eight group, giving an age-nine birth. If each such enlargement had a proper-arc carrier compatible on shared edges, its cone triangle would map to a contractible arc. Adding the four triangle boundaries cancels the four radial paths, leaving the rim with zero winding. The displayed choices were found on a grid of 180 literal roster positions. The verified contradiction applies to these two candidates. It uses only the four tested positive faces on each stage. A stricter local proposal uses entire true-twin classes, sites with identical closed candidate neighborhoods. Suppose an arm consists of two whole classes $A,B$. If it is to be a maximal group, no third class may meet both. Otherwise any member of that class can enlarge the arm. This necessary condition also applies to the adjacent pairs in a cycle of such arms. A holding block witnessing a cross pair between $A,B$ then meets precisely these two classes. Hence it suffices to inspect all such holding blocks and test the exact common neighborhoods of their two classes.

At age eight there are 25,654 true-twin classes on 03, including 1,844 with multiple members. On 14 there are 161,870 classes, including 425 with multiple members. On each stage just two maximal holding blocks meet exactly two classes. Of the two class pairs, only one has no third common-neighbor class. Its union is a held pair of single sites. Thus no higher-arity arm of this specified kind exists, even before the test for its next turn.

There is a general reason to expect difficulty for this last proposal. Folding true twins preserves the filled complex up to homotopy: a clique and its image together still form a clique. In the quotient each candidate edge without a third common neighbor belongs to no filled triangle. A cycle of such edges would have nonzero first homology, since no sum of triangle boundaries could contain any of its edges. The literal counts give the stronger result here that only a single edge passes. The private-parent control shows why a local arm can still work when it has additional parents on individual links.
