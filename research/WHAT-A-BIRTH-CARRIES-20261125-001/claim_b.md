## Where ticket A left it

Under the recorded ordinary count with MET, the course from X only depends on how many of the second pattern's
members a bar left usable, through one count the law hands over at a start knot
([result](../WHAT-A-BIRTH-CARRIES-20261125-001A/results.md), computed, referee pending).

This ticket asks for the link instead: two parts the growth made that meet only through knots the growth made. With
it, the ticket asks whether the course of one depends on the other through that link, and under which of the listed
laws. A difference composed over several births is a later ticket; here it gets only the m reading (BD2 6.6).

The owner's premise stands (A244): a law is not a reader. His picture of a knot both sides descend from (A241)
gives the second verdict its name. In the record that knot has always been a start knot, except under a control law
of the fold study that looks at the parts.

## Settled by argument (argued, not refereed; each can be contradicted by a run)

- **Lemma N** (BD2 7.2, with its six hypotheses).
- **Lemma A, corrected.** Under every listed ration a newborn's fresh count is a function of (p, D, the implied flag),
  or, under the owing rations, of (p, the parent's owing, the implied flag). It is never a function of what its
  parents hold loose.
- **Lemma P, corrected.** Under the three recorded treatments a member's usable loose count at the start of the round
  in which it acts is fixed by its own birth. This fails under SHORT-BY.
- **Lemma L, corrected.** The course from X only through T is a function of X's state after round s and of the
  values used at X's births (RD 3.3, the implied flag and the guard's stop included).
- **No meeting without a taking rule** (FD argument 1). The negative control is empty for meetings by construction.
- **Under an unbounded ration** a difference that arrives at one birth is one integer handed, so the m reading can be
  ≥ 2 only through several births.

## Verdicts, as questions with exclusive answers

1. The link: only through made knots (strict), through a start knot, no meeting, or no linking knot (BD2 5.3).
2. Ancestral: yes in the FD 1.5 form (and in the every-member form, or not), or no.
3. Dependence: differs through the meeting; differs, not attributed (with the class named); equal; or undecided
   (the knot test not run on the barred run among the reasons).
4. m: no difference, handed in one value, or composed over several births.
5. The start's after all: each of the four checks, yes or no.
6. Supplied or made:
   - the law, the start, s, the bars and the bounds are supplied;
   - the parts, their meeting and the linking knots are made by births;
   - "the start supplies the ancestors; the knot and both parts are made".

## What a result would and would not show

**It would show:**
- whether, under a listed law that uses no names, two parts the growth made meet through a knot the growth made that
  both descend from;
- whether one part's course then depends on the other through it;
- which listed laws are the least that do each.

**It would not show** a fold of emergence (A223: a pattern the rules make, whose making depends on another pattern,
and which makes a pattern of its own). Where one value at one birth separates the cases, the law handed a count over
and no pattern read anything.
