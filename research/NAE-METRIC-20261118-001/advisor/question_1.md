Setting. A base is a set of constraints ("knots") on Boolean variables ("distinctions"); its allowed states are the satisfying assignments. A set S is FREE if the projection of the allowed states onto S is all of {0,1}^S, CONSTRAINED otherwise. A BOND is a constrained set all of whose (|S|-1)-subsets are free. Bond distance b(x,y) = (size of least bond holding x and y) - 1, infinite if none. A base is COMPLEMENT-CLOSED if the complement of every allowed state is allowed (true for every base of not-all-equal knots NAE(a,b,c) and of betweenness knots "forbid 010 and 101" in any place order, and any mixture). Known (previous study, refereed): b is a metric on every parity base, fails on two NAE knots sharing two variables {a,b,c},{a,b,d} (no bond holds c,d); on knot trees of NAE-class knots b - 1 = steps apart (number of knots on the path).

Question set by the coordinator: where is NAE bond distance a true distance? Aim: a condition on how the knots meet.

My candidate theorem (please check every step):

Setup. B complement-closed with at least one state. A distinction y SEPARATES if the knots split into nonempty groups LL and RR whose variable sets meet only in y. Let L = vars(LL) - y, R = vars(RR) - y.
(F1) Allowed states of B = pairs of allowed states of LL and RR agreeing on y.
(F2) Each side allows both values of y (complement-closed, nonempty), so the projection of B onto L u {y} is LL's own state set.

Lemma 1 (sides). For x, w in L u {y}: b_B(x,w) = b_LL(x,w). Proof: by F2 bonds of B inside L u y are LL's bonds. Let T be a bond of B holding x, w with T_R = T n R nonempty, T_L = T n L. Case y in T: T_R u {y} is free (proper subset), so the projection onto T is P(T_L u y) x {0,1}^{T_R}; T constrained forces T_L u y constrained, a proper subset: contradiction. Case y not in T: projection onto T = {(u,r): Y_L(u) n Y_R(r) nonempty}, Y_L(u) = allowed y-values with u on T_L, nonempty since T_L is free; T constrained gives u0, r0 with Y_L(u0) = {v}, Y_R(r0) = {not v}. So T_L u {y} is constrained and holds a bond B' of LL; y is in B' (else B' inside T_L, a free set). If x not in B': B' - y lies inside T_L - x; B' misses some pattern pi, so pi restricted to B'-y forces y = not pi_y, and the complement pattern forces y = pi_y; pick the one forcing y = v and combine with r0: then (T_L - x) u T_R is constrained, so T - x is constrained: contradiction. So x in B', likewise w in B', and |B'| <= |T_L| + 1 <= |T|.

Lemma 2 (additivity). x in L, z in R: b(x,z) - 1 = (b(x,y) - 1) + (b(y,z) - 1), infinite if either is.
Upper: S1 least bond of LL holding x,y; S2 least bond of RR holding y,z; T = (S1 u S2) - {y}, |T| = |S1| + |S2| - 2. Every T - t is free: if t in S1 - y, given values on T - t the R side's values on S2 - y (free) allow some y-value; S1 - t is free and holds y, so the L side extends with that y; join by F1. T is constrained: S2 misses rho; rho on S2 - z occurs (free) and forces z = not rho_z; choose an assignment of S1 - y forcing y = rho_y (complement gives both values); then on T - z that assignment forces y and then z, so a pattern of T is missing. So T is a bond.
Lower: T a bond holding x, z. y is not in T (else the projection is the join over y of two full projections, full). As in Lemma 1, T_L u y and T_R u y are constrained and contain bonds B_L, B_R holding y; x in B_L and z in B_R by the same complement argument (else T - x or T - z constrained). So |T| = |T_L| + |T_R| >= b(x,y) + b(y,z).

Theorem (blocks). Take the blocks of the incidence graph (variables and knots as vertices; biconnected components; a knot in no cycle is its own block). For x, z in a common block, b(x,z) is the block's own; otherwise b - 1 adds along the cut variables between their blocks. Hence b is a metric on B iff it is a metric on every block (gluing: d = b - 1 is a tree-sum; the triangle inequality for b, i.e. d(x,z) <= d(x,y) + d(y,z) + 1, reduces to the block where the three routes meet).

Rings. A ring of m >= 3 NAE-class knots K_i = {v_{i-1}, p_i, v_i} (indices mod m), p_i private. Computed: b = steps + 1 on every pair for m = 3..8, untwisted and twisted (one knot switched at a ring place). Upper bound proved: the path set along the shorter route ({x, z} and the relative third of each route knot) is a bond, since the off-route arc has free thirds and so completes any end values. Lower bound: for x, z both ring variables v, if a bond T misses a third p_j on one route and p_l on the other, forgetting them cuts the ring into two arcs with x and z apart, so T projects as a product; hence T holds all thirds of one route: |T| >= 2 + steps. For x = p_i (a private third) the cut at x's own knot is at a ring variable v_{i-1} shared with K_{i-1}, and forgetting it does not cut. I do not have the lower bound for pairs involving thirds.

Split bases (tonight's grown lines and grids). States = the two constant states plus both sides of every split of a split system. Then b(x,y)=1 iff no split separates x,y (equal columns); b(x,y)=2 iff some z separated from both has one of x,y,z between the other two in the split metric (no split isolates it). Lines (>= 3 points) and full grids satisfy this for every pair, so b <= 2 everywhere and b is a metric. Explored: every step of 18 growth runs (six rules) has b <= 2.
