# Bond distances expose the false equation on the four-place cycle

**The six proposed distinctions cannot be a parity equation in any reachable arrangement with the same bond-distance table.** All fifteen pairs among the six look close enough. Two surrounding four-place junctions have equations that the table forces. If the proposed equation also held, adding those three equations would give a four-distinction equation on a pair too far apart to appear in one. Bond distances through three already see the contradiction. It survives every later mixed join, even when every distinction is renamed. **The equation and the next join have different cutoffs.** A width-26 source supplies a real legal triple. With the table clipped at distance two, a renaming turns the six distinctions into a true six-bit bond and sends the triple to a forbidden choice. At distance three the false equation is excluded, yet a different renaming still changes the triple verdict. Distance four fixes every junction and every legal choice in this example, even against competitors with entirely different histories. The same bound is sharp on all five live continuations tested. At 71 distinctions a renaming preserves all 2,485 entries through distance four and sends a legal triple to a forbidden one. The introduced cycle and marked holders belong to the same part after every ordinary bridge is removed. Twenty entries distinguish the marks at distance five.
