## The answer

The direction asked whether the structure itself can hold the count that tells a knot which of the knots it meets is
its parent, so that the last supplied record of the births-one-at-a-time line (assumption A247's stamp) is removed.

**The record is moved, not removed.** It can be shown in a knot's own places, but only because a ration writes it
there, and only relative to a start supplied with its meeting knots already agreeing.

1. **Under the recorded laws nothing the structure shows can give the order on a population that holds one structure
   both as grown and as a start** (the note's first argument; argued, not refereed). Any function of the structure
   gives one answer at a place that is a parent place in one history and a place between two start knots in the other.
   The smallest case is a lone knot and its first offshoot, which a renaming exchanges, under ten recorded rations
   (checked: 10 of 10). The record's own census holds such structures: 515 of its 3,348 starts of up to five knots are
   reached within round 1 from a smaller census start (computed; 4 after a complete round, 511 only within one). A
   structure after a complete round, handed in as a start, grows exactly as the continuation does (140 of 140).
2. **A ration that counts places in threes shows the order** (the second argument; argued, not refereed). Give each
   new knot 0, 1 or 2 further fresh distinctions so that its count is one more, counted in threes, than its parents'.
   Then across a shared distinction the two holders' counts tell parent from child: right at every place on all 20,677
   runs where the argument's four conditions hold, wrong somewhere on all 1,800 runs where some birth's parents
   disagree, and wrong at a place between start knots on all 45,142 runs from a start whose meeting knots disagree.
3. **This is the stamp, written by the law.** With the added distinctions barred, births, parents and rounds are the
   base law's in 616 of 616 runs; that the rule can be deleted without changing a birth is what shows it is a stamp.
   Its one effect on the growth: a structure that would close goes on growing (22 of the 48 runs whose base law
   closes).
4. **It needs a start supplied flat.** A start's meeting knots agree in their counts on 133 of 3,348 census starts
   (804 of 82,372 at six knots), or by the convention that a start has no barred place.
5. **It needs parents at equal parent steps, counted in threes, and that fails far more often than the earlier study
   said.** Under the least-record study's waiting law some birth has parents at different parent steps on 510 of the
   3,348 census starts (19% with each numbering counted once); on 223, 155, 351 and 400 under four other waiting laws;
   on 48, 48, 369 and 462 under four holding laws, where no knot waits; on none under the three laws without waiting or
   holding. Counting in fives repairs 501 of the 510; a difference of three parent steps between a child and a parent
   occurs, so no fixed count is known to be enough.

Sixteen predictions and a set of checks were judged. All hold on what was run; two sentences of the frozen text are too
broad as worded (what a planted error changes, right only where the four conditions hold; a range "by law" that left
out one law). Every prediction was written after the drafter's scratch runs, so the run tests the counts, and the
arguments are the study's content.

## The three tests, answered

- **Inputs.** The recorded rations take no input that determines the parents' counts: *not written*. The count forms
  do: *written*.
- **Factoring.** Deleting the rule leaves every birth as it was under the barred forms (*factors*: it is an
  annotation), and changes births under the usable form (*does not factor*: equal to the base law's in 28 of 308 runs).
- **Restart.** On the census and on every population that admits each allowed start: *two records*.

The order would be made, in the only sense the note admits, if a law that is *not written* showed it on a population
of *one record*. No law here does.
