# A first mixed birth on the smallest identified source

The seven-knot, 25-distinction [source](source.md) also supports a pair-specific meeting. This is a secondary result with an explicitly designed observer. Its recognition test was written down after the first, 21-knot proof and before measuring these words. [Secondary design](minimum_test.md)

The two physical return paths can be written A=(4,0,2,5) and B=(4,0,3,6). Recover the original holding pattern, both possible orientations and the possible exchange of the two arms from the accumulating holdings. No ordering of the two arms or old knot names is an input to the reader. The two paths have the same initial two points and differ in their last two points. All their concatenations, including across a period boundary, are nonretracing in the witness map. Hence each window is a real straight cluster whose two parents are its two endpoint deletions. At sizes of interest the two parents are different.

For an even n≥2 put U=AⁿB and V=Aⁿ⁺¹B. Take both infinite periodic walks obtained by repeating U⁵V and U⁶V. Let L be the largest number of consecutive points the two walks share, allowing all phases and either reversal. Set K=L+1. The source reading accepts a straight window if it contains no K-point factor of either walk for any permitted n and either arm choice. This is a finite test on each finite window. The source remains active on unbounded all-A windows. Its accumulated holdings are connected by repeated parent deletion back to its connected preparation. Complete current cuts suffice for anonymous recovery. At an arbitrary partial writing, old knot 2 can be indistinguishable from the very first new child of 4 and 6. Such a partial display is outside the origin-recognition theorem. The source keeps issuing at unbounded sizes. There is no claim here that this particular live source stays connected at every size. The primary 21-knot source has that additional property.

**Exact threshold.** Put h=n+1 and count first in whole excursions A or B. The two periods are p=6h+1 and q=7h+1 excursions, with greatest common divisor one. Match their long V and center U⁵ V U⁵. On the left the next V and U have the common suffix U. On the right V and U have the common prefix Aⁿ. In total 13h consecutive excursions agree. A common run of p+q−1=13h+1 excursions is impossible. In such a run, of period p and q, comparing positions i and i+q for 0≤i≤p−2 equates p−1 adjacent links of the cyclic permutation i↦i+q modulo p. The permutation is one cycle, so all p excursion letters would agree. Both A and B occur. In a long common physical interval the repeated type 4 aligns the four-point phases. Disagreeing excursions have common prefix (4,0) and no common suffix. A forward interior contains 4→0 and never 0→4, excluding a long match with the reverse orientation. Thus L=52(n+1)+2 points and K=52(n+1)+3. This also bounds the indices the reader must check at a given size.

**Two releases and a comparison.** Every length K−1 factor of either walk passes the source reading. For its own n it is shorter than the forbidden size. For a different even m, a forbidden word of size K_m is long enough to contain complete consecutive minority excursions with exactly m or m+1 majority excursions between them. The possible gaps n and n+1 are disjoint from these. Under arm exchange, such a forbidden word contains consecutive visits to the original minority arm, which does not occur in the tested walk. Hence every launch member has both parents in the source. At K every phase first fails the source test. Later, the two parents of each of its phases are in its own packet. Each subfamily has exactly its primitive period in phases: there is one longer run per period, the physical type 4 identifies the four-point phase, and L already exceeds both point periods. Distinct n remain distinguishable by their gaps. For a given n, all phases of each of the two packets are disjoint from the other because they do not agree on K sites. The reader returns the family of physical packets. Exchanging the symmetric arms exchanges family members. If each product must be a single invariant set, take both arm images together for each exponent. These two invariant products still make a joint birth. The opposite-arm comparison below is about the individual phase cycles within them.

A longest common interval I has a predecessor on the first walk and a successor on the second. The whole interval xIy is nonretracing, since any consecutive triple in it lies inside xI or Iy. It is an actual child of one member of each packet. Each packet continues forever with its own two pure parents. In the *same initial structure*, exchange the two arms of just the second party. Long intervals of the first walk contain consecutive visits to its majority arm, which cannot occur in that version of the second walk. The two parties have no common (K−1)-parent and cannot share a child at the next size or any later size. Individually the exchanged packet has exactly the same physical production up to renaming. What differs is which of these two parties can produce something together. Their individual total rosters are fixed inside this comparison. At n=2 the packets of 76 and 88 phases first coexist at size 159 and have two certified straight mixed births at 160. Branching mixed births are not enumerated by this count. The first selected n is a property of this observer. [Exact checks](periodic.md)

This gives the sharp seven-knot, 25-distinction minimum for a recurring source with a *pair-specific* mixed output in the class with a proved map. The lower bound uses the source requirement. It is not a minimum for isolated encounters between arbitrary pre-existing families. The [21-knot matched preparations](meeting.md) additionally change the total output and actual fresh count while both parties continue. Their minimum, and minima outside the identified class, are open.
