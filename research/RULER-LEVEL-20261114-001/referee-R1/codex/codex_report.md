## 1. Theorem A** and its dependency

**Not a block:** T1 genuinely applies. Its standard statement explicitly covers finite 3-uniform Berge-acyclic patterns, including several components; it imposes no minimum incidence degree. The leaf lemma handles distinctions in just one knot. Distinct addresses are ordinary distinct variables. The builder discards triples with repeated images and deduplicates triples, giving precisely the simple 3-uniform object T1 assumes. T1 minimizes over **all** bond supports containing the chosen pair, not just a path or levels above the target. Its Lemmas A–C, incidence count, lower bound, and alternating-chain construction establish the stated result; this is a proof, not an inference from its previous checks. The same-knot case is explicitly `d=1`. The harmless implicit requirement that the pair be distinct is automatic here because `s>=1`.

**Not a block:** There is no distance off-by-one. T1 counts knots on the incidence path, equivalently the number of co-knotted-pair steps. Traversing any knot changes address length by at most one. Thus `s <= sum |level change| <= d`. A descending chain has exactly `s` traversals and attains this bound. The final `+2` is bond **size**, not bond distance, whose glossary definition subtracts one.

**Block — overview, not the displayed theorem:** `ruler_level.md:16` says “there is no bond otherwise.” That is false. The two arms of `proof.md:18–20` are correct but do not cover a connected pair of levels with no descending chain. The complete statement is

\[
D_k(s)=\min\{d_H(u,v): |u|=k,\ |v|=k+s,\ u,v\text{ in one component}\},\qquad
L_k(s)=D_k(s)+2.
\]

If the minimum has no candidates, there is no cross-level bond. In a forest, `L_k(s)=s+2` **if and only if** a descending chain exists; otherwise a path can give a larger finite value.

For example, at `N=3` take knots `{0,00,01}`, `{00,10,11}`, `{10,000,001}`, where strings are addresses and all other addresses are isolated. From level 1 to level 3 there is a three-knot path, no two-knot descending chain, and exact reach **5**. The crossing bond `{0,01,11,000,001}` forbids `(0,0,1,0,0)` and every proper projection is full. This is an addressed finite pattern satisfying A**'s hypotheses, not a claim that this particular pattern is generated by a registered description.

Fresh tests include every incidence forest on seven labelled distinctions (1,156 patterns, 24,276 pairs, 5,530 bonds) and 120 random forests on 5–13 distinctions, plus layered addressed controls with within-level knots, paths that ascend before descending, and several components. The random set includes 77 patterns with several components and 75 with within-level knots. Exact full-state/support enumeration found no T1 or complete-formula violation; the permitted prior referee exact search independently agreed on all 423 random level queries. See `forest_audit/` for the detailed line audit and outputs. The lead also reran the displayed detour counterexample and obtained 5.

## 2. Theorem A

**Not a block:** Every proof step survives inspection:

| Step in `proof.md:37–42` | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| Three distinct addresses | One has length `n`; the other two have length `n+1` and different last letters. |
| Distinct source words give distinct knots | The sole upper member is `g(w)`; injectivity settles equal lengths, and minimum length distinguishes different lengths. |
| Exactly one upper/lower occurrence | `g` permutes each level. The unique parent of `v0` is `g(h1^-1(v))`, and analogously for `v1`. |
| No two distinct knots share a pair | Knots with the same upper level have disjoint upper members and disjoint lower members. Knots on consecutive level pairs can share only the deeper knot's sole upper member. Other level pairs are disjoint. |
| Deepest-address cycle argument | If a cycle used the downward knot of a deepest cycle address, another cycle address in that knot would be deeper. Both cycle knots must therefore be its parent knot, contradicting their distinctness. This also excludes a two-knot Berge cycle. |
| Connectedness and descending chains | Repeated unique-parent ascent reaches the sole level-0 address; every non-bottom address has its own knot and two children. |
| Words are length-preserving automorphisms | Each binary Mealy state has an output permutation; one recovers the input letter and recursively inverts the selected successor action. Compositions remain rooted-tree automorphisms. |

Here “tree” and “incidence graph” have their classical meanings: acyclicity is Berge/incidence acyclicity, not absence of three-pair cycles inside a single knot. The knot parent need not be the word prefix.

Fresh structural tests: **120 descriptions × seven depths `N={0,1,2,4,6,7,9}` = 840 patterns**, with independent builders agreeing and all incidence-forest, occurrence, pair-sharing, ascent, and descending-chain checks passing. Registered words were checked as level permutations compatible with prefixes through depth 7. Fresh exact searches at **N=6 and N=7 give 24/24 expected values**, including descriptions whose knot parents differ from word prefixes. Negative structural controls and independent small truth-table controls pass. See `tree_audit/`.

## 5. Known mathematics

**Unverified background from memory; no network or priority search was performed.** Leaf elimination, extension over incidence forests, and localization of constraints to connecting paths are standard ideas from tree-structured constraint satisfaction. NAE's alternating forcing along a path is an elementary Boolean constraint/resolution argument. The exact quantitative **minimum support `d+2`**, including support-minimality and minimization over arbitrary off-path members, is stronger than merely saying that a tree CSP is easy to solve; I cannot certify its external priority from memory. It is already the explicit local theorem T1, so A** adds no new version of that theorem beyond applying the level bound. A adds an elementary structural observation about the chosen address placements.

Do not equate arbitrary prime-implicate supports with bonds: a prime clause can have a support containing a smaller non-full set with a different sign pattern. A bond requires **every proper support to be full**. Also, Berge acyclicity of the **NAE constraint pattern** is the relevant notion; expanding each NAE knot into two CNF clauses does not give an incidence-acyclic CNF, because those clauses share all three variables. General claims about “acyclic CNFs” need the precise acyclicity notion and translation.

**Not a block:** The defensible contribution is the application to these addressed patterns, the explicit placement-family observation, and independently reproducible census/counterexample accounting. This audit establishes no new general CSP theorem or external novelty claim.
