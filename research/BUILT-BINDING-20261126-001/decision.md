## What stands, in four parts

1. **(a) Bindings exist after ordinary growth, on built starts.** On all 32 built ring families (three or four rings
   of four to seven knots joined through connecting knots that hold no loose distinction), at rounds 2 and 3, each
   ring's new carriers are class facts read by no single knot under both readings of a small change, and together
   they obey one parity rule with every proper part free (62 rows read; 2 past the size cap not read). Also on the
   advisor's 14-knot start, and under the shortest changes on the binding referee's sampled ten-knot start. [R1,
   Block:] the earlier disjoint-cycle search gave none on its roster, but disjoint parity carriers bind on a held-out
   eight-knot start under changes of at most five, and on 7 of 67 stages of the 400 sampled starts of nine to twelve
   knots at rounds 2 and 3; under the shortest changes none of 203 grown sampled stages binds.
2. **(b) The relation is the start's own sum of knot laws.** Every carrier has a start representative, the
   representatives obey the same relation on the start's allowed states, and its sum is the sum of the start's laws.
   One loose distinction placed far from every ring dissolves it. With the advisor's argument (its premises held on
   every birth record of 24,616 funded rounds and its ranks on the 24,568 stages within the 400-distinction limit, the
   referee checking the other 48; R1 gives an independent proof for finite funded ordinary stages), ordinary growth
   makes no relation among class facts on any stage read.
3. **(c) Growth turns that relation into one among class facts, with carriers of its own.** Before growth the rings'
   parities were read by no single knot but were not class facts under changes of at most five distinctions on any
   family, and under the shortest changes only on the double-link families: a five-distinction change ran from one
   ring's loose distinction through the connecting knots to another's. Growth raises the least size of a change
   flipping a ring's parity (5, 7, 9 and 11 at rounds 0 to 3, R1), so from round 1 every parity is a class fact under
   both readings, and from round 2 it is carried again by disjoint distinctions born the round before [R1: each
   carrier needs all its members when read alone, but the same fact has a smaller reader; on rings of even length a
   carrier splits into two parts that are themselves unread class facts].
4. **(d) The forbidding cuts make a relation the start lacked.** On the 8 three-ring detour families with the middle
   knots joined, both cuts give one three-parent birth no fresh distinction, and its law binds the rings' parities
   (8 joint values at the start, 4 after); ordinary growth leaves them free; all one-fewer runs and six of eight
   arity-floor runs stall the next round, and two arity-floor runs stall at round 4 (R1).
