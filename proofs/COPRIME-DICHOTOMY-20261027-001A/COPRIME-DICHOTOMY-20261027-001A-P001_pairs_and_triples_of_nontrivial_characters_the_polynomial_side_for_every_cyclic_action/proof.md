# Pairs and triples of nontrivial characters: the polynomial side for every cyclic action

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

**Setting.**
- p is an odd prime. N is a finite p-group of exponent p and nilpotency class c < p, and 𝔫 = log N is its Lie algebra over
  F_p (Lazard correspondence).
- C_r = ⟨σ⟩ acts on N by automorphisms, with r | p − 1, and G = N ⋊ C_r. Fix ζ ∈ F_p^× of order r.
- The action on 𝔫 is semisimple (r is prime to p), so 𝔫 = ⊕_{j ∈ Z/r} 𝔫_j, with σ acting on 𝔫_j by ζ^j and
  [𝔫_i, 𝔫_j] ⊆ 𝔫_{i+j}.
- **Sector orders.** For d | r, let C_d = ⟨σ^{r/d}⟩. Its characters are the classes ϑ mod d, with
  𝔫^{(d)}_ϑ = ⊕_{j ≡ ϑ (mod d)} 𝔫_j. Write 𝔫′_d = ⊕_{ϑ ≢ 0} 𝔫^{(d)}_ϑ for the part on which C_d acts nontrivially.
- **The criterion at d.**
  - (P2_d), **pairs:** [𝔫^{(d)}_α, 𝔫^{(d)}_β] = 0 whenever α, β and α + β are nonzero mod d.
  - (P3_d), **triples:** [[𝔫^{(d)}_α, 𝔫^{(d)}_{−α}], 𝔫^{(d)}_β] = 0 for all nonzero α, β mod d.
- **Cochains.** For ψ: π → C_r with image C_d and a class ϑ mod d, F_p^{ϑψ} is F_p with π acting through ψ followed by the
  character ϑ of C_d. Z¹(π; F_p^{ϑψ}) denotes the twisted 1-cocycles.
- No grading of 𝔫 is assumed in this ticket.

**Lemma 1 (pairs collapse the action to an involution).** For d | r the following are equivalent:
- (i) (P2_d) and (P3_d);
- (ii) [𝔫′_d, 𝔫′_d] ⊆ 𝔫^{(d)}_0 and [[𝔫′_d, 𝔫′_d], 𝔫′_d] = 0;
- (iii) the subalgebra generated by 𝔫′_d is 𝔫′_d ⊕ Z, where Z = [𝔫′_d, 𝔫′_d] ⊆ 𝔫^{(d)}_0 is central in it;
- (iv) the map θ_d, equal to +1 on 𝔫^{(d)}_0 and −1 on 𝔫′_d, is an automorphism of 𝔫, and the Lie triple system of its −1 part
  is abelian.

Moreover:
- (P2_d) alone is equivalent to θ_d being an automorphism.
- The criterion at d implies the criterion at every divisor d′ of d.

**Theorem 2 (rigid count).**
- **Hypotheses.** π is a group with H¹(π; F_p) = 0 and H²(π; F_p) = 0. ψ: π → C_r is a homomorphism whose image has order d, and
  the criterion holds at d.
- **The count.**

  |Hom_ψ(π, G)| = Π_{ϑ ≢ 0 (mod d)} |Z¹(π; F_p^{ϑψ})|^{dim 𝔫^{(d)}_ϑ}.

- **Rigidity.** Every homomorphism over ψ lands in the class-2 subgroup exp(𝔫′_d ⊕ Z) ⋊ C_d. It is determined by its image in
  the vector group 𝔫′_d ⋊ C_d, which is an 𝔫′_d-valued twisted cocycle.
- **Without the criterion** the right-hand side is still an upper bound for |Hom_ψ(π, G)|.

**Corollary 3 (polynomial counting).**
- Let M be a closed oriented 3-manifold with H₁(M; F_p) = 0. Then

  |Hom(π₁M, G)| = Σ_{ψ ∈ Hom(H₁(M), C_r)} |Hom_ψ(π₁M, G)|.

- Every sector order divides D(M) = gcd(r, exponent of H₁(M)). If the criterion holds at D(M), Theorem 2 gives every term.
- **Polynomial time.** On a class of such manifolds with |Hom(H₁(M), C_r)| bounded, and with the criterion at every D(M), counting is
  polynomial in the size of a triangulation or balanced presentation. The terms are coranks of twisted Fox matrices over F_p.
- In particular, if the criterion holds at d = r, counting is polynomial on all closed 3-manifolds with H₁(M; F_p) = 0 and a
  bounded number of sectors.

**What it says.**
- **(P2) means the action is really an involution.** The C_d-action collapses to θ_d: nontrivial characters bracket only with their
  inverses, and only into the fixed part. A hard pair of the class-(K) dichotomy is exactly a failure of this collapse.
- **(P3) is then the r = 2 criterion for θ_d.** The triple system of the collapsed involution must be abelian.
- So at every sector order, "easy" means: the action collapses to an involution whose symmetric pair has an abelian triple system.
  The count is then a pure dimension formula, in every class c < p.
- **Interpretation boundary.** r counts the characters of the action, not Boolean variables. This is a statement about counting
  complexity only, not about emergence.

## Nearest known results

- **This program.**
  - `TRIPLE-SYSTEM-20261027-001A` is the case r = 2, with the same lower-bound and upper-bound argument.
  - `COPRIME-CLASS2-20261026-001C` and `CLASSK-DICHOTOMY-20261026-001C` give the class-2 polynomial side at every r.
  - Lemma 1 (iv), the collapse to an involution, is new here. It reduces the polynomial side at every r to the involution case.
- **Samperton–Weiss (arXiv:2602.02885).** Class-2 nilpotent targets are polynomial with bounded H². Their method and ours are both
  obstruction theory on a 3-manifold whose relevant cohomology is controlled. Here the coprime action is what makes the trivial
  part rigid.
- **Lie triple systems and symmetric pairs** (Jacobson; Loos). Standard.
- **Novelty.**
  - Candidate new: the criterion for nilpotent-by-cyclic targets of every class, and its reduction to an involution.
  - Folklore risk: medium. The proof is a few lines of nonabelian cohomology once the subalgebra 𝔫′_d ⊕ Z is noticed.

## Proof

Write 𝔫_ϑ for 𝔫^{(d)}_ϑ and 𝔫′ for 𝔫′_d.

### 1. Lemma 1

- **(i) ⇒ (ii).**
  - [𝔫′, 𝔫′] = Σ_{α, β ≢ 0} [𝔫_α, 𝔫_β]. By (P2) only the terms with β = −α survive, and [𝔫_α, 𝔫_{−α}] ⊆ 𝔫_0.
  - Then [[𝔫′, 𝔫′], 𝔫′] = Σ_{α, β} [[𝔫_α, 𝔫_{−α}], 𝔫_β] = 0 by (P3).
- **(ii) ⇒ (i).**
  - If α, β and α + β are nonzero, then [𝔫_α, 𝔫_β] ⊆ [𝔫′, 𝔫′] ∩ 𝔫_{α+β} ⊆ 𝔫_0 ∩ 𝔫_{α+β} = 0. This is (P2).
  - (P3) is part of (ii).
- **(ii) ⇒ (iii).**
  - The subalgebra 𝔥 generated by 𝔫′ is spanned by left-normed words [[[a₁, a₂], a₃], …, a_k] with a_i ∈ 𝔫′.
  - Words of length 3 or more vanish, since [[a₁, a₂], a₃] = 0. So 𝔥 = 𝔫′ + Z, and the sum is direct because Z ⊆ 𝔫_0.
  - **Z is central in 𝔥.** [Z, 𝔫′] = 0 by (ii). By Jacobi, [[a, b], z] = [a, [b, z]] − [b, [a, z]] = 0 for z ∈ Z.
- **(iii) ⇒ (ii).** [𝔫′, 𝔫′] = Z ⊆ 𝔫_0, and [[𝔫′, 𝔫′], 𝔫′] = [Z, 𝔫′] = 0.
- **(P2) ⟺ θ_d is an automorphism.**
  - θ_d preserves the brackets [𝔫_0, 𝔫_0] ⊆ 𝔫_0 and [𝔫_0, 𝔫′] ⊆ 𝔫′ in any case.
  - On [𝔫_α, 𝔫_β] with α, β ≢ 0, it is compatible iff the bracket lies in 𝔫_0. That is (P2) together with [𝔫_α, 𝔫_{−α}] ⊆ 𝔫_0,
    which always holds.
- **(iv) ⟺ (i).** Given (P2), the −1 part of θ_d is 𝔫′, and its triple system [[𝔫′, 𝔫′], 𝔫′] vanishes iff (P3) holds.
- **Divisors.** Let d′ | d, and write 𝔫^{(d′)}_{α′} = ⊕_{α ≡ α′ (d′)} 𝔫_α.
  - (P2_{d′}). If α′, β′ and α′ + β′ are nonzero mod d′, every lift α, β has α, β and α + β nonzero mod d. So (P2_d) kills
    [𝔫_α, 𝔫_β].
  - (P3_{d′}). Take lifts α ≡ α′, γ ≡ −α′ and β ≡ β′, all nonzero mod d.
    - If α + γ ≢ 0 mod d, then [𝔫_α, 𝔫_γ] = 0 by (P2_d).
    - Otherwise γ = −α, and [[𝔫_α, 𝔫_{−α}], 𝔫_β] = 0 by (P3_d). ∎

### 2. Theorem 2

Write Z¹_ϑ := Z¹(π; F_p^{ϑψ}). The hypothesis H¹(π; F_p) = 0 gives Z¹_0 = Hom(π, F_p) = 0. Every homomorphism over ψ lands in
N ⋊ C_d.

- **Lower bound.**
  - 𝔥 = 𝔫′ ⊕ Z (Lemma 1) is C_d-stable, so H = exp(𝔥) ⋊ C_d is a subgroup of G.
  - Z is central in 𝔥 and fixed by C_d, so exp(Z) is central in H.
  - The quotient H/exp(Z) is the vector group 𝔫′ ⋊ C_d. An abelian Lie algebra exponentiates to its additive group.
  - Homomorphisms π → 𝔫′ ⋊ C_d over ψ are exactly the twisted cocycles ⊕_{ϑ ≢ 0} Z¹_ϑ ⊗ 𝔫_ϑ.
  - Each lifts through the central extension by exp(Z), on which π acts trivially:
    - the obstruction lies in H²(π; Z) = H²(π; F_p) ⊗ Z = 0;
    - the lifts form a torsor under Hom(π, Z) = 0, so the lift is unique.
  - So |Hom_ψ(π, H)| = Π_{ϑ ≢ 0} |Z¹_ϑ|^{dim 𝔫_ϑ}, and these are homomorphisms into G.
- **Upper bound (no criterion needed).**
  - The lower central series γ_k of N is C_r-stable. L_k = γ_k/γ_{k+1} is central in N/γ_{k+1}, and C_d acts on it.
  - The fibres of Hom_ψ(π, (N/γ_{k+1}) ⋊ C_d) → Hom_ψ(π, (N/γ_k) ⋊ C_d) are empty or torsors under Z¹(π; L_k), with π acting
    through ψ.
  - Split L_k = ⊕_ϑ L_{k,ϑ} (semisimple action). Then |Z¹(π; L_k)| = Π_ϑ |Z¹_ϑ|^{dim L_{k,ϑ}}, and the factor ϑ = 0 is 1.
  - As C_d-modules, 𝔫 ≅ ⊕_k γ_k(𝔫)/γ_{k+1}(𝔫), and Lazard identifies γ_k(N)/γ_{k+1}(N) with γ_k(𝔫)/γ_{k+1}(𝔫) equivariantly.
    So Σ_k dim L_{k,ϑ} = dim 𝔫_ϑ, and

    |Hom_ψ(π, G)| ≤ Π_{ϑ ≢ 0} |Z¹_ϑ|^{dim 𝔫_ϑ}.

- **Conclusion.** Under the criterion the two bounds agree. So every homomorphism over ψ lies in H, and the count is as stated. ∎

### 3. Corollary 3

- **The hypotheses hold.** For M closed and oriented with H₁(M; F_p) = 0:
  - H¹(π₁M; F_p) = Hom(H₁(M), F_p) = 0;
  - H²(π₁M; F_p) injects into H²(M; F_p) ≅ H₁(M; F_p) = 0 (Hopf).
- **Sectors.** Every homomorphism has a C_r-part ψ ∈ Hom(H₁(M), C_r). Since H₁(M) is finite (b₁ > 0 would give H₁(M; F_p) ≠ 0),
  the image of ψ has order dividing D(M). By Lemma 1 the criterion at D(M) gives it at that order. The sector ψ = 0 contributes the
  single trivial homomorphism into the p-group N.
- **Polynomial time.**
  - From a triangulation or a balanced presentation, H₁(M) is computed by Smith normal form.
  - The boundedly many ψ are enumerated, and for each ψ and ϑ the corank of a twisted Fox matrix over F_p gives dim Z¹_ϑ.
  - All of this is polynomial. ∎
