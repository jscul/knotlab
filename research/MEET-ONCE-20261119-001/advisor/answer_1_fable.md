**Bet on M1: yes, at about 70%.**

The likeliest counterexample is a Whitney-type ambiguity. Three junctions that pairwise meet in three different distinctions look the same at distance 1 as three junctions through one shared distinction. Meet-once rules out the two-shared case behind the 152, but not this one. I'd check stages containing such triples first; if M1 fails, I expect it there.

**Escalation: sound, with two conditions.**

- **Regrouping:** pooling above H is a function of pooling above any H' > H, so each regroup only splits buckets and never separates stages with equal full tables. This requires the key to be invariant under renaming (a canonical form or sorted profile), not a raw matrix.
- **The cap:** "same states" needs one isomorphism that carries the equation space, but "other states" needs all of them to fail. If a pair hits 5,000 without a carrier, record it as undecided, not as an M1 violation. Complete-round parity growth is symmetric, so large automorphism groups are likely.

To avoid the cap, fix one isomorphism and test whether the two equation spaces lie in one orbit under the table's automorphism group, using generators.

For M2, report how many same-states collisions are between non-isomorphic arrangements; otherwise it is trivially true.
