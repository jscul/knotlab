# The advisor's answer on the route

A colleague's view, checked before use. Model: the Fable model this host serves. [Question](advisor_question.md)

1. **Yes, and the simplest correct form is a typed transfer matrix along the quotient's single ring.** Dimension two means the map's incidence graph (members and groups as nodes) has cycle rank exactly one: one ring of length L with finite trees hanging off it. Check this first; rank 0 gives a finite cover and rank ≥ 2 gives exponential growth with no slope. A cluster in the cover is then a ring interval plus a rooted sub-piece of each hanging tree it meets, or a bounded piece inside one hanging tree. For each hanging tree compute a finite type polynomial D(y,u,v) by brute force over its connected sub-pieces containing the attachment member (y marks members, u marks non-cut members, v marks boundary members). Build the L-step transfer matrix of these polynomials and expand the trace series. The count of type (p,f) at round t is a coefficient of a rational function. Its double pole at y = 1 gives the slope and the simple poles give the constants. Predict the constants as L-periodic sequences, not scalars; the census may be hiding a period.

2. **Where it breaks.** Groups of three must be nodes of the incidence tree with weight y⁰, and connectivity, p and f must be redefined on that incidence tree (a cluster holding two members of a group holds the group). Blocks lift to finite cycles only if the unrolling is not the universal cover of the incidence graph. Treat a block as one decoration with its type polynomial computed by enumeration of its subsets. The 2^k slope arises when k optional attachments per period leave (p,f) unchanged, which is a statement about your type definition. Write down which attachments are type-neutral before trusting the matrix.

3. **Static predictors to freeze first, all read off the quotient map.** Slope of (2,2) joins equals the product over one period of (1 + number of type-neutral optional attachments). Offshoot constant equals the number of degree-one members of the hanging trees per period, counted with a relay as two. Each group of three per period contributes one group-of-three and one (2,3) join. Settling round equals L plus the largest hanging-tree depth. Compute these with a script that takes only the quotient map and never calls the growth.

4. **Held-out test.** Fit nothing beyond the ≤5-knot census. Before growing, predict the full late view and the settling round for every dimension-two start of six and seven knots, chosen by a stated seed so that L, k and pendant count vary independently. Add two dimension-three starts as negative controls, where the predictor must output "no slope." Score an exact match on every type past the predicted settling round, not a correlation.

5. **Stop rule.** One held-out start where the exact rational function disagrees past its predicted settling round is a Block on that formulation; log it and stop that line. A static predictor refuted on one start with no one-parameter repair is dropped, and the exact matrix remains the authority. A positive result needs every held-out start to match, including the periodic constants and both negative controls.
