# Does the heavy part of the sixteen jumping growths continue?

The unrestricted heavy-width question is open on 03 and 14. This proof establishes the dichotomy they face, their all-round heavy lower bounds, the conditions used to settle other starts, and the scope of the finite witnesses.

## What a reached growing end carries

At a funded stage let $V$ be the live junctions. A block lists live junctions held together by one older junction. Use the maximal blocks. Two distinct junctions are candidates if a block holds both. A maximal set of at least three mutual candidates gives a child when no block holds the set. Each candidate pair outside all such sets gives its own two-parent child. Every spare distinction gives a one-parent child. At the next growing end the children of each old parent constitute one block. A reached live junction with $p$ parents and group demand $d$ has $\max(d,3-p,1)$ loose distinctions, subject to the law's single implied closing birth. The complete stage and the open counts determine the exception. All growth here uses this already established funded law. [Block reduction](../../REDUCE-BLOCKS-20261119-001A/REDUCE-BLOCKS-20261119-001A-P001_reduce/proof.md)

Mark each recurring one-parent tip and its forced companion in the current round. The heavy part is the restriction to the complement of their union. Its width is the number of those remaining births. This records actual births even when one of their parents belonged to the deleted set. Deleting all descendants of a tip gives a different construction. On the explicit constant-width examples, that operation eventually deletes the entire generation. [Boundary theorem and realization](../../../research/HEAVY-GROWTH-20261119-001/interface.md)

For a single settled tip $e$, let $b$ be its companion and $A$ the other candidates of $b$. They are all children of its older companion, so $A\cup\{b\}$ is held. All eligible groups touching either deleted point are pairs. The groups internal to the remainder are therefore exactly its ordinary groups on the restricted blocks. At every $a\in A$ reserve one distinction for the pair with $b$. Its child has one visible parent in the remainder and two actual parents. The new $A$ consists precisely of these children, sharing $b$, so retain their additional common block. This is an exact evolution on the remainder **with** that marked set. The bare remainder can even close on its own while the actual remainder stays at width three. On 03 and 14, $A$ has four elements at every age from two. Their only surplus distinction makes the recurring tip. Multiple simultaneous tips use the same literal union until their separate marked sets are disjoint. ## Three fates for the number of births

**Finite visits to small widths.** After the first funded round, only finitely many reduced states up to renaming can occur with $n$ live junctions. The blocks form a family of subsets of an $n$-set. A junction belongs to at most $2^{n-1}$ different groups, and its open count in a reached live state is at most $\max(2,2^{n-1})$. Keeping the bounded count suffices, even when the previous age and the junction's actual number of parents were arbitrarily large. A reduced state determines the next one up to renaming, including the closing case and multiplicities of one-parent children. Therefore, if a nonending growth visits width at most $N$ infinitely often for some $N$, it eventually repeats a whole reduced state and is periodic from that point. Conversely an exact repeat at two positive open ages gives a bounded infinite future. If the growth is infinite and never repeats a reduced state, then for every $N$ its width eventually exceeds $N$. Removing at most two births per continually marked tip gives the same bounded-versus-divergent alternative when their number is fixed. In the two single-tip starts the heavy width is exactly two below the total from age one. This is a characterization. It supplies a finite witness for bounded growth but no uniform time to find it.

**A closing test.** In the connected regime with no one-parent birth, the whole growth ends exactly when some reached growing end consists of mutually compatible junctions, including the small terminal cases. A common parent for all children is sufficient. If a full block has width $m$, the remainder follows the subset layers of an $m$-set, ending after its final singleton birth. At a preceding pure stage, its children all have a common parent $v$ exactly if $v$ is universal in the old candidate graph and belongs to every residual pair. Each old site participates in a child. Every maximal candidate set includes a universal $v$, and the residual pairs account for the other children. [Complete-end proof](../../GROWTH-CONTINUES-20261119-001/GROWTH-CONTINUES-20261119-001-P001_continuation/proof.md) · [Applied closure certificates](../../../research/HEAVY-GROWTH-20261119-001/quotients.md)

**A divergence test.** Use an oriented old pair $(a,b)$ that has its own two-parent child. It can be followed by a second such pair $(b,c)$ exactly when $a,c$ are incompatible, in the sense that the resulting two children again have a two-parent child. Any third child meeting both without $b$ would have to contain $a,c$. If that pair is compatible, coverage gives a child containing them and missing $b$. The three then have an unheld common extension. The turn thus propagates in either direction of the implication. Successive windows of an allowed walk give actual births at arbitrary later ages and recover the walk up to reversal. Two independently repeatable returns of equal length give exponential divergence when the concatenations are distinct up to reversal. The two length-four returns through five certified junctions at age six of start-13 give at least $2^k$ births at age $7+4k+r$ for all $k\ge0$ and $0\le r<4$. [Walk proof and actual returns](../../../research/HEAVY-GROWTH-20261119-001/pure.md)

There is a second useful divergence test for a tip. Write $d$ for shortest **candidate adjacency count** from the tip in its connected finite graph. For every new child $Y$ other than the next tip, $d'(e',Y)=1+\min_{y\in P(Y)} d(e,y)$. A shortest old path lifts through children covering its consecutive pairs. Conversely consecutive children on a new path share an old parent and the parents of any one child form a clique. This projects a path of length $l$ to one of length at most $l-1$. Hence if an initial set omitting the tip has a child at every later age whose whole ancestry stays within that set, its descendants gain at least one step per round measured from each new tip. The paths to these descendants force total and heavy width to tend to infinity. One recurring sequence of residual pairs around a closed walk away from the tip suffices. One protected cycle without a tip certifies continuation, but its count can stay constant. The partnered arrangement of two pairs is such a bounded example. The exact partnered recurrence at order three and above gives another sufficient test for divergence. ## What the two actual tips determine

For radius $d$, a child at candidate adjacency count at most $d$ has a parent at count at most $d-1$, and all its parents lie within $d$. The entire neighborhood of such an inner parent, every group through it and its spare multiplicity are determined by the old rooted restriction together with its one-parent marks. Those data also generate every retained common-parent block in the new restriction. The precisely matched and verified rooted restrictions of radius five at ages seven and eight therefore recur at all later ages. Their 75 and 102 junctions give 73 and 100 heavy births at every such age on 03 and 14. The old and new sixth layers have 271 and 4,334 members on 03, and 559 and 14,749 on 14. Thus this same certificate does not extend across the sixth layer for those two ages. A fixed finite restriction can coexist with a growing exterior. Indeed the bounded and divergent examples have the same four marked boundary children, and even a path expanding from two tips has every individual finite-radius restriction eventually stationary.

On 03 the previously certified round-ten junction with at least 2,174,695,764,692,054 parents is heavy since the two marked births have arity one and two. Its arity supplies at least that many distinct parent junctions in round nine, at most two of them marked. It proves a large finite count and a single large subsequent birth. Beyond the stationary central restriction we lack both a whole repeated state and a permanent disjoint ancestral family for either start. Neither mathematically possible behavior has been eliminated. ## How long a held part can reproduce on its own

Let \(B\) contain at least three junctions in one held block at a positive age, properly contained in its connected live component. A birth descends wholly from \(B\) when all its ancestors in this chosen generation belong to \(B\). Let \(F\) retain the pairs in \(B\) given their own two-parent child by the full law. There is exactly one wholly descended birth \(j-1\) rounds later for each \(j\)-element subset whose pairs are all in \(F\). The parents are all the immediate smaller subsets. In particular the last nonempty wholly descended generation occurs after \(\omega(F)-1\) rounds, where \(\omega(F)\) is the largest size of such a subset. If \(F\) has no pairs there is no wholly descended child after the chosen generation.

The induction proves that a pair of facets of a born complete subset occurs in no other birth, even one with outside parents. Conversely, if two candidate facets have union missing an original pair, a mixed child produces an unheld group covering them together. Thus they cannot make an extra two-parent child. Positivity of demand and the coexistence of an outside descendant in the component exclude an isolated unary relay. If a held set comprises a whole separate component with another live component elsewhere, that relay can occur after the top birth, which is why the component hypothesis matters. [Full two-direction induction, realizations and exceptions](../../../research/HEAVY-GROWTH-20261119-001/held_ancestry.md)

This applies to the 19,819-junction family on 14 at age nine. One verified exterior group covers every pair. The other six outermost junctions make precisely one child together at age ten, and none of that generation's exterior can descend wholly from the old outermost layer one round later. On 03 the round-ten giant survives both isolated erasures through the two tested transitions. These are exact finite statements for the two actual starts.
