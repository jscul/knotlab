# Five is the sharp bond-distance cutoff for the next triple

**Theorem.** Start with one three-place even-parity junction. The first two additions each take one parent and introduce three fresh distinctions. Exactly one more addition introduces three fresh distinctions and it takes three parents. Every other addition takes two or three parents and introduces two fresh distinctions. At every finite stage after that special addition, the present bond distances through five determine every permitted unordered triple. The comparison is against *every* reachable presentation under the mixed two- or three-output law. A bijection can rename every distinction and every entry above five has a single common value. The reader receives just this table and the proposed triple. The initial prefixes before the special addition already have the stronger cutoff four. Five is necessary. At width 26, a symmetry of the entire table through four carries a legal triple to a forbidden one. Whenever any triple is legal, the table through five also fixes all the actual junction supports. At width 28, with an empty triple menu, even the complete table can have a symmetry which changes the parity equation of the six-place junction. ## What a legal triple and a near pair mean

A permitted triple uses three loose distinctions, each on a different ordinary junction. The three holders have an ordinary common neighbor for each pair and none common to all three. The exterior in the cut representation is bookkeeping for loose distinctions and is never such a neighbor. A bond is a nonempty inclusion-minimal support of a parity equation. For distinct \(x,y\), put \(K(x,y)=D(x,y)+1\), the least size of a bond containing both. Giving distances through five is equivalent to giving \(K\) through six. Wire each loose distinction to the common exterior to calculate cuts. An ordinary subset gives the sum of its junction equations, and these sums give every equation. The ordinary junction equations are independent. [Representation and triple law](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/separator.md)

Write \(H\) for the six-place junction and \(n\) for the number of ordinary junctions. A **shrink** is a three-parent addition making two fresh distinctions, so it uses one more loose distinction than it produces. After the first two unary additions, seven distinctions are loose. A binary two-output addition and the birth of \(H\) preserve this count. Starting from the two possible three-junction prefixes, close the legal transitions on the live holders, their loose counts, their adjacencies and all their shared witnesses. An exhausted holder will never acquire a new neighbor, so retaining the subsets of live holders it already meets preserves exactly which moves are possible. The resulting 5,722 records and 25,345 transitions prove that at most two shrinks can occur. No triple is possible after two, at any later depth. [Exact transition proof](closure.md)

## Why an unrestricted rival has the right size

Let \(L\) be the span of the \(n-1\) source junction equations other than \(H\). Every one of these equations occurs in any matching rival. The short-equation theorem gives this through four places. For a five-place shrinking junction, an appropriate pair of its three inputs forces its whole five-place support. The chronological boundary estimate and the absence of a neighbor common to all three parents exclude each competing support at that pair. For all nine pairs consisting of an input and an output of \(H\), \(K=6\). No word of \(L\) of size at most six covers such a pair. Thus the rival needs at least one more independent equation. [Short supports and all nine pairs](low.md)

The sole three-place junction is read at \(K=3\). Its uniqueness forces the rival's first two additions to take one parent and produce three distinctions. If the rival has \(r\) junctions and \(a\) three-output additions, the width is \(2r+1+a\). The source width is \(2n+4\), so \(a\) is odd and at least three, and \(r\le n\). By the preceding lower bound \(r=n\) and \(a=3\). The rank is now fixed even if the last equation differs. The count \(t\) of source shrinks is also readable. If \(n_4\) is the number of four-place stars, then \(t=n-2-n_4\). The four-place stars are the cliques of size four in the pair relation \(K\le4\). Every relevant short bond is a single physical star, and mutually meeting distinctions have a common holder by ordinary simplicity and bipartiteness. [Explicit reader](reader.md)

## Proof in the three possible cases

1. **Zero shrinks.** No ordinary subset of three or more junctions has boundary through six. The reverse five-place theorem excludes any five-place rival junction, so the rival cannot shrink either. As both have the unique ternary seed, the previous nonshrinking reconstruction identifies every junction from distances through five. This includes all later binary continuations. 2. **One shrink.** The short-support and rank arguments identify \(n-1\) *physical* junctions in both presentations. The one remaining junction must have six places. Both presentations belong to this same one-shrink family. Delete its still-unknown support, keeping all distinctions. A distinction with no low holder must belong to the six-place junction. Among those with exactly one low holder, select enough to complete it. The selected low holders must be distinct and of the same color in the connected ordinary graph of low holders. Both the proposed six-place support and the six loose distinctions in its proposed exterior must have every pair at \(K\le6\). Every true completion passes these tests. At **every** reachable one-shrink stage with a legal triple, these tests admit exactly one support. The arbitrary-depth proof is given below. A matching positive rival is subject to exactly the same tests under the given bijection. Thus an empty source menu also cannot acquire a legal mark through any matching rival. 3. **Two shrinks.** The frontier theorem gives the empty source menu. Any matching rival with a six-place junction has the same three-output allocation and at least two shrinking sites by the four-place count, hence exactly two and the same empty menu. For a rival without a six-place junction, four or fewer loose distinctions exclude every legal triple by the pair bound. For five or more, every source four-place junction must be physically present. Otherwise an ordinary four-bit boundary could be built first as a prefix using all three three-output additions, leaving at most four loose permanently. The rival's remaining three stars all have five places. Each belongs to the source equation space. For a star outside \(L\), its five distinctions must co-occur pair by pair on minimum boundaries separating the seed and both shrinking junctions from the exterior. The special input and output cases are accounted for in the proof. Minimum shores are closed under union and intersection. The cut orientation of a crossing distinction is consistent across them, so the pair witnesses combine into one minimum shore whose boundary is the whole five-place star. The equal ranks now give the same state space, which suffices for the same triple permissions under this growth law. This covers rivals whose third three-output junction takes one or two parents as well. [Two-shrink proof](low.md#every-two-shrink-source-has-its-empty-menu-forced)

### Finite proof at every depth for the one-shrink case

Follow every move on the base records. Work backward from the 104 positive one-shrink records to retain the 400 records that can reach any of them. Augment these records with the ordinary colors, six slots for the places of \(H\), and the minimum cut sizes for each possible membership of the live holders and each pattern of crossings at those six slots. At birth the flags are set directly. At a later addition with \(p\) inputs and \(h\) outputs, excluding the new junction preserves the cut size. Including it changes the size by \(p+h-2j\), where \(j\) selected old holders supplied an input. Both \(j\) and every updated flag are determined by the retained record. Thus taking minima over equal records is exact at every future step. Before the single shrink, a cut can fall by at most one over the entire remaining history. After it, a cut can only increase or keep its size. Retaining minima through seven and six respectively accounts for every possible final pair through \(K=6\). With one ternary equation, every nonempty cut through six is a bond. The six flags and the loose-holder memberships suffice to test every pair needed by either proposed support. Returning to a record after an arbitrarily long sequence preserves every continuation by the update rule. This is the finite step of the proof for arbitrary sizes. ## Sharpness and the information that can be lost

At width 26, 19 and 20 were loose at the same holder before the six-place addition used 19. The triple \(\{12,20,21\}\) is legal. Its image \(\{12,19,21\}\) uses a distinction already shared by two junctions. Swapping 19 with 20 preserves all 325 pair entries through \(D=4\). The distances to each of \(10,16,23,24,25\) are exactly five from 19 and six from 20. These ten entries expose the swap at \(D=5\). ## Earlier results and open direction

The previous all-size inverse through four allows exactly two three-output additions. The present increment is one three-parent, three-output addition anywhere that the mixed law admits it, together with arbitrarily many later two-output moves. The cutoff rises from four to five. The unrestricted mixed-count question is open.
