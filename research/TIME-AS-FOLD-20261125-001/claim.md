Epic `TIME-AS-FOLD-20261125-001`, ticket `TIME-AS-FOLD-20261125-001A` (sub-goal B329; assumptions A223, A228, A229;
goals G5 with G15 and G18). Abbreviations, used in this file only: **SD** is
[the shared definitions](../RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001/definitions.md) (cited by line), **FD** is
[the definitions of the study of parts that meet again](../PARTS-MEET-AGAIN-20261124-001/definitions.md) (cited by
section), **TD** is [this study's definitions](definitions.md) (cited by section). The registered runs and predictions
are in [the pre-registration](../TIME-AS-FOLD-20261125-001A/preregistration.md). Words are the glossary's, SD's, FD's and
those decided with the advisor for the guide's chapter on time (clock and tick, made and supplied; return and period,
always with a unit; parent step and holder step; history; priority against choice of take; "a pattern that reads the

## The question, and whose words are whose

The owner asked, on 24 November 2026 (Pacific time; the record's tickets carry 25 November, its date in UTC): "Is time
itself a fold like intelligence/life through DNA?" ([The owner's request, as given](owner_request.md).) That question is
the only text here that is the owner's.

The rest is the thread's interpretation. The owner's "fold of emergence" (A223) is taken to mean a pattern that the rules
make, where what it makes depends on another pattern, and that makes a pattern of its own. Three things are kept apart:
an order (a knot comes after its parents), which is not a pattern; a clock, here a made clock, a pattern in which one
named event recurs, which takes nothing in; and a made count of returns, one pattern counting another's returns, which
would have the form of the owner's fold. **The stop below is the thread's wording**, revised before the freeze from the
stop filed on the ticket (`tickets/TIME-AS-FOLD-20261125-001.json`, its objective: "Stop at one made part whose births
follow another's returns at a fixed ratio over at least four returns and cease when the other is stopped, with the
reader and the law's choice said plainly to be supplied or made; or at a reason no law of that kind holds both a pattern
that returns and one that meets it at each return"). Nothing in it is the owner's. It is taken with the decision rule of
TD 4.6: a law in which some tests are undecided gives "none among n decided, m undecided", never "none".

## 1. Three things kept apart

- **The law** is the rule the growth follows: which knots join, what a new knot receives, what a short knot does, what a
  birth takes and the choice of take. It is fixed in section 2 before any pattern is looked at, and never changed for a
  pattern.
- **The made clock, Y**, is a pattern the growth builds under that law in which one named event recurs. The event is named
  here, before anything runs: **a return of a part of the live roster** (SD lines 85–88, as defined in TD 2). Each return
  is a **tick** of Y; a tick is the event, not a count of it. "Made" speaks of the pattern only: the law, the start and the
  study's check that a return has happened are supplied and are said to be.
- **The second part, X**, is another part the growth makes under the same law. It is asked to read the clock at each
  return; it is called a **counting pattern** only if the claim of section 5 holds. It is never called a clock or a
  reader. What Y gives off at its ticks is Y's (section 4).

## 2. The law, fixed first

- **Primary laws: the cheapest laws with a confirmed case under each ration in the study of parts that meet again** (its
  P11 under the three-component reading of FD 3.3's cost; by that vector MET costs no more than STEPS-1 in any
  component):
  - **L1 = STEPS-1 under COPY-LAW(t)**: an offshoot also takes one eligible distinction held by a recorded ancestor one
    parent step above its parent and not held by its parent (FD 3.3), under "just enough for three places" (FLOOR1) with a
    short knot retiring, making offshoots or waiting (t), and release (FD 3.1);
  - **L2 = MET under CAP-3(t)**: an offshoot also takes one eligible distinction held by a knot its parent meets and not
    by its parent, and a join one held by a common knot of its parents and by no parent (FD 3.3), under the cap of three
    with each t; CAP-3 with waiting is RING-LAW, the ring's own law (SD lines 69–70).
  - Each with all four choices of take, OLDEST, NEWEST, IN-TURN and BY-COUNT (FD 2.5), each a stated rule for a whole run;
    the claim is stated once for each of the 24 combinations of law, treatment and choice.
- **Said plainly: these laws were cheapest for meeting, not for returning.** The verdict on them is known before the
  freeze (the prototype's census: TD's series under them never give parts off twice; the pre-registration discloses it).
  The informative arm is the second.
- **The other laws that the fuller (four-component) reading of P11 also makes cheapest** (STEPS-2 and UP-TO-2 with
  BY-COUNT under FLOOR1; STEPS-2 with IN-TURN under CAP-3) are run in the same census and each gets a verdict; none is
  primary.
- **Knot laws**: not-all-equal and parity (with IMPLIED, FD 2.8), every run under both.
- **The control**: no taking rule, under the same base laws; it must reproduce the record's ring and the parts that copy
  themselves.
- **The second arm, registered beforehand as a family** (TD 1.4, the pre-registration's `rules.json`): MET with each of its
  two clauses on or off; STEPS-k applied to the offshoots of joins, of offshoots, or of both, for k = 1, 2, 3 (offshoots of
  start knots never take under STEPS-k, since a start knot has no ancestor); and STEPS-k for k = 1 to 8 under RING-LAW on
  the ring starts. Each with its cost, including the component "kinds of birth treated differently". Two members were
  tried in the prototype after its census and are marked so: **MET's join clause alone** (a candidate, a sub-rule of a
  frozen law, chosen after the census) and **STEPS-k for offshoots of offshoots** (a fitted control, added by hand after
  the census: it spares exactly the ring's own offshoots).
- Nothing in this claim adds a rule for counting.

## 3. The patterns

- **Y, the made clock**: a series of returns (TD 2) whose parts have **two or more members**, with **at least four ticks
  of equal period** (in rounds) after the round at which X is chosen. A series of one-member parts is the glossary's
  endless chain, one knot after another, which the thread's reading counts as an order and not a clock; it gives no case.
  Series are taken with and without "past its start" (TD 2.2); the claim uses series taken without it, and every count is
  reported both ways.
- **X, the second part**: a part of the live roster after a tick round t_j of Y (j ≥ 0), wholly apart there from Y's part
  (FD 1.6), with a member made after round 0. Its origin is recorded (TD 3.3).
- **X's own knots**: the knots made after t_j with a parent among X and its descendants and **no parent among Y's part at
  t_j and its descendants**. Joint knots (a parent among both) are counted and reported apart and never enter the claim's
  count.

## 4. The parts given off are Y's

At each tick Y may give parts off (SD line 87; TD 2.7). Those parts, their knots and their births, and any count of them,
are **the parts given off** and belong to Y. They are a record of Y's ticks only if some made pattern's course depends on
their number. Where two or more parts qualify at one tick (a part that copies itself), the others are copies, counted
apart (TD 2.7). A part given off at an earlier tick may be X only from a round at which it is wholly apart from Y's part,
and only its own knots afterwards are X's.

## 5. The claim (the stop)

**C.** For one law (section 2), one choice of take and one start, all fixed beforehand, the growth makes a clock Y with at
least four ticks of equal period, and a second part X, such that:
1. over the four periods from t_j to t_{j+4}, X and its descendants make the same number c > 0 of own knots (section 3)
   in each period;
2. with Y barred after its (j + r)-th tick (TD 4.1), for each r = 1, 2, 3, they make no further own knot after a stated
   lag of at most two periods, in at least two further periods grown, while run A makes own knots of X in those periods;
   and the own knots X makes in B(r) after t_{j+r} number one and the same e for r = 1, 2, 3;
3. the dependence goes through a meeting made by births (TD 4.4: the masked run reproduces the run with Y barred);
4. every knot made passes the knot test under the run's knot law (TD 7);
5. it holds under every registered numbering of the start (TD 5.4: some (Y′, X′) in the renamed run meets 1 to 4).

When C holds, the number of Y's ticks since t_j is recoverable from what X made: with Y barred after its (j + r)-th tick,
X's own knots since t_j number c·r + e, so the ticks are (their number − e) / c, shown for the three bars tested (TD 5.3).
X is then a **counting pattern** in the decided sense. C is the minimal test of a made count of returns.

**C-iii, beside it: X reads the clock at each of three returns.** For each r = 1, 2, 3, X's course (FD 5.2, its trace from
t_j) with Y barred after its (j + r)-th tick differs from run A within two periods, through a meeting. It is the test of
reading with no count. Whether X's own knots fall by a fixed amount when Y is barred is reported as a description of a
C-iii case, never as the stop.

**Where the treatment makes C impossible.** Under release with a short knot making offshoots (CANCEL-OFFSHOOT), under
not-all-equal and without FILLERS, every member makes at least one birth in every round and every birth receives a usable
fresh distinction, so it is a member: so X and its descendants make knots in every round; they are own knots unless every
such birth has a parent among P_j and its descendants. The verdict "ruled out by the treatment" is given to a test in which
B(r) makes no joint knot after the bar; any other test is run in full. This covers L1 with COPY-LAW(CANCEL-OFFSHOOT) and L2
with CAP-3(CANCEL-OFFSHOOT) under not-all-equal.

## 6. What C would and would not show

- **A positive would show** one made part whose making follows another made pattern's ticks and leaves a count of them,
  under a supplied law, start, choice of take and round.
- **It would not show**: that anything reads that count; that it counts ticks and not rounds (the limit below); any
  generality beyond its start and law; anything about time. Of the owner's fold as the thread takes it, C tests "made" and
  "depends on another pattern" in full, but "makes a pattern of its own" only as c own knots per tick.
- **Clause 1 is weak, and is said to be.** Any part with a steady roster passes it: in the two-knot case X makes 6 own
  knots a period whether or not anything depends on Y. The weight of C is in clauses 2 and 3.
- **The limit.** Every period is measured in rounds, which are supplied. So C cannot tell "X counts Y's ticks" from "X
  counts rounds while Y is there". A conditional test separates them, registered now and run only if some candidate passes
  C under every registered numbering (the pre-registration's run 8): for d rounds after a tick (d = 1, 2) the members of
  Y's part not among X and its descendants are treated as holding no usable loose distinction (no members: in no group
  and no demand roster), then hold them again; reported: whether X's own knots between Y's ticks stay c (the ticks) or
  rise with the d added rounds (the rounds).

## 7. The verdicts expected, and their reasons

- **Under L1 no series gives parts off at more ticks than start knots act during it, on every start.** The count (argued
  in the pre-registration, "Settled by construction", item 2): under FLOOR1 with the fresh count by distinct inputs, a
  join takes at least two usable loose distinctions and receives one, and an offshoot of a made knot always takes and so
  receives one; only an offshoot of a start knot receives two. So the usable loose distinctions held by a part and its
  descendants never rise unless a start knot among them acts, and a tick that gives parts off, or has two or more
  qualifying parts, needs a rise of at least one. A start knot acts in one round only under release. **The laws of the
  count**: FLOOR1, the fresh count by distinct inputs, RELEASE, no FILLERS or NO-OFFSHOOT, FD 2.2's eligibility, and a
  taking rule under which every offshoot of a made knot has a candidate (Q, MET, MET's offshoot clause alone, STEPS-1,
  UP-TO-k). Under Q the candidate is the place g took from q. For STEPS-k the bound is the number of acting knots with no
  ancestor k parent steps above them. **It rests on one scaffold**, the fresh count by distinct inputs: under the fresh
  count by parents an offshoot that takes receives two, and the count fails. Under that swap L1 is "seen, not argued",
  like L2: the census repeated under it still has no clock under L1 that gives parts off at four ticks.
- **Under L2: seen, not argued.** On the ring starts the takes begin in round 1; round 1 is the control's in its parents
  and loose inputs, and the parts after it are unions of the control's, but its fresh counts already differ (demand is
  taken after the takes, and the takers have two inputs). From round 2 the comparison with the control is lost. The
  return fails in two ways seen: the would-be parts stay joined, and the takers' holdings change so that they hold a
  different number or become short.
- **The reason for laws in which every offshoot of a made knot takes.** A tension, stated as such and not as an
  impossibility, in two forms: arithmetic (a take is an input and costs the birth a fresh distinction, while a part that
  gives parts off must gain loose distinctions each period) and joining (a take puts the would-be founder of a part given
  off in a second met set, with the knot it took from). So the reason is argued only for the laws of the count and seen
  for L2; laws in which only joins take, or only some offshoots, keep clocks in the prototype and are the undecided case.
- **D, the delay of STEPS-k.** Under STEPS-k the rule cannot touch the ring's own births before round 2k + 1, when the
  start knots are first k parent steps above the parent of a ring member's offshoot. Until then the ring is the control's
  ring, wherever the births of the parts given off leave it alone, and it never made the next return in the runs. Three
  clauses: in no run does the start's roster return at round 2k + 2 after rounds 2, 4, …, 2k; under OLDEST and IN-TURN,
  for k ≥ 2, the start's roster returns at exactly those k rounds; for k = 1 it never returns, under any choice (the parts
  given off take from about round k + 1, which is then the round of the first return). k is the law's delay, not a count
  held by anything made. Returns within the first 2k rounds are therefore no evidence that a clock survives a law that
  lets parts meet.
- **The expected end reaches neither part of the filed stop.** It finds no made part whose births follow another's
  returns and stop with it (no count), and it gives a reason only for one set of laws (the laws of the count), not for
  every law "of that kind".

## 8. What the prototype already shows

Disclosed in full in the pre-registration ("Known before the freeze"), with the readings of the first draft that changed.
In short: no case of C anywhere (12,901 tests on the 3,083 finished runs of 3,086, every one decided as failed under
TD 4.6). Under the primary laws C-iii, at each of three returns, appears only on the two-knot start under L1 with OLDEST or IN-TURN and
COPY-LAW(CANCEL-OFFSHOOT), where the two three-member parts made at every odd round read each other, under one third of
the numberings of that start, each numbering weighted once (80 of 240: those in which the shared start distinction is the
least-named place of the start knot listed second). Outside them it appears in two more places: under STEPS-2 with
COPY-LAW(CANCEL-OFFSHOOT) on seven starts (with BY-COUNT, one of the fuller reading's cheapest laws, on five of them),
three-member parts of period 3 rounds that read each other, each returning only while the other is there; and under
STEPS-2 for offshoots of joins with COPY-LAW(CANCEL-WAIT) on the two-knot start, where X reads Y and Y's series is
unchanged with X barred, X reads what Y gives off as well, and X's own knots grow each period (7, 15, 32, 74), so there is
no fixed ratio. The first does not survive (each case fails under at least one registered numbering; it passes between
44 of the 112 run and 69 of the 80 run); the second is found under 112 of 240 and is undecided under the other 128, so it
is not shown to survive. The near case of the first draft is stated now as: "A part of four has a course that depends,
period after period, on one member's descendants being there. That member's period of two rounds is owed to the part of
four: with the part of four barred its period is one round. The meeting goes through a start distinction that the choice
of take picks by its name, and the case appears under 112 of the 240 numberings of the start and not under the other 128.
X goes on making knots and holds no count." Its Y is a one-member series, the endless chain, so under TD 2.5 it gives no
case; it is kept as the first draft's reading, disclosed.

## 9. Law, clock and count, in every sentence

- The law (the taking rule, its choice of take, the base law, the knot law) is fixed in section 2 and is the same for Y
  and X.
- Y and X are patterns the growth makes under that law; nothing is placed after the start.
- "Made by the growth" is three questions with exclusive answers (TD 6): who made Y and X; what made the meeting; what
  fixed the takes. It is earned only when all three answers are the growth's.
- No claim is made about physical time, physics, living things or minds.
