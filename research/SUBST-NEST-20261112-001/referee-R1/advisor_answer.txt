Two wording points, neither a gap in the claim:

1. **The converse is overstated as phrased.** "Every (v, p) can be flipped freely" is false for arbitrary states. What holds: put u on every copy, and then every choice of the values (v, p) gives a state, for any P. That family is enough for "tied to nothing", because (v, p) varies while every (w, q) with q ≠ p is fixed and the (w, p) are independent.
2. **σ(P) is always satisfiable, whatever P is.** Every diagonal state (all copies equal to one B-state s) satisfies all between-copy knots. So under this T, σ(P) never sees whether P is contradictory, and the claim is never vacuous. If anything downstream assumes σ carries P's contradiction, that fails.

## (ii) Is it known?

I know of no reference for this exact statement. The ingredients are standard:

- **Majority of three solutions:** the proof shows that majority, applied to a template triple off p, lands in the 2-colourings of B with p removed from its knots. This is a polymorphism-style argument.
- **Hall / Frobenius–König on a 3×3 0/1 array:** this is the "no constant row and no constant transversal" step.
- **Spare:** "p spare" says the hypergraph obtained by deleting vertex p from each edge (keeping the shrunken 2-sets) is 2-colourable.
- **σ(P) as a CSP:** σ(P) is the CSP on P with domain Sol(B) and one symmetric ternary relation R containing the diagonal. "Rigid" means R is equality.

I would expect it to count as folklore-level in the CSP gadget literature. Treat novelty as unverified.

All three hold for connected P with a knot.

- **Rigid iff no distinction is spare:** correct. If nothing is spare, the theorem gives s_x = s_y = s_z. If p is spare, (u, p=0), (u, p=1), (u, p=0) is a non-constant triple, and both are B-states.
- **Every pin is an equality:** correct, reading "pin" as a forced tie between (v, p) and (w, p). The dichotomy is equal (not spare) or independent (spare), so opposition never occurs. If "pin" means something else in your text, recheck against that definition.
- **The tie formula:** correct. I checked the four cases:
