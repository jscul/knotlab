# Jump-selected starts and serial orders

The fourteen qualifying archived starts each completed four funded steps with one witnessed live part throughout, under both parity and not-all-equal. Opposite writing orders of an already planned cohort produced the same physical incidence. In contrast, both serial policies that recompute from the current holdings gave states with different distinction counts in the two tested orders on every qualifying start. The difference appeared by action four. ## Complete cohorts

For each law the fourteen qualifying sources give fourteen transitions at each of four successive sizes. All 56 plans were funded and stayed within 512 accumulated knots and 4,096 distinctions. The largest checked stage had 247 knots and 3,886 distinctions after its fourth transition. All 56 were open, so there was no complete-round closure in this panel. The checker rebuilds the live holdings and witnessed relation from the actual current incidence. It matches each cohort, its chosen physical slots and lookahead counts against the archived complete-round implementation. It then writes exactly those parents, spent slots and fresh slots in each of two opposite orders. The literal support collections agree with every existing distinction held fixed. The two writings can assign different position numbers to the new knots.

The full shared-witness reading and the joint reading that also retains direct meetings between live knots each report a single part, with no split or merger on any transition. This agrees with the complete-layer argument. Every live parent is used, every witnessed adjacency has a common child, and each new knot has all its parents in one old part. In every positive stage here, live knots are newborns of that stage and have no direct meetings with one another. Erasing all spent witnesses therefore leaves only isolated live knots in the weaker reading, between 8 and 136 per stage. The isolated readings lose the evidence for the single witnessed part. ## Recomputing one action at a time

The alternative uses the archived serial menu. At each action it rebuilds the multi-parent proposals over currently loose holders. It suppresses a proposal when an already made child has used all those parents, and admits an offshoot when a holder has excess loose distinctions after the remaining multi-parent demands. The `any` policy admits both kinds. The `joins-first` policy defers offshoots while a multi-parent option is present. After spending a single loose distinction from each chosen parent, the rule forecasts the newborn's demand using that newborn and the current remaining loose holders. Its cost uses this forecast and the law-specific arity and closing clauses. The already made child relation is tracked by the archive rule. There is no fixed future map or fixed cohort budget in this arm. Lowest and highest lexicographic parent tuples give two reproducible schedules using archive names. They do not define a separate intrinsic selector. [Archived serial definition](../ROUND-FREE-20261119-001/round_free.md)

### Witnessed coalescence and actual separation

Every admitted serial birth has all its parents in one *pre-action* witnessed part. An apparent coalescence therefore does not witness a birth with parents from two previously released parts. For example, the `any` low trace on the six-knot draw 91 leaves holder 15 alone in its witnessed part at action 15. It still directly meets holder 20 at distinction 44. When holder 15 makes unary 21 in the next action, it witnesses the new pair 20 and 21. The larger witnessed part acquires 21 even though 21 has only one parent.

The same trace gives an actual separation of live holdings at action 19 under the stronger reading that retains both direct meetings and shared witnesses. The parts are `{19,23,24}` and `{20,21,22}`. Each contributes all the parents of a birth in the next two actions. Under this stronger reading, distinct parts cannot rejoin under the recomputing serial rule. An old surviving pair preserves its ordinary meetings and old witnesses. Any new witness connecting two old survivors is a newborn whose parents were already in a common old part. A newborn directly meets only its parents; a witness between that newborn and another live holder must be one of those parents. The other live holder was already linked to that parent by a direct meeting. Thus every new live part descends from just one old part, although that part can split. The sixteen observed splits test this structural distinction. They do not establish indefinite production of new parts by a serial schedule. Global joins-first priority and the sole parity closing clause also affect replay with other parts removed.
