# Linear flows: class-3 vertex-root pair failures are #P-hard when the dual slot has no layer-1 data

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

Let p ≥ 5 be prime, r | p − 1, and G = N ⋊ C_r, where N = exp 𝔫 for a graded nilpotent Lie algebra 𝔫 over F_p with a
grading-preserving C_r action. Fix a sector order d | r and let 𝔪_d be the ideal generated by the components of nonzero
character; assume it has nilpotency class at most 3. Counting homomorphisms from π₁ of the closed 3-manifolds S³_{d/p}(K), over
knots K, into G is #P-hard whenever 𝔪_d has a *linear-flow edge*. That is a pair of characters whose bracket fails the pair
condition, and whose three-slot gadget satisfies every condition of the clean-edge theorem except the vertex-root condition, with
the third slot carrying no degree-1 data.

## Nearest known result

- **Within this program.** `COPRIME-PAIRS-20261027-001F` (Theorem 6) proves the same hardness when the vertex flow vanishes off
  the diagonal and is bilinear on it (conditions (F0)–(F2)). Theorem 8 replaces those three conditions by one linearity condition.
  It decides 8 census algebras that Theorem 6 leaves open, and together they decide the whole vertex-root family.
- **Classical.** The Fourier duality between conserved flows and vertex potentials (flow–tension duality), and the non-negativity
  of character sums of linear forms over subspaces.
- **Outside the program.** Galindo (unbounded homology) and Samperton–Weiss (bounded H²) treat other regimes.

Classification: candidate local delta. The novelty is the use of linearity to make the Fourier dual non-negative; folklore risk
medium.

## Statement

Notation is that of `COPRIME-PAIRS-20261027-001E` and F:
- slots θ₀ = α (vertex), θ₁ = β, θ₂ = γ = α + β;
- root sets R_i = R(θ_i), and Λ_k(i) the characters of R_i at which V_k ≠ 0;
- the leaves ℓ₀, ℓ₁, ℓ₂ of an edge's insertion, and its private data π = (π₁, π₂) for slots 1 and 2.

**Linear-flow edges.** A hard pair (α, β) of 𝔪_d, of class ≤ 3, gives a *linear-flow edge* if
- **(LF)** Λ₁(2) = ∅: 𝔪_d has no degree-1 component at a character of R₂; and
- **(B₂′)** of Theorem 5 holds: [V_{1,c}, V_{1,c′}] ⊆ γ₃ for c ∈ Λ₁(0), c′ ∈ Λ₁(1) with c + c′ ∉ R₂ ∪ {0}.

Every other condition of Theorem 5 except (V₃) then holds automatically. (B₀), (B₁′) and (V₃′) only involve brackets with
V₁(Λ₁(2)), which is zero, and (K) is vacuous in class ≤ 3. The vertex-root condition (V₃) may fail.

**Theorem 8.** If 𝔪_d has a linear-flow edge at some d | r, counting |Hom(π₁ S³_{d/p}(K), G)| over knot diagrams K is #P-hard
under polynomial-time Turing reductions, for each fixed G.

**What it covers** (check 1). Among the 121 interacting census algebras open after Theorem 5, 16 have an edge failing only (V₃).
- 12 of them have a linear-flow edge.
- 8 of those 12 are not decided by Theorem 6: free class 3 on (1, 1, 4) at d = 5; on (0, 1, 2) and (1, 2, 2) at d = 6; on
  (1, 1, 6) at d = 7; on (1, 1, 8) at d = 9; on (2, 2, 8) at d = 10; on (0, 2, 4) and (2, 4, 4) at d = 12.
- The other 4 vertex-root algebras have slot-2 layer-1 data and are decided by Theorem 6.

So the whole vertex-root family is decided, and 105 census algebras remain open.

## Proof

Everything below the vertex equations is as in E and F:
- the summand lemma, zero-jump blocks, the gadget, Brunnian insertions and steering;
- on the layer-2 variety 𝒱 the private equations are local;
- the vertex equations at layer 2 vanish by (B₀).

By F §1, on 𝒱 the vertex equations at layer 3 are a conserved flow: for each edge e there is f_e ∈ F_p^k, a function of
(σ_u, σ_v, π_e), with Σ_{e: head v} f_e − Σ_{e: tail v} f_e = 0 at every vertex v. Here f_e is the components at R₀ of
[ℓ₁, ℓ₂]^{(3)}, plus the character-0 terms of (V₃′), which vanish. The vertex blocks' own terms vanish by the summand lemma. By
F §2,

  P_τ(K_Γ) = C p^{−k|V|} Σ_{σ, λ} Π_{e=(u,v)} Â(σ_u, λ_u; σ_v, λ_v),
  Â(σ, λ; σ′, λ′) = Σ_{π ∈ S(σ, σ′)} ω^{⟨λ′ − λ, f(σ, σ′, π)⟩},

where S(σ, σ′) is the set of private configurations solving the edge's private equations.

### 1. The dual slot's leaf is linear

By (LF), slot 2 has no layer-1 data, so ℓ₂ = log Y₂ has no layer-1 part.
- Its layer-2 part is b₂, a bijective linear image of slot 2's free layer-2 data. Determined parts would be brackets of slot 2's
  layer-1 data, which is zero.
- Its layer-3 part is top-layer data and enters no equation.

### 2. The flow is linear in π₂

In [ℓ₁, ℓ₂]^{(3)}, every term other than [ℓ₁^{(1)}, b₂] has a layer-1 factor from ℓ₂, or lies in layer ≥ 4. So

  f(σ, σ′, π) = M(σ, σ′, π₁) π₂,

with M(σ, σ′, π₁) a linear map: no term of f is free of π₂.

### 3. The slot-2 solution set is a linear subspace

- **Slot 2's equations** come from log W₂, whose monomials all contain ℓ₀ and ℓ₁. A monomial that also contains ℓ₂ has weight at
  least 1 + 1 + 2 = 4, so it vanishes in class 3. Slot 2's equations involve (σ, π₁) only.
- **Slot 1's equations** come from log W₁, whose monomials all contain ℓ₀ and ℓ₂: deleting bundle 2 trivializes the cabled braid
  (E, Lemma 3). A monomial containing ℓ₂ twice has weight at least 2 + 2 + 1 = 5. The summand terms of slot 1 vanish by the summand
  lemma. So slot 1's equations are homogeneous linear in π₂, with coefficients depending on (σ, π₁).

Hence S(σ, σ′) = {(π₁, π₂) : π₁ ∈ S₁(σ, σ′), π₂ ∈ U(σ, σ′, π₁)} with U a linear subspace.

### 4. The dual weights are non-negative

With μ = λ′ − λ,

  Â(σ, λ; σ′, λ′) = Σ_{π₁ ∈ S₁} Σ_{π₂ ∈ U} ω^{⟨μ, M(π₁) π₂⟩} = Σ_{π₁ ∈ S₁} |U(π₁)| · [M(π₁)ᵀ μ vanishes on U(π₁)].

This is a non-negative integer, equal to F(σ, σ′) at μ = 0. Neither off-diagonal vanishing nor diagonal bilinearity is used.

### 5. Hardness

The argument is F §5 verbatim.
- Subdividing every edge by a vertex ordered last gives P_τ(K_{Γ′}) = C′ Σ_x Π_{uv} M′(x_u, x_v) with M′ = Â Âᵀ: symmetric,
  non-negative, integral, with positive diagonal.
- At x₀ = (0, 0) and x₁ = (Δ, 0) for a pair-failing Δ, the rows of Â restrict to (F_max, F(0, Δ)) and (F(Δ, 0), F_max), which
  are independent by E, Lemma 7. So M′ has a non-bipartite component of rank ≥ 2, and EVAL(M′) is #P-hard (Bulatov–Grohe;
  Cai–Chen–Lu, Theorem 2.1).
- Disjoint unions, isolation by Lemma I and the total count across sectors follow as in E §5. Membership in #P is standard. ∎

## Evidence and checks

`check.py`:
1. **The census:** 16 vertex-root algebras; 12 with linear-flow edges; the 8 new ones listed above.
2. **The linear structure** on free class 3 on (1, 1, 4) at d = 5, p = 11, slots (1, 1, 2), where Theorem 6's off-diagonal
   condition fails. At a diagonal, two random and a pair-failing vertex pair, exhaustively over the 11⁴ private configurations:
   - endpoint flows are exact negatives on solutions;
   - for each slot-1 configuration the slot-2 solutions form a linear subspace;
   - the flow is additive in slot-2 data and vanishes at slot-2 data 0;
   - the Fourier values at μ = 0 and 300 random μ are real and non-negative;
   - off-diagonal flows are nonzero somewhere, so Theorem 6 does not apply.
3. **Control:** an edge whose dual slot has bracketing layer-1 data (free class 3 on (1, 2, 3) at d = 5, slots (1, 1, 2)). The
   linear structure of check 2 fails there.

All three checks pass.
- At the diagonal all 14 641 configurations solve; the flows are nonzero on 12 100 of them; the smallest Fourier value is 1331.
- At the pair-failing pair, 1100 of the 1331 solutions carry a nonzero flow, so off-diagonal flows do not vanish there, and the
  smallest Fourier value is 121 > 0.
- The control is not homogeneous-affine in slot-2 data at 7 of 8 and 8 of 8 sampled slot-1 solutions.

## Scope and gaps

- **Proved.** Theorem 8, for each fixed G.
- **Cited.** E (Theorems 4–5 and lemmas), F (§§1–2, 5), A of `COPRIME-PAIRS-20261027-001` (bundle identity), Lemma I,
  Bulatov–Grohe via Cai–Chen–Lu.
- **Not covered.** Class 4, treated in this epic's other tickets. The 26 open class-3 census algebras fail (B₀) together with
  (V₃), or (B₂′) at a nonzero character.
- **A possible extension.** (LF) is sufficient, not necessary. The proof needs only that the flow and slot 1's equations be linear
  in slot-2 data and slot 2's equations free of it. With slot-2 layer-1 data that is a condition on finitely many brackets.
- **References.** [BG] A. Bulatov, M. Grohe, Theoret. Comput. Sci. 348 (2005) 148–186. [CCL] J.-Y. Cai, X. Chen, P. Lu, SIAM J.
  Comput. 42 (2013) 924–1029, Theorem 2.1.
