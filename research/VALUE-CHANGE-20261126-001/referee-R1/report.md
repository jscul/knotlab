## A. Births and closing signs

**Rebuilt.** The input was the archived census of 7,075 starts of three to five knots. The weighting gives each census entry one vote. The original seed 20261128 selected 300 without replacement. We rebuilt ordinary parity growth, assigned the signs of rule R from the holders, and compared each settled knot-product sign with the prediction from descent. Rule R gives a minus interchange to two distinct values with no common holder. The 5,206 open knot pairs and the 23,547 settled pairs involving a start knot are outside the birth-pair table. All 300 per-start rows match the archive. [Rebuild](birth_rebuild.py) · [Prior referee’s scope for signed R](../../EVENTUAL-R-20261126-001/referee-R1/report.md)

In the fifth class added by the study, adjacent-round nonparents share no distinction. A third knot holding a place of each would have to meet both births. Meetings between births connect consecutive rounds, and no third round is consecutive to both. At the start boundary a founder can meet the first birth but cannot meet a second-round birth. This gives the stated product rule.

**Why every six-round case fails.** Follow parents from any sixth-round birth to obtain one birth in each of rounds 1 through 6. If the first two or the last two are both even in place count, that parent and child have a minus sign. Otherwise there is an odd birth in each of those two pairs. They are at least three rounds apart and have a minus sign. Both witnesses are permanent because the earlier birth has no remaining loose distinction after the next funded round. The rebuild found an explicit witness in **134/134** such starts. The sample had 96 closed, 70 stalled and 134 growing starts, and **290/300** had a settled failure. Failure concerns simultaneous certainty of the knot laws under signed R. The ordinary even-parity equations still allow the all-zero word. [Witnesses](birth_results.json)

Holder symmetries permute and toggle labels. Fixing the founder leaves the permutations. We solved the interchange equations on their classes of pairs and checked the closing sign by direct cancellation. On eight knots, permuting three distinct labels in the pair of distinctions `({},{a})` and `({b},{b,c})` gives six pairs. Giving exactly these six the minus sign preserves all 28 knot-pair commutations and makes the closing odd. [Derivation and checks](symmetry_evidence.md)

For a knot U, let N(U) list the distinctions whose values anticommute with its product. The parity of N(U)’s intersection with any knot V equals the interchange exponent of U and V. Hence N(U) is an allowed change exactly when U commutes with every knot. The ten commuting closers give **56/56** allowed lists, **14** empty. ## C. Separated readers and the signed game

Fix any possible middle value word m. Since no knot holds a distinction of both readers, each law with m fixed restricts at most one reader. The allowed completions are a Cartesian product. Their equal positive amplitudes give a product state in that record. Forgetting m gives a mixture of those product states, weighted by their completion counts. This proves separability for every finite parity pattern satisfying the hypotheses. As a direct check, all **667** nonempty middle-value records in **256** small patterns had product support. Allowing one ternary knot to meet both readers broke factorisation in all four control records. [Exact word check](word_tests.py) · [Counts](word_results.json)

Entries are e, the number of shared pairs. We rebuilt all five families, the 15 stages, both reader choices and the controls. For each row the change-read rank equals the number of independent value laws on both readers together after removing the laws supported on either reader alone. The analogous identity with allowed changes holds for the value-read rank. Reading only one shared distinction between the connecting knots gives **1 on 36/39**. The three zeros belong to the first pair in the family with the extra loose distinction, once at each round. Frozen prediction 3 holds on 15/15 controls, prediction 4 on 6/8 round-zero rows, and prediction 5 on 6/6. All 117 reader rows matched both saved runs on the other fields. Only the two square fields reported in the post-freeze deviation differ between the preserved and revised outputs. [Rank rebuild](joined_rebuild.py) · [Archived field comparison](archive_results.json)

**The game.** The signed calculation recovered two change/value pairs whose local commutation pairing is the identity on each reader. On each of the 18 cases it verified nine joint certainties, all row and column commutations on **both** readers, and the local product signs. Choosing the conventional sign for the last cell gives row products `+,+,+` and column products `+,+,−`. Certainty belongs to the **product of the two readers’ answers** at a cell. Individual answers can be fair. The complete local triples have the same marginal for every other-reader setting. [Signed check](game_check.py) · [Certificates](game_results.json)

For a deterministic pair of local answer lists, matching all nine intersections would give the same total parity by multiplying the three rows and the three columns. The required totals are opposite. Of the **4,096** pairs of legal local strategies, **144** match eight cells and none matches nine. Mixtures obey the same bound. The winning probability bound is **8/9 for a uniform, independently chosen row and column**. [Strategy counts](game_results.json) · [Cleve, Høyer, Toner and Watrous, §3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0404076)

## B. What the two-word list covers

Write S for the allowed even-parity words and S⊥ for all sums of knot laws. Draw w uniformly from S and p independently and uniformly from S⊥. Answer a pure value question z with the sign of w·z, and a pure change question x with the sign of p·x. For any jointly readable collection of these pure questions, every product moment is one precisely when the aggregate x belongs to S and z belongs to S⊥, and zero otherwise. This also follows directly by summing the equal amplitudes at w and w+x. Those moments determine the entire joint distribution. The five-distinction check matched all **528** commuting aggregate moment pairs using eight allowed words and four sums of laws. [Word check](word_results.json) · [Delfosse et al., §V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.5170)

The list also covers multiplying answers already readable together. The square needs joint access to products such as X₁Z₂ and Z₁X₂. These two products commute, although their individual factors cannot all be read together. The square, the two-reader game, the stabilizer pair count and event-ready preparation have precedents. The contribution here is identifying which reader choices on these built starts realise them. The earlier six-rule study already calculated the game, and the free-clocks study already counted disjoint value/change tables. ## Fixes

1. **Narrowing · State the hypothesis on a knot’s list.** The decision’s full sentence is “Its commutation conditions for rule R hold on all 75,224 settled pairs of births to six rounds (with a fifth class it left out, which follows the far-pair rule), and every one of the 134 structures with six rounds of births fails; on the eight- and sixteen-knot closures holders alone never sign an odd closing, but holders with descent can (so its conditional on direction 1 is not met); every knot's non-commuting values form an allowed change (56 of 56).” The README gives the correct restriction to the ten commuting closers. For a counterexample in the same sample, take the already closed five-knot start at census index 4376. Its second knot holds `{0,4,5}`. Its list is `{6,7,8}`, which meets the fifth knot in all three places and breaks its parity. The exact hypothesis for an individual list is that its knot commute with every knot. [Decision, item 1](../decision.md#L10) · [README, part A.3](../README.md#L27) · [Five-knot witness](birth_note.md)

2. **Narrowing · Correct the six loose-chain rows.** The README table says “e = 1 on 74; 0 on the 4 of the family whose chain holds a loose distinction, after round 0”. It also says “Predictions (frozen): 1 held on 74 of 78 rows (the 4 others are the loose-chain family after round 0, outside what was derived); 2 held on 36 of 39 (the 3 others the same chain); 3 held (15 of 15); 4 held on 6 of 8 (the loose-chain family gives e = 1, not 2); 5 held.” **72/78** had e = 1. The first pair in that family had e = 0 for both reader choices at **all three** rounds. The literal prediction 1 therefore passes 72/78. The score 74/78 is valid for the combined prediction if prediction 5 overrides prediction 1 on two round-zero rows and correctly predicts their zeros. [README table and score](../README.md#L76) · [Frozen predictions](../preregistration.md#L16) · [Scoring exception](../tally.py#L15) · [Archive check](archive_check.py)

3. **Narrowing · Say what separability rules out.** “Separated readers get nothing unless the middle is read for changes.” The valid conclusion is that their joint table admits separate responses to the readers’ settings with shared randomness when the middle is unread or read for values. It can already contain shared value information. In the checked example the knots enforce a₁+a₂=m and b₁+b₂=m. With the middle unread, both readers know the same fair parity. The 72 joined-ring rows with one independent cross-reader value law illustrate the same distinction. In the separability argument, “their shared certainties are value laws only” holds after removing products of local certainties. A Bell-local response may depend on a reader’s entire local setting. [Decision, item 3](../decision.md#L18) · [README argument](../README.md#L70) · [Example counts](word_results.json)

4. **Narrowing · Specify the game’s use of the middle record.** The README says “With them, readers whose regions share no knot win the square's game with certainty on three joined-ring families (one reader holding two rings).” Two signs from the middle record determine whether the readers agree at each intersection. Each sign pair has weighting 1/4, independently of the two settings. For the fixed reads the four classes win **9, 5, 5, 5** of nine. A herald selecting the two plus signs before settings are chosen gives an ordinary perfect game on a quarter of trials. Alternatively, give one reader the two signs in advance and relabel its answers locally, preserving its required parities. Relabelling in analysis reconstructs those conditional tables. Merely grouping already returned answers leaves their raw scores intact. Neither protocol sends a setting or answer between the two players. Full middle records can have different signs within the same experiment, and the classical comparison uses the same heralding rule. The related sentence about the earlier squared sums, “which amount to reading every unread distinction for its change”, specifically requires retaining the all-plus record and normalising within it. [README, event-ready claim](../README.md#L100) · [README, squared sums](../README.md#L44) · [648 full conditional setting distributions](game_results.json) · [Żukowski et al., event-ready selection](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.71.4287)

5. **Not a block · Identify what the original square checker returned.** Its docstring says “Returns the nine cells' A-side reads (as change part, value part), whether each cell's A-read times its B partner is a certainty of the sorted state, whether every row's and column's A-reads commute, and the row and column product signs.” It returns only an unsigned membership count and a first-reader commutation flag. The missing signed checks matter because agreement and disagreement both give a certain product. The independent calculation supplied both readers’ context products and all four sign classes. [Original return statement](../css.py#L164) · [Signed context products and a nonzero middle record](game_evidence.md)

6. **Narrowing · Scope the native claim and distinguish legality from a read.** The decision says “Allowed changes are native as objects; nothing native reads one.” and “Every function of two allowed words in the record is supplied.” The ordinary parity laws determine whether x is an allowed change. The inspected laws, shared distinctions and birth rule provide no outcome of a selectable change measurement. The two earlier readings examined explicitly add a comparison or a weighting. This supports a conclusion about those primitives and readings. A general impossibility for every native construction would require a specified class and a proof. The README also says “A *change read* of a set x asks of the state whether flipping x is allowed: it compares two words, w and w + x.” Legality is a predicate with a definite answer. The supplied sign read has mean equal to the fraction of allowed words whose flip is allowed. In the five-distinction control the proposed flip is illegal on **8/8** words, yet the read gives plus on **16/32** equally weighted draws.
