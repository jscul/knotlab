# The advisor's answer before the freeze

A colleague's view, checked before use. Model: the Fable model this host serves. [Question](advisor_question_freeze.md)

Short answer: P1 as written is mostly a test of two fitted rules, and the rest is a consequence of the refereed theorems. Freeze it, but narrow the claim to those rules, pre-register the accepted set, and sharpen the settling bound into an exact prediction. Details follow by question.

**1. Is P1 a real test?** On the proved class, no. There the predictor and the engine are two computations of the same theorem, and agreement is a check on code, not on the model. The only content under test is the two extensions: distinct maximal blocks and the cell rule. So the headline should be P1 restricted to starts that use at least one extension, with the proved-class agreement reported separately as a regression check. Two things weaken even that. The predictor takes its round-1 groups from the engine, so it is not independent where the cell rule bites. Compute the groups from the start's incidence inside late.py and compare. And the test is conditional on the predictor's own acceptance. Write the accepted IDs with their per-kind polynomials to a file, hash it, and record the hash before any growth is opened. The seven bursting starts labelled unread should not count toward P1 at all, since agreement on an unread view is not defined. What would make it more than a re-implementation is a prediction the engine does not state: the exact settling round and the full coefficient vector per kind, fixed before opening. That is available, see point 4.

**2. Where the cell rule fails first.** The rule has three parts: the membership rule, the size correction of g minus 2 per whole cell, and the parent and fresh-distinction rules. Up to five knots these were exercised almost entirely at g equal to 3, and a four-cell only in the bare start where all knots share one neighbour. Six knots is the first size with a four-cell carrying pendant chains and a spare knot, so I expect the size correction and the parent rule to break there first, if anywhere. Two disjoint three-cells also first fit at six, and test the "all of it" clause for two cells at once. Two cells sharing a knot need a dense start that will usually hold a cycle and be rejected, so I would not expect that case to decide anything. A block meeting a cell in two knots plus a chain is the other place to watch, because the filled-cycle reading was fitted on very few instances. Do stratify. The predictor can classify all 82,656 starts by structure without growing anything, so enumerate the strata first, then draw the 2,000 as a stratified sample with the same seed and ensure at least thirty starts in each of those rare strata. Report results under both the uniform and the stratified weighting.

**3. Rings before or after freezing.** Freeze the tree case now. Rings need a different formula and would mix a tested rule with an untested one. Make sure the acceptance rule rejects every piece with a cycle by structure, so no failure can be removed after the fact. Expect coverage to fall at six knots because cycles become more common, and say in advance that falling coverage is not a failure and high coverage is not support.

**4. Scaffolds and the bound.** Your bound aggregates over kinds. Each kind's count is a sum of binomials, which is a rational generating function with denominator a power of one minus x, and Stanley's Enumerative Combinatorics volume 1, section 4.3, gives the exact point where such a sequence becomes polynomial: just past the numerator degree minus the denominator degree. Per kind that means the count is polynomial from the largest size among its sets, exactly, unless maximal-size terms cancel. Your 48 percent equality and slack up to 4 most likely come from taking the maximum size over all kinds and the maximum degree over all kinds separately. Take the maximum over kinds of each kind's own size plus its own degree, and I expect equality nearly everywhere. Check that on the five-knot data before freezing; if it holds, freeze the exact K as a prediction rather than a bound. On scaffolds: the knot law is already swapped, good. The seed is cheap to swap and should be. The two readings are close cousins; a reading that also records grandparents would depend on chains of length exactly 2 as well, and the formula generalises cleanly, so predicting it is a cheap third reading. The horizon matters mainly for stall and burst labels; grow a hundred starts to round 40. The scaffold most worth challenging is the census itself: every cell in it is small. Hand-build a few starts with a cell of five or six knots and chains and run them outside the census.

**5. Known mathematics.** The object is connected subsets of a finite graph with pendant rays, the literature's lattice animals or connected induced subgraphs, counted by size. Your binomial is the composition count of the chain lengths. For subtrees of trees see Székely and Wang, "On subtrees of trees", Advances in Applied Mathematics 2005; for the eventual-polynomial behaviour cite Stanley as above. What this adds is that the growth reads only the down-closure of the blocks, and that round-1 groups collapse to single points, which neither source states.
