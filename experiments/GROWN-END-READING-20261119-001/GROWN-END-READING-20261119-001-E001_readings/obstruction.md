# What one or two interior marks reveal across two junctions

Let two connected parts of a wired incidence graph, H and Q, share precisely the distinct junctions A and B and have disjoint distinction sets. Both contain an A–B path. For cuts only, the exterior is a vertex. Write K(X) for the minimum cardinality of a bond containing all the named distinctions in X, assigning infinity when there is no such bond. The pair distance is K−1. Write L_H(X) for the minimum cardinality of a cut in H containing X that separates A from B with both shores connected in H. Define L_Q the same way. The empty marked set is allowed in these terminal-separating minima.

**Two-junction theorem.** Suppose a permutation of the distinctions of H extended by the identity on Q preserves every pair and triple minimum. Replace Q by a connected Q′ with the same two attachment junctions. If the minimum A–B cut size in Q′ is at most that in Q, extending the permutation by the identity on Q′ preserves the entire new pair table. This works for ordinary parts of any arity, girth, or meeting multiplicity. The pair-flip bit is unnecessary here.

For any nonempty sets X in H and Y in Q, a bond containing X and Y must separate the two terminals. If both terminals were together, the opposite shore would have vertices in both pieces and no connection between those vertices. When the terminals are apart, connectivity of each global shore implies connectivity of its parts in H and Q. An isolated part missing that shore's terminal could have no route into the other piece. Conversely any two connected separating cuts glue to a bond. Complementing one choice if needed aligns the two terminal orientations. Consequently

$$
 K(X\cup Y)=L_H(X)+L_Q(Y).
$$

Choose a Q distinction q on a minimum connected cut separating A and B. Such a cut exists in a finite connected graph, and the set of crossings is nonempty. Its size is λ = L_Q(∅), and L_Q({q}) = λ. Keeping this one q fixed proves that L_H(X) is unchanged by the permutation for every one- or two-element X. The mixed readings used here have exactly two or three marks and the assertion includes infinite values.

For a pair X in H, let B_H(X) be the minimum cardinality of a bond containing X with A and B together. That minimum depends only on H with A and B identified. In fact a bond of this type cannot cross any Q distinction since the opposite shore already contains an H endpoint of a marked crossing. Q connects A to B on their own side. Thus for the original and new graph respectively,

$$
 K(X)=\min\{B_H(X),\ L_H(X)+λ\},\qquad
 K'(X)=\min\{B_H(X),\ L_H(X)+λ'\}.
$$

When λ′ ≤ λ this gives K′(X)=min{K(X), L_H(X)+λ′}, entirely from two quantities invariant on H. A pair split between H and Q′ uses the mixed-cut formula and the invariant one-mark L_H. Pairs confined to Q′ are fixed pointwise. If λ rises, the first minimum can have hidden B_H above the old separating value. The two displayed formulas describe the exact remaining condition for that case.

In a complete growth round confined to Q, λ′ ≤ λ follows directly. Place every new junction on the old exterior side of a cut in Q, with the new exterior there. An old loose distinction still crosses exactly when its old holder was opposite the exterior. All fresh distinctions stay on the exterior side and all former ordinary meetings keep their crossing status. Thus every old A–B cut extends at the same size. Minimizing gives the claim, even if a particular extended cut has a disconnected shore. The terminal closing round replaces the exterior by its sole new junction and also preserves this bound. When H itself contains the exterior, using this argument requires that the operation really replaces only the Q side and keeps both terminals and every distinction of H fixed.

The conclusion concerns one changed piece. A sequence of changes all confined to that same piece also satisfies the same inequality against the original stage. The theorem gives pair readings at each later stage without assuming its triple table remains fixed.

## Three attachment junctions

For three distinct terminals, write t for the terminal alone on its side of a nonconstant partition. In each piece record two costs for a marked set. The strict cost requires both shores connected. The relaxed cost requires the t shore connected and each component of the other shore to meet one of its two terminals. There are at most two such components. If marks occur in both pieces, every containing bond gives one of the three nonconstant terminal partitions. Its t side must be connected in both pieces, and its two-terminal side must join those terminals in at least one piece. Conversely these conditions suffice. The mixed minimum is therefore the least, over the three choices of t, of the two sums obtained by taking strict cost in one piece and relaxed cost in the other. A single marked exterior distinction now gives a minimum over several terms. The two-terminal proof used only one term. This formula gives a direct test for extensions that try to conceal a difference among the three partitions.

On the recorded seven-vertex wired control, the three-terminal formula agrees with all 210 mixed pair and triple minima. Requiring each piece to connect both of its shores gives a wrong minimum in 92 of these comparisons. [Executable check](obstruction.py)

## Several complete-round branches attached to a simple core

Choose a connected simple graph as an ordinary core and mark three or four distinct ordinary junctions as attachment sites. Give each site its own connected branch to the same outside vertex O. Distinct branches have no other shared junction. The ordinary core and O with one virtual distinction from every marked site to O will be called the compressed graph. Each virtual distinction is weighted by its branch's minimum terminal cut size.

For marks including an ordinary core distinction, a bond passes through a particular branch only if that branch's endpoints are on opposite shores. Both restrictions to the branch must then be connected. Contract each uncrossed branch to its virtual distinction and replace each crossed branch by one virtual crossing. The result is a bond of the compressed graph. Connectivity follows since a path staying on one shore can pass through a branch between its endpoints only if they belong to that shore. Conversely a compressed bond lifts by using a connected separating cut in each crossed branch. If a subset of the marks lies in a branch, require its virtual distinction to cross and add the difference between that branch's marked separating cost and its unmarked separating cost. Costs add because the branches have disjoint distinction sets.

Thus a permutation of ordinary core names fixing all other names preserves the actual pair and triple minima exactly when it preserves the weighted readings through those orders with every attachment distinction fixed. For the converse, choose one distinction from a minimum terminal cut in each branch to represent any marked virtual crossing. A reading on one ordinary or virtual name, if needed after repeated branch marks, is the minimum of its two-name readings since the compressed graph has no bridge. Comparisons involving only fixed names hold identically. All pair flip answers are automatically preserved for the ordinary permutations tested here. Each moved distinction has a unique unordered pair of ordinary holders.

These weights have literal positive-age realizations under the cheapest-count law.

Test every single transposition of ordinary core distinctions, with every branch distinction fixed pointwise. A transposition of distinct nonparallel core names e,f changes a cut word w by either zero or the two-name vector {e,f}. Some cut distinguishes e,f, so this transposition preserves the cut space exactly when the two-name vector belongs to it. For cores through six ordinary vertices also enumerate every ordinary permutation preserving the pair table and fixing the branches. The rank test compares the full cut space for those general permutations.

## Two useful persistence checks for a split ordinary junction

If a permutation of a closed graph G preserving pair and triple readings fixes an ordinary distinction e=st, delete e and use H=G−e. Let G be two-vertex-connected, so H is connected. Replace e with a new branch s–U–O–V–t, where the two meetings at O on each side are given by two separate loose distinctions. The branch has minimum separating size one. For an H-only marked set X of any size, the preceding formulas give precisely its old minimum in G. For a mixed query with at most three marks, the one- or two-mark separating reading L_H(X) equals K_G(X∪{e})−1. Thus the full reached triple table is preserved even when no matching of the equations existed in G. The two loose sites U,V have no common ordinary neighbor. Their first complete round gives four one-parent junctions. The original links at s and t keep the separating size one thereafter, since every open round preserves connectivity to the outside. All future growing sites are outside H. Pure branch marks are fixed and the new branch has no ordinary st distinction parallel to an H name. Any original pair-flip symmetry therefore persists. Failure of equation transport persists as well. Send the single e coordinate of an old cut to a fixed one-distinction separating cut of the branch. Conversely that branch pattern determines whether s and t are on opposite shores, so a purported transported cut would give the corresponding transported cut in G.

Suppose instead two distinct ordinary vertices have r distinction-disjoint ordinary paths between them, preserved in every continuation. Identify the two vertices in a graph used only to compare equation spaces. Every cut separating them in the actual graph has size at least r. Every bond of the identified graph with cost below r is still a bond of the actual graph. It lifts to a cut with the two vertices together, and an uncut path among the r paths connects them on that shore. Conversely an actual bond below r has those two vertices on the same shore and remains a bond under identification. If the permutation preserves the identified graph's cut space and fixes the current growing end pointwise, fixing each later fresh name continues to preserve that cut space. Each added equation is then fixed. An old bond below r persists in the identified graph of every open continuation by placing the new junctions on the exterior shore. Connections there through former loose distinctions can use the new junctions' fresh distinctions. Consequently any current pair with an identified-graph bond of size below r remains matched in every continuation. This gives a test for surviving a new–old comparison when a new name first appears. A pair of new names is itself fixed.

For this obstruction to reveal a difference on a pair of old names, either the identified graph must lack a below-threshold bond on that pair or enough separating paths must pass through the changing exterior for the threshold to fall. The three-terminal formula identifies one additional possibility: a formerly hidden terminal partition can control the new minimum. The checked families give no marked failure preserving every old triple. In particular, changing the equation space alone does not certify such a failure.
