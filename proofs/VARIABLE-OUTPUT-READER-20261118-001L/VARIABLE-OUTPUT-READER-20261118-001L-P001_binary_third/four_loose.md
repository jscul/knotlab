# Four loose distinctions against an unrestricted rival

Suppose the fourth shrinking join has occurred. The [complete frontier](frontier.md) proves that the binary three-output site \(B\) then preceded every shrink, in every lawful ordering that reaches this presentation. Four distinctions are loose. Each later source move is binary with two outputs. The following argument shows that every reachable rival with the same pair table through \(D=4\) also has an empty triple menu. It uses \(K=D+1\) for minimum bond size. A large shore here means the ordinary side of a cut that contains at least three sites. Its size as a boundary counts distinctions.

## Which short cuts are possible

The first three selected ordinary sites have boundary at least seven. Every later site can lower it by at most one. Thus any large shore with boundary at most five has at least five sites. The last three of its sites each make at least two outputs, with at most two meetings among the three. At least four outputs therefore leave the shore. A four-boundary has no entering bit. A five-boundary has at most one. In the latter case, with one entering bit all four outgoing bits arise from the latest three sites.

Every such shore contains the seed and both initial unary additions. Without the seed, the first included site requires the sole entering bit, so it is unary. Only a second unary site attached to it could follow. The two are adjacent and cannot be parents of a third site. With an entering bit and the seed inside, all children of the seed lie inside because the seed is earlier than the last three. In the chain case its first child likewise lies earlier than the last three and includes the second unary site. With no entering bit the shore is parent-closed. Such a lawful prefix with three or more sites must contain both initial unary sites. Parent-closed shores can be built first and the remaining history can be replayed in its original relative order. The first witness of two parents predates the younger parent, and a later first common witness of an already used triple would itself require an illegal triple addition. These facts preserve the full parent test when reordering. [Parent-closed reordering](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001E/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001E-P001_marked_symmetry/frontier_exchange.md#the-shore-can-be-built-first)

The net number of outgoing minus incoming bits of any shore containing these initial sites equals
\[
             7+\mathbf{1}_{B\text{ inside}}-\#\{\text{shrinking sites inside}\}.
\]
Each four-shore contains \(B\). Otherwise putting it first gives four loose bits in the predecessor's minimum-width family, where five is the proved minimum. The displayed balance then puts all four shrinking sites inside. [Earlier minimum](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J-P001_seven_to_six/family.md)

Every large five-shore is also parent-closed. One incoming and four outgoing would give net three. This requires \(B\) outside and all four shrinking sites inside. A directed path of parent meetings from the seed inside to \(B\) outside would then already have exported a bit before \(B\)'s birth. That bit comes from an earlier site than all four selected shrinking sites, whereas every export had to come from the latest three. A parent-closed five-shore omitting \(B\) would have two shrinking sites. Putting it first would produce a complete four-shrink history with two shrinks before \(B\), excluded by the frontier theorem. Hence every large five-shore contains \(B\) and exactly three shrinking sites.

There is exactly one such five-shore \(P\). To prove this, let \(W_0\) be the intersection of all large four-shores. They include the full source. Their intersection is parent-closed and contains the three initial sites, \(B\), and all four shrinking sites, so its boundary is four by the balance. For any large five-shore \(U\), build \(U\) first. It must admit the fourth shrink immediately. Indeed, only binary additions could intervene before that shrink, and the unique five-loose record with a legal triple has no incoming binary transition from any five-loose record. In particular \(U\) itself has a legal triple. Appending the fourth shrink \(C\) gives a four-shore \(U\cup\{C\}\), so \(W_0\subseteq U\cup\{C\}\).

For any four-shore \(W\), its intersection with \(U\) contains precisely the same three shrinking sites and \(B\) as \(U\). Every site in \(U\setminus W\) is binary with two outputs. Reorder the parent-closed \(U\cap W\) first, within \(U\). Any remaining site would give a nonempty binary path ending at the positive five-loose record, which the exact frontier rules out. Thus \(U\subseteq W_0\), and \(W_0=U\cup\{C\}\). In \(W_0\) the four loose bits have multiplicities \(1,1,2\) on the three latest sites \(A,M,C\), with ordinary adjacencies along \(A,M,C\). Only \(C\) has all its outputs loose, since every site makes at least two outputs. Therefore the single site that can be removed from \(W_0\) leaving such a parent-closed five-shore is uniquely \(C\). Existence follows from the positive prefix immediately before the fourth shrink. Write \(P=W_0\setminus\{C\}\) and \(X=\partial P\). [Unique positive record and its predecessors](frontier.md#shrinks-and-positive-menus)

After \(W_0\), the only binary holder pair is \(A,C\). A move uses the unique loose bit at \(A\) and one at \(C\), then the three live holders become \(M,C\) and the new site. In a fixed completed presentation that unique bit of \(A\) has at most one later holder. It therefore forces the next actual site in any continuation of this prefix inside that presentation. Iterating gives a single ordered sequence of binary sites. The large four-shores are exactly its successive prefixes \(W_0,W_1,\ldots,W_k\). Every one is a four-shore by the balance, and every possible large four-shore contains \(W_0\) and can be built first. Thus the entire list is known in the structural proof at arbitrary suffix length \(k\).

## How many independent equations a rival needs

Every physical star in \(P\) is forced as an equation in any rival matching through \(K=5\). All bits with both ordinary holders in \(P\) avoid every large four- or five-boundary. They expose the four-place sites, the three shrinking sites and \(B\) by short pair neighborhoods. The fourth shrinking equation at \(C\) is also forced. The proof gives a forcing pair into \(C\) and checks the two fresh outputs against the common five-word \(X\). [Prefix and fourth-shrink transfer](structural.md#the-positive-prefix-before-the-fourth-shrink)

Let \(n_P\) be the number of sites of \(P\). Of its five loose bits, three enter \(C\). Let \(T\) consist of the other two together with every bit born at \(C\) or later. Any space of equations supported on \(T\) intersects the forced span of \(P\) trivially. The two collections of bit names overlap in just two positions, and the system has no nonzero equation on one or two positions.

If \(k=0\), the known \(n_P\) prefix equations and the forced equation at \(C\) are independent and give the whole source rank. A matching rival has exactly the same equation space by the width bound below. Its menu is therefore empty.

Suppose \(k\ge1\), and call the \(i\)-th binary site after \(C\) the site \(v_i\). The six bits
\[
                  H_i=\partial W_{i-1}\ \cup\ \{\text{the two outputs of }v_i\}
\]
all lie pairwise at \(K=4\). The three four-words \(\partial W_{i-1}\), the physical star of \(v_i\), and \(\partial W_i\) cover all fifteen pairs. These six bits are away from the unique ternary seed, and every nonzero four-word is a bond.

Take the output \(q\) of \(C\) spent at \(v_1\). Its entire closed neighborhood through \(K=4\) is \(H_1\). The only four-star containing it is at \(v_1\), and the only large four-shore it crosses is \(W_0\). All five of its other pair readings in \(H_1\) must be realized by four-words in the rival wholly inside \(H_1\). One word containing \(q\) can cover only three other bits. There are therefore at least two distinct, independent rival words on \(H_1\).

For each \(i\ge2\) take the output of \(v_{i-1}\) used by \(v_i\) and an output newly born at \(v_i\). They have \(K=4\). The former bit can only occur on the two local four-stars or on \(\partial W_{i-1}\) among the short boundaries. Consequently its threshold-four neighborhood is contained in the part of \(T\) born by stage \(i\). A rival four-word through this pair includes a new bit of stage \(i\) and has no later bit. These \(k-1\) words increase the rank at each stage, in addition to the first two. The rival thus has at least \(n_P+(k+1)\) independent equations, exactly the source site count \(n\).

The rival's one ternary star is read directly from \(K=3\). Its first two additions must be unary with three outputs, and at width \(2n+4\) its number of three-output additions must be odd. From the identity \(\mathrm{width}=2r+1+a\) for \(r\) rival sites and \(a\) three-output additions, \(r\le n\). Hence \(r=n\) and \(a=3\). This counts dimensions of the equation space and makes no identification assumption about equations in the later binary suffix.

A rival with a legal triple must have at least five loose bits. The whole exterior is a bond, and every pair of legal triple inputs has \(K\ge5\). Such a rival with exactly three three-output additions cannot have a large four-shore. Building it first gives four loose bits. The third three-output addition must already have been used because a one-ternary prefix before it belongs to the minimum-width family. Remaining sites then take at least two parents and make exactly two outputs, so the loose count cannot return to five. In the absence of a large four-shore, all words through four are ordinary three- or four-place stars, as there is a unique ternary site. A clique with pair readings through four can have at most four members. Give each loose distinction its own leaf for this incidence argument. Mutually meeting distinctions in the simple, triangle-free ordinary graph share a holder. The six members of \(H_1\) contradict that bound. This rules out a legal triple in every reachable rival, including a rival whose third three-output addition takes one or three parents.
