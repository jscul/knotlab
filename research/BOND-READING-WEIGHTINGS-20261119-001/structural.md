# When two distinctions suffice

The results concern a specified renaming of a single finite open reached stage. Every distinction on every live ordinary junction is fixed. Write \(K(x)\) and \(K(x,y)\) for the least sizes of bonds through the indicated distinctions, including infinity. In the graph presentation a bond cuts two connected shores and the exterior is one vertex. A full match of the binary equation space and the growing end carries every later round. If a lawful successor exists, unequal old spans give a different reading on some old set at every later stage. These two implications come from the predecessor. [Limiting-reading theorem](../BOND-READING-SCALE-20261119-001/limiting-reading.md)

## An exact decision on ordinary trees, with arbitrary multiplicities

Merge all shared distinctions with the same two ordinary holders into a bundle of size \(h_e\). Suppose the resulting ordinary graph is a tree \(T\). Each of its leaves has a loose distinction: otherwise deleting that leaf's neighbor would disconnect the leaf from the exterior, contrary to the genuine-star condition. A single-site open tree is immediate. Give each loose distinction its own unit pendant tip in a drawing of \(T\). The tips represent the fixed names, and their common exterior remains a single vertex when computing bonds.

Call a maximal path of bundles whose intermediate ordinary sites have exactly two ordinary neighbors and no loose distinction a corridor. A single bundle also qualifies. At each end of a corridor let the external cost be the least total cost of cutting off all branches there other than the corridor, including the loose distinctions at that end. These costs are given explicitly below. Let \(c\) be the least among its two external costs and its bundle sizes.

**Criterion.** Pair readings and the pointwise growing end carry the full equation space exactly when no corridor contains two different unpinned bundles of the same size \(h=c\ge2\). A bundle is pinned when one of its holders is live. Then all its literal names are fixed. If the criterion fails, transposing one literal name from each of the two bundles preserves every singleton and pair reading and changes an old equation. If a successor is lawful, this changes a higher-order reading on old names. Both the criterion and one offending transposition are computable in time linear in the input incidences. [Tree and general certificates](structural_features.py)

**The branch calculation.** Every nonempty connected ordinary subtree is a bond shore: every complementary component reaches an original leaf or a loose distinction, hence joins the common exterior. Conversely the shore of a bond avoiding the exterior must be a connected ordinary subtree. For an ordinary bundle \(uv\) let

\[
g(u,v)=\min\left(h_{uv},\ \ell(v)+\sum_{w\sim v,\ w\ne u}g(v,w)\right),
\]

where \(\ell(v)\) counts the loose distinctions at \(v\). One can either cut the bundle or include \(v\) and optimize each of its other branches. Leaf positivity gives \(g\ge1\). One traversal in each direction computes every value. An individual loose direction has cost one.

For different positions \(e,f\), meaning two different bundles, a bundle and a loose tip, or two different loose tips, let \(D(e,f)\) sum the branch costs in directions *off* the unique path between their midpoints at all ordinary vertices along that path. A loose position has \(h_e=1\). A connected shore crossing both must contain the ordinary path between them, cut both marked positions, and can optimize the other directions independently. Consequently

\[
K(e,f)=h_e+h_f+D(e,f).
\]

If a shared position \(k\) lies between \(e\) and \(f\), then \(D(e,f)=D(e,k)+D(k,f)\). For any two distinct shared positions \(e,f\), choose loose tips \(a,b\) beyond them in their respective end directions. The differences \(K(e,a)-K(f,a)\) and \(K(e,b)-K(f,b)\) are \(h_e-h_f-D(e,f)\) and \(h_e-h_f+D(e,f)\). Equality of all fixed loose readings therefore requires equal multiplicity and \(D(e,f)=0\). Since every off-path branch costs positively, zero occurs precisely when the sites between the positions have no off-path direction. This is exactly membership in one corridor. Conversely a probe away from the corridor enters from one of its ends, so equal-size positions in it have identical readings to every loose tip.

Consider a multiplicity \(h\) attained at several bundle positions in that corridor. Distinct literal names at different such positions read \(2h\). Two names in the *same* bundle read \(h+c\), also its singleton minimum. Indeed a shore meeting that bundle pays \(h\) plus the cheaper exit on its chosen side. Its possible exits are another bundle in the corridor, or the exterior branches at one of the two ends. There is a second occurrence of \(h\), so the minimum excluding the chosen bundle is still \(c\). For \(c<h\), the pair table identifies each entire size-\(h\) bundle as a class. If \(c=h\), all literal names at these positions have identical outside entries and internal pair reading \(2h\).

Every permutation of whole equal-size bundles in a corridor preserves the cut space. For positions \(e,f\), the sum of the intervening site equations is the indicator of the union of those bundles. Any cut is constant on each bundle, so exchanging the two complete bundles changes a cut by zero or by that sum. Within a bundle its names can always be permuted. These operations account for every allowed pair isometry unless two unpinned size-\(c\) bundles of size at least two are present. In that case their partial exchange splits a bundle: take an endpoint star of the first bundle at the end away from the second. Its transported word takes different values on two names at the first position. No cut can do this. This proves both directions, including the case where live supports pin entire end bundles.

**The simple-tree formula.** With single ordinary meetings, a connected shore \(U\) costs \(2+\sum_{v\in U}(d(v)-2)\), where \(d\) is literal degree including loose distinctions. Every term in the sum is positive. The minimizing shore through distinct marks is their intervening ordinary path. Thus \(K(e,f)-2\) is the path distance on distinction midpoints giving the incidence from \(v\) to a midpoint length \((d(v)-2)/2\). All leaves are fixed loose tips. Distances to them identify every other position. More generally the exact criterion admits every weighted tree without a sealed two-neighbor site, regardless of bond size.

## A class of sampled starts with least order exactly two

Choose any ordinary simple tree \(H\) with at least two leaves and no vertex of degree two. At each original leaf \(h\), attach either two or three new pendant sites. Give each site \(\max(0,3-\deg(v))\) loose distinctions as in the frozen sampling law. Exactly those new pendant sites are live, with two loose distinctions apiece. At age one the ordinary graph has cycles, so this class is independent of membership in the preceding tree theorem. Every such labelled start has positive probability under both tree weightings: it has a Prüfer word, and it can also be built by recursive attachment in a parent-before-child ordering. [Sampling law](preregistration.md)

Within a group of two pendant sites \(a,b\) at \(h\), the first round adds the pair junction \(n\) and two unary junctions \(A,B\), one at each pendant. Their parent sets give the two next groups \(nA,nB\). The fewest rule supplies two fresh distinctions at each of \(n,A,B\), so both groups and the two remaining unary offshoots are lawful. At a group of three pendants it adds all three pair junctions, since the three pendants already share \(h\). These three new sites have pairwise overlapping parent sets and no single common parent. The next group uses all three and each has exactly one fresh distinction. Different pendant groups have no common parent. At least two groups of pendants are present, so the next round is open and every stated nonimplied fresh count applies. Each start is admissible by the tree-shore test.

In a group of two the fixed shared probes \(aA,bB\) read respectively \(3,4\) on \(ha\), and \(4,3\) on \(hb\). The 3 is the star of the corresponding pendant site. Its two disjoint routes onwards to the exterior and the route through \(h\) force at least three for that pair; alternatively the sole possible two-name support would cross the permanent four-cycle \(h,a,n,b,h\) oddly. For \(ha,bB\), the shore \(\{h,b\}\) gives four. In a cut of size at most three the four-cycle needs another crossing besides \(ha\). The three available places then contain no loose name, so \(A,B,n\) are on the exterior side, using their two fresh distinctions apiece. Since \(bB\) crosses, \(bn\) must be the other cycle crossing. The uncut path from \(h\) through the other pendant group to the exterior and the uncut \(hb\) now contradict \(bB\). The fixed probes read equally on every old name outside this group, by the symmetry that exchanges its two pendants and their unary children. Thus each of the two old spokes is identified individually.

With three pendants, there is an ordinary six-cycle alternating the three old pendants and the three new pair junctions. All six names on it are fixed. Each pendant has a spoke to \(h\), and each pair junction has one loose name to the exterior. A spoke reads three exactly to its two incident six-cycle names. Its own star gives that upper bound. If \(h\) and the exterior are on the same side, the other side of a bond marked in this group is a connected six-cycle interval. It costs its length plus two, or six if it is the whole cycle. Size three requires a singleton interval. If \(h\) and the exterior are separated, this group costs at least three by its three distinction-disjoint outlet paths. The route through the rest of the ordinary tree to a different group costs at least one more. No two-name bond can realize the successful pair. The local permutation of the three pendants is transitive on the six cycle names and fixes every outside old name. These incidence profiles again identify every spoke individually.

Each pendant group attaches to the remainder only at \(h\) and the exterior. Its minimum separating cost equals its number of pendants, by the disjoint outlet paths and the cut of all its spokes. A marked spoke realizes this same minimum. For a core distinction and a probe spoke, a bond must separate the terminals since both interiors are marked. The optimal connected separating shores can be glued at each terminal. Unmarked groups have the same ordinary two-terminal substitution. Thus the core-to-probe values are exactly those on \(H\) with two or three unit loose tips at each leaf as appropriate, one of them probed. Every remaining core position is identified by the simple-tree formula using probes at two leaves beyond the positions being compared. Hence the pair reading and growing end fix every name. [Two-terminal gluing](../LATER-BOND-READING-20261119-001/boundary-proof.md)

Finally swap two old spokes in any one group and fix every other name. Their singleton minima agree by pendant symmetry. The old spoke-to-incident-probe pair had size three. Its image has a larger value and still has a larger value in the next round. In a two-pendant group the wrong spoke lies on a permanent old four-cycle excluding the marked unary probe. In a three-pendant group the wrong spoke lies on two old four-cycles sharing only that spoke, so one of them excludes the marked rim probe. If a new bond of size at most three contained the mismatched pair, it would need another old distinction on the indicated cycle. Its whole support would be old, and conservation of old equations rules it out. The successful old three-star persists as a bond. This proves exact order two even when the subsequent target is merely a pair, regardless of choices of fresh labels. The test \(\texttt{cherry_start(start_stars)}\) recognizes the stated initial family.

**Every feasible tree start without degree two.** The same exact order holds for every simple tree start under the specified initial loose counts if it has no ordinary degree-two site, has at least one nonleaf, and its first round is lawful. These conditions have a direct test. Only the leaves are live, each with two loose distinctions. If \(k\) of them have the same neighbor, every candidate pair there grows and no group of three or more grows, since their ordinary neighbor backs the group. Each leaf needs \(k-1\) inputs for those pairs. Thus the first round is available exactly when \(k\le3\) for every neighbor. [Start recognizer](structural_features.py)

When there are at least two nonleaf sites, the subtree they induce has two ends, each with at least two original leaves. Every group has a route from its old holder \(h\) through the rest to the exterior. The preceding size-three tests therefore still pin all spokes at holders with two or three pendants; the mismatched entry in a two-pendant group need only exceed three when \(h\) has several other neighbors. If exactly one leaf \(a\) hangs at \(h\), the two unary births at \(a\) each get two fresh distinctions. They have a common parent and the next pair and two offshoots are lawful. Keep \(a\) and the old shared name \(ha\) as part of the backbone. Its new branch has two independent outlets to the exterior at \(a\), and the two fixed shared names to the newborns are a minimum cut there. Replace it by two unit loose tips at \(a\). At every other old holder, the two- or three-pendant groups compress as before. The resulting ordinary tree has degree three at each retained pendant and retains the old degree at every original nonleaf. Each of its ordinary leaf sites has at least one probed outlet. Its positive midpoint metric distinguishes every remaining old name. At least one multiple-pendant group supplies the strict order-one failure at the next round.

With just one nonleaf, the available case is a three-leaf star. Its first stage, including the wired exterior, is the eight-vertex cube. Its six-cycle names and its loose names are fixed. Its only bonds of size at most three are the singleton-vertex stars: the smaller shore has at most four vertices, and a two-, three- or four-vertex set in the bipartite cubic graph has boundary at least four, five or four respectively. Hence a spoke reads three exactly at its two incident six-cycle names. Exchanging two spokes preserves singleton values and fails on a pair. The next round consumes all three loose distinctions in a single implied cap, preserving the equation span and this same failure. This covers sampled start size four as well.

**Degree-two sites with one-parent births.** The conclusion extends to any available minimum-loose simple tree start on at least three sites in which every group with multiple parents uses only tree leaves. In a tree, three or more sites pairwise at ordinary distance two have a common neighbor: the first two determine a unique two-link path, and a third at distance two from both must meet its middle site to avoid a cycle. Thus all first-round multi-parent groups are the individual pairs of live neighbors at each holder. A degree-two site has one loose distinction. The stated condition makes its only birth a unary junction. Its branch meets the rest at the old degree-two holder and at the exterior, has separating cost one, and its single near distinction realizes that cost as a fixed probe. Replace this branch by one unit outlet at the original site. Its two ordinary directions and its one outlet give degree three in the compressed tree.

All other sites and branches have the preceding forms, and the second round is lawful: the unary child of each new degree-two case has two fresh distinctions and no sibling sharing its parent. A multiple-leaf group exists by the live-neighbor counting argument below. If it is the only branch at all, the tree is the three-leaf star already settled; otherwise the path to another branch supplies the outside crossing used in the local argument. The old pair misreading and the recovery of every tree position now follow by the same fixed probes. The test \(\texttt{pendant_join_start(start_stars)}\) counts the live neighbors at each site, checks that any two or three are leaves, and enforces the available first round. It takes linear time. This class includes all the feasible degree-two-free examples.

Under the specified minimum-loose initialization a pure tree start of size at least three always has two live sites with a common ordinary neighbor. Otherwise each vertex of degree at least three has at most one live neighbor, and hence at least two neighbors of degree at least three. A nonempty finite induced forest cannot have minimum degree two. If all degrees are at most two, the tree is a path and an internal vertex already has two live neighbors. A lawful first round must cover such a pair in a group, producing an ordinary cycle through their old tree path. Later rounds retain that cycle. The added-link sampling law already starts with a cycle. The ordinary-tree **stage** criterion thus has zero coverage in these three sampled positive-age populations; the pendant-group **start** criterion has the positive support just proved. Neither structural result implies a percentage.

## Certificates for arbitrary reached stages

These tests use the observed stage's literal supports and pair table, without searching its permutation group or running another round. They decide whether each map preserves the equation span. An open stage with no lawful successor receives order zero separately under this study's convention. [Implementation](structural_features.py)

**Interchanges.** Write \(C\) for the cut space. Transposing names \(i,j\) changes a row \(w\in C\) by zero or by the two-name word \(\{i,j\}\), according as its entries there agree or differ. Thus this transposition preserves \(C\) exactly when its two incidence columns agree or \(\{i,j\}\in C\). Equal incidence columns mean the same ordinary holders and the same exterior incidence. Because no singleton is an equation, the second condition is exactly \(K(i,j)=2\).

At order zero the permitted permutations fix the growing-end names individually and otherwise are arbitrary. At order one they are arbitrary within the equally valued singleton classes of unfixed names. Thus testing the interchanges from one representative to every other member of its class gives an exact answer at both orders. This uses the fact that those interchanges generate every permutation of the class.

At order two partition the movable names by their singleton value and the vector of their pair values to the individually fixed end names. One can refine each class further by its members' multisets of pair values to each current class, iterating to stability. Every marked pair isometry retains each class setwise. If every class has only space-preserving interchanges then all allowed maps preserve the space. Conversely two unfixed names of equal singleton value with identical pair entries to every other third name are interchangeable in the full pair table. If their interchange fails the cut-space condition, this is a concrete counterexample. Between the sufficient and failure tests the output stays unresolved.

**Forcing equations with two marks.** Write \(N_m(i)=\{i\}\cup\{z\ne i:K(i,z)\le m\}\). For \(m=K(i)\) this is the union of all smallest bonds through \(i\); if its cardinality is \(m\) it is one forced bond. For \(m=K(i,j)<\infty\), every minimizing bond through the pair is an \(m\)-subset of \(P=N_m(i)\cap N_m(j)\). If \(|P|=m\), the pair reading forces the entire \(P\) as a bond. Adjoin all rows so obtained to the pointwise-fixed live rows and the exterior row (all loose names). The resulting subspace \(D\) is invariant under all the marked pair symmetries. A full rank equal to the number of ordinary junctions certifies the complete span. This extends the unique-singleton test even when each individual name has several minimizing bonds. The straightforward pair computation and the binary rank test are polynomial in the physical width.

If \(\dim C-\dim D=1\), one can also certify the missing coset. For any pair with finite value \(m\), form the \(m\)-subsets of \(P\) containing it on which all pair readings are at most \(m\). Reduce these words modulo \(D\). The resulting set of residue classes contains the class of an actual smallest bond for this pair. If it omits zero, it must therefore contain the sole nonzero class of \(C/D\). Intersect these candidate sets over such pairs. A singleton intersection forces the missing class and hence the full span. The argument requires neither uniqueness of the bond nor uniqueness of its support modulo other unknown equations. For a finite computational budget the implementation includes all pairs whose binomial count of possible completions is within the specified bound. This rule is invariant under every renaming. Skipping requests can only weaken the certificate. A failed certificate is not a counterexample.

## Literal controls

The controls use the archived group generator, replay each of the two complete rounds with the separately written round verifier, and compute the current pair table by enumerating connected complementary shores. Two equal-price double bundles have a genuine countercontrol. On the four-site ordinary path with bundle sizes \(2,2,2\) and two loose names at each end, the initial live supports pin enough bundles for safety. One round later the three double bundles are unpinned and the transposition \(0,2\) preserves the literal pair table while changing the old span. With the bundle sizes \(3,2,3\) the smaller middle exit price separates the repeated outer bundles' within- and across-bundle readings. Directly testing all marked pair isometries gives 672 equation-changing maps out of 720 in the first case, and zero out of 144 in the second. The general color-and-rank certificate leaves that second case unresolved, whereas the tree criterion decides it. [Replay](structural_checks.py)

On a single backbone link with pendant counts \((2,2),(2,3),(3,3)\), the reached sizes in junctions and distinctions are respectively \((12,25),(13,25),(14,25)\). Their next stages reach \((20,41),(18,34),(16,27)\). On all three the established source pair reads three and its spoke-exchanged image exceeds three. Elimination on each actual successor rules out *every* cut of size at most three on the exchanged pair. The three-leaf star reaches \((7,12)\) then closes at \((8,12)\); a tree combining single and double pendants reaches \((16,33)\) then \((27,55)\); a tree with a degree-two unary parent reaches \((18,37)\) then \((31,63)\). Literal pair readings and the fixed end certify exact order two at each of those first reached stages. These are specified examples, separate from the random estimates of coverage.
