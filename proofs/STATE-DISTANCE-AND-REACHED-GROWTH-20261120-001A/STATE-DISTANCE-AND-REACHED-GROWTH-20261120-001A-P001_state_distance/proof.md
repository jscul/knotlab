# A state distance and an arrival are different readings

Assume the ordinary funded growth law on a finite tree of knots, distinct live queried holders R and S, and one marked loose distinction in each. A parent step counts order. Use equal weight on the allowed not-all-equal words. The marked-bit disagreement rate is r and their signed agreement is C = 1 − 2r. Marking a bit and adopting a weighting are choices. The parent groups and their funding follow from the growth law.

## What each mark and each parent step reads

List the holders on the unique ordinary route from R to S as 0 through h, inclusive, with arities qᵢ. Complement symmetry gives the same number of completions of every attached tree at either value of its attaching bit. Thus, given the incoming bit at a route holder of arity q, its remaining entries are uniform over all 2^(q−1)−1 admissible choices. There is one more way for the outgoing mark to differ than to agree. The conditional sign is −1/(2^(q−1)−1). Composing these responses gives C as the product of the h+1 signs. In particular −log₃|C|−1 equals h for ternary holdings. At mixed arities there is no general unweighted holder count in that scalar.

Under parity any two such loose marks disagree on half the allowed words. A minimum parity support containing both marks is the boundary of the physical route. To see this, attach all loose marks to a single formal exterior holder for the cut calculation. Every connected set containing R and S and excluding the exterior includes the full route. Every off-route component of the route's complement reaches a loose mark, so both sides of the route cut are connected. Adding any further holder increases the cut size by q−2. Hence the shortest canonical bond containing both marks has size Σqᵢ−2h, and the frozen bond distance is one less.

For the parent reading a witnessed step in a tree uses two ordinary meetings via a common holder. Every witnessed route is an even ordinary walk with a live holder at every even-numbered position. Every ordinary walk between R and S visits their full route and can change a visit's index only by an even number. Thus the endpoints lie in the same witnessed part precisely if h is even and every even-indexed holder is live. In that case their witnessed distance d is h/2. Otherwise it is infinite. Funding gives a true child at each co-input and gives each live parent a child. At later cuts two children are witnessed together exactly when they have a common parent. Passing from two families to their entire next cohorts therefore reduces their witnessed separation by one until they meet. A witnessed part remains separate forever. This uses continuing cuts, counting a terminal common child itself if present. A stage-wide funding veto is a separate ending. For an explicit check even with two finite delays, take arities (3,7,3) and (3,3,4,3,3), with R and S at the ends. All holders are live and both ordinary first cuts are funded. On each tree there are 1,134 full allowed words, exactly 568 with unequal endpoint bits. The witnessed distances are respectively one and two. The smallest ordinary near/never example uses only two three-place holders, sharing one name. Of its 18 allowed words, eight disagree on the two loose marks. Its state ruler is one, but its two families never acquire a common descendant. ## What an actual matched edit changes

Supply one further loose name at the already live S and match births recursively by their complete old parents and local allocation. This keeps the initial witnessed graph fixed. At finite d>0 the initial child of S newly paid by the edit has no counterpart. The combined true parents of R's step-one children are the closed witnessed neighborhood of R. Consequently the extra child is at distance 1+dist(S,N[R])=d from that cohort, since a first-cut child route lifts old co-inputs and projects to consecutive shared parents. Their first common descendant is an unmatched receiver birth at d+1. Matching protection excludes an earlier change of birth.

The unique first common group on the tree consists of the d+1 live originals on the shortest witnessed route. Count its old neighboring originals outside this group and add all the loose inputs of its members left after that cut's binary groups are paid. If the sum b is positive, the matched birth pays b fresh names in the ordinary preparation and b+1 in the edited one. Its earlier matched receiver births have identical prices. If b is zero, the group exhausts its witnessed part. The one fresh required to continue the finished part masks the edit at that birth. In the edited preparation the new spare contributes a second parent at the next birth. Hence no later receiver birth can be recursively matched. The matched-price delay is infinite. Under continuing not-all-equal growth the three delays (common descendant, first changed matched price, first unmatched birth) are (d,d,d+1) when b>0, (d,∞,d+1) when b=0 and (∞,∞,∞) across parts. A witness-changing edit can act earlier. ## The two supplied rivals

One rival adds direct sharing live pairs to the ordinary groups. The other enlarges an ordinary group with a live common witness supplying a loose name of its own. All births at the first successful step have disjoint fresh holdings. Hence these two provisions affect only the starting cut. Each funded nonclosing birth can complete any old word on its own fresh names, and a funded solitary parity closing is already implied. A veto retains every old state. The usual co-input bound on influence holds in each supplied rule's own true-parent reading. On a funded all-live tree of constant arity, direct joining gives common descent after ⌈h/2⌉. Including a live witness gives this from three holders upwards. Adjacent live pairs in a tree of that size are covered by at least one enlarged group. A single group can traverse at most two ordinary meetings. [Costs and full-state hypotheses](../../../research/STATE-DISTANCE-AND-REACHED-GROWTH-20261120-001A/rival_joins.md)

Hold the arities at six on an ordinary five-holder path and mark its endpoints. In a second tree attach four further six-place holders at each of the three interior holders by converting their loose marks to shared ones. The original route and its arities are identical, so both state readings agree in both trees. They give not-all-equal r=14314576/28629151 and parity bond distance 21. All three initial rules are funded in both trees. The first has a two-step co-input route between the endpoints. In the second tree no co-input crosses consecutive spent holders and subsequent children remain in their parts. Under each rule the delays are hence two and infinity on equal inputs to the proposed scalar formula. Neither supplied rival can give a single arrival function of the two marked-state readings on all funded trees. These comparisons also hold under both knot laws for the fully specified ordinary cuts. The two rivals each lose the seven-knot repeating source to their first funding veto and retain all descendants of the larger 21-knot source.

This obstruction concerns the frozen marked observations. In the full named not-all-equal reconstruction class, recover the holdings and use the chosen law to read the extended co-input distance, with infinity across parts. There the state relation and supplied rule together determine common descent. The independent route check compared 31,998 marked pairs across all 1,441 labelled trees with two through six holders and 2,626 allowed realizations of liveness. Further sample estimates, including their distinct conditional denominators, remain confined to their named weightings.
