# What passes to the next enrolled holder?

**Theorem.** Fix the recorded five-founder succession. At a return, two binary inputs record a signal by their disagreement. Under each tested law, for every fixed number of shared-holding steps and every integer $m\ge1$, there is one later return and its actual next child whose respective local observations are identical for histories with each earlier signal count from $0$ to $m$. All those histories use the same founding valuation, named payments and enrollment. Consequently a newborn's own marks and any inspection with a fixed contact budget cannot always report the exact signal count, its parity, or even zero versus one. The entire growing collection of holdings does retain the evidence for every signal.

**Most recent signal.** At a return under even parity, the disagreement of the holder's *two fresh values* equals the disagreement of its *two incoming values*. Under not-all-equal every fresh pair is compatible with both silence and a signal when the incoming values may vary. The enrolled child receives one of the two fresh marks. Immediately after that payment, either law permits the child's own four values to coincide after a silent or a signal-bearing return. Given the continuing-parent role, two coholding steps are necessary in the worst case and sufficient to recover that predecessor's signal under the frozen physical reading. A separately supplied pointer identifying its original inputs makes one step sufficient. ## Which fresh marks record the most recent signal?

Write $x,y$ for the two incoming bits and $u,v$ for the two new ones. The complete supports are as follows.

| Incoming | Lawful fresh pair under even parity | Lawful fresh pair under not-all-equal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 00 | 00, 11 | 01, 10, 11 |
| 01 | 01, 10 | 00, 01, 10, 11 |
| 10 | 01, 10 | 00, 01, 10, 11 |
| 11 | 00, 11 | 00, 01, 10 |

Parity imposes $u\mathbin\oplus v=x\mathbin\oplus y$. Each named fresh bit alone takes either value for each incoming word. Not-all-equal permits the pair 01 or 10 on every incoming word. If the new bits agree, at least one incoming bit must have the opposite value. For each of the four possible fresh pairs, the table includes an equal input and also an unequal input. The preceding return proof realizes all four inputs on the actual first return under either law separately. Thus the whole fresh pair by itself determines no nonconstant function of the most recent signal under not-all-equal. The original incoming pair still records it under both laws.

### One real payment that shows or hides the signal

Use the previously recorded structural allocation. Return 29 receives names 55 and 57 from 23 and 24. The quiet past through cut 3 is common. At cut 4 keep 23 quiet. Let 24 change both 56 and 57 from zero to one under parity, or only 57 under not-all-equal. It previously received 11, so these are lawful. Name 57 goes to 29 and 56 goes to 28. The first return sees 01. The quiet comparison gives it 00. At cut 6, 29 pays 66 to child 33 and 67 to the next enrolled child 34. The outside payer 28 contributes 65 to 34 and 64 to child 32. None of these destinations changes between the following alternatives.

| History | 29 receives 55,57 | 29 fills 66,67 | 28 pays 65 to 34 | 34 receives 65,67 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Quiet | 00 | 11 | 1 | 11 |
| Signal, changed fresh bit paid to child 33 | 01 | 01 | 1 | 11 |
| Same signal, changed fresh bit paid to enrolled child 34 | 01 | 10 | 1 | 10 |

In either signal arm, holder 28 fills 64,65 with 01 for parity (its input is 01) and 11 for not-all-equal (its input is 00). In the first two rows 34 can also give both its own fresh values zero. Its complete word is 1100 in both. Under not-all-equal, 29 can even use its unchanged fresh pair 11 on input 01. All siblings extend their actually received payments lawfully, as checked on the full words. The latter two rows share the same paid values and entire past at 29 before either new mark is filled. A choice of which value to write is sufficient to change what is physically delivered.

At the one-step observation of child 34, the only holders are 34, 28 and 29. To match the quiet row to the row with the changed bit paid to 33, exchange names 57 and 66 among the marks incident to 29. For parity also exchange 56 and 64 among the marks incident to 28. Each exchanged mark has two holders in the whole presentation and exactly its indicated parent inside this observation. The exchanges match the values, keep every mark of 34 fixed, and keep every pointed role. They give an explicit isomorphism of the two one-step readings. In particular the most recent signal cannot always be recovered at one step. The named swaps are a comparison of observations. Both runs used the same physical binding and never exchanged old values.

Two steps always suffice for this most recent signal immediately after any positive return pays its next child. From the rooted child inspect the continuing parent. All four of that parent's names now have a second holder. Follow each to that holder. Both of its children were just born and each holds two loose distinctions. Its two older payers have spent all their loose distinctions. Thus the two marks whose other holders are completely spent are exactly its original paid inputs. Read their two values. This works without prior role labels at the visited parent. With their two identities separately pointed out, visiting that parent once supplies both values. Neither procedure changes the count. ## The same later receipt after different totals

First take the two **archived** histories, quiet and a single first signal. For parity, both 28 and 29 have one changed input. They send one changed outgoing bit each to their common child 34, on 65 and 67. It receives 00 in place of 11. The two changes cancel in its parity equation and it gives its fresh pair the quiet values 00. No other newborn at cut 6 has a changed input. For not-all-equal only name 57 was changed. The receiving 29 with input 01 can keep its whole fresh pair 11 at reference. At cut 5 its whole cohort's fresh values already coincide with the quiet history. Permanently differing old names are exactly 56,57,65,67 under parity and 57 under not-all-equal. All future *new* distinctions can be written identically in the two histories, since every subsequent payer has the same loose values and the bindings may be fixed in advance.

The next positive return after recovery is holder 50 at cut 10. It receives names 98 and 100 with values 11 from 45 and 46. Its own 108 and 109 are both zero in both histories. At its **next real payment**, 108 from 50 and 110 from 51 give enrolled child 55 the paid pair 00. It adds 118 and 119 with values 11. Companion 109 goes to child 56 and remains zero there. All these holders, names and values agree literally. The counts differ, zero versus one, at both 50 and 55. Both comparisons also give the same age, number of actual returns, and current return flag. The complete histories, including the old holdings, recover the different totals by revisiting the two original payments at the first return. ### For every fixed contact budget

The same single signal can be realized at any selected positive return $h=5j$. Start just before its two payers form, with all the current loose values at reference. Fix in advance any sequence of legal physical bindings, the same for all histories under comparison. Under not-all-equal, complement the age-$(h-1)$ outside payer's one fresh value destined for $R_h$. Its other fresh value stays at reference. Its own unswitched word had two zeros and two ones, so its new word remains mixed. At $R_h$ complementing one incoming bit likewise leaves a mixed word with both fresh reference values. No further fresh deviation is needed.

Under parity, complement both fresh values of the same outside payer $K_{h-1}(1)$. Its two actual children are $R_h$ and $K_h(1)$. Each now has one changed input. At each of these two children complement only the outgoing mark bound for their *common* enrolled child $R_{h+1}$. Its two changed inputs balance and its fresh values can both be at reference. All other fresh values at ages $h$ and $h+1$ can stay at reference. Under either law the original two incoming values at $R_h$ were equal and exactly one changes. The structural payment and enrollment stay the same. The latest holder incident to any changed name has age at most $h+1$. Old differences persist at their named holdings, and the live loose values have recovered. This also explains why the argument works for every predetermined binding. An additional allocator that chooses a different binding after one history changes would be a separate control question.

Repeat the construction independently at any subset of the first $m$ positive returns. Consecutive positive returns are five handoffs apart. After a selected return the common loose values are already restored before the banks needed for the next return form. Each construction therefore produces exactly its selected signal at those returns. Continue all later births at reference on their fresh names. Every changed name is incident only to holders of age at most $5m+1$.

Choose a later positive return time $T$ with $T-r>5m+1$. A coholding joins adjacent formation ages, apart from sharings within the founders. From $R_T$ or $R_{T+1}$, every holder accessible within $r$ coholding steps has age at least $T-r$. Thus no mark incident to any visited holder can have changed in any of these histories. All accessed names, incidences, values, roles, ages and return flags agree **even before renaming**. Each history has the exact same next real payment from $R_T$ to $R_{T+1}$. All counts $0,\ldots,m$ can occur there. This simultaneously excludes a required decoding of the count, of its residue modulo any $k\ge2$, or of any nonconstant fixed function of the cumulative count. The compatible continuations can repeat quietly on fresh names forever. A deliberately revisited, arbitrarily old history can recover each stored receipt.

## What a supplied choice could write

Even for an unlike input pair, either law allows either bit on *one specified outgoing slot*. Fix which slot will pay the next enrolled child and grant a writer the incoming parent roles and return flag. There is a simple supplied choice that carries count parity, starting at the existing zero-valued loose founder marks. Write $p$ for the incoming bit from the continuing parent and $q$ for the one from the other payer. Off a return write $p$ on the designated recipient's slot. On a return write $q$. If $p$ represents the previous count parity, updating it by the signal gives $p\oplus(p\oplus q)=q$. Induction proves correct transmission. Complete its sibling payment by the table. The outside payer at any return can supply either value of $q$ on its own chosen payment, so this construction can coexist with any prescribed return stream under each law. The audit checks a six-return stream and thirty deliveries as an existence control.

The ordinary transition also admits the other value on that same outgoing mark. It supplies no such selection, no return flag to an unpointed newborn, and no promise to a writer about rebinding after a fill. The proof concerns what *all* ordinary fillings force at one continuing receiver. Intentional encodings, growing access to the retained record, and other preparations have their own premises.
