# How many parent steps until two releases have a common descendant?

At a complete cut, take the knots with loose distinctions. Two different such knots are one step apart when a present knot witnesses them together. A witnessed pair can belong to a larger group. Write d(A,B) for the fewest such steps between two nonempty marked sets of current knots, and infinity if no route exists. Either endpoint can be an unordered class recognized from the current holdings. The statements about subsequent growth condition on the ordinary plan being funded. They apply to parity and not-all-equal and require no map from the original start. [Ordinary end rule](../ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/end_operator.md)

## Exact transfer

Let I(A) be the next knots with at least one actual parent in A. At each continuing complete step,

    d(I(A), I(B)) = max(0, d(A,B) − 1).

Infinity stays infinity. For d>0 choose a shortest witnessed route. For each successive pair choose an actual child using both. Coverage follows because a candidate pair belongs to a proposed group, and the complete step funds all groups. Children of consecutive pairs coincide or share the intervening parent and hence are at most one step apart. The first child descends from A and the last from B. At d=0 any common live member gives a common child, since each positive holder spends all its loose distinctions.

Conversely take a next route of length e between I(A) and I(B). When e>0, take one actual common parent across each of its e steps. These exist because the only witnesses between positive-age children are their parents. Prepend an A parent of the first child and append a B parent of the last. Each two successive old holders coincide or belong to one actual child's candidate group. This is a route of at most e+1 old steps, proving the lower bound and the infinity case. If e=0 the common child's A and B parents coincide or are candidates. Induction gives exactly d parent steps to the first common descendant whenever those steps occur. A sole closing parity child can itself have both ancestries, but it supplies no live growing end. A globally unfunded plan supplies no step to which the formula applies.

A pure continuing product has **every** immediate parent in the previous members of that product. In the reverse proof, for a route starting at a pure child its first shared parent already belongs to the product. The old route needs at most e steps even after appending the other party's parent. For e=0 the two old families overlap. Thus the distance from a pure family to the inclusive descendants of another release can never decrease, for as long as those families remain live. At distance one from the pure receiver's **present** members some group of the next complete step uses both. At distance two or more, no future birth can use a then-pure member with a co-parent descended from the other release. This also applies if the group takes an input from a third thing. [All-size obstruction and unequal release sizes](indirect.md)

There are three distinct answers to the question about a shared source. Two families in different witnessed parts have no common later output by parentage while growth is funded. Two already separate families in one part can acquire common descendants at exactly the distance above. Their common earlier source proves neither influence nor a delayed direct encounter. Every causal path from a released product consists of actual later children. It cannot run backwards through the release into an older source knot. Later mixed children can supply further inputs after both releases. A change of start can also affect another part through the whole-stage funding or closing rule. The positive comparisons here use continuing funded stages and explicit chains of parents. [Lasting sources on one part](generator_delay.md) · [Changes to physical holdings](start_edits.md)

## Reading the growing end

The current live holdings, their open counts and the witnessed relation suffice for this distance and first common output on any marked pair. An anonymous reader may return the two equivalent ends as an unordered pair. Recognizing *which* earlier product a live member belongs to is a further task. Full present holdings can distinguish original returns erased by restricting the reading. At one checked cut even a swap of a one-input with a two-input member preserves the anonymous end.
