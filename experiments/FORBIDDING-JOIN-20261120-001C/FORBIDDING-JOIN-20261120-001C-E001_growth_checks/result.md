# Which growth predictions survive a join that can forbid?

**An old parity bond still detects a requirement that is already implied. Its size cannot decide whether the one-fewer rule will leave a ternary birth without a fresh distinction.** Two starts have the same least bond size on all seven nonempty subsets of its three inputs. The one-fewer rule gives one birth zero fresh distinctions and the other one. Both rival rules also stop the ordinary seven-knot periodic source before its first release. A separate, post-freeze example loses old states at a genuine join, preserves every displayed knot and keeps producing two new knots per round forever. Independent referee pending. ## A bond can predict implication

In a connected presentation of homogeneous parity equations with each distinction held once or twice, an equation on loose distinctions alone is obtained by summing entire connected components of holders. Indeed, to cancel each shared distinction one must include both holders or neither. The only nonempty such equation therefore contains **all** loose distinctions. This argument uses the actual present holdings and applies after a previous restricted step. At a funded proposal with p ≥ 3 parents, the earlier bond theorem shows that a least bond of size p on p − 1 of its inputs detects exactly this exhaustion. Its proof uses the same grouping rule at the present stage. A zero grant under a rival rule can also occur at a nonimplied birth. For the arity-floor rule every proposal with three or more parents gets zero. [Pre-birth bond theorem](../../../research/BOND-CLOSURE-20261120-001/criterion.md)

Take six knots in cyclic order `U,Wuv,V,Wvt,T,Wtu`. Give each consecutive pair, including the last and first, two different shared distinctions. Give `U,V,T` one loose distinction each. Keep the three as the inputs of the marked ternary proposal. Place two additional loose distinctions on `Wuv` or, in the other start, on `U`. Every source support has its stated knot law. The proposed group is legal because each pair of parents has a common witness and no witness holds all three.

For parity, the five loose distinctions form a bond. Any proper nonempty set of holders has at least four shared distinctions crossing its cut. A cut through one or more queried loose distinctions costs at least five. Hence the three singleton minima, three pair minima and triple minimum are **all five in both starts**. The exact cut enumeration also compares with the minimal supports of the row span. The receipts use the same input names in both preparations. In the parent preparation one-fewer leaves the ternary child live with one open distinction. It owes two uses next round, so the whole round stalls. The arity-floor version gives that child zero, leaving only its two unary siblings live. Their actual next round is funded. Thus **a further reduction in the grant can avert a stall**. The arity-floor version there retains only 1,024 of 2,048 old parity states. After either zero-fresh restriction, the two extra distinctions at their common holder are tied, so that older holder fails K2. Its former knot status and the present ternary knot status are recorded individually.

Under not-all-equal, all seven queried minima were also recorded. Each singleton reads its own parent’s arity. No bond includes inputs from two different parents here. To see this, partially pin any set of distinctions that contains no whole holder. Give each partly pinned shared bundle a missing colour when necessary, and make each wholly free bundle mixed. Any holder still monochromatic has its bundles fully pinned and an unpinned loose distinction, which can supply the other colour privately. Thus the only minimal constrained sets are the individual displayed supports. The marked ternary proposal is nonimplied in both preparations. The witness allocation retains 62,556 of 83,278 old states under either rival. The parent allocation retains all 78,952 under one-fewer and 59,268 under the arity-floor rule. Every displayed not-all-equal support remains fully lawful. The marked birth itself is a full knot under both laws in every arm.

## A restricted start can keep producing knots

**Post-freeze existence checks.** A three-knot control shows the repeating end. Let `U,W` share two distinctions, and `W,V` share one. Give `U` one loose distinction, `V` two, and `W` none. Its two live knots have one common witness. One binary birth spends an opening from each, and one unary birth spends the second opening of `V`. The two children share a parent. Each therefore has demand one. The binary child receives one fresh distinction, the unary child two, under all three grant rules. They again form the only two live knots, with a common witness and the same respective openings. This proves two new knots at each round for every finite number of rounds.

To reach that end **after forbidding old states**, use the doubled six-knot cycle above. Keep one opening at each of `U,V,T`, and put two at `Wuv` and one at `Wvt`. The three marked parents make the ternary birth. The two live witnesses make one binary child, and `Wuv` has a unary child as well. The ternary birth has demand zero and ordinary grant one. Both rivals grant zero. The binary and unary children receive one and two. The first restricted round gives the following counts.

| Law | Old states | Old states with an extension | New complete states | Actual displayed knots after the restriction |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Parity | 4,096 | 2,048 | 4,096 | All 9 have full even-parity projections |
| Not-all-equal | 176,872 | 132,820 | 597,804 | All 9 have full not-all-equal projections |

From then on the two live children reproduce the end just proved, adding two knots of three places and three distinctions in every round. Every surviving old state extends further because both births have private fresh distinctions. Each later new support has its whole stated law. In parity the one or two inputs form a proper subset of three loose distinctions, so every input assignment occurs by the connected parity-sum argument. Under not-all-equal remove the selected loose inputs of one proposed birth. Each holder retains at least two distinctions, the old shared incidence stays connected, and at least one other loose distinction remains. With loose ends represented as one-place tips, the connected incidence is outside the odd-cycle exception to the two-colouring lemma. All holders can already see both colours on the unpinned distinctions, allowing arbitrary values on the selected inputs. Each admissible assignment on the new support can therefore be completed, including the other birth’s independent fresh distinctions. The induction retains all old full projections. Choosing a completion successively gives a compatible infinite assignment extending each surviving first-round state. This establishes existence and continued production. No probability on infinite histories or source-of-openness result at infinity is used. [Two-colouring lemma used by the prior study](../../../research/NAE-GROWTH-20261119-001/nae_growth.md) · [Separate supplemental receipt](../../../artifacts/FORBIDDING-JOIN-20261120-001C/FORBIDDING-JOIN-20261120-001C-E001_growth_checks/supplement_split_spares.json)

The input order is the ordinary stable order. For each birth reserve q distinct names in a private block. Map retained name k in the first-f convention to name q − f + k in the last-f convention for 0 ≤ k < f. This fixes all old names, preserves each displayed support and transports every placement. Composing these maps as each birth is installed transports all future loose-input allocations and their setting-independent readings. More generally a bijection of a whole fixed history that carries each allocated input and each output to its image preserves its conjunction under any installation order. Choosing another set of currently live parents amounts to a different map. The seven-knot stall explicitly prevents the ordinary all-future map from being carried over.
