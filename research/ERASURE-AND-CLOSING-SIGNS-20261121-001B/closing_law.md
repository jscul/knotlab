# Which closing signs can a reader register?

## How to recognize a closing birth

Write one parity equation per physical knot and one binary column per named distinction. A loose distinction occurs once. Each other used distinction occurs twice at two different knots. Joining the holders of every twice-held name partitions the knots into their connected components.

Consider any ordinary paid birth with input set \(I\). Every input is an older loose name and each comes from a different parent. Two parents are proposed together only by witnesses among the physical knots, so all parents of a paid birth are in one old component. An input consisting of just one name also belongs to one such component.

A sum of old parity rows equals the row on \(I\) only if the two old holders of every twice-held name have the same inclusion coefficient. No twice-held name belongs to \(I\). The coefficients are therefore constant within each connected component. The sum of all rows in an open component is precisely the set of its loose names. The open components have disjoint sets of loose names, and a closed component contributes zero. Consequently a nonempty \(I\) is implied exactly when it consists of **all** loose names of its own component. If this happens, that component has no second birth in the same cut. Every one of its loose names has already been allocated to the closing birth.

The ordinary law assigns zero fresh distinctions to this implied equation. That component becomes entirely twice held. A continuing birth has at least one fresh distinction, which remains loose at this cut. These facts give a check independent of the simulator's zero-price label: compare complete component memberships and the occurrence counts before and after every cut. The same test works when two components close simultaneously and other components continue. Funding is still computed on their whole union. The unsigned not-all-equal law has the continuing floor on every actual paid birth under the rules studied here. ## Zero ordinary completions have no internal failure register

In a connected closed holding with \(h\) physical knots and \(m\) used names the only row dependence over the two-element field is the sum of all \(h\) rows. The same two-holder argument forces coefficients to be constant. The rank is \(h-1\), also when two holders meet repeatedly. A vector of requested parities admits ordinary words exactly when its total on this component is even; then exactly \(2^{m-h+1}\) words realize it. Reversing the one closing sign with every other sign even gives zero words. Erasing any one equation at the same physical names leaves \(h-1\) independent equations and exactly \(2^{m-h+1}\) completions, including when one remaining equation is odd. Every strictly smaller family of equations is therefore jointly satisfiable. Projecting the already empty full relation onto fewer names still gives an empty projection. These are two different erasures.

For a disconnected whole every closed component has its **own** sign consistency requirement. Two single-odd components in one cut are each empty, even when the sum of both proposed charges is even. A continuing component cannot compensate. For a supplied operator reading the necessity of each component's admissibility follows by restriction. Sufficiency follows by tensoring the separate finite readings and using the identity on the other factors. The ordinary fresh-opening controls and the four assignments on actual simultaneous births test these statements.

Before the closing step, all allowed ordinary words already give the implied product on the actual old input names. Paying zero leaves those words intact under the ordinary sign. Growth adds no ready bank, outcome-controlled exchange, source of random signs or record of a failed preparation. Under the reversed sign the formal ordinary post-step state set is empty; a post-step read probability on that set is undefined. Erasing the attempted birth restores the older allowed words. An external checker can preserve a failure record in a different, nonempty system.

## A supplied two-reader preparation

For the all-even reading, draw one uniform complete ordinary word and let both keepers read their named places in it. For one odd sign, use the prior classification of operator readings of connected two-holder-per-name patterns. Repeated meetings retain their separate names in the signed check. The executable constructs and checks a Hermitian involution \(A_i\) for every physical name. The involutions occurring at any one holder commute, and their product is the chosen signed identity for that holder. This classification and its original source were already admitted in the preceding study. [Earlier classifier, sign convention and source](../FOUR-READINGS-20261120-001A/results.md)

For holder \(u\) with named places in their stated order and an answer word \(a\), use its commuting joint projector
\[
 P_{u,a}=\prod_{j\in u}\frac{1+(-1)^{a_j}A_j}{2}.
\]
Give the two instruments a maximally entangled pair of the indicated finite dimension \(D\). Use \(P_{u,a}\) on the first instrument and \(P_{v,b}^{\mathsf T}\) on the second. Each trial probability conditional on its ordered settings is
\[
 \Pr(a,b\mid u,v)=\operatorname{tr}(P_{u,a}P_{v,b})/D .
\]
The response is normalized by completeness. When the named places overlap, unequal answers on a shared place have orthogonal projectors; tracing the product gives zero. Summing over the remote holder's outputs gives \(\operatorname{tr}(P_{u,a})/D\), independent of the remote setting. Reading and immediately repeating the same holder on the same instrument preserves the complete outcome by idempotence. None of these statements supplies a global assignment to all signs.

The exact implementation expands each joint projector as a short sum of signed Pauli products. Distinct unsigned basis products are orthogonal under normalized trace. A second implementation compiles operations on the named homogeneous-word carrier and performs exact stabilizer projections for all settings. The supplied writable register uses ordinary ternary allowed words 110 (ready), 101 (records zero) and 011 (records one). Reading, refusing a start, performing the controlled physical write, skipping the read and disconnecting the writer are distinct operations. The keepers are outside the sampled whole.
