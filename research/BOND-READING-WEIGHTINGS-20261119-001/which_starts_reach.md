# Why reaching even one round changes these three weightings

This all-size result concerns the probability that a generated start has a positive-age stage. The conditional distribution of the least bond reading among the starts that grow is a separate quantity. The argument was developed after the sampled first-round attrition was known.

Consider five successive starting junctions at the end of a tree, one leaf and four junctions of ordinary degree two, attached by a single further meeting to the rest. Under the shared start rule the leaf has two loose distinctions and each of the other four has one. Take the third junction counting from the leaf. Both the leaf and the fifth junction are its candidates by way of distinct neighbors. They cannot be candidates of one another. Any two candidate pairs involving that third junction must therefore be covered by different groups, even if there are further candidates. The middle junction has only one loose distinction. Thus this shape forces a first-round stall. The same argument applies in any larger meeting pattern if the five keep those degrees and that single external meeting.

**Uniform labelled tree.** Let \(m>10\), let \((m)_s\) denote a falling factorial, and let \(X\) count oriented pendant paths of the specified five junctions. For any chosen ordered five names, the rest is a labelled tree and may attach at any of its \(m-5\) junctions. Cayley's tree count gives
\[
 E X=(m)_5(m-5)^{m-6}/m^{m-2}\sim e^{-5}m .
\]
Two different such paths have disjoint sets of five. Root the tree at a centroid: a pendant side with five names, less than half, lies away from the centroid, and two such equal-sized descendant sets cannot partially overlap. For two ordered, disjoint paths both attachment junctions lie outside them. The corresponding factorial second moment is \((m)_{10}(m-10)^{m-10}/m^{m-2}\). Expanding the fixed powers of \(1-c/m\) shows that it is \((EX)^2(1+O(1/m))\). Thus \({\rm Var}(X)=O(m)\) and the probability of no such path is \(O(1/m)\).

**Uniform labelled tree with extra meetings.** Retain only these same pendant paths that the added meetings do not touch. Put \(M=\binom m2-(m-1)\) and \(q=\lceil(m-1)/4\rceil\). For one path, or for two disjoint paths, let \(s=5\) or \(10\). There are \(D_s=\binom m2-\binom{m-s}2-s\) non-tree pairs touching the chosen names. The expectation and second factorial moment above acquire factors
\[
 h_s=\binom{M-D_s}{q}/\binom Mq=e^{-s/2}(1+O(1/m)).
\]
This follows by taking logarithms of \(\prod_{i=0}^{q-1}(1-D_s/(M-i))\). In particular \(h_{10}/h_5^2=1+O(1/m)\), so the variance of the retained count is still \(O(m)\) and its mean is linear.

**Uniform recursive tree.** Use the birth labels only to bound the probability of generating the shape. Consider increasing five-tuples of labels in the final half of insertions. Require the first junction's parent to be in the first half, each of the other four to choose its predecessor, and every other insertion to avoid all these five when choosing its parent. The tuple forms the required pendant path. Uniformly over tuples the probability is \(\Theta(m^{-4})\): the four prescribed parent choices have order \(1/m\), and the remaining avoidances multiply to a quantity bounded below by a constant. There are \(\Theta(m^5)\) tuples. Two different successful tuples cannot overlap since each starts at a child of the first half and its later path has exactly one next child at each nonleaf. For disjoint tuples the joint probability is at most \(1+O(1/m)\) times the product, uniformly. At each insertion outside the ten prescribed ones the avoidance of both sets has probability at most the product of the separate avoidance probabilities. At the ten prescribed insertions, removing the redundant avoidance factors changes the ratio by at most \(1+O(1/m)\) in total. This proves the same second-moment upper bound. A uniform final renaming preserves this shape.

Consequently, for **each named weighting** with the declared loose-count rule, the unconditional probability of reaching a first round tends to zero, with upper bound \(O(1/m)\). This gives no bound on the reading order conditional on having reached a positive-age stage. In the frozen 384 draws per weighting the simple degree test detected a forced stall on 67 uniform labelled trees, one tree with extra meetings, and 18 recursive trees. Other reasons for stalls occur.
