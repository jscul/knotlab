# Can hiding a parity equation change a three-parent join?

Use the serial even-parity growth law. The seed is one junction on three distinctions. Each later junction consumes one loose bit from each of one, two, or three distinct parents and introduces two or three fresh bits. Each two parents must already have a common ordinary neighbor. Three parents must have no single common ordinary neighbor. Two parents are neighbors when they share a bit. A bond is an inclusion-minimal nonzero support of a parity equation valid on the allowed states. The scalar for two distinct bits is the minimum size of a bond containing them, minus one.

The inputs are the current complete scalar table and an unordered triple, up to arbitrary renaming. The decisive positive claim is invariance of the true triple verdict under every marked isometry of reachable tables. The decisive counterexample consists of lawful histories, the actual operands at each join, a map carrying every original scalar entry to its counterpart, and opposite physical verdicts at the matched marks. Distinguish symmetries of a single physical arrangement from isometries between different arrangements. If the counterexample can be reduced to one arrangement, give that symmetry explicitly. Minimum means fewest total distinctions anywhere in the law. An optimized specimen gives an upper bound until every smaller feasible width is excluded.

Begin at the published width-32 witness and check whether its transposition can ever change legality even under further mixed growth. Pursue only targeted mechanisms with a conceivable opposing witness. In particular test whether one can grow a live triple first and obscure one of its marks by a cap, whether the third common neighbor can be changed while marks stay loose, and whether the action on a six-junction witness can be recovered under a fully anonymous scalar isometry. A new width census or a probe experiment is outside the task. Classifying smaller widths to certify minimality is a separate claim.

If ambiguity is found, compare supplemental observations using the explicitly nested data consisting of minimum bond sizes through each marked set of at most k bits on the whole input, for k increasing from two. Keep direct readings of parity equations and readings through only the three proposed bits distinct. Prove sufficiency against every reachable competitor and give a counterexample for smaller observation levels before claiming a least arity. Test the ternary quantity on the literal width-32 state-changing swap as a diagnostic. A universal upper bound on sufficient data is a different claim from either a scalar counterexample or a sharp minimum.
