- **Merge and counting arguments.** At a knot the partners of its marked settings must occur evenly at each other knot. One setting is impossible. With two, the partner holdings coincide. In an ordinary merge the two old contributions to each commutation check cancel at the common knot, and the same cancellation holds for the interleaving sign in any knot order. A doubled pair cancels completely. Each ordinary merge contracts two contributions to their sum. A commuting subcollection after the contraction lifts to one before it. Minimality therefore persists and excludes a doubled pair. A fewest odd word is minimal because a proper commuting part and its complement would give a smaller odd word between them. An ordinary forced merge preserves \(c=V-F\), the number of active knots minus failing pairs. A merge through a knot with other settings increases it by one. Both bases have \(c=1\). [Proofs and scope](merge_arguments.md)
- **All merge orders.** Each of the 3,718 fewest words obeys the count \(1-c\) and encounters zero doubled pairs on every maximal route. Some have more than one route (440 words) or more than one terminal holding with knot labels fixed (240). The earlier depth-first search remembers the unordered multiset of paired holdings, including multiplicities. Strictness makes setting names interchangeable once those holdings and their pairing are fixed. Legal moves, signs and the base test depend only on this multiset, and every move decreases the number of pairs. Memoization is therefore sound for existence. Returning at the first success proves existence only. The exhaustive replay settles uniqueness of the base kind. The largest fewest-word search had 11 distinct states. Restricting to forced moves gave one labelled remainder under every order for every listed word. Both fewest words at the four-reader example grown from census start 786 need one forced merge.
