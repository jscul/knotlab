## What the readings count

For either allowed relation, a bond is a nonempty support whose projection misses an assignment, while every proper projection is full. There is at least one allowed state. This is the canonical knot test on the projection. The pair and triple readings below are \(b-1\), where \(b\) is the least size of a single bond containing all the queried distinctions. The reading is \(\infty\) if there is no such bond. In particular a freely assignable triple can have a finite reading through a larger bond. A larger nonfree projection containing a proper bond is excluded. The checker exercises that exclusion on a five-distinction superset of the proposal which contains a written three-place knot. ## Why every finite not-all-equal bond ball can miss exhaustion

Take the three-route preparation of arbitrary length \(L\ge4\). Both ends of each route have single meetings, and each interior meeting is doubled. Only the three holders nearest the hub have two loose distinctions each. The far holder can have one additional loose distinction. Every first pair and the optional unary proposal can have a constant assignment on its old inputs. For example, give every doubled meeting opposite values and choose mixed values on the three single meetings incident to each end. The initial loose values are then free. The arity and next demand give the same first counts under parity and not-all-equal. At the first reached stage, alternating values on the six meetings of the three leaves and three shared pair births satisfy those six knots independently of \(T\). Choose mixed values at the ends. If there is an extra unary birth, a choice of its incoming value or a mixed fresh pair satisfies it for any of its prescribed fresh values. Thus the full loose projection is free, of size eight or thirty-two. The common ternary is funded with next demand zero. It requires a fresh distinction under not-all-equal, even in the arm that exhausts the loose set. [First-layer groups and demand](../ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/end_checks.md) · [When the not-all-equal implication test can close](../NAE-GROWTH-20261119-001/nae_growth.md)

Remove all doubled meetings to partition the holders into connected parts. For purposes of reading the relation of a part, retain both distinction names of each removed meeting wherever they occur in a written knot of that part. A doubled pair with at least one unpinned value can be completed with opposite values. Both its holders are then satisfied whatever their other values. A doubled pair with both values pinned already agrees in the two parts. It follows that an assignment to any marked set extends in the whole stage exactly when its restriction extends in each part. For the reverse direction, take an extension in each part and set every partly unpinned doubled pair to the same opposite-value completion in both parts. This preserves all satisfied constraints and makes the extensions agree on every repeated name.

Consequently a support is nonfree exactly when its intersection with some part's support is nonfree there. In these routes, two parts' supports intersect in at most a single doubled pair. Each such projection is full within each part. The near part allows a pinned doubled pair by using the alternating six-meeting cycle and mixed hub values. An intermediate knot uses its other pair to admit a fixed word on one pair. At the far part the single meeting from the holder incident to a pinned pair can be chosen to satisfy that holder. The other two single meetings can be mixed to satisfy the far holder. This also allows the extra unary birth. The actual bonds are therefore the union of the parts' primitive supports. A support drawn from one part cannot lose minimality to a smaller support in another part, since their common projection is free.

The near part and its immediately adjacent intermediate knots have identical supports in the two arms under the common naming. Every bond touching any \(T\) distinction lies in the near part. Every bond contributing to a pair or triple entry on names within the near part lies either there or in one of those adjacent intermediate parts. This follows because an outside part can use near names only through a shared doubled pair. Thus every finite bond ball of any proposal input lies in the near part, and the two arms give the same full decorated readings there, including which displayed distinctions are loose. The actual projected allowed relation on the near part also agrees. The observation supplies the displayed ball and its readings. Giving the complete loose roster would settle exhaustion directly.

The construction has the same lawful local relations for arbitrary \(L\ge4\). A nonconstant word fixed on a near leaf permits mixed values on the other two hub meetings and free completion of each pair birth. A nonconstant word at the hub permits the alternating cycle values. A nonconstant word at a pair birth permits mixed doubled pairs at its near parents. For an intermediate knot, its two adjacent parts each see only one doubled pair when its word is pinned. The preceding extension argument applies. A word fixed on a route's last knot fixes one single meeting into the far end. The other two single meetings can make that end nonconstant, and free doubled pairs satisfy the other route knots. A nonconstant word pinned at the far end extends with mixed doubled pairs and a mixed fresh pair at the optional unary. A word on that unary itself fixes only one value at the far end, which can use its other meetings to be nonconstant. The same choices certify source supports using mixed doubled pairs or one freely chosen leaf distinction. All these local relations are exactly not-all-equal, with full proper projections.

At \(L=4\) the finite balls saturate at 18 distinctions. All 153 pair and 816 triple entries agree at saturation, and the replay checks the intermediate radii individually. The complete support of each of the three parents lies in the near part. All 511 nonempty marked subsets of the union of their nine distinctions give the same bond-size minima, from singletons through all nine at once. The full two stages have 67 and 76 bonds. Giving a reader a walk of bounded length through successive pairs in a common bond still leaves a range bound in this construction. Each such step stays within one part, and adding fixed intermediate knots between the two ends moves the changed part arbitrarily far in that reading. The meet-once version remains open here. This construction uses doubled meetings.

## The continuing demand obstruction also holds under not-all-equal

Take the seven-knot demand example and write \(Q_U,Q_V\) for the complete supports of its two proposed parents. Each ordinary meeting has \(M\ge2\) parallel shared distinctions. The two arms move two loose distinctions from \(Z\) to \(W\), keeping the proposed pair at \(U,V\) and their complete supports fixed. [Construction and parity comparison](bond_radius.md#demand-changes-while-both-histories-keep-growing)

For a partial assignment, complete each ordinary meeting with an unpinned distinction to a mixed word. Either an already pinned value can be opposed, or two of the unpinned values can be made different. This satisfies both holders of each mixed meeting. At an unsatisfied knot all shared values are now pinned, because each incomplete meeting was made mixed. Choose an unpinned loose value to differ if one remains. The only obstruction is a completely pinned monochromatic written support. Thus a set of distinctions has a nonfull projection exactly when it contains an entire written support. The primitive not-all-equal bonds are the inclusion-minimal written supports.

Here every holder either has a private loose distinction or meets at least two distinct neighbors. In the second case no other holder has all its shared distinctions. The seven supports are therefore incomparable. Pinning any nonconstant word at a knot leaves no other support completely pinned, so it extends by the preceding argument. In particular, when a word at \(S\) pins a uniform meeting to \(U\) or \(V\), the affected leaf can use one of its own loose distinctions. When a word at a neighbor of the spent knot \(B\) pins one of its meetings, the other can be made mixed. Pinning a word at \(B\) leaves each of its neighbors another meeting. The same argument covers \(Z\) with either loose count and \(W\) with zero or two. This gives the exact projected not-all-equal relation and the knot test at every source support.

Every bond has size at least \(M+2\). For \(M>R+1\), the radius-\(R\) bond ball of each root is a singleton. The same holds at every distinction externally supplied in \(Q_U\cup Q_V\). The named sets of displayed parents, their loose counts, and their common witness restricted to them agree. There is a stronger exact comparison. The only bonds meeting \(Q_U\cup Q_V\) are the two parent supports and the support of \(S\), all fixed in both arms. Write \(B_U,B_V\) for the two shared bundles into \(S\). For any nonempty query \(X\subseteq Q_U\cup Q_V\), the complete deck is

| Query \(X\) | Least bond size \(b(X)\) in each arm |
|---|---:|
| Entirely within \(Q_U\) or entirely within \(Q_V\) | \(M+2\) |
| Touches both supports and lies within \(B_U\cup B_V\) | \(4M\) |
| Touches both supports and includes a loose distinction | \(\infty\) |

In particular the exact cross-root reading between the two proposed loose inputs is \(\infty\) in both arms. It remains uninformative even when the whole parent-support deck is supplied. Supplying all holders met by \(S\) together with their loose counts would be a stronger incidence observation.

The preparation with \(W\) spent gives a pair birth and two unary siblings, which create demand two at the proposed birth. With \(W\) live the three pair births make one unheld group, giving demand one at the same proposed birth. To see that not-all-equal implies neither birth, set all ordinary bundles mixed and assign the two proposed loose inputs equally. The fresh counts are two and one. Both first steps are funded. The \(Z\) part stays separate at the growing end, and the fresh counts fund later rounds, so both histories continue. In the shared-parent reading, radius one sees whether the \(U,V\) pair is part of that live triple and distinguishes the updates. The earlier radius-one sufficiency in the identified class and radius-two sufficiency on general lawful starts apply to funded not-all-equal steps. [Shared-parent radius proof](../ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/end_operator.md#where-the-finite-radius-rule-holds)

A literal twofold-meeting check with two retained loose distinctions at \(Z\) found exactly the seven written bonds in each arm. SAT verified the local relations and every immediate proper projection. All 255 nonempty subsets of \(Q_U\cup Q_V\) have equal readings across the arms. Of these, 30 have bond size four, nine have size eight, and 216 have no containing bond. The end replay gives demand and fresh counts \(2,2\) versus \(1,1\). This small check accompanies the proof for arbitrary \(M\).

## Replay and scope

The checker forms the literal allowed relation and forgets variables by union of their two possibilities. It descends through all supports of each factor, stopping at a full projection because each further forgetting is then full. It retains only nonfree nodes with full immediate proper projections. For the four-knot example the archived state enumerator checks every allowed row and all 66 or 105 pair entries under each constraint law. Enumerating connected cuts independently recovers the entire small parity bond families. A separate literal not-all-equal SAT check verifies every displayed primitive support on the complete arrangement and every maximal proper subset, including the stretched case. Recoding the distinction names and permuting the knot order preserves all displayed minima. The stronger all-length claim uses the doubled-meeting proof above. The two built comparisons have no prevalence weighting.
