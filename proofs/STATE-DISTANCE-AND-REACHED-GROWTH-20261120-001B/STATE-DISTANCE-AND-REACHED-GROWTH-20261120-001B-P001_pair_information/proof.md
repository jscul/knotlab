# Four births forced by the two readings of the 28 names

Supply a positive ordinary reached cut of a finite unsigned not-all-equal presentation. Each name has at most two holders. Each initial knot has at least three distinct places and its full projected law. A funded step consumes every old loose name, takes one from each distinct parent of a child and gives each new child at least one private fresh name. Write C(x,y) for one minus twice the pair disagreement rate. The reading is either uniform on the current words or starts uniform and divides the weight of each word equally among its extensions at each genuine step. Both have full support. On an earlier cut, a still-loose name is either an original with uniform starting words or fresh in its latest step. In either weighting, changing just such a name between two permitted values with everything else fixed has equal weight. **A loose name identifies its own holder.** Let u be loose in a current newborn H and let M be the event that H without u is constant. If it is mixed, the two values of u have equal conditional weights. If it is constant with sign s, u must be −s. Thus C(u,v)=−Pr(M) for every other v in H. This is the minimum of the row. Every exterior z has strictly greater correlation. Indeed pin H without u to zero, u to one and z to one. Every other holder meets H at most once, and a holder with two pinned values already sees both. Each holder still needing satisfaction has at least two unpinned names. The pinning theorem extends this word. It has positive weight and gives strict inequality for z. This calculation also works for an arbitrary loose holder for the containment direction even if several names meet another holder. It identifies H when u is known to be loose. The identity by itself does not identify which names are loose.

**The candidate loose names in this table.** No triple has disagreement rates adding to two, so no original or later holder can have three places. For a loose u with k other minimum names, their probability of being constant must equal −min C(u,·). For two names this probability is (1+C)/2. For three it is (1+the sum of their three correlations)/4. For k>3 any proper triple of these other names has a strictly greater constant probability, since the holder realizes its full local relation. Apply these tests and the row identity. With names as in the archived presentation, only 16 through 27 can be loose under either reading. Their six candidate supports are S01=(4,7,16,17), S02=(5,10,18,19), S03=(6,13,20,21), S12=(8,11,22,23), S13=(9,14,24,25) and S23=(12,15,26,27). In each such support the last two entries have interchangeable readings on every other name. A competing positive-age cut must contain at least one such current support. Its actual current supports are mutually disjoint.

**Each permitted current support has both candidate names loose.** Under uniform weights suppose f is fresh, x is the other candidate and has an older holder K. Exchanging f and x preserves H. Among the words for which the exchanged word is also allowed, opposite contributions to C(x,z)−C(f,z) cancel for z distinct from them. Every remaining word has K without x constant at sign s, x=−s and f=s. Such a word exists by full projection of K, after freely setting f opposite x. Thus for every z in K without x this difference is strictly negative. This contradicts the two identical rows. More generally, when x is an actual *input* to H, the most negative values of the difference must contain all other names in its old holder K. The mate need not have a different response at every exterior mark for this containment.

In the equal-extension reading the old marginal is exactly its preceding preparation. If only one twin f were loose, its twin x and both other inputs a,b would have been old loose names. Removing every actually new position from the observed table leaves those old pairs. The minimum of x's preserved row includes a,b and no old positions outside them. Every other mark of its previous holder must lie among those minima, by one-name cancellation in the preceding marginal. This would require a physical holder of at most three names, already excluded. Thus both twins are loose under either weighting. Every possible Sij is a two-parent birth with two fresh names.

**Recovering the former parents.** Under uniform weights the strict negative minimum of the swap difference for each of the twelve ordinary inputs selects three names. For instance with x=4 and f=16 it selects 0,5,6. In the alternative weighting remove the fresh names of the selected supports. For an input x, the three minimum entries in its old row persist under every such deletion; they are old under every possibility. Other entries outside its own current support are strictly greater. The earlier loose-row containment now gives the same three names. As every holder has at least four names, these inclusions fix the old parent holding when it participates. They are K0=(0,4,5,6), K1=(1,7,8,9), K2=(2,10,11,12), K3=(3,13,14,15); Sij has exactly Ki and Kj as parents. **Excluding the remaining rival presentations.** Enumerate the 63 nonempty subsets of the six proposed two-parent births. At the current cut two such births are witnessed together just when they share a former parent. Every selected birth has two fresh marks even though its input count and arity floor would require only one. Its ordinary lookahead must therefore count exactly two next multi-parent groups through it. This leaves four possible types: three pairs through one common parent, four pairs in one cycle, five pairs, or all six. If an old parent participates in three pairs, it has used three loose marks. Its fourth mark is its only possible meeting with a witness. The same witness must therefore meet its three neighbors as well. These neighbors must themselves be covered pairwise by births. This excludes the three-pair and five-pair alternatives.

For the four-pair cycle, the preceding stage has 20 names, eight loose. Counting once per holder gives 32 occupied places. The four Ki take 16. Each of the four adjacent pairs requires its own additional witness, since a witness of three Ki would force another pair to be covered. Exactly 16 places remain, so these four witnesses have four places each and exhaust all other holders. Each meets the two Ki on its named pair. Four remaining names belong to two witnesses each and fill exactly two remaining places in each witness. They either make one four-cycle among witnesses or double each of two disjoint meetings. On four named witnesses there are three choices of each kind. Assigning names differently within these incidences leaves the number of words invariant.

In the uniform reading the least common multiple of the disagreement-rate denominators is **15,488,560**. Every competing word count must be divisible by it. The six rival totals, with duplicates removed, are **53,502,474, 53,913,902, 55,707,884 and 56,329,350**. None is divisible by 15,488,560. In the equal-extension reading a putative twenty-name preceding stage cannot itself have been reached. Removing the four Ki would leave just the four witnesses with a four-cycle or two double meetings. Their ordinary witnessed pairs number two or zero respectively, so they cannot give the four required distinct two-parent Ki. The twenty-name stage would have to be the uniformly prepared source. In the observed marginal on its putative old names the least common denominator is **3,087**. Its six possible source totals, with duplicates removed, are 356,098, 358,842, 370,890 and 375,034. None is divisible by 3,087. Thus the four-pair alternative is excluded under either reading. The calculation enumerates monochromatic-holder subsets exactly. Its inherited calculation independently averages each of 33,614 equally weighted preceding words. All six Sij must occur. Each former Ki then has just one shared name remaining. The six pair meetings force all four Ki to meet one and the same witness, holding exactly 0,1,2,3. Every physical holding is now determined. The next four groups are {S01,S02,S12}, {S01,S03,S13}, {S02,S03,S23} and {S12,S13,S23}. Each receives one fresh distinction. The proof admits arbitrary positive ages and competitors for each of the two readings within the stated ordinary model. Assignments to marks and old state weights were withheld from the decoder.
