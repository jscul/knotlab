# Paid reuse of a conditional affine conclusion on the five-product path

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

Work over the two-element field. The raw observations are six input bits and three displayed bits \((x_0,\ldots,x_5,s,u,v)\). Let \(Q=\sum_{i=0}^4 x_i x_{i+1}\). The rich evaluator accepts exactly \(s=Q, u=x_0x_1, v=x_2(x_1+x_3)\). The plain evaluator requires \(s=1+Q\) and leaves both summaries free. A binary affine question, a check of one whole evaluator under the current affine assumptions, and each citation of an earlier derived statement cost one unit each. The cost includes the derivation of the stored statement. Each ordinary structural child is listed separately and carries no citation charge. These units price a supplied proof on the given evaluator tables. The search for a proof and the individual bit operations inside Gaussian elimination are separate costs.

The ordinary tree minimum is 23 units. **The exact minimum remains 23 when one named implication with a single affine equation as its premise and a single affine equation as its conclusion is available**, with arbitrary adaptive visible parity questions and paid citations. Training \(x_0=0\Rightarrow x_2=0\) costs five. Its checked main proof costs 18, including one live citation, and attains 23. No proof in that format improves on the direct tree. For the previously shared literal \(v=0\Rightarrow x_0=0\), **every proof that trains this single named literal takes at least 25 units**, even with arbitrary mixed visible parity questions. An explicit 26-unit proof cites it once, leaving its optimal trained cost at 25 or 26. With questions restricted to the nine displayed coordinates, its exact optimum is 32 with two or more genuinely live citations. It is 33 when their common ancestor still allows \(v=1\). One such 33-unit proof uses a paid rich check to establish the guard on one branch and an explicit question about \(v\) on the other. [One-implication theorem](one_premise.md) · [Named-literal sector proof](sector_proof.md) · [Saved checked proofs](certificates.json)

Across all 288 independently signed coordinate implications, the minimum under coordinate questions is 23 for one live citation and **30 for genuine repeated use**. Requiring the common ancestor to permit a violation of the learned guard still gives exactly 30. The two citations at the minimum each remove points that survive both evaluators' full affine propagation, and both continuations still require further work. A coordinate tree already achieves 23. These minima for repeated use apply to the stated single-literal family and question menu. With multiple conditional scalar facts or premises containing several equations, the proved interval for optional learning is 21–23. A faster proof in that broader model would have to cost 21 or 22. ## Why the direct tree costs exactly 23

In the three groups \((y_i,w_i,p_i)\), use \(y=(x_0,x_2,x_4)\), \(w=(x_1,x_1+x_3,x_3+x_5)\) and \(p=(u,v,s+u+v)\). This linear recoding is invertible. In one group the good rows satisfy \(p_i=y_iw_i\). Its four good rows are affinely independent, and its four bad rows are their translate by the vector that changes only \(p_i\). Rich requires all groups good. Plain requires an odd number bad. With any positive number of groups, each separate evaluator spans the unrestricted root. For plain one may require just a chosen group bad and all others good to see this. A source call at the root therefore makes no progress.

In one group, every nonconstant question divides both the good and the bad rows, so each answer needs at least two further operations. With just one charge one could only close by an empty source check, impossible there. The lower is five. With more groups let the first nonconstant question involve group \(j\). Choose two good representatives of \(j\) giving opposite values for its local summand. Into **each** answer embed the entire smaller instance on the other groups, choosing one of those two fixed rows as the required bit dictates. The choice is an affine function of the other coordinates because it interpolates between just two points using the remaining affine part of the question. The inserted group is always good, so this affine injection preserves both sources. Any constant question in a larger ordinary tree disappears on pullback, and a source check on the smaller surviving rows maps into the larger checked hull. Thus \(T_m\ge1+2T_{m-1}\) for \(m\ge2\). This gives 5, 11, 23. In the displayed problem, asking all three coordinates of \(y\) makes the total affine on each of eight leaves. One rich check and one plain check then close each leaf, at cost \(7+16=23\). This derivation requires no restriction on which mixed visible questions the ordinary tree can ask.

## What is checked at a citation

A raw guard \(C\) is an affine set in the nine displayed coordinates. A check with evaluator \(D\) costs one and replaces \(C\) by \(\operatorname{aff}(C\cap D)\), closing if the set is empty. Gaussian consequences of current affine assumptions are free. A binary question splits the current guard into its two affine answers. To declare \(G\Rightarrow L\), give a complete ordinary refutation at \(G\cap\neg L\). Discharge retypes that refutation. One use under \(C\) requires raw containment \(C\subseteq G\), costs one citation, and continues at \(C\cap L\). This is sound since every joint solution surviving \(C\) satisfies the derived literal.

Let \(A(C)\) be the greatest fixed point of repeatedly taking both separate local hulls inside \(C\). A **live citation** qualifies for the census when \(A(C)\setminus L\ne\varnothing\) and \(A(C\cap L)\ne\varnothing\). The fixed points qualify a candidate citation offline. The execution still pays every check required to establish its assumptions. This definition ensures that immediate propagation cannot just replace the learned fact and that the fact leaves its consumer with a real continuation.

For a simple misuse, \(x_2=0\) leaves some raw states with \(v=1\). The plain state with all six inputs zero, \(s=1,u=0,v=1\), is an explicit example. The rich check at \(x_2=0\) forces \(v=0\) and costs one. Removing that check makes the saved citation invalid. The negative controls also reject a circular citation, an unproved discharge, a forbidden coordinate and an unused declaration. The matching-charge control has 64 full solutions. ## Hoisting and the two incomparable consumers

For one shared lemma with \(k\) consumers, suppose their least common ancestor already entails \(G\). At that ancestor ask once whether \(L\) holds. Under the opposite answer specialize the stored derivation to the stronger assumptions. Under the positive answer specialize the old consumer subtree and erase every call to the lemma. Restriction cannot increase cost. It can suppress constant questions, replace a paid check by the same check on the smaller guard, drop a check whose hull now equals the current guard, or close earlier if the tested evaluator is empty. Both sides have surviving points at a live citation before and after the cut, and the construction also works when specialization removes entire continuations. The new ordinary tree costs at most the old DAG cost minus \(k-1\). This comparison assumes that the remainder of the proof is an ordinary tree and that these are all citations of the one stored statement.

The same one-unit replacement applies locally if an evaluator already entails the learned conclusion at a consumer. Replace that citation by the evaluator's own check and specialize its continuation to the possibly stronger output. If this applies at every consumer, the banked proof can also be deleted. The live-citation test excludes this redundancy.

For \(G=(v=0)\), the original two consumers follow the split on \(x_2\). Their joint fixed points have sizes 128 and 64. After applying \(x_0=0\), the remaining fixed points have sizes 32 and 16. Their shared proof costs 32. Directly materializing and specializing its tree expansion costs 35, while the generic common-ancestor rewrite costs 31. Reoptimizing the positive and outside branches after moving the use to \(v=0\) costs 27. At this point asking \(x_0\) once costs exactly the same 27. Choosing the unrestricted first question of the ordinary optimum costs 23.

In the 33-unit proof the first question is instead \(x_2\). At \(x_2=0\), a rich check establishes \(v=0\). In the \(x_2=1,x_1=1\) branch, querying \(v\) reaches the other citation. Their common ancestor is the full 512-point space. Its ordinary fixed point contains 256 states with \(v=1\). The two consumers have fixed sizes 128 and 16, which become 32 and 8. Thus there is actual sharing across independently reached guards even after charging for the rich check. The following table prices these programs and ordinary competitors.

The main program first asks \(x_3\), then \(x_1\) independently on each answer. The two \(x_1=0\) branches cite the fact. Each consultation takes a 32-point fixed set to a 16-point fixed set. Reordering the first two questions brings both uses under one premise. The saved ordinary proof after reordering and reoptimizing costs 23. Thus a common ancestor's failure to establish the fact in one proof does not by itself establish that the order of its questions is optimal.

For the old 32- and 33-unit proofs this gives 39 and 40, respectively. The table uses the stronger comparison that actually specializes every pasted derivation to the consuming guard. As a sensitivity check, charging one additional bookkeeping unit per declaration adds one to every one-lemma cost and zero to an ordinary tree. ## Exhausting the coordinate model

Choose an ordered pair of distinct displayed coordinates and one sign for each, giving \(9\cdot8\cdot4=288\) candidate implications. The body is a cheapest ordinary tree under its forbidden affine cell. In the main tree either side can be checked before, between or after questions. The raw guard is retained even if all currently counted evaluator traces happen to satisfy an additional equation. This is necessary when validating a citation under its raw premise.

For ordinary cost, an absent evaluator closes with one call. Otherwise minimize over a proper one-side hull reduction (one plus the child cost) and a coordinate question giving two proper raw restrictions (one plus both child costs). A same-guard check contributes no new available condition. A constant question can be omitted. Every retained successor has strictly fewer raw states, so this recursion is exhaustive. The 33,715 reachable raw guards include the body of each of the 288 candidates.

For a chosen lemma tabulate four minimum costs at each guard, counting its body separately once. The categories are zero citations, one citation, at least two with their common ancestor inside the premise, and at least two with their common ancestor outside the premise. A legal live citation gives one plus the ordinary cost on the positive restriction. Another citation cannot occur below it since the literal then holds throughout the remaining guard. A source step inherits its child's category. A query sums its children's costs and adds one. If both children have uses, the query node itself is their common ancestor. If only one contains them, its category survives. An already outside common ancestor remains outside on passing to a larger guard. When the fixed point contains no point of \(G\cap L\), or none of \(G\cap\neg L\), no descendant can make a live use, by monotonicity of the greatest fixed point. These cases ensure both completeness and correct early termination.

## When deleting a cheap forbidden sector looks promising

Two candidate forbidden cells are \(F_b=(x_1=0,b=0)\) with \(b=u+x_2\) or \(b=u+x_2+x_4\). For each, the forbidden part costs five to refute. On the input tables with all of \(F_b\) deleted in advance there is a 13-unit ordinary proof using just the questions \(x_2\) then \(x_4\). Both saved 13-unit proofs fail when replayed on the actual source rows. The issue is where a branch can legally remove that sector.

There are three possible affine premise hyperplanes containing a fixed codimension-two cell. In the menu of the nine coordinates and four additional masks 132, 134, 148 and 150, both candidates have identical prices. For the orientation \(x_1=0\Rightarrow b=1\), one use costs 23 and repeated use costs exactly 30, whether the common ancestor has established the premise or still allows \(x_1=1\). For either alternative orientation the corresponding prices are 29 and 32. All six orientations have saved, fully checked proofs with genuinely live uses in the two different root branches. [Focused proofs](focused_certificates.json) · [Mixed-menu search and local checks](focused_cases.py)

A concrete comparison isolates one extra inference. Take either raw context \((x_0=0,u+x_1=0)\) or \((x_0=1,u=0)\). Both allow \(x_1=1\). One rich check in each sets \(x_1=u=0\), giving a 64-point guard where the citation is legal. For the second candidate, citing \(x_2+x_4=1\) leaves exactly the same 32 points as computing the rich hull with the forbidden sector deleted globally. For the first candidate, citing \(x_2=1\) also leaves 32 points, but the globally filtered hull has only 16. It additionally satisfies \(v=x_1+x_3\). One further rich check enforces the missing equation. The plain check alone leaves all 32. Both after-citation continuations remain live.

An independent bound tests where two **live** citations could fit into a hypothetical 22-unit proof for a five-unit training sector. Each positive continuation costs at least five. At its nonempty common fixed point both sources span the current cell, and each useful question leaves both source types in both answers. Two live citations are incomparable because the conclusion remains true below a citation. Three cost at least \(5+3(1+5)+2=25\) including training and two separating queries. With two, both cannot lie in one answer to the root question, since its other ordinary answer contains a two-group instance of cost at least 11. After allocating 5 for training, 2 for citations, 10 for their live continuations and 1 for the root question, **only four units remain across the two root answers**. Unless the question is the premise, which rules out a use in one answer, each side needs at least one unit to establish its guard. Thus each has at most three.

The bounded check gives away the positive continuation for its five-unit lower bound. A path can take a source check or ask a question. In at most three remaining units, if a question immediately makes the premise true on its kept half, that half must be the intersection with the premise because the two proper affine hyperplanes of the current cell have the same dimension. Its sibling must close in one or two checks. If the kept half still permits a premise violation, it needs another paid step and its sibling must close in exactly one check. The checker tests every visible parity in this case. Once the premise is reached, either an immediate live citation is available in the relaxation or no smaller cell can support one because common fixed points are monotone. Checking 511 root questions for each of twelve orientations of the four lowest sector candidates leaves zero 22-unit proof skeletons. This is a separate test in the all-live single-literal format. ## Bounds beyond coordinate questions

For one affine equation as guard and one as conclusion, a stronger lower bound handles every choice of the stored statement. A proper independent pair of equations determines a forbidden affine cell of codimension two with three possible guard hyperplanes. The group permuting the four affinely independent good states in each product triple and the three triples preserves both evaluators. It partitions the 173,740 possible cells into 126 orbits. For each of 378 representative guard orientations, compute the ordinary cost of training. For each of 511 possible first questions in the main tree, embed the two untouched triples into either answer as in the ordinary lower. The inherited fact is available on that smaller problem without training, but each citation still costs one and must establish the pulled-back guard. An exact six-coordinate calculation prices all smaller continuations, including checks that strengthen only the raw geometry needed for a citation. Taking the best lower across all possible root questions gives at least 23 for every orientation and at least 24 for 357 of the 378. The old literal specifically requires seven units for training and at least 18 for its main proof, giving the sharper individual lower bound of 25. The original-coordinate replay checks both source memberships pointwise on 589,824 image points. If the forbidden set is a hyperplane, splitting on it directly inlines the training and removes the original citations under its complement. An empty forbidden set gives no new consequence, and whole-space training already costs 23. Together with the 23-unit tree this proves the optional-one-implication exact minimum. For the single named literal, let \(F=G\cap\neg L\). The stored derivation must solve the ordinary problem on \(F\). After removing \(F\) from both source tables, simulate the main proof on the reduced tables. Every surviving row at a legal citation already satisfies \(L\). All citations can therefore be removed, along with branches that contain no source rows. This lower-bounds total work by the two sector costs plus the number of citations. For the old literal the forbidden sector costs exactly 7 and the complementary sector costs at least 15, with all mixed affine questions admitted. The resulting lower bound of 24 for two uses applies even when their guards are reached using intermediate mixed queries or rich-side checks. There is also a lower bound for any finite acyclic list of guarded affine literals in which each ordinary proof is a tree and sharing occurs by a paid citation. An effective plain check excludes every honest rich row in its guard and at most 12 of the 64 inputs. Thus at least six such checks are necessary. Looking at the 192 plain rows violating exactly one of the three individual product equations shows that at least five rich-side checks are also necessary. Removing named citation references gives an ordinary forest with one component per stored proof plus the main program. The six effective plain checks are an antichain within its components. A binary forest count then gives at least six paid questions and citations together when a statement is actually shared. Two finite covering obstructions sharpen this bound. After every effective nonempty plain check, the remaining guard contains only plain rows, so its continuation may close with one rich check. Counting how many of the 192 single-error rows those mandatory checks can exclude forces at least 21 total units. With six effective plain calls at least nine rich calls are required. With seven effective plain calls at least seven rich calls are required. Eight or more already suffice for the lower bound using the five-rich-source minimum. Thus the optimum with any finite collection of guarded scalar facts lies between **21 and 23**. Values 21 and 22 remain open. The 23-unit upper bound uses the direct tree.
