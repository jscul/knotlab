# A fixed numerical bond radius does not determine the update

**Proved for the stated readers, referee pending.** A funded proposal on a lawful identified source can have a different fresh count when every bounded bond ball about all its inputs has the same reading. This holds even with the parents' complete stars and every exact common-bond reading on their union supplied. The witness changes demand, and both histories continue. A separate reached family makes the same bounded reader miss whether a common birth closes. [Frozen readers](preregistration.md)

## First identify what an exact exhaustion query supplies

For the parity argument wire each loose distinction to the bookkeeping exterior $\Omega$. An ordinary connected presentation has one bond consisting of exactly all its loose distinctions. Every other bond containing a subset of loose distinctions crosses an ordinary meeting as well. Therefore for a set of $p$ proposed loose inputs its least containing bond has size exactly $p$ if and only if those inputs exhaust the loose set. This is an exact query on the given proposal. It can consult the entire presentation.

The ternary threshold has a separate reason. A genuine unheld multipart proposal cannot cross a bridge of the ordinary graph. For a bridge $ab$, a pair apart on its two sides can have a common holder only if one is a bridge endpoint and the other meets the opposite endpoint. The two endpoints themselves have no common holder. If the proposed parents occupy both sides, the chosen endpoints cannot both be in the set. On the side omitting its endpoint all the parents must therefore meet that endpoint, while the other side can contain only the opposite endpoint. The whole proposal is held after all. A bond of size three through two proposed loose inputs either crosses only loose distinctions or spends its one remaining distinction on such a bridge. In the latter case it must also contain the third input on the same side, which exceeds the budget. Thus on a genuine ternary, one chosen pair with bond size three already certifies exhaustion. This affirmative exact test leaves open how far a fixed numerical bond ball reaches. [Whole-end update and its closure rule](../ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/end_operator.md)

## Demand changes while both histories keep growing

Take seven ordinary knots $U,V,S,W,A,B,Z$. The ordinary meetings are $US,VS,WS,SA,AB,BW,AZ$. Replace every meeting by $M$ distinct parallel shared distinctions. Give $U$ and $V$ two loose distinctions each. Give $Z$ $b+2$ loose distinctions and $W$ none in one arm. In the other, move two of those same loose distinctions from $Z$ to $W$. Every other holder is exhausted. Both arms have $T=b+6$ loose distinctions, where $b\ge2$. For any prescribed reader radius $R$, choose integers with $T-1>R$ and $M>T$. All physical names, widths and number of knots can stay fixed across the two sources. The complete stars at $U,V$ are literally identical.

Both ordinary presentations are connected. After deleting $S$, the two root leaves still reach the wired exterior through their opens, and $W$ reaches $Z$ by $W,B,A,Z$. Deleting $A$ leaves the old $Z$ component attached to the exterior by its own loose distinctions. Deleting $B$ leaves $W$ attached at $S$. Deleting $W$ leaves $B$ attached at $A$. After deleting any of $U,V,Z$, all remaining knots reach the exterior through a remaining live holder. Therefore the boundary of each singleton ordinary knot is a bond. All arities are at least three. The full projected parity relation on each star passes the knot gate. These starts also meet the initial identification conditions. Their source proposals are pairs, every live pair has at most one common holder, and all pairs are funded.

In the first source, $S$ witnesses the held pair $\{U,V\}$. It forms the common birth $P$. The second slot at each parent makes a unary child, and those two children do not share a parent with each other. The next demand of $P$ is two, and it receives two fresh distinctions. With $W$ live, $S$ witnesses the held set $\{U,V,W\}$. The three pairs make three children, with the common $P$ one of them. The children have different common-parent witnesses for their three meetings and no parent shared by all. They make one ternary next proposal, so $P$ receives one fresh distinction. The first steps are funded even at $W$. The $Z$ branch is an independent live end component in both histories. On any funded step each nonempty end component has at least one child, and no child can cross components. Two components exclude a sole global closing birth, so every child gets enough opens for the next full step. Both histories keep growing. The difference **two versus one** survives both a funding and a nonclosing promise. It does not depend on the global closure exception or on a remote stall.

Every cut that separates ordinary knots crosses at least one full $M$ bundle. The only nonempty cut with no ordinary meeting across it consists of all $T$ loose distinctions. The least bond size is consequently $T$. Every distinct pair bond distance exceeds $R$ throughout each of the two measured sources. The ordinary naming correspondence preserves the **whole table truncated at radius $R$**. For one marked loose input the ball is a singleton. Giving all specified inputs yields only those seeds. If the observer is supplied the complete $U,V$ stars and a ball of radius $R$ at every displayed distinction, nothing further enters. It sees all the shown holders, the shared witness $S$, and the same loose-count and witness information among the displayed parents. The status of the other possible live holder $W$ remains outside this reading. Supplying all $S$'s neighboring holders and their opens by an independent ordinary-step read would supply stronger data.

### Exact distances among externally supplied seeds

The result also survives supplying every exact bond minimum on any subset of the two complete parent stars. Denote the two shared bundles by $B_U,B_V$ and the two loose sets by $L_U,L_V$. Either touch a class with at least one mark or omit it. Physical distinctions within one class always cross cuts together. The deck is the same in both arms:

| Marked shared classes | Marked loose classes | Least bond size |
|---|---|---:|
| None | Any nonempty choice | $T$ |
| $B_U$ only | None, or $L_U$ only | $M+2$ |
| $B_U$ only | $L_V$ only | $M+T-2$ |
| $B_U$ only | Both | No bond |
| Both bundles | None | $2M+T-4$ |
| Both bundles | At least one | No bond |

The rows with $B_V$ alone exchange $U$ and $V$. The one-bundle minima isolate $U$ or the remaining ordinary knots, as required by the specified loose side. If a cut spends another bundle its cost is larger, since $M>T$. A connected shore away from the exterior containing both leaves has to contain $S$. This precludes crossing either ordinary leaf bundle while including both loose classes in the away shore. When both bundles cross, the two leaves cannot form a connected away shore without $S$. Taking the entire remaining ordinary part away from the exterior attains the stated two-bundle minimum. These connected shores certify all finite upper bounds and the leaf constraints give the claimed absences.

Thus even the exact distant distances *between the supplied seeds* cannot restore the next demand. The exact global table on unnamed remote distinctions is a different information supply. Nothing in the construction bounds a physical propagation time. It concerns the information needed to compute a single formation layer.

## Closure alone at a reached growing end

For any integer $k\ge3$, attach $k$ ordinary leaves to an exhausted common hub $S$, once each. Each leaf has $k-1$ loose distinctions. In the other source give $S$ one more loose distinction. All old stars are lawful, since deleting any holder leaves the other vertices attached to the wired exterior. The source candidates among the leaves have the single common witness $S$, so they make all the pairs. The extra source has one isolated live $S$ in its end, supplying a separate unary with two openings. Its continuing descendants never acquire a parent in the leaf component.

The core births are exactly the nonempty subsets of leaves. This can also be seen inductively. At subset size $i\ge2$, two current subsets can join if they share an $(i-1)$ subset. In a pairwise adjacent family, two such subsets either share the fixed $(i-1)$ subset common to the family or lie in the same $(i+1)$ union. To see the dichotomy, write two members as $U\cup\{a\}$ and $U\cup\{b\}$. A third adjacent member omitting a point of $U$ has to contain both $a,b$. Once such a third is present a remaining member extending outside the union cannot meet it in $i-1$ places while also meeting the original two. Consequently the retained unheld groups are exactly the families of $i$-subsets of an $(i+1)$-subset. Every candidate pair belongs to such a group. Each current subset gets $k-i$ loose distinctions until the potentially implied final birth. The initial $i=1$ held case gives the same recurrence by its residual pairs. This gives the exponent-size count directly and agrees with the earlier subset growth. [Fewest fresh distinctions and subset growth](../FRESH-COUNT-20261118-001/fresh_count.md)

At age $k-2$ the common core comprises one ordinary knot for each proper subset, including $S$ as the empty subset. Wiring the current loose inputs to $\Omega$ makes the core the classical $k$-dimensional cube, with $\Omega$ as the full set. Every ordinary cube meeting is an actual physical distinction, and the $k$ inputs of the prospective top are exactly the core distinctions incident to $\Omega$. The other branch, when present, gives a separate route from $S$ to $\Omega$ outside the core. Thus in both arms every cut crossing any core distinction crosses at least $k$ core distinctions. Splitting the cube along one coordinate proves the lower bound by induction. If the cut partially divides both faces it crosses at least $2(k-1)$ meetings. If it partly divides only one it crosses $k-1$ within and at least one between faces. If its faces are whole and opposite, it crosses $2^{k-1}$. For $k\ge3$ equality forces a singleton ordinary cube vertex or its complement. In the one-partial-face case equality requires just one vertex colored differently from the entire other face.

Choose $k-1>R$. Every bounded ball at a proposed core input contains that input alone, even in the presence of the extra branch. Giving all $k$ inputs, and even all their parent's current stars, makes no other name visible by any further radius-$R$ query from those displayed distinctions. The ordinary typed incidence at their stars and their complete witnessed end component match exactly. Under parity the top has zero fresh distinctions in the base source and one with the extra branch. Both decisions are funded. The closing predicate can therefore fail to be local even for a strictly positive-age reached end and even when the proposed inputs are all supplied.

Here the **untruncated** distance between two displayed input marks does suffice. Their least bond size is $k$ without the branch and $k+1$ with it. A $k$-cut through two marks incident to $\Omega$ must be its star in the cube, which separates $S$ from $\Omega$. The separate path between those vertices costs at least one further crossing. For the upper bound take all ordinary core knots away from $\Omega$ and the whole added branch with $\Omega$. Both shores are connected and only the branch's initial meeting at $S$ crosses in addition to the $k$ top inputs. The same sizes are the least common bond through all $k$ inputs. Thus this particular closure witness does not survive adding exact cross-seed distances. That additional information is precisely what the seven-knot demand witness cannot use to distinguish its two arms.

## Even the complete global bond table can omit who holds the opens

Without the actual loose labels and witnessed incidence, a complete numerical table can miss another part of the update. Use wired terminals $S,\Omega$ and four live leaves $U,V,W,W'$, each meeting $S$. The leaves $U,V$ have three loose distinctions each. Keep fixed the seven physical distinctions at each of $W,W'$. At $W$ attach four of them to $S$ and three to $\Omega$. At $W'$ attach three to $S$ and four to $\Omega$. In the second source switch which terminal every distinction at these two leaves touches. Add in both arms a dormant path $S,A,Z$ with two parallel distinctions on each of its two meetings and two loose distinctions at $Z$. The separate live component at $Z$ again prevents closure and preserves continuation. Every source star is a bond by the same wired deletion test. The two compared parent supports at $U,W$ and all physical labels stay fixed. Their open counts differ at $W$.

The sets of crossing labels of **all cuts** are identical. If $S$ and $\Omega$ have the same side, keep the old coloring. If they have opposite sides, complement just $W$ and $W'$ in the switched source. This is a bijective correspondence of cuts and preserves each crossing label. Inclusion-minimal nonzero crossing supports and therefore all exact bond decks agree, even with global rather than bounded access. Yet the pair $\{U,W\}$ receives two fresh distinctions in the first arm and three in the second. The held four-leaf witness supplies demand $3+h(W)-4$. Both arms have all pairs funded. This optional table-only comparison does not grant the original set of loose labels at $W$. The fixed-input enriched comparison is the separate seven-knot result above.

An individual funding shortage is detectable from its local witnessed end roster. Availability of the complete round asks whether any holder has such a shortage. The seven-knot example passes that global test in both arms, and the closure example also compares two available plans. Neither relies on mistaking a cancellation for a local result.
