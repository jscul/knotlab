# How many present marks expose the later split?

In the two-anchor example below, three or fewer present distinctions have identical readings under the specified renaming. Four present distinctions have readings 158 and infinity. At the following complete round an old pair has readings 67 and 92. These numbers are minimum bond cardinalities. Subtract one from each finite pair reading to obtain the reported pair distance.

## A two-anchor certificate

Use the finite wired graph of the parity equations. The outside is $O$. The hubs are $R,B$. The two ordinary anchors are $v,w$. There are four special middle vertices $a,b,c,d$ and $s$ shield middles of each color. The special vertices $a,b$ meet $R$ and $c,d$ meet $B$. Each special middle has $L$ distinctions to its hub, $L$ to $v$, and one to $w$. Each shield middle has $M$ distinctions to its hub and one to each anchor. It also has its own growing branch to the common outside. Write $N=2s+4$ for the number of middles. The map interchanges $a,c$, carrying each incident distinction by its type and copy, and fixes all other names.

For this example $s=11,L=17,M=9$. The current stage is reached in one complete round. The next round changes each branch's smallest separating cut from $M$ to one.

**A two-round branch for every $M\ge3$.** At each shield root introduce $A,c$ and $n=M-1$ ordinary sites $b_1,\ldots,b_n$. Join the shield to $A$ and every $b_i$. Join $c$ to every $b_i$ and connect consecutive $b_i$ along a path. Give $A$ exactly $n$ loose distinctions, each $b_i$ two, and $c$ one. These are the only loose distinctions. Each branch adds $M+1$ ordinary starting sites. The shield root and the other core sites are exhausted.

Among the live sites of a branch, all pairs except $A,c$ have a common neighbor. The shield witnesses every pair from $A,b_1,\ldots,b_n$. Each $b_i$ has a path neighbor $b_j$ who also meets $c$. The only neighbor of $A$ is the shield, which does not meet $c$. The maximal candidate sets are thus $A$ with all the $b_i$ and $c$ with all the $b_i$. Only the former has a common neighbor. Its pairs $A,b_i$ remain uncovered by the growing set at $c$. Accordingly one round makes $P_i$ on each pair $A,b_i$, and $P_M$ on $c$ with all $b_i$. These consume all loose distinctions exactly once.

At the reached stage every $P_i,P_j$ with $i,j<M$ meets $A$, and $P_i,P_M$ meet $b_i$. No ordinary junction meets all $M$ new sites. The next round joins all of them. The fewest count is one at each new site in both rounds. Each branch has its own parents in a connected start with several branches, so no new site takes all the global loose distinctions. No candidate pair crosses branches. Every name at every live junction is fixed. [Complete round rule](../EXTEND-ROUND-20261119-001/extend_round.md)

At age one, route one path from the shield to the outside through $A,P_1$, one through $b_1,P_M$, and one through $b_i,P_i$ for each $2\le i\le n$. They share no distinction and end at the $M$ different loose names. Cutting those names provides the matching upper bound. At age two a single new loose name gives separating cost one.

All ordinary stars are genuine bonds. Removing a core anchor or hub leaves each middle linked to the remaining core. Removing a shield root leaves its branch connected to the outside. Within a branch, $A$ and all $b_i$ have direct links to their root, $c$ has at least two neighbors among the $b_i$, and every first-round site has at least two parents. Removing another ordinary site consequently leaves a downward connection to the root, or an upward connection to the outside when the root itself was removed. Starting loose names and the first- or second-round loose names join the other branches through the common outside.

Call the weighted link from a shield middle to $O$ its virtual link. A core bond crossing such a link lifts by a minimum separating cut of the branch, whose two sides connect to their respective terminals. Conversely, suppose a bond meets a branch and also crosses a distinction outside its interior. Both shores then have a vertex outside that interior. Every nonempty shore portion inside the branch must reach such a vertex through a terminal on its own shore. The two terminals are therefore on opposite shores, and every branch vertex connects within its shore to that shore's terminal. Projecting the crossed branches to virtual links gives a core bond. Consequently marks in different pieces impose the corresponding virtual marks and an additive excess equal to each marked piece's least marked separating cost minus its ordinary separating cost. A query wholly inside a fixed branch is unchanged under this map. These facts also cover absent bonds. All the branches share $O$ and otherwise have disjoint interiors.

**Every moved present query of at most three names has the same minimum.** In the graph where the hubs are identified, the exchange of $a,c$ is an incidence automorphism. Every cut separating the hubs in the original costs at least $sM+2L=133$. The $s$ shield routes from each hub to $O$ give $sM$ crossings. The two links through each color's special middles to $v$ give a further $2L$ on disjoint distinctions. Every bond smaller than 133 therefore has exactly the same support before and after identification. For the reverse direction, removing fewer than 133 names leaves the two hubs connected. This lifts any path through the identified hub inside its shore.

It suffices to exhibit a smaller bond for each moved query in the identified core. At least one of its queried middles is special. An anchor is unavailable as the sole positive anchor if one marked middle has a mark to that anchor and a mark to another outer vertex. When an anchor is available, put it on the positive shore. Put a marked middle on the positive shore exactly when its marks go to other outer vertices. Every mark crosses. The positive shore is connected through its anchor. The negative shore contains the hub, outside, unmarked shield middles and the other anchor.

- If $w$ is available, start at its singleton cut of size 26. Adding a special middle increases its size by 33. Adding a shield middle increases it by 18. The resulting size is at most $26+3\cdot33=125$.
- If $w$ is unavailable and $v$ is available, there are at most two marked middles and at least one is special. The singleton cut at $v$ has size $4L+2s=90$. Adding a special costs one and adding a shield costs 18. The total is at most 109.
- If both anchors are unavailable, the same middle carries marks to both, since there are at most three marks. When all marks are at this middle, its singleton works. Otherwise put both anchors on the positive shore and this doubly marked middle on the negative shore. If the third mark has an anchor endpoint, leave its middle negative and add an unmarked special to connect the anchors. If its outer endpoint is the hub or outside, put its middle on the positive shore to connect them. Starting with the two anchors costs $26+90=116$. Adding a special changes this by minus one, and adding a shield changes it by 16. Every case costs at most 132. The remaining middles keep the other shore connected.

All three bounds are below 133. Parallel copies in a marked bundle impose the same crossing. Removing repetitions of a virtual link cannot increase the number of queried middles. Hence the proof covers mixed queries on actual growing-branch distinctions as well. A query fixed as a set automatically has the same minimum.

**Four present marks detect the map.** Choose a red shield $r_0$. Mark one direct distinction on each of $Rr_0,vr_0,vc,wc$. A shore containing $R,v,w$ must exclude $r_0,c$. For these two middles to connect on their shore, $c$ requires $B$ there and $r_0$ requires $O$ there. This fixes all outer vertices. The two red specials can stay with $R$. The remaining blue special costs $L$, the forced blue special $L+1$, the forced red shield $M+2$, the other red shields $(s-1)M$, and the blue shields $2s$. These minimizing placements have connected shores. The reading is

$$
2L+sM+2s+3=158.
$$

In the image, the red special $a$ has only $R,v,w$ as neighbors. They are all opposite it. Its shore must also contain $r_0$, so no such bond exists. Four is therefore the first distinguishing arity for this map at this stage.

**A later old pair detects it.** Mark the two red special spokes at $a,b$. When each branch costs one, the shore consisting of $R$ and its red shields costs $2L+3s=67$. The image marks one spoke of each color. Each of the two edge-disjoint weighted circuits through its own hub, its two specials and $v$ must pay at least $2L$. Thus the image costs at least $4L=68$. The exact minima, by exhaustive shore counts, are 67 and 92. Reassigning any later fresh names leaves these old-only entries intact. The earlier equation-transport theorem now also certifies that the initial permutation changes the equation space, since it fixes the growing end and fails at the next round. **One anchor is insufficient in this family.** With only $v$, omit the mark at $w$ from the four-name query. The three remaining marks force $R,v$ opposite $r_0,c$. Connectivity forces $O,B$ with those middles. Minimization at each remaining middle gives $2L+sM+s+1$, with both shores connected. The image isolates $a$ beside $r_0$ and has infinite reading. This applies at positive integer $L,M$ and $s\ge1$ at a stage with virtual price $M$. It bounds the number of anchors for this construction and specified exchange. No minimum number of junctions follows.

## Every present arity has a first missing reading

For every integer $r\ge2$, there is a first reached stage whose specified permutation preserves every current reading of arity at most $r$ and fixes the entire growing end pointwise. A current query of exactly $r+1$ names has finite reading before the permutation and infinite reading after it. At the second round an old pair has different readings. Both the number of ordinary junctions and the number of literal distinctions are $O(r^2)$.

Use $I=r-1$ ordinary anchors. One is the special anchor $v$ as above. Connect each middle to every other anchor by one ordinary distinction. Use $s=4(r+2)$, $L=sr$ and $M=8s$, and implant the two-round branch at every shield. The exchange of $a,c$ carries each named incidence by type and copy. It fixes all other middle sites, all ordinary anchors and the complete growing branches.

**Current upper bound.** Identify $R,B,O$ to a single vertex $H$. The resulting simple incidence graph, with weights restored for counting, is complete bipartite between $r$ outer vertices and $N=2s+4$ middles. The outer vertices are $H$ and the $I$ anchors. Identifying the hubs and outside makes the named exchange an exact automorphism. Every cut separating any of $R,B,O$ costs at least $sM$, using the $sM$ edge-disjoint routes from each hub to $O$. Thus all bonds below that threshold agree before and after identification, by the uncut-path lifting argument.

Take any set of at most $r$ marked virtual or ordinary links. Form its bipartite incidence graph, including every outer vertex even if isolated. Repeated marks on the same two endpoints impose the same crossing. If the component of $H$ contains all $r$ outer vertices and $t$ middles, at least $r+t-1$ distinct links are necessary to connect them. Thus $t=1$ and all marks are at that one middle. Its singleton shore is a bond.

In the other case choose a component containing an anchor and excluding $H$. Put its outer vertices on the positive shore and every other outer vertex on the negative shore. Put the selected component's marked middles on the negative shore and all other marked middles on the positive shore. Every marked link crosses. Add one unmarked positive middle if necessary to connect the positive outer vertices. Every middle meets every outer vertex, and many unmarked middles remain negative, so both induced shores are connected. This uses at most $I$ positive anchors and $r+1$ positive middles. The total degree of all anchors is $4L+2s+(I-1)N$. There are only four special middles in the entire graph, each of degree $2L+I-1$. The shield middles have degree $2M+I$. These same bounds include the preceding singleton case. Every query therefore has a bond of size at most

$$
4L+2s+(I-1)N+4(2L+I-1)+(r+1)(2M+I)
=30sr+14s+r^2+8r-17.
$$

The difference between $sM=8s^2$ and this upper bound is $7r^2+208r+417>0$. The full literal observations through arity $r$ therefore agree by the branch reduction, including queries inside one branch and queries spread across several branches. The symbolic replay checks every marked incidence pattern on up to $r$ middle labels for $r=2,3,4,5$, respectively 10, 129, 2,516 and 68,405 choices. It verifies that all marks cross, both shores are connected and the displayed bounds hold. The proof above supplies the all-size conclusion. **The first missing arity.** Mark $Rr_0,vr_0$ and a direct incidence of every ordinary anchor at the blue special $c$. There are $I+2=r+1$ marks. They put every anchor and $R$ on one shore, and $r_0,c$ on the other. Put $B,O$ with those two middles. A different blue shield connects $B$ to $O$. A red special on the first shore connects all its anchors to $R$. Put other red middles on the first shore and other blue middles on the second, so each connects inside its shore. Every listed mark crosses. In the image, the red special $a$ has all its neighbors, namely $R$ and all the ordinary anchors, opposite it. Its shore also contains $r_0$, violating connectedness. The fourth-mark argument for two anchors is the $r=3$ case of this mechanism.

**Next round and size.** At price one the cut taking $R$ and all its red shields costs $2L+s(I+1)=3L$ and contains the red pair. Its image intersects two edge-disjoint circuits, each forcing at least $2L$. No fresh renaming can match these entries. The core has $I+6+2s$ ordinary sites. At the reached stage each of the $2s$ branches adds $2M+1$ sites, giving $I+6+4s+4sM$ in total. Each branch has $3(M-1)$ starting ordinary links, $3(M-1)+1$ starting loose distinctions and $M$ first-round fresh distinctions. The ordinary core has $4(2L+I-1)+2s(M+I)$ names. As $I,s,M$ grow linearly in $r$ and $L$ quadratically, the claimed quadratic size bound follows in both units.

If every pair of ordinary sites must meet at most once, keep one ordinary link in each parallel bundle and replace each remaining copy by a closed two-terminal unit of separating cost one. One such unit has four sites forming the complete graph on four vertices minus one link, whose two unmatched endpoints connect to the two host terminals. Each new site has arity three. Every old pair of sites in such a bundle keeps its original meeting, and no added site meets two old sites. All current and next candidate groups and counts are therefore preserved. For a query crossing different units, the same marked two-terminal projection adds identical fixed excesses to matched core readings. A query entirely inside a moved unit can either bound an internal shore with its terminals together, or separate its terminals. The latter costs its own least marked separating cut plus $K(e)-1$ in the rest, where $e$ is its cost-one virtual link and $K(e)$ is the host singleton reading. Both terms transport. The displayed $r+1$ marks and the later pair all use direct surviving names. Every extra unit has constant size, so the same $O(r^2)$ bounds hold. In particular an arity bound sufficient on every source of at most $n$ junctions must grow at least as a constant times $\sqrt n$ along this sequence. These examples give no matching upper bound for general sources.

There is a small obstruction to uncovering this particular mismatch by repricing a single fixed core edge. For a wholly core query $T$, its minimum as the edge changes to price $t$ is $f_T(t)=\min(A_T,B_T+t)$, by splitting candidate bonds according to whether they cross that edge. For $1\le t\le b$, this gives $f_T(t)=\min(f_T(1)+t-1,f_T(b))$, also when a class is absent. Two maps that agree at both prices one and $b$ agree at every intermediate price on these core queries. This explains why a single downward change from a matching inflated price cannot expose a hidden old triple in the unit-matching base.
