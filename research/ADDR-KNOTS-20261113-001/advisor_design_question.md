You are an advisor to a research thread. Give a colleague's critical view: find holes, say what is settled by construction, say what is known, and push back on the design. Be concrete and brief (under 1,200 words). Plain words.

## Setting

A research program studies patterns of three-place not-all-equal (NAE) constraints ("knots") on Boolean variables ("distinctions"). A "nested pattern" comes from a description language (straight-line macros, each with 3 ports, local distinctions, calls to a knot K, earlier macros, or SELF, which puts the macro's pattern inside each of its own knots, to a depth t). Descriptions are sampled by fair coins under a prefix-free code, weight 2^-length (the "description weighting"). Proved there (NEST-COUNT): every such nested pattern is finite-state, because each nested copy meets the rest through 3 distinctions (16 relations on 3 ports up to... at most 2^8 relations), so life at depth t is eventually periodic in t, the bottomless limit is decided by a greatest fixed point, and the top cannot read depth except modulo a period. They read that "the interface size is what separates decidable nesting from undecidable, plane-like patterns" (an interpretation, not a theorem). Many follow-up studies read the least bond (smallest jointly constrained set of distinctions holding a top distinction and one at depth s) against depth; a recurring artefact was that a top port carried unchanged down the recursion gives a bounded bond by identity.

The owner's picture is the excluded case: "recursive structures... the nested structures depend on the larger structures to know where they are... but for logic, it can hop around... a logic based fractal with emergence as the guiding principle". The coordinator asks: define a class of nested NAE patterns in which the number of distinctions shared between a part and what contains it grows with depth, or in which a part refers back to distinctions of the larger pattern; under a description weighting, ask what is typical or rare among three fates (dead at once / lives a while then dies / lives at every depth) and whether influence reaches across depth. Avoid drifting into flat neighbour-rule patterns (cellular-automaton rows, tilings of the plane), which the program has already done (rows growing by one per depth: every window-1 rule settles by depth 4; with fresh distinctions additive rules count depth in binary, known).

## My analysis so far (check it)

Any finite description of a growing interface must apply a fixed rule to unboundedly many interface distinctions. I see three ways:
(a) uniform per class: a copy knots its own distinctions to every distinction of a named class its ancestors enlisted;
(b) position-shifted pairing (child position i with parent positions i, i+1): flat rows, known territory;
(c) pairing computed by a finite synchronous transducer on addresses: the literature's self-similar (automaton) groups. (b) is the odometer special case of (c).

Class A (case a, "shared lists"; the literature's vertex replacement / clique-width): extend the language so a self-holding macro may enlist own distinctions on one of h lists that its descendants see, and place list knots (u, p, q) for every u on list c, with p, q own distinctions. Lemma A (my claim): still finite-state. What a copy's subtree allows depends on its 3 ports and, per list, only on the set of values present among the list's members (capped counts up to 3 if a knot may take two or three members of one list), so a macro's "relation" is a subset of 8 x 4^h profiles, Kleene iteration, eventually periodic life, decidable limit. So growing interface size alone does not escape finite state; what matters is whether a part can tell interface distinctions apart one by one. Also by construction: if a copy at every depth knots to a list still holding a top distinction, the bond to the top is at most 3 at every depth.

Class B (case c, "addressed knots"; the literature's automaton groups / Mealy machines): a description is a binary invertible Mealy automaton with s states (each state: a swap bit and two successor states) plus three designated states q1, q2, q3. Level-n pattern: distinctions are binary words of length n; for each word x a knot on {q1(x), q2(x), q3(x)}, placed only when the three are distinct (alternative rule: {x, a(x), b(x)}). The part under prefix w is the set of words starting with w; inside it the transformations act by their sections at w, so a part's rule depends on where it is. The number of knots leaving a part grows with depth exactly as the automaton's activity grows (Sidki: bounded, polynomial of degree d, exponential). Bounded automata give bounded interfaces (finitely ramified limit spaces): finite-state, which is Bartholdi 2022 (Groups Geom. Dyn., "MSO and the domino problem on self-similar graphs": domino decidable on post-critically finite self-similar graphs; undecidable on a linear-activity "long range graph" and the Barbieri-Sablik H-graph; conjecture: for contracting groups, decidable domino <=> finitely ramified). Weighting: s by Elias gamma, swap bits and successors uniform, designated states uniform; caps s <= 4 or 5. A first census: all binary automata with s <= 3 (the 3-state classification of Bondarenko, Grigorchuk, Kravchenko, Muntyan, Nekrashevych, Savchuk and Sunic 2008 exists), fates at levels 1..16 by exact SAT, split by activity class, with a flat share (odometer-like, rings) reported apart. Influence across depth: at level n, the least bond from a word x to the nearest word in the other first-letter half, or the law's bond distance against the knot pattern's own growth (bounded automata have polynomial growth with possibly fractional exponent, e.g. log2 3 for the Hanoi graphs).
Covering lemma (mine): the prefix map sends each level-(n+1) knot to a level-n triple; a level-n state lifts to level n+1 unless some level-(n+1) knot maps to a degenerate triple. So with degenerate triples read as NAE on the multiset life is monotone upward (never "lives a while then dies"); with degenerate triples dropped, it is not.
