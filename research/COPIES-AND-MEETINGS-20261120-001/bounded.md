# How many lasting products can have bounded width?

**Theorem.** From any finite start in the identified ordinary-growth class, only finitely many disjoint lasting packets can be released with at most $w$ knots apiece. Their widths may grow without limit *after* release. The result holds under parity and not-all-equal, for every choice of source and every recognition rule, including one that knows the full history. Requiring identical physical holdings only strengthens the obstruction. The unrestricted ordinary law also has a finite bound for singleton launches of arbitrary later widths, and for packets of width at most three throughout. [Frozen definitions](preregistration.md)

Here width counts actual newly formed knots in **one** packet at a size. Each surviving member has *all* its immediate parents in the preceding members of that packet. The packet can ignore any other children they make. Different packets have disjoint members whenever both are living. We impose no restriction on shared old ancestry or on other births nearby. Sizes are cuts of the ordinary parent structure. Neither a numerical size label nor a selection of ancestry is an input to a physical reader. The bound applies before imposing any reader requirement.

## What one narrow layer forces

In the identified class the physical knots at size $m$ are the orbits of connected $m$-point sets in the unrolled point–witness incidence. An ordinary cluster contains an original live point. Its parents are all possible single-point deletions which preserve connectedness and retain such a point. For a finite piece that relays, a cluster entering the relay must already contain the entire finite top. The positive finite sizes and parent relations are the same under the two laws on infinite pieces. An isolated finite top can close under parity and relay under not-all-equal. [Fixed-map theorem with multiple meetings](../../proofs/DOUBLE-IDENT-20261119-001A/DOUBLE-IDENT-20261119-001A-P001_doubles/proof.md) · [Endless chains](../../proofs/CHAIN-IDENT-20261119-001A/CHAIN-IDENT-20261119-001A-P001_chains/proof.md) · [Relay prerequisites](../../proofs/RELAY-IDENT-20261119-001A/RELAY-IDENT-20261119-001A-P001_relays/proof.md) · [The two knot laws](../NAE-GROWTH-20261119-001/nae_growth.md)

Suppose a lasting packet has at most $w$ members at some single size $q\ge2w$. Pick a compatible infinite sequence of its clusters at successive sizes from $q$ onward. Such a sequence exists. There are finitely many physical clusters at each size, every later chosen cluster has parents in the previous packet, and some member at $q$ has descendants at unbounded sizes. Successively choose a child with that same property. Choose representatives of the orbits so that each cluster really contains its predecessor, by lifting its one-point deletion onto the representative already chosen. The union is an infinite connected part of a locally finite incidence tree. From a fixed role in it, an infinite route exists by successively choosing a branch containing infinitely many roles.

Any connected finite set along that route containing an original point is an actual ancestor of a sufficiently large chosen cluster. To see this, retain a spanning tree of the target and extend it to a spanning tree of the cluster on its point adjacencies. Delete leaves outside the target until just the target remains. Each step is an allowed parent. Consequently **all** such sets of any one size $m\ge q$ belong to this packet at size $m$. This holds for the single chosen infinite union regardless of which other children the packet retained.

There are two possibilities for the route.

- **An attached endless chain.** A cluster reaching arbitrarily far down such a chain must contain the attached original point and the intervening pendant. Its prefix from that original, extended to size $m$, is an actual compulsory ancestor at every sufficiently large $m$. The same argument at a relay retains the whole finite top and its relay point before the chosen chain prefix. These prefixes are legal under the relay deletion rule, which deletes the other chain's tips first. There are finitely many types of such anchored chains in the finite starting map, counted before any extra symmetries of the source are imposed. Unrolling a cyclic piece repeats the same attachment in its other lifts. All those prefixes represent the same physical knot. Finite twigs cannot carry an infinite route.
- **A route continuing among original point roles.** After dropping any initial attachment, read the consecutive original point identities as a one-sided infinite word. Consecutive points specify the witness uniquely in the identified class. The route never immediately retraces and uses different witnesses at successive turns. Each length-$q$ window is a compulsory ancestor. One physical interval gives at most two ordered words, the two directions of its path. Thus this infinite word has at most $2w$ distinct ordered length-$q$ factors. This counts literal point identities in the finite source for the proof. Renaming them changes no equality, and the observer has received no marks.

Here is the needed elementary word argument. Let $f(j)$ count distinct length-$j$ factors of this one-sided word, with $f(0)=1$. Each factor extends to the right. Hence $f(j+1)\ge f(j)$. If growth were strict at all $j<q$, then $f(q)\ge q+1$, contrary to $f(q)\le 2w\le q$. At an equality every length-$j$ factor has exactly one right extension. The successive factors therefore follow a deterministic map on at most $f(j)$ states. Their tail repeats with some least period $p\le f(j)\le2w$. For $j=0$ this just says that the word is constant.

In fact **$p\le w$**. The periodic tail is a closed walk which never retraces in the finite point–witness incidence. Its $p$ length-$q$ phases are all distinct, even up to reversal, as $q\ge p$. An equality in the same direction would give a shorter period. A reversed equality covering a whole period would give $x_i=x_{c-i}$ on the cyclic word. If $c$ is odd, two consecutive positions are equal. If $c$ is even, the two neighbors of position $c/2$ are equal. Either would make the route repeat immediately, in the second case through the unique witness of its point pair. Thus there are at least $p$ physical interval ancestors in the width-$w$ packet. This proves the claim.

For every $m\ge q$ the packet contains **every** size-$m$ phase of that same periodic tail. All of them occur arbitrarily far along the infinite route, so the ancestry argument applies at the later size too. An arbitrarily long nested route by itself need not be periodic. For example the seven-knot source permits arbitrary concatenations of its two different returns. The bound on the number of compulsory windows does the work.

## Counting distinct products, including different release sizes

Let $a_w$ be the number of primitive closed non-retracing walks with at most $w$ point steps in all finite ordinary cores, up to cyclic shift and reversal. At each point turn the two witnesses have to differ. Include walks visiting the same original more than once. A proper power contributes only its least period. Let $r$ count anchored endless chains up to translations of the unrolling, including the two in each piece that really relays under the chosen law. Both numbers are finite and computable from the given start.

Every lasting packet with a width-$w$ observation at $q\ge2w$ contains one of these $a_w+r$ sets of compulsory ancestors at all sufficiently large sizes. If two packets had the same one, take a common size beyond both observations and any relay prerequisites. They would contain the same actual knot at that size. This argument allows packets to be released at different sizes, to occupy several components, to widen, and to have more than one possible compulsory set.

Consequently **$a_w+r$ bounds all disjoint ongoing packets of uniform width at most $w$**. For any finite selection of them one can use a common late observation. For the launch-width-only condition the same bound applies to all launches at sizes at least $2w$. Under disjointness from release, a bound on the total is

$$
             a_w+r+\sum_{q=1}^{2w-1}N_q ,
$$

where $N_q$ counts actual knots at size $q$. If scoring begins disjointness one size after release, use $N_{q+1}$ for those finitely many early cohorts. The finite term covers an early narrow launch that later widens enough to support an aperiodic route. A stall or a closed end has no further lasting launches.

The main bound is sharp for **packing continuing parent diagrams**, with no demand that they were all released by one source. Each primitive walk of period $p\le w$ supplies its $p$ cyclic windows with just their two phase parents forever. Each anchored chain has its one continuing prefix at every sufficiently large size. Different periodic walks have disjoint sufficiently long windows, since a common word as long as a common multiple of their periods would identify the two periodic sequences up to reversal. Anchored chain prefixes are distinct from core windows and from one another once past their forks. Thus all these diagrams can be taken at one sufficiently late layer. A physical symmetry can exchange them. Selecting one member of a tied pair by name is unnecessary for either the counting bound or this unlabeled packing statement. Packing alone supplies neither a common emitting source nor equality of physical costs.

## The seven-knot source

Its six witness blocks on the live original knots are $\{0,4\},\{4,6\},\{4,5\},\{0,2,3\},\{3,6\},\{2,5\}$. The core has three distinct routes of lengths three, five and five **in point–witness steps** between point 4 and the block $\{0,2,3\}$. It has no attached chain or relay. The pair of short-plus-long returns gives two primitive walks of four point steps. The two long routes give one of five. Any other primitive walk must traverse at least four of the routes, requiring at least eight point steps, because the short route cannot follow itself. Thus there is no ongoing all-parent packet of width at most three on this start. At width four there are at most two and at widths five through seven at most three. These bounds are attained as ongoing diagrams. [Physical seven-knot source and its initial projections](../GENERATORS-20261120-001/source.md)

For any $w$, form the finite matrix $M$ indexed by oriented candidate pairs $(u,v)$. Put a one from $(u,v)$ to $(v,z)$ if the two pairs use different witnesses, and put zero otherwise. Then $T_p=\operatorname{tr}(M^p)$ counts oriented closed walks rooted at a specified step. Each primitive walk of period $d$ considered up to rotation and reversal contributes $2d$ to $T_p$ exactly when $d$ divides $p$. Subtract contributions for proper divisors and divide by $2p$ to get the new primitive count at period $p$. On the sixteen oriented pairs of the six displayed blocks the resulting cumulative bound is:

| Width $w$ | 1–3 | 4 | 5–7 | 8 | 9–11 | 12 | 13 | 14–15 | 16 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Primitive returns of period at most $w$ | 0 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 14 | 16 | 19 |

There is a further obstruction when the observed product includes its full finite holdings. Every size-$m$ cluster on this seven-knot start has at least $\lceil(m+8)/5\rceil$ fresh distinctions, even if its parent diagram is entirely regular. In the unrolled incidence tree take $U$ to consist of its points and all their incident blocks. External links from $U$ land at different outside points, since two routes into one outside point would close a cycle in the tree. They therefore count the fresh distinctions exactly. All roles have degree two or three. If $s$ of the degree-three roles occur in the finite tree $U$, summing its degrees gives exactly $s+2$ external links. If $s>0$, at most $3s-(s-1)=2s+1$ distinct connecting corridors touch its branch roles. The $s-1$ corridors connecting those roles within $U$ were counted at both ends. All its other points lie on those corridors, each of which has at most two interior point roles. Thus $m\le s+2(2s+1)=5s+2$. For $s=0$ there are at most two points. This proves the bound. An actual fixed finite holding with at most $H$ fresh distinctions cannot occur past size $5H-8$ on this start, even before requiring independent continuation.

As a direct scale check, applying the archived complete end operator to the six displayed blocks and openings $(3,3,3,3,2,2)$ in the live order $(0,2,3,4,5,6)$ gave minimum fresh counts $2,3,3,3,3,4,4$ at sizes one through seven under each law. The respective numbers of live knots were $6,8,12,19,32,56,101$. Size one uses the supplied loose openings. The all-size bound is the tree calculation above. [Complete end operator](../../experiments/ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001A/ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001A-E001_ordinary/end_operator.py)

More generally, on a periodic core walk the fresh-count increment when a window gains one entire period is the sum of $|B|-2$ for each crossed block $B$, plus $|D|-1$ for each unused block $D$ incident at a visited point, counted per visit. To see this, count the distinct neighbors off the infinite path. Each crossed block supplies its other points once; every other incident block supplies all points except the visited one. End corrections repeat at the same phase. Equivalently the boundary of a finite path equals the sum of its points' adjacency degrees minus the sum of the sizes of its traversed blocks. The increment vanishes exactly when every crossed block has size two and every visited point has just its two crossing blocks. Then the walk fills its entire bare cycle component. In a genuinely branching component, its required periodic phase holdings therefore grow even though the number of phase knots stays fixed. This concerns actual holdings with all outside births still present. Re-running ordinary growth on a removed packet is a separate intervention.

## One-member launches and three-member continuations without identification

The following arguments use the actual complete funded rule alone. At positive age let $p$ be a holder's number of birth parents, $D$ its number of current multi-parent groups and $h$ its opening count. Except at the sole implied parity close, $h=\max(D,3-p,1)$. Its number of unary children is $h-D$. Every neighboring pair belongs to a group, so $D=0$ means the holder is a singleton component of the live end. Whole live components neither split nor merge on a funded continuing step. [Actual ordinary rule](../ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/end_operator.md) · [Component correspondence](../GENERATORS-20261120-001/components.md)

In a nonsingleton part $D\ge1$. A multi-parent-born holder has no unary child. A unary-born holder can make at most one, and to do so must have $D=1$ and $h=2$. All neighbors of a unary-born holder are its siblings at its sole old parent. Any group of them containing the unary-born holder is held there; its eligible groups are exactly the separate pairs with those siblings. For $D=1$ it has just one sibling. Its two births are one binary and one unary, so that new unary has just the binary as sibling and repeats the same pattern forever. Such unary chains cannot merge. In a component which never becomes a positive-age singleton they originate among the finitely many first unary births. Indeed any unary born at age two or later had to be the just-described successor of a unary holder. The associated mixed or binary sibling receives another input; tracing that sibling as a new independent unary line would fail the parent test. [Literal one-neighbor chain](../GROWTH-CONTINUES-20261119-001/decision.md)

If a part becomes a positive-age singleton, its one holder has one or two openings by the same formula. One opening produces a sole unary with two fresh, unless the whole parity growth closes. A singleton with two openings makes two unary siblings with two fresh each. They each have just one candidate, hence each now begins a separate perpetual unary chain. The entire part from the two-opening singleton has the familiar two-ended continuation: without another part or with one, its two children are already distinct, and the identical witnessed end gives the same future as the two-ended relay. Its all-parent packets must eventually contain one of the two chain prefixes. Indeed, in the relay a very large cluster has long extent on at least one of the two arms. Its whole prerequisite and that one long prefix are compulsory ancestors. This part can therefore support at most two disjoint lasting packets of any widths. It cannot become a singleton again. A stall or the sole parity close supplies no subsequent births.

A singleton packet launched at positive age in a never-singleton part has at most one all-own next child. That child must be unary and follows one of the finitely many unary chains just described. The packet cannot widen there. In an eventually singleton part, every lasting packet must survive into the two-ended continuation, where the two-anchor bound applies. Consequently there are only finitely many disjoint everlasting singleton launches on **every** finite ordinary start, even allowing later widening. A generous explicit bound is the number of first unary births plus twice the number of initial live components, adding the number of original live knots if launches on the initial cut itself are also allowed.

For **width at most three throughout**, every persistent packet likewise takes one of finitely many unary-chain anchors. It suffices to consider a never-singleton part, since the eventually singleton parts were handled above. An all-parent family that ever loses a particular perpetual unary chain cannot acquire a later member of it. If a packet retains no chain arbitrarily late, after a finite cut none of its continuing members is unary. There are only finitely many chains and, past the first births, every unary in such a part lies on one. With at most two internal parents and no unary, at most one child is possible and then none. From three, the only way to obtain three distinct internal multi-parent children is to obtain exactly the three pairs. An eligible triple would itself exclude its pairs. In the next end those three pair children meet pairwise and have no common parent. Their triple therefore extends to some unheld maximal group, so none of their pairs is a residual binary group. At most the internal triple itself can follow, and the remaining singleton cannot continue unaided. The argument also covers a packet distributed across parts by choosing a part in which it persists at arbitrarily large sizes, of which there are only finitely many. This gives the same finite anchor bound for uniform widths one through three. It gives the width-three **launch-only** obstruction in a part with no later unary births. Wider unrestricted packets and unrestricted two- or three-member launches that later widen in the presence of unary help remain open here.

## What weaker copying would count

On the two-return source with its two routes physically distinguished, take the periodic words $A^kB$. When their full phase packets release, keep just the window whose first point is the unique marker on $B$, in the recovered orientation. At every later size extend that fixed-start window at its far end. Its prefix is the previously kept knot and its suffix is a different phase. This supplies unboundedly many disjoint singleton lines with the *same selected one-parent diagram* and with source parents at release. Each later knot really has a second parent outside its singleton line. Its actual fresh costs also grow. The new result distinguishes this inclusive copying control from the all-parent and full-holdings readings without changing the growth law.
