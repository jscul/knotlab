Under the inherited weighting, each preceding word retains its mass and divides it equally among all complete extensions. The actual choices of fresh words are independent across the births conditional on the preceding word. Let $I$ be one child's inputs, $O$ its $f$ fresh distinctions and $a=2^f$. Its conditional weight factor, after multiplying by $a(a-1)$, is

\[
(a-1)+\sum_{b=0}^1 \mathbf1[I\text{ all }b]-a\sum_{b=0}^1\mathbf1[I\cup O\text{ all }b].
\]

This follows by counting $a$ extensions for mixed inputs and $a-1$ for constant inputs. Unary input always supplies the latter divisor. The exact contraction sums these integer factors together with the original holder indicators. Complement symmetry makes each single distinction fair at both weights. For disjoint physical components the conditional extension factors preserve the original product, so all cross-component pair rates are one half. Under parity all preceding words have the same number of extensions at each cut, including the implied zero-fresh closure, so the two entire word weightings coincide.
