# A learned crossing, a learned undo, and one frozen reader

On November 14, 2026 (Pacific), we tested whether local Boolean actions could discover a mixing reversible crossing, learn to undo it, and preserve exactly what a rewired reader would need. In the primary experiment, one frozen policy answered all 2,792,448 questions across 606 public wirings and all 512 possible input relations. It used the minimum possible number of payload bits on 605 wirings. A separately trained agent that mutated sequences of guarded single-bit flips found a crossing and an undo from terminal rewards. We also ran its actual frozen instructions with the original reader gates. ## Which action was learned?

Each strand can carry 0, 1, or 2. For the primary fit these were represented by three Boolean input wires. The learned action on a pair `(x,y)` can be read directly from its six primitive output circuits. If `x=1`, exchange the meanings of 0 and 2 in `y`; otherwise leave `y` alone. Output that new value first and the old `x` second. For example, `(1,0)` becomes `(2,1)`. A different six-gate program learned from round-trip scores returns this pair to `(1,0)`. The model executes AND, OR, XOR and NOT expressions or copies an input wire. Neither a crossing, an example transformation table, nor an inverse was supplied as an action. [Atomic program search](../../experiments/BRAID-ACTIONS-20261114-001/BRAID-ACTIONS-20261114-001-E001_atomic_policy/actions.py)

We did supply the goal and a small language. Selecting the crossing rewarded a bijection, dependence on both input values in at least one output, a forward move that could differ from its inverse, unchanged equal pairs, and agreement of two three-step schedules. Crossing the left pair, then the right pair, then the left pair must agree with crossing right, left, right. The search considered 46,656 pairs of registers built by joining Boolean predicate programs. Each register was required to permute the value on one strand when the other was held fixed. The fit saw the 21 triples with a repeated label. It passed those 21 and the six all-distinct triples. Both orders of applying the learned forward and learned inverse restored all nine inputs (18/18). All nine input pairs were available for learning undo. Longer words and rewired embeddings on four strands gave 11,745/11,745 restored starts and 1,944/1,944 three-move agreements. Reversing the scheduled order for a longer undo was supplied. [Post-freeze checks and ablations](../../artifacts/BRAID-ACTIONS-20261114-001/BRAID-ACTIONS-20261114-001-E001_atomic_policy/assessment.json)

Removing all braid-equation feedback from the primary selection gave the identical forward program. The remaining constraints and the preference for short expressions matter here. Among the 14 actions satisfying the other structural conditions, all six with minimum cost (six gates) were braids. Of the other eight, two were braids and six failed the braid equation. Identity and plain swap can pass the braid equation while doing no mixing. The result therefore demonstrates discovery of an action under a specified task and inductive biases. General spontaneous discovery of braid structure from an unrelated reward is open.

## An agent editing individual actions

The second fit started from random instruction sequences. Each of 12 possible instructions toggles a single Boolean bit when the other three bits match a literal guard. The two-bit encodings `00,01,10` represent the three legal strand values. Each executed instruction expands to its literal negations, two ANDs and one XOR. The reward evaluated the sequence's completed behavior, with no demonstrated move sequence or intermediate action labels. Undo started fresh. Its only signal was whether going forward and back, or back and forward, restored each input pair. The guided forward program used 12 guarded flips and the separately learned undo used 10. The forward needed 60 primitive Boolean operations under the declared expansion, and undo needed 50. This program swaps the two output positions and exchanges 1 and 2 in `y` whenever `x=0`. A simultaneous renaming of the three labels takes the primary forward **and** inverse to the corresponding sequential programs. In a post-fit transfer, we kept the primary read and slot gates byte-for-byte, serialized them with the guided action sequences, and changed the same public wiring. This frozen sequential policy also scored 2,792,448/2,792,448, with the same single excess-bit case. This comparison used one declared seed and gives no speedup or success-rate estimate for independent training runs. ## What the reader gets after the fold

Each input relation is an arbitrary set of the nine possible ordered pairs. After crossing, the sender sees the crossed table and nine public ports. Each port describes a possible read, now or after undo. The actual port is revealed only after the sender commits a physical tuple of bits. Both parties know the wiring. The receiver has no access to the original relation, crossed table, or private computation. The sender chooses its packet length before seeing the relation. If a wiring has k possible answer vectors, the minimum fixed width is the smallest integer b for which 2^b is at least k. Here that count was evaluated separately on every wiring.

The learned read transition starts at zero and accumulates “some allowed pair reaches this port.” Its learned reuse transition starts at one, compares the physical reach of two port descriptions one cell at a time, and remains one precisely while every comparison agrees. A supplied allocator lets the sender use an earlier slot or open a new one. The decoder recomputes the same binding from the public wiring. Different descriptions can share one slot if crossing or undo connects them to exactly the same logical test. [Read and reuse training](../../experiments/BRAID-ACTIONS-20261114-001/BRAID-ACTIONS-20261114-001-E001_atomic_policy/reader.py)

| What the nine public ports might ask | Distinct possible answer vectors | Minimum bits | Sent |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| One promised yes/no read, repeated | 2 | 1 | 1 |
| Two descriptions pointing to the same moved cell, before and after undo | 2 | 1 | 1 |
| Read now or undo first, choices with independent answers | 4 | 2 | 2 |
| Any of k different individual cell memberships, k from 1 to 9 | 2^k | k | k |
| Any of nine cells in the original table after undo | 512 | 9 | 9 |
| Three individual cells in a row, plus whether any of the three is present | 8 | 3 | **4** |

The final row identifies a limit of this policy. Its packet can merge two reads that have the same answer on every possible relation. The row question differs from each of the three individual questions. A three-bit code would store the three membership bits and let the decoder compute their OR. Adding such a decoder operation would change the supplied slot interface.

One matched example needs pairing even to produce a **one-bit** answer. Let the original table contain every pair except `(0,1)`. After the learned crossing, the one missing pair is `(1,0)`. Individually, each strand still permits all three values. A read of `(1,0)` now returns 0; the same pins after undo return 1. With the next read promised, the corresponding packets were `[0]` and `[1]`. With either read still possible, the packet was `[0,1]`. Starting with only `(1,0)` missing reverses both answers while leaving both individual lists unchanged. With all nine cell reads possible, **512 distinct packets** let the actual frozen receiver reconstruct all 512 original tables. This is retention of the entire arbitrary pairing under this interface. An optimum offline lookup was unnecessary.

Training used 156 wirings with at most three different public read descriptions and 256 even-parity input tables. The test panel included 450 further wirings and all 256 odd-parity tables. On the intersection of unfamiliar wirings and odd tables, the score was 1,036,800/1,036,800. A separate interpreter reproduced every one of the 310,272 physical packets in the primary panel and independently recovered all 512 full tables. On another control that swapped every pair of the nine port positions, the frozen reader followed all 18,432 changes requiring a different answer. Leaving the old wiring in place got zero of those changed answers correct. Training both experiments again reproduced their checkpoints and scores.
