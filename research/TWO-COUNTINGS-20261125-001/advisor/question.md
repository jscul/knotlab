## 2. How they are read

  in every sentence (TD 2.4).

  counting 2 counts every allowed state of the start once and, at every birth, divides each old state's weight

## 4. The claims

Each is decidable by a program; each names its class.

- **C1. The table (computed).** For every statement in `statements.json` (the record's statements that give a rate,

  **unchanged**, **changed** (by how much, in the statement's own measure), or **not recomputed** (why).
- **C2. The setting lemma (argued, not refereed; checked).** Hypotheses: (h1) the setting births of the two
  observers are made after stage s; (h2) every allowed state before them has at least one completion under every
  setting (no refused weight); (h3) the two observers' setting births share no fresh distinction and no knot holds
  fresh distinctions of both. Then under counting from stage s (counting 2 included) both complete records are
  hidden, a setting keyed to an observer's own old values changes nothing on the far side, and the four-correlation
  score over all outcome functions is exactly 2. So every hidden excess of the record's setting-birth line (from
  REVIEW-LEADS-20261123-001C to SETTING-AS-REREAD-20261123-001A) is a property of counting from a stage at or after
  the settings. Checked on every registered setting test (run R1).
- **C3. When the two agree (proved in the record, referee pending; tested).** Along one history the two countings
  give the same weighting at every stage exactly when, at every round, the product over the round's births of each
  old state's number of completions is the same for every allowed old state
  (`proofs/UNREAD-COMPANION-AND-ORDER-FREE-WEIGHTING-20261121-001B/.../proof.md:30-40`). The study adds: per law, the
  census of starts on which the countings differ, and from which round (run R4).
- **C4. What counting 2 needs (argued; checked).** Counting 2 is a weighting of the states of one history's finished
  structure. It does not depend on the order of births within a round, on births made one at a time rather than as
  a round when the same knots result, or on the numbering. It does depend on which knot brought which distinction
  as fresh, and on where the start is placed (TD 3.3). A statement pooled over several histories (open choices,
  settings, sampled takes) needs a weighting of histories under counting 2; counting 1 pooled over histories carries

- **C5. Whether a growth law fixes the counting (argued, not refereed; one part seen).** (a) Under a value-blind law
  the structures made are the same whatever the weighting of states, so no pattern the growth makes, and no clock,

  weight (counting 2's property) and one that depends on the finished structure alone (counting 1's) are the same
  exactly when C3's condition holds; under any law that makes a round with unequal completions, one of the two
  properties must be given up, and the law does not say which. (c) On the unlimited structure (A110) counting 2 is

  whether counting 1's weighting of the start settles. If it fails to settle on some growth, that law leaves only
  counting 2 (or a supplied subsequence) on the unlimited structure: the one way found in which a law could fix the
  counting.

## 5. What is settled by argument (stated, not tested as predictions)

- Under parity every birth with f ≥ 1 fresh distinctions gives every old state 2^(f−1) completions and an implied
  birth gives one, so the two countings agree along every parity history (the record's theorem, ibid. line 42).
- A not-all-equal birth with f fresh distinctions gives 2^f − 1 completions to an old state whose inputs are all
  equal and 2^f otherwise; a birth with one old input gives 2^f − 1 to every state (ibid. line 44).
- Counting 2 keeps every earlier state's weight (ibid. line 34). Counting 1 does not, except under C3's condition.
- Both countings are carried by any renaming of distinctions and knots (TD 7.1).

## 6. Law and pattern kept apart

The law is the rule the growth follows; the structures and parts it makes are patterns; a clock is a pattern that
returns; a counting is a weighting of states, supplied by us, never made by the growth. "Counting" here is our
procedure: no rule of any law in the family counts, and no pattern is claimed to count.

# Two countings: definitions (first draft, "TD")

(`research/RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001/definitions.md`, "SD line n") and on those of the study of parts that meet

## 0. Words

- Kept from SD and FD with their meanings: structure, knot, place, distinction, holder, loose, shared, start, birth,
  join, offshoot, fresh distinction, round, law, ration, treatment, take, eligible, the choice, parent, the birth record.
- **State** is the glossary's joint state (a value for every distinction); **allowed state** one that every knot
  allows. The record's "word" and "allowed word" mean the same and appear only in citations.
- **Weighting** keeps the glossary's meaning, the chance a declared random process gives to each set, and is always
  said with what it weights: **a weighting of states**, **of meeting patterns** (of starts), **of histories**. The
  glossary's entry now names meeting patterns only (`publication/knotlab/INTUITIONS.md:5506-5511`); the study
  proposes adding states and histories to it (report, "Words").
- The record's other names for counting 1: "uniform present words", "uniform stage", "every allowed word counted
  equally", "complete reached-word weighting". For counting 2: "old-word weighting", "the control weighting",
  "equally lifted start", "inherited", "transported", "recursive division", "forward weighting", L. They are cited,
  not used.
- Not used: "typical", "rare", "likely" without a weighting; "reads" except in the owner's sense; the banned words of
  the common brief.

## 1. Structures and histories

1. **Knot laws.** even (the places' values sum to 0 mod 2), odd (to 1), not-all-equal (not all the same). "Parity"
   is even or odd. A structure may hold knots of several laws (as the record's hand-built structures do).
2. **Allowed states** A(S) of a structure S: the states of its distinctions that every knot allows.
3. **A history** H = (S_0, S_1, ..., S_t): a start S_0 and, for j ≥ 1, the knots added at stage j (a round of a law,
   one birth, or the supplied setting births). The distinctions first held at stage j are its **fresh**
   distinctions F_j; every other place of a knot added at stage j is an **old input** (held before stage j).
4. **Restriction.** For an allowed state w of S_j, w|_(j−1) is its values on the distinctions of S_(j−1).
5. **Completions.** For an allowed state o of S_(j−1), c_j(o) is the number of assignments of F_j that, with o,
   satisfy every knot added at stage j. A stage **extends** when c_j(o) ≥ 1 for every o (SD line 140).
6. **A stage's product.** When stage j adds births b_1..b_m whose fresh distinctions are disjoint and private,
   c_j(o) = Π_i c_(j,i)(o), where c_(j,i)(o) counts b_i's fresh assignments alone (TD 7.2).

## 2. Three weightings, kept apart

1. **A weighting of states** of a structure: nonnegative weights on its allowed states summing to 1 (or the
   refused weight stated, TD 3.4).
2. **A weighting of meeting patterns (of starts)**: as the glossary, e.g. the copier study's census weighting W1
   (each start once up to isomorphism) and W2 (each labelled start once).
3. **A weighting of histories**: the chance of each history given its start, e.g. the copier study's T1 (each take
   uniform over its candidates) or uniform over a law's open choices.
4. A statement's weighting is named by all three that enter it; a statement about one fixed start and one history
   names only a weighting of states.

## 3. The countings

1. **Counting 1** on a history H: every allowed state of S_t has weight 1/|A(S_t)|.
2. **Counting 2** on H: w in A(S_t) has weight (1/|A(S_0)|) · Π_(j=1..t) 1/c_j(w|_(j−1)).
3. **Counting from stage s** (0 ≤ s ≤ t): (1/|A(S_s)|) · Π_(j=s+1..t) 1/c_j(w|_(j−1)). Counting from stage t is
   counting 1; from stage 0, counting 2. The record has used an intermediate stage once
   (`research/SIBLING-ANNOUNCEMENTS-AND-RECEIVER-BIRTHS-20261121-001B/results.md:16`).
4. **Refused weight.** If some c_j(o) = 0 the weight of o is lost; it is reported, and the counting is given both
   unconditioned and conditioned on acceptance (`proofs/UNREAD-COMPANION-AND-ORDER-FREE-WEIGHTING-20261121-001B/
   UNREAD-COMPANION-AND-ORDER-FREE-WEIGHTING-20261121-001B-P001_order_free/proof.md:30-34`). Under every ration of TD
   5 that gives each birth a fresh distinction no weight is refused (FD 7).
5. **Pooled over histories.** For a set of histories h with finished structures S_h:
   - **counting 1, pooled**: each pair (h, w), w in A(S_h), once: P(h, w) = 1/Σ_h |A(S_h)|. Its weighting of
     histories is |A(S_h)| / Σ |A(S_h)| (THIRD-HOLDER-20261123-001A's "pooled" reading);
   - **counting 1, per history**: P(h, w) = ν(h)/|A(S_h)| with ν supplied (the record's practice in its setting
     tests, "normalized");
   - **counting 2, pooled**: P(h, w) = ν(h) · (counting 2 on h)(w), ν supplied.
   When ν is uniform and the totals |A(S_h)| are equal, the two forms of counting 1 agree.
6. **Fair reveal** (the record's third weighting, A185): a newborn's fresh distinctions revealed in a fixed order, each
   with a fair coin whenever both values still have a completion. It keeps old weights, like counting 2, and differs
   from counting 2 only inside a birth's completions; it depends on the reveal order. Reported only where a
   statement of the record uses it.

## 4. Records, settings and scores (the setting-birth line, as frozen there)

1. **Observers and settings** (REVIEW-LEADS-20261123-001C, `preregistration.md:47`): Alice holds x1, x2, x3; setting
   a ∈ {0, 1} adds nae(x1, x2 or x3, za) with one fresh distinction za; Bob likewise with y1, y2 or y3 and zb. Where a
   study read the setting as rule Q's open choice (A239), its own options are used (SETTING-AS-REREAD-20261123-001A).
2. **Complete record**: Alice (x1, x2, x3, za), Bob (y1, y2, y3, zb), unless a study's frozen text says otherwise.
3. **Far change**: the largest total-variation distance between an observer's complete-record distributions under
   the two far settings, for either own setting. **Hidden**: far change 0, exactly.
4. **Score**: the largest four-correlation value E00 + E01 + E10 − E11, over the four placements of the minus sign
   and over all two-valued functions of each complete record (score (i) of SETTING-AS-REREAD-20261123-001A); where a
   study registered parities of the record's bits only, that score is given beside it.
5. **Keyed setting**: one observer's setting a function of its own three old values (every such function), the
   other's fixed; the far change between two keyings, maximized (THIRD-HOLDER-20261123-001A, pooled reading).
6. **Every state can occur** (the support): a combination of settings and start values occurs under a counting when
   some allowed finished state with them has positive weight. Under any counting of TD 3 with no refused weight, the
   support is the same: every combination of settings and allowed start states.

## 5. The family of laws (fixed before any run)

1. The laws of SD and FD as frozen: ORDINARY = (ORD, STALL); Q-OLDEST, Q-NEWEST, Q-ALL (rule Q with every round-1
   choice; FD 3.2); RING-LAW = (CAP-3, CANCEL-WAIT, RELEASE); COPY-LAW(CANCEL-RETIRE) = (FLOOR1, CANCEL-RETIRE,
   RELEASE), alone and with MET(OLDEST); swap RATION-k, k = 1..6, in place of each law's ration. Under parity and

2. **Value-blind.** A law is value-blind when its next structure is a function of the structure, the names, the
   birth record and, where it has them, its sampled takes, and of no state. Every law of 5.1 is value-blind, by

## 7. Settled statements used by the engine as checks

1. **Renaming.** A one-to-one renaming of distinctions (and any reordering of knots) carries every counting of TD 3

2. **Factorization.** If the births of a stage have disjoint private fresh sets, c_j(o) = Π_i c_(j,i)(o); so

3. **Projection.** Under counting from stage s the weighting of A(S_j), for j ≥ s, is the restriction of the
   weighting of A(S_t).

## 8. The limit question (R6)

For a history grown round by round, P_r is counting 1 at round r restricted to the start: P_r(o) = (number of states
of A(S_r) extending o) / |A(S_r)|, o in A(S_0). The **change at round r** is TV(P_r, P_(r−1)). Counting 2's
restriction to the start is uniform at every round (7.3). Computed by elimination in floating point with
rescaling; exact where the stage has at most 22 distinctions (checked against enumeration there).

## 9. The knot test

call `knotlab.knots.check_knot` with the projected relation, every other distinction a witness). Every knot under its
own law; a structure of one law is also run under the other law where the record's statement is about both.

## In standard terms

The literature's words, not the program's. A constraint network grows by adding constraints with new variables. Two
probability laws on its solutions: the uniform law on the final solution set, and the sequential law (uniform on
the initial solutions, then each solution's mass split equally among its extensions at each step), a projective
family. The sequential law equals the uniform one exactly when every extension count is constant (the record's
theorem). On a CHSH test with the settings as two added constraints, the sequential law with the settings added last
is a local hidden-variable model (Bell's measurement independence holds by construction); the uniform law on each
final network is a model with measurement dependence (post-selection). On an infinite network the sequential law
extends by Kolmogorov's theorem; the uniform law exists only as a limit of finite-volume uniform laws (an infinite-
volume limit with free boundary).

observer's record, so the structure can also carry a message for anyone able to key a birth to a value, which

## What happened

- **A setting keyed to an old value.** Over all 256 functions of an observer's three old values, the other observer's

## The ceiling

**Statement.** Let an old word's weight at settings (a, b) be a common weight times a factor 1 or ρ for each observer,
set by that observer's setting and old values, and let both complete records hide the far setting exactly. Then for
any outcomes the score is at most 2 + 2 T(c) T(c'), where c and c' are the two ratios of the totals of the finished
structures and T(c) = (ρ − c)(ρc − 1)/(c(ρ² − 1)). T is largest at c = 1, where it is (ρ − 1)/(ρ + 1). So the score
is at most 4(1 + ρ²)/(1 + ρ)², with equality only when the four totals are equal.

**Why.** Hiding lets the totals be absorbed into the factors, so that the four distributions are one distribution
times (1 ± δ) for Alice and (1 ± ε) for Bob, with δ averaging to zero given everything Bob holds and ε likewise. Each
of δ and ε can be split into its two extreme values without changing any table, and then the sign of δ is
independent of everything Bob holds. The two settings of an observer change the distribution of its sign by T in
total variation. So with chance T an observer's sign follows its setting and otherwise it does not depend on it. If
neither sign follows its setting, or only one does, a common explanation exists and the score is at most 2; if both
do, it is at most 4. The full argument is in the [draft](results_draft.md#the-argument); the thread read it step by
step and found no gap. The implementer found it; it was not a pre-registered method. It is of the kind the literature
has for relaxed measurement independence (Hall 2011); this exact form was not checked against the literature.

**What it gives.** The 18-knot structure and its copies sit exactly at the ceiling for their ratios. No better
structure exists for this weighting, of any size or with any number of holders. A seeded search of 4,000 starts per
ratio found nothing above the family value.

# Every order of fair feasible reveals

For a nonempty finite set of binary words, revealing the distinctions in any order, tossing a fair coin whenever both values still have a completion, gives the uniform law **exactly when the set is an affine binary space**. At an ordinary growth step one needs an additional condition. Every old word must survive and have the same number of children for two successive uniform stage laws to agree with equal division of a uniform old law. Ordinary parity has both properties. A physical not-all-equal birth can change a marked probability arbitrarily many holder meetings away while preserving every old word.

## The relation theorem

Number the \(n\) distinctions and let \(S\subseteq\{0,1\}^n\). For nonempty \(S\) write \(U_S(w)=1/|S|\). Fix a permutation of the distinctions. At a realized prefix the picker sees only which values of the next distinction occur in at least one compatible full word. It chooses uniformly among those possible values. If there is no word to launch, there is no probability distribution. The empty word on zero distinctions is one word and has probability one.

1. **One order.** Each word receives \(2^{-b(w)}\), where \(b(w)\) is the number of positions at which both next values were possible along that word. Thus this order is uniform precisely when all its leaves have the same \(b\). Equivalently, at every branching prefix for that order the two child sets have equal cardinality. Necessity follows by conditioning the desired uniform law on that prefix. Sufficiency follows by induction down its successive splits.
2. **Every order.** This is equivalent to requiring that at *every* feasible partial assignment and each remaining coordinate, the two child counts are either \(0,c\), \(c,0\), or \(c,c\), with \(c>0\). Any partial assignment and proposed next name can be visited by a fixed order. The remaining suffix can be arbitrary. The criterion suffices even if the selection of the next coordinate uses the revealed history and fresh independent coins. With the uniform law as the target, the algorithm uses its exact conditional law at every choice. Equivalently, follow any fixed full word: along every ordering of its coordinates the conditional probabilities telescope to \(1/|S|\). The order-selection coins are independent of the unknown word conditional on what has already been revealed.
3. **Affine sufficiency.** If \(S=v+V\), any nonempty partial slice is a translate of the vectors in \(V\) vanishing on the already specified coordinates. A remaining coordinate is constant on that slice or is a nonzero binary linear functional there. In the latter case adding any direction on which the functional is one bijects the two classes. Thus every split satisfies the criterion.
4. **Necessity.** Induct on \(n\). The zero-coordinate nonempty case is immediate. Choose one coordinate \(i\). Each nonempty \(i\)-slice, with \(i\) removed, also passes every order: reveal \(i\) first and then use the proposed order on that slice. By induction these slices are affine. If only one exists, insert its fixed coordinate. If both exist, they have equal sizes because the first coin is fair. Write them \(a_0+V_0\) and \(a_1+V_1\).

   Suppose the direction spaces differ. Then their annihilators \(V_0^\perp,V_1^\perp\) differ. Choose a vector in their symmetric difference with smallest nonempty support \(C\), and switch slice names if necessary so its parity equation is present only in slice zero. No nonzero vector supported on a proper subset of \(C\) lies in either annihilator. Such a vector would lie in both by minimality, and adding it would produce a smaller member of the symmetric difference. Here the binary alphabet matters: a nonzero coefficient is one, so the sum deletes its entire proper support. Consequently every proper subset of \(C\) is freely and uniformly projected in both slices. On \(C\), slice zero has exactly the one parity equation and slice one projects onto every word. These projection facts follow because the annihilator of a coordinate projection consists precisely of the original parity equations supported on those coordinates.

   Fix any \(j\in C\). Reveal \(C\setminus\{j\}\) first, at any values. The two \(i\)-slices still each have conditional mass one half. Within one of them \(j\) is determined, and within the other it is fair. Thus the uniform target's conditional split at \(j\) is \(1/4,3/4\) in some order. Both values exist. The requested next fair coin fails. This contradiction forces \(V_0=V_1=V\). The full relation consists of the translate of \(V\) with \(i=0\) together with the translate reached by adding \((a_1-a_0,1)\), writing \(i\) last. It is affine.

In fact, even demanding only that all fixed orders produce *the same* full-word distribution forces uniformity here. Induct on \(i\)-slices in the preceding proof. Conditional on \(i\), the common law is uniform on each affine slice by induction; the mass of each nonempty slice is one half when both exist. Even if their sizes were unknown, conditioning on \(C\setminus\{j\}\) gives the same likelihood on both slices. The \(1/4,3/4\) contradiction again forces parallel directions. The slices then have the same size. This is stronger than invariance of the *mixture* over orders. The ternary not-all-equal example below has a uniform mixture despite failing every individual order.

Self-contained tests of affineness include closure under \(a\oplus b\oplus c\) for all \(a,b,c\in S\), or equality with a complete consistent family of parity equations. To see the closure test, translate by any one member; the translate contains zero, is closed under binary addition, and is therefore a vector subspace. In the literature, Simonis and Ashikhmin give the related binary almost-affine-code theorem in §5, Proposition 3, crediting an earlier equivalent result of Beimel and Chor. Their definition uses powers of two for the sizes of *all coordinate projections*. The argument above works directly with the feasible-coin protocol, gives an unequal split certificate, and identifies the separate conditions required at a funded growth step. [Simonis and Ashikhmin, *Almost Affine Codes*](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1008244215660)

## From one allowed relation to a local law

For any fixed structure the verdict is exactly about its *complete* relation \(S\). A family of affine local laws stays affine or empty under conjunction on identified binary names, fixed substitutions, pins, coordinate bit complements and existential forgetting. These operations respectively combine equations, substitute coordinates, set constants, translate, or take a linear projection. Thus each nonempty instance in this language passes. Conversely, if each primitive nonempty law may be used **by itself** as an admissible presentation, universal passage implies that each such relation is affine by the relation theorem. Unsatisfiable instances cannot be normalized. If a family excludes isolated realizations, the valid test is whether the *admitted visible relations* are affine. A nonlinear predicate can then be hidden. For example, adjoining a zero first input to the graph of AND leaves two affine words, even though the unconstrained graph has four nonaffine words. On those four words the two operands can be revealed first, and four of six orders are uniform; taking the output first asks for a \(3:1\) split. The same phenomenon occurs if a not-all-equal predicate on four names is conjoined with odd parity on those very names: the complete relation is just odd parity. A restricted composite cannot certify the isolated predicate.

A single holder of canonical arity has a sharper result. If its entire relation passes K1–K3, each deletion projects onto all \(2^{n-1}\) words, while K1 excludes the full cube. A single uniformly successful fixed-order picker requires power-of-two cardinality, hence exactly \(2^{n-1}\). Each one-coordinate deletion is then a bijection. At each word, flipping any one bit exchanges membership and nonmembership. The bit-flip walks connect every word to zero, so membership depends precisely on the parity of the full word, in one of its two signs. Conversely either full-support parity equation passes the canonical and picker tests. The zero-coordinate ambient set has no proper nonempty relation. The Boolean argument at one or two names concerns the bare deletion clauses only. The program’s full checker requires at least two loop variables and one contact. Systems of several parity equations, fixed names or the full cube can pass the picker while failing to be one canonical holder. The conclusion for an arbitrary language refers to nonempty, independently realizable holders, and to its allowed operations.

## Extending old states and keeping histories

Let \(p:N\to O\) be the declared restriction from finite new words to old ones, and put \(c(o)=|p^{-1}(o)|\). For nonempty \(N\), uniform new states induce the old law \(c(o)/|N|\). Given an old probability \(\mu\), equal division gives each new word \(w\) mass
\[
L_\mu(w)=\mu(pw)/c(pw).
\]
An old state with no child has refusal mass \(\mu(o)\). Total lifted mass is \(s=\sum_{c(o)>0}\mu(o)\). Its old marginal is exactly \(\mu\) on the surviving old words and zero off them. Thus arbitrary priors are preserved *without loss* iff their support survives; all priors survive iff \(p\) is onto. The unconditioned lift is uniform on \(N\) iff \(\mu(o)=c(o)/|N|\) on all \(O\), including zero-mass dead words. With explicit conditioning on acceptance and \(s>0\), replace \(\mu\) by the prior conditional on \(c>0\); the rejected mass remains recorded separately. For \(\mu=U_O\) and \(O,N\ne\varnothing\), unconditional equality of the two uniform procedures requires **every** \(c(o)\) to be the same positive integer. With dead words and equal positive counts only on the image, conditional agreement loses the excluded prior. An empty \(N\) has no normalized state. With empty \(O\), an ordinary restriction \(N\to O\) can exist only for empty \(N\). A known empty relation differs from the unique empty-coordinate word.

When \(N\) is affine and \(p\) is coordinate restriction, every nonempty fibre is a translate of one kernel, so all fibres over its image have the same size. Surjectivity onto the specified old relation still needs justification. For example, adjoining the equation “old bit equals zero” to an old unrestricted bit leaves an affine two-word enlarged set if a fresh bit is free, but kills half of the uniform old law. An arbitrary old prior, a nonlinear transition, or old-dependent choices of growth structure also need their own test.

# (8) The guide's glossary entry "Weighting"

- **Weighting.** The chance a declared random process gives to each set of
  meeting patterns, adding up to one (lesson 7.3): the literature's
  probability distribution. A process has a weighting; two processes can
  share one. No weighting gives infinitely many finite meeting patterns equal
  chances, so each must be declared, and a claim of what is typical or rare
  names its process.
- **Outcome.** What becomes of a knot pattern grown one knot at a time
  (lesson 7.3), one of three: it **resolves to nothing**, allowing no state
  from its first knots; it **ends**, at the first knot after which it allows
  no state, its **last base** being the pattern before that knot; or it
  **goes on forever**, a base at every stage, said only with a proof. A
  computation reports only that a pattern is **still a base at the horizon**.
  The outcome belongs to the growth, in its order, not to the pattern it
  reaches. The intuition: the owner's combinations that "resolve to nothing",
  "go for a while then die" and "go on forever".
- **History.** One whole run of a process or a growth with nothing left
  open. For a process whose updates are given: the value of every cell at

  beyond what its joins use (lesson 8.1); the growth records call it a spare,
  a word the guide keeps for distinction substitution.
- **Eddy.** The owner's word (18 November 2026, assumption A109): a cycle of
  knots that feeds its own growth, so that the count is multiplied by about
  the same rate each round. Going once around the cycle multiplies the count
  by its **lap weight** W, and the rate x solves W(1/x) = 1 (lesson 8.2). The
  intuition, in the owner's words: "eddies in a river", shared like library
  routines. The owner's "loops of logic" are read as these cycles; they are
  not the guide's loops (lesson 1.4).
- **Explosive growth.** The record's label for the kind of growth it cannot
  yet describe: where cycles of the map of joins overlap, the counts leap and
  no fixed rate has been found to fit (lesson 8.2). It is a label, not a
  defect. A growth **blows up** when its count grows faster than any
  polynomial, as eddies and explosive growth do, and blow-up is expected
  (assumption A110, lesson 8.3).
- **Partnered; runaway growth.** A set of live knots is partnered when they
  come in pairs of partners, each able to join every knot of the set but its
  partner (the literature's cocktail-party graph; for three pairs, the
  octahedron). Runaway growth: each round's count is about two to the power of
  half the one before (the literature's iterated exponential), as in a start
  whose live knots are partnered (lesson 8.2).
- **Generator; packet; release.** The owner's word generator (20 and 23
  November 2026): a start that keeps making separate things. In the record's
  test (assumption A125) a **packet** is a finite set of knots made in one
  round whose later members take every parent from the packet's own earlier
  members; it is **released** in the round from which that holds, which is
  about parents and not about a distinction becoming loose; a packet lasts
  when it has members in every later round; and a start is a generator when it
  releases infinitely many packets that last and share no member (lesson
  8.7). The growth records call the generator a source and a packet a
  product or a family. The literature's picture is the glider gun of Conway's
  Life, which makes one fixed pattern each time; under ordinary growth no start
  is known to keep releasing lasting packets of one fixed width, and in the
  class where every knot can be reconstructed from the start none can.
- **Fold of emergence.** The owner's phrase (23 November 2026, assumption
  A223), always written in full and in his words: a pattern that the rules make,
  where what it makes depends on another pattern, and that makes a pattern of
  its own,
  as he pictures DNA and intelligence. An interpretation. Under laws added by
  hand the record has cases in which what one made part builds differs between
  a run with another made part growing and a run with it barred, but none in
  which it differs with what the other is, or in which a part makes a pattern
  of its own from the other (lessons 8.7, 9.6, 10.1 and 10.3). It is not the
  two-fold of a two-fold weave, which counts knots, and the guide makes no
  claim about living things.
- **Copier.** The owner's word (24 November 2026: "a copier is a pattern the
  growth makes", assumption A244 and sub-goal B327; and "a 'copier'" in his
  inkling of the same evening, assumption A248). In the guide: a pattern the
  growth makes that meets a second made pattern and leaves two of the second.
  A copier is a pattern, never a law: the law (which knots join, what a new
  knot receives, what a short knot does) is supplied and named separately. A
  claim of a copier names the class of second patterns and, in the owner's
  words, whether "two of it" means "the same form, the same built structure,
  or the same values". The record has none. Not the part that copies itself
  (lesson 8.7), which takes nothing from another pattern and leaves two of
  itself, though its record's heading calls it "the copier"; and not the
  literature's replicator under HighLife, which also copies itself. The
  owner's inkling that no copier can be general is A248, a working premise
  (lesson 10.4).
- **Weaned; brood.** In the laws that make one birth at a time (lesson 8.1),
  a knot is weaned when none of its parents is still in the live roster: its
  parents have finished giving. The **brood** of a knot is the smallest set
  that holds it and, with each knot in it, every child of each of that knot's
  parents: the knots made alongside it. The intuition: a brood is one part's
  round, seen from the knots that round made.
- **Ancestral common knot; ancestor-taking.** A common knot of two members
  that is also a recorded ancestor of both and not the parent of both: the
  knot through which two sets of descendants are joined. The owner's phrase
  for it was "axis point", and afterwards perhaps "access point" (24 November
  2026, assumption A241). A **taking rule** is a rule added to a growth law
  under which a birth also takes a distinction that is already shared, by a
  supplied convention for which: rules R and Q take only what a parent holds,
  and the rule that takes from a knot the parent meets takes from no ancestor
  (lessons 10.0 and 10.3). **Ancestor-taking** is a law under which a birth
  also takes a distinction that an ancestor of one of its parents holds, so
  that the distinction gains a holder. Under the laws of lesson 8.1 a shared
  distinction has two holders; where a birth may take a distinction that is
  already shared, shared means held by two or more. In the study of such laws
  ([PARTS-MEET-AGAIN-20261124-001](research/PARTS-MEET-AGAIN-20261124-001/decision.md),
  computed, referee pending) such a knot was among the common knots of the
  members of two parts that had come apart and were one part again for 62
  pairs, and the only one for 6; it was always a start knot (lesson 10.3).
- **Course of a part; with-and-without test.** The **course** of a part X of
  the live roster after round s is what X and its descendants build, round by
  round: each birth whose parents all lie among them, with its parents, what
  it takes and the number of its fresh distinctions, and which sets of their
  members one knot meets, up to a renaming of the knots made after round s. A
  birth's demand is recorded separately and is not part of the course. The
  **with-and-without test** is the owner's test (by 24 November 2026, Pacific
  time: "run each start twice, once with the second part present and once
  without it. A part reads another when its course differs between the two
  runs") as one study measured it (assumption A246, a measure; the records'
  "paired test"). One start is grown under one law, and grown again with every
  usable loose distinction of a second part Y barred from round s, so that Y
  stays built and makes no birth, and X's course is compared between the two
  runs. Removing Y is the registered alternative, used only where it is
  defined; in the study of lesson 10.3 it was defined for 182 of 302 pairs and
  gave the same result for each. A masked run, in which no member of X or its
  descendants can join a member of Y or its descendants, is the study's check
  of whether a difference went through the two parts meeting (lesson 10.2).
  The test shows whether X's course differs with Y barred; it says nothing of
  what Y is. A course is not a history, which is one whole run.
- **Clock; tick.** A clock is anything in which one named event recurs and
  against which other changes are counted; each recurrence is a tick (lesson
  9.4). The guide always says which kind. A **supplied clock** is given by
  whoever runs a system: the update of every cell of the Game of Life at once
  (lesson 1.3), or a round applied to every live knot at once (lesson 8.1). A
  **made clock** is a pattern the growth builds from a supplied start in which
  the named event, a return or a release, recurs; the study names the event
  (assumption A150, a scaffold). "Made" speaks of the pattern only: the law,
  the start and whatever counts the ticks are named separately, and in the
  record so far whatever counts a made clock's ticks is supplied. A tick is
  the event, not the count of it. The owner's words: "Time doesn't have ticks"
  (by 18 November 2026, Pacific time); "what if 'time' is a device or pattern
  just like the other ones?" (23 November 2026, assumption A228).
- **Return; period.** A part of the live roster returns at a later round when
  the members then descending from it, a member that waits counting as its own
  descendant, include a part that is the same as it: the same up to a renaming
  that keeps each member's loose distinctions, usable and never used, and
  which sets of members one knot meets (lesson 8.7). It may be made of the
  same knots, which waited, or of new knots, and the study says which. A state
  returns when every distinction or cell again has its earlier value (lessons
  1.3 and 8.6). The **period** is the count from one return to the next,
  always with its unit: rounds, births, parent steps, updates or changes; a
  state of a harmony has its period in shifts (see Harmony). A release that
  recurs while each packet is larger than the last is not a return. Not the
  foundation's "native return artifact" and "causal return handoff", written
  in full, which are a runner's return of control.
- **Priority.** The owner's word (23 November 2026, assumption A229): "not
  really like a sequencer, but more like a priority system ... How often
  updates happen given some property". In the guide: a rule that says which of
  the parts or births that are ready goes first, by a named property of them
  (a part's number of members, the loose distinctions its members hold, the
  rounds its members have taken, a birth's age or kind), by stated chances, or
  in turn (lesson 9.5). One order of births taken by itself, as in a search
  over every order, is an order of births, not a priority. Which distinction a
  birth takes is not a priority. Every priority in the record so far is
  supplied.
- **Live roster.** What one round of the law reads: which junctions hold a
  loose distinction, how many, and which sets of them one junction meets
  (lesson 8.4). The growth records call it the growing end.
- **Three-way distance.** For three distinctions, the size of the smallest
  bond that holds all three (lesson 8.4).
- **Newest-w growth.** Growth in which every new knot holds only distinctions
  among the w that arrived last (lesson 7.3). The process supplies the order
  of arrival.
- **Tie class.** Two distinctions are **tied** when they are equal in every
  allowed state, or unequal in every allowed state (lesson 4.7). Being tied is
  an equivalence, and a tie class is one of its classes with two or more
  members. Under not-all-equal nothing is frozen, so a tie class is the most
  the law can force.
- **Minimal contradiction.** A set of knots that allows no state while every
  proper part of it allows one, minimal under removal and not necessarily
  smallest (lesson 7.3): the literature's minimal unsatisfiable subformula.
  The smallest for three-place not-all-equal knots is the Fano pattern of
  seven knots.
- **Step ball.** The distinctions within r steps of one distinction: a count
  on the meeting pattern (lesson 7.3). A ball, unqualified, is measured by
  bond distance.
- **Block; descent; finite-reader state.** In patterns placed by maps on
  addresses (lesson 4.8), the addresses one address is sent to by the maps form its
  orbit (the literature's word, as for a state under shifts; see Harmony). A **block**
  is a set of addresses at one level with their children one level down,
  holding the knots placed at that level whose members all lie in it; the
  block of an orbit holds every knot placed at the orbit, and blocks of
  orbits meet only a block and the one above it. A **descent** follows a
  block down the levels, each address extended by one letter, the letters
  eventually periodic (the literature's orbital Schreier graph along a
  rational point). A **finite-reader state** is a state at every depth whose
  value at each address a finite machine outputs after reading the address
  letter by letter (the literature's automatic sequence). The intuition: a
  block is a piece the pattern can be solved in, and a descent is how a few
  distinctions carry the top down without the rest of the level
  (`DIRECT-REACH-20261115-001`, 16 November 2026).
- **Pair projection; product; two-orbit bond; exposed; unexposed dependence.**
  The **pair projection** of two orbits is the projection of the allowed
  states onto them. It is a **product** when every pairing of the two
  orbits' own projections is allowed, so neither orbit says anything about
  the other. A **two-orbit bond** is a bond inside the two orbits that
  contains a distinction of each. An assignment of an orbit is **exposed**
  when some free set of the orbit's addresses takes its values in that
  assignment only. A dependence is **unexposed** when the pair projection is
  not a product but every missing pair has a side that is not exposed: the
  orbits depend on each other, but no bond inside them shows it, because one
  orbit's own constraint stands in the way. The intuition: a two-orbit bond
  is a bond that two orbits far apart hold between themselves alone, and an
  unexposed dependence binds them too, but only a larger set of addresses
  can show it (`KEEP-REACH-20261117-001`, 17 November 2026).
- **Stem.** For an orbit at one level and an orbit some levels below it, the
  stem of depth d is the upper orbit together with the lower orbit's
  ancestor orbits at the d levels below the upper one: the start of the way
  down from the top toward the lower orbit (the advisor's word). A bond inside
  the stem and the lower orbit, holding a distinction of each end, holds the
  top to the lower orbit, as a two-orbit bond does. Stems of depth 2 hold the
  top in three patterns (ten descriptions) where no bond inside two orbits does: their unexposed
  dependence shows once the orbits just below the top are held too
  (`CARRIER-20261117-001`, 17 November 2026).
- **Bracketing; bare bracketing.** A line (the one-order base) of n distinctions presented by the fewest betweenness knots,
  n − 2 of them, is presented exactly when its knots form a **bracketing**: every knot B(a, c, b) has c between a and b, its
  **halves** are the line's steps from a to c and from c to b, and each knot other than the **ends' knot** (the one holding
  both ends) lies directly within a half of exactly one other, while every half holds one knot directly within it or is a
  single step. The **bare bracketing** forgets which half: only which knot lies directly within which. Two knots are
  **step-disjoint** when they share no step, which is the same as neither lying within the other. The literature's bracketing,
  or rooted binary tree, drawn as a triangulation of a polygon rooted at a marked side; the bare one is the unlabelled rooted
  binary tree. The intuition: how the knots meet, read against the line, is a hierarchy of which knot lies within which, and the
  growth that reads which knots meet reads this hierarchy: a fill makes a knot's halves cross, and a second fill keeps two orders
  only when its knot is step-disjoint from the first or joins it to a knot it shares a pair with (`MEET-ORDERS-20261118-001`,
  18 November 2026).
- **Same way, other way.** For knots χ within ν within π of a bracketing, χ lies in ν **the same way** when it sits in ν at the
  same end as ν sits in π, and **the other way** otherwise. With the bare bracketing these choices make up the whole bracketing
  up to mirror image. The shared distinction of the line of five's three knots sits at an end when the chain lies the same way and
  next to an end when it lies the other way (`MEET-ORDERS-20261118-001`).
- **Odd set.** While no new distinction has added a state, each new distinction of a line takes, in every allowed state, the
  parity of an odd set of the line's distinctions: its odd set. The fill of three distinctions a < c < b is their parity (the
  advisor's parity form; `MEET-ORDERS-20261118-001`).
