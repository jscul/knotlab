## 2. The pairs of a growth

1. **A made pattern**: a part of the roster after a round s ≥ 1 every member of which was made after round 0. At s = 1
   X's members are children of start knots, so X's form is one round from the supplied start; cases are reported by s.
2. **The pairs of a growth**: for each round s from 1 to 4 (and less than the last round read), every ordered pair (X,
   Y) of parts of the roster after round s with X a made pattern, Y another part, and X and Y wholly apart (FD 1.6). Y
   may be a made pattern or hold start knots. When a round has more than eight such pairs, eight are sampled uniformly,
   without replacement, from the list of pairs in lexicographic order of their parts' smallest member names, with
   `random.Random(<the growth's seed> + '|P|<s>').sample(pairs, 8)`. Sampling uniformly keeps the rates independent of
   the numbering (PC, Settled 1); taking the first eight by name, as FD 5.8 does, would not. With each growth the number
   of ordered pairs (X made) that "wholly apart" removes is reported. (A scaffold; its swap, every pair, on the starts
   of three and four knots.)
3. **The meeting round** m of a pair: the first round m > s at which X and Y are one part again or one part for the
   first time (FD 4.3), read in the growth. Under a taking rule the growth makes the meeting.
4. **The unmet pairs**: the pairs with no meeting round by the last round read.

## 3. Descent and the readings

1. **Descent**: D 2.6 with the pair's s (X's own members are from X only). X's form and Y's form are the forms of X and
   Y in the roster after round s.
2. **The readings**, each read at every round t > s:
   - **form** (claimed; ticket A, D 3.1, with D 5.1's condition 3): Y's form is not X's form, and at least two parts are

   - **from both** (claimed; ticket B, DB 3.4): at least two parts the same as Y's form, each containing a member made
     after round s, every member descended from both;
   - **values, both from X** (claimed), under parity only: two usable loose made distinctions tied to one place v of Y,
     v free and tied to no place of X after round s, **both descended from X only**;
   - **values with one from both** and **values, both from both** (reported apart): the same with the two descended from
     X only and from both, or both from both. With Y barred nothing descends from Y, and masked nothing descends from
     both, so two of the controls of DC 4.3 cannot see these, nor from both (DC 4.3);

## 4. Cases

1. **The continuations of a pair** (the paired test of D 4.2, 4.3 and FD 5.6, with takes sampled): from the state after
   round s of the growth (DC 1.6), continuations under the same law, their takes sampled under the growth's weighting,
   through round 8 or to the knot bound, of four kinds, each with its seed (DC 1.4): **nothing barred** (`|BN|<s>|<i>`);
   **Y barred** (`|BY|<s>|<i>`: every usable loose distinction of Y's members barred, FD 5.1); **X barred**
   (`|BX|<s>|<i>`); and **X and Y masked** (`|BM|<s>|<i>`: FD 5.6, from round s + 1 no member of X or its descendants is
   joinable with a member of Y or its descendants, in the round and in its demand step). A continuation stopped by the
   knot bound or the guard is read through its last round read, and counts as not meeting an event not met by then; the
   number stopped is reported for each kind, and the rates are also given over the continuations not stopped.
   Continuations of different pairs of one round with the same kind and index have the same seed, and with nothing
   barred they are one continuation; under the family-wise rule of DC 4.4 this is harmless.
2. **Y's own**, in a continuation with X barred: two parts the same as Y's form, each containing a member made after
   round s, every member descended from Y only (for the form readings); or two usable loose made distinctions tied to
   one place of Y, both descended from Y only (for the values readings; D 3.5).
3. **A case** (his "hit", read): a pair of a sampled growth whose reading is met after the meeting. A case is one
   sample; whether it is met more often with Y is a comparison of rates. **The rates of a case**, from K′ continuations
   of each kind: q₁, the share with nothing barred in which the reading is met after the meeting, the meeting read in
   the continuation (and, reported, q₁ at any round after s); q₀, the share with Y barred in which the reading is met at
   a round after s; q_Y, the share with X barred in which Y's own is met; q_M, the share masked in which the reading is
   met at a round after s. The events differ (q₁ after the meeting, the controls at any round), and with nothing barred
   the knot bound is reached sooner; both lean toward the negative, and the test keeps them so. For from both and the
   values readings with a distinction from both, q₀ = 0 and q_M = 0 by construction (with Y barred nothing descends from
   Y; masked, no birth has a parent among X's descendants and one among Y's), and only q_Y tests anything.
4. **The test.** For each control, a one-sided Fisher exact test on the 2 × 2 table (met or not; nothing barred against
   the control), the null being that q₁ is not greater than the control's rate. **Passes at 100**: with K′ = 100 of each
   kind (i = 0 to 99, masked runs not read), q₁ above q₀ and above q_Y, each test at level 0.01, with no split of the
   level between them. **Confirmed**: of the m cases of the claimed readings (form, from both, values both from X) that
   pass at 100 in R1, R2, R5 and R6 of PC together, all counted before any confirmation, each is read on 1,000 further
   continuations of each of the four kinds (i = 100 to 1,099), and the three tests (Y barred, X barred, masked) are
   repeated on these alone, each at level 0.01/m. So over the ticket the chance of confirming any case whose q₁ is not
   greater than all three controls is at most 0.01 (family-wise, not a false-discovery rate: one confirmed case would
   overturn the main prediction). The reported readings (DC 3.2) form a second family, m′ counted the same way, each at
   0.01/m′. **A case met more often with Y**: a confirmed case. **A case that needs the meeting**: a case met more often
   with Y whose reading is met in none of the 1,000 continuations with Y barred and none of the 1,000 masked ones (D
   4.2's sense of "needs": none). **A one-history case**: a case met after the meeting in at most 2 of its 100
   continuations with nothing barred, reported with its numbers. **Power**: at 100, a case passes only with about 7 of
   100 against 0 (p = 0.007), so a zero speaks only of cases with q₁ about ten points above a control near zero; the
   difference q₁ − q is reported with its interval for every case. **The swap**: K′ = 300 at the first stage on the
   starts of three and four knots, with kinds `BN3`, `BY3`, `BX3` in place of `BN`, `BY`, `BX` and i = 0 to 299.
5. **The rate of cases**, for a law, a knot law and a reading, under a weighting of starts and one of takes: the mean
   over starts, by the weighting, of the share of a start's sampled growths holding a case met more often with Y; with
   it the same share for cases (any), for cases that pass at 100 and for one-history cases. Each rate is given with its
   sample size and seeds, by stratum (start size; sampled or read), and twice: over all growths, and over the growths
   with no failing knot (the same weighting conditioned on no failing knot, DC 8). No bound is given on a share of met
   pairs, since the pairs of one growth are not independent. **A zero bounds the rate**, each law separately: with K
   sampled growths of each of N starts and p_i the chance for start i, the chance of no case is Π(1 − p_i)^K ≤ exp(−K Σ
   p_i). Where the N starts are every start, under the census weighting a zero bounds the mean below 3/(K N) at
   confidence 0.95 (e^−3 < 0.05); where they are a prefix of the order of DC 1.5, a uniform sample without replacement,
   the same bound holds for the census's mean (sampling without replacement is no worse than with it). Under the
   labelled weighting, its mean is at most max w / w̄ times the census weighting's (w̄ the mean weight: 76.7 over the
   3,344 starts passing under parity, 76.2 over the 3,376; max w = 120), so a zero bounds it below (max w / w̄) × 3/(K
   N), about 4.7/(K N). For all 84 laws at once the bound is about 7.4/M in place of 3/M (ln(84/0.05) = 7.4). The
   sentence registered for a zero: "No growth held a confirmed case. Under the [weighting], the rate of growths holding
   a case the test can see is below [bound] at 0.95, each law separately. Smaller differences are not bounded."
6. **The unmet-pair rate**: the share of unmet pairs on which a reading is met by the last round read; a reading met on
   pairs that never meet is not the meeting's doing.

## 5. Candidates

1. **A candidate**: under one law and knot law, one form F of made pattern with cases met more often with Y for at least
   two second-pattern forms G, each other than F (in one growth or in several), under the take-by-take weighting.
2. **A candidate seen again** (his "copier", read under sampling): for each G of the candidate and each start where a
   case (F, G) met more often with Y was seen, 100 further growths of that start under the same law (sample indices
   1,000 to 1,099, seeds as DC 1.4), every pair read; at least 3 of the 100 each hold a case (F, G) met more often with
   Y, confirmed in **stage 2's own family** (its m₂, the cases (F, G) of all stage-2 growths that pass at 100, counted
   before any of them is confirmed, each at level 0.01/m₂); the rate is reported with its exact interval, and no
   percentage is named. **Not of the others**, read on the same growths: for each other form G′ that F met there, the
   rate per met pair (F, G′) of two parts of G's form (any descent, a member made after round s) after the meeting is
   below the rate per met pair (F, G) of the same, by a one-sided Fisher exact test at level 0.05, reported with the
   number of met pairs (F, G′); a G′ with fewer than 20 met pairs, too few for the test to reject, is reported as not
   read, so that "not for others" is not taken from low power. Also reported, from R1: how many starts make F and meet G
   under the census weighting.
3. **Again in the same growth**: whether a form F with one case has another case in the same growth, against the forms
   with none (counted under the take-by-take and census weightings, reported).

## The question

The owner, 24 November 2026 (Pacific time; the record's tickets carry 25 November): "What if we need to statistically
hit a 'copier' like DNA and primordial soup?" His standing words for the study keep a law and a pattern apart: "a law is
the rule the growth follows (which knots join, what a new knot receives, what a short knot does); a copier is a pattern
the growth makes." Read (claim, section 1): under a stated family of laws with a taking rule, with the record's census
of small starts read (every start under parity) and every take sampled, how often does the growth make a pattern that,
on meeting a second pattern the growth also made or kept, leaves two of it more often than the law does without the
meeting, and is any one pattern seen doing so for several second patterns, again in fresh growths? DNA and a primordial
soup are his pictures; every pattern that meets here is descended from one supplied start of three to five knots, and
the meetings are made by the law's takes.

## In standard terms

In the literature's words, not the program's: each law with its takes sampled is a Markov process on finite states
(hypergraphs whose hyperedges carry a parity equation over the field of two elements or a not-all-equal condition,
together with their bars, retirements and record of parents), started from a stated distribution over small connected
starts; a sampled growth is one trajectory; a rate is the chance of an event under the process, estimated from
independent trajectories, a zero bounded by the rule of three; the continuations are a paired comparison of the process
continued from one state with nothing barred, with one component barred, with the other barred, and with the two kept
from joining; a case met more often with Y is a difference of binomial proportions, first passed and then confirmed on
fresh trajectories with a Bonferroni correction for the number of cases tested. A candidate seen again would be what the
origin-of-life literature calls, as the nearest term in that literature, a **template replicator** (a pattern that
leaves copies of patterns other than itself), as against a pattern that copies itself, which the record already has.

## Settled by construction (stated, not tested)

Arguments of the drafter, not refereed; the runs check them.

1. **Lemma C-S1 (renaming, with takes sampled).** *Statement.* Let φ be a renaming of a start: a one-to-one map of its
   knots and a one-to-one map of its distinctions (any order of names; DC 1.1 names distinctions by label). For every
   law of the family and both swaps of the law, under the take-by-take and the holder weightings, the weighting of the
   sequence of states (DC 1.6: the structure with its barred marks, its retired knots, the birth record, the parity
   rules, the round number) grown from φ(start) is the image, under renamings extending φ, of the weighting grown from
   the start. So every event defined up to renaming (forms, descent, ties, the readings, the meeting round, the
   continuations of DC 4.1 with their kinds, pairs sampled uniformly by DC 2.2, the knot test) has the same chance, and
   every rate of DC 4.5 is the same for every numbering of the start. *Argument*, by induction on rounds, from two
   states S and S′ = ψ(S), ψ a renaming of states (knots and distinctions, keeping holders, bars, retirements, parents,
   kinds, rounds made):
   - (a) The roster, met sets, joinability, groups, parts, owing and short members are defined from the state without
     names (FD 1, SD), so ψ carries them.
   - (b) **Rations.** FLOOR1, FBP, CAP-2, CAP-3, CAP-4, ORD and RATION-2 give a birth's fresh count as a function of p
     (its distinct inputs, or its parents under FBP) and its demand D only (the fold engine's `Law.fresh_count`); none
     uses names or the order of making.
   - (c) **Treatments, RELEASE and HOLD** use names only to say which usable loose distinction of a member goes to which
     birth, or is kept, or is barred. Two runs of the round from S and from S′ can therefore differ, beyond ψ, only by a
     permutation of each member's usable loose distinctions, and such a permutation is a renaming of the state,
     **because a loose distinction has one holder** (and a usable one is not barred): exchanging two of them keeps every
     holding, bar and birth record. The knot laws' indifference to the order of a knot's places is needed only so that
     ties and the knot test are carried too.
   - (d) **Takes.** Under MET, STEPS-1 and STEPS-2 a birth's candidates and their eligibility are read on the state at
     the start of the round and leave out only the birth's own inputs (FD 2.2, 2.4; the fold engine's step 5′: one list
     per taking birth, `take_candidates` read on the structure before the round); one birth's take changes no other
     birth's list. The take-by-take weighting gives each candidate of a list one over its length; the holder weighting a
     chance in proportion to its number of holders at the start of the round; both are read off the state, so ψ carries
     each list with its chances, and the takes of different births are independent. The order in which the takes are
     called changes which sample a seed gives, not the weighting.
   - (e) **Demand.** The round's takes change other births' demand (the demand roster is read after the takes, DC 1.6;
     the fold engine's step 6), and so their fresh count under ORD and CAP-k. Demand is a fixed function of the state
     and of the round's takes together, so it is carried.
   - (f) **Implied births** (parity, IMPLIED) are each tested against the parity rules before the round with their own
     inputs: carried. **Joins of three or more parents**: names enter only through birth order: carried. New knots and
     fresh distinctions are named in birth order; a renaming of them extends ψ.
   - (g) **The guard** (checked in the fold engine, `pm.py` lines 20–38, 389–548, 789, 820, 1005): it compares with a
     fixed limit the number of incidences, of candidate pairs within the largest met set, of joinable pairs, of maximal
     cliques, of groups, of births and of distinctions. Each is a function of the state. The cliques are counted by
     networkx's `find_cliques` on a graph built in name order, so the clique at which the count passes its limit depends
     on names, but the count passes the limit exactly when the number of maximal cliques does, which does not; the
     joinable pairs are counted met set by met set in name order, and the running count passes the limit exactly when
     the total does. So whether the guard stops a round does not depend on names, and "a round the guard stops is not
     read" is carried. (Ticket A's engine has a bound reached in name order in its search for renamings of Y's knots,
     `mc.py` lines 560–612; ticket C does not read built structure.)
   - (h) **Eight pairs sampled uniformly** (DC 2.2): carried in weighting. **Seeds** name the census's numbering: they
     change which sample is seen, not a rate.
   *Why ticket B's renaming statement does not suffice.* DB 7 is stated for no taking rule and says that under a taking
   rule it fails in general; the failure came from choosing a candidate by name (OLDEST, NEWEST, IN-TURN, BY-COUNT).
   With every take sampled, each candidate's chance is read off the state, and (d) replaces that step. *Status*: argued

## What a result would and would not show

- **Verdicts, three questions** (DC 6). A case is made only when X and Y are made patterns, the knots through which they
  meet are made by births, and the takes are sampled.
- **The scale.** About 182,000 sampled growths of at most 8 rounds under parity and the take-by-take weighting (MET at
  every start twice, STEPS-1 at 2,000 starts once), 102,000 under the holder weighting, about 46,000 under not-all-equal
  (MET at 1,900 starts, STEPS-1 at 270), and about 37,000 in the swaps of the law, each run within about an hour
  ("Runs"). His "statistically" suggests very many tries; these are many short growths from starts of at most five
  knots, and every bound is stated in those terms.
- **What the study can say and cannot.** It can say whether sampling, at this scale and under these weightings, makes a
  case met more often with Y (his "hit", read) and a candidate seen again; it cannot say whether a copier can arise only
  by such chance, or that none can.
- **What follows a case**, whether the two left are met and left twice again, is read only where DC 5.3 ("again in the
  same growth") reads it, and stage 2 (DC 5.2) reads only whether the pair of forms comes back in fresh growths.
- **A candidate seen again** would show, under one law and one weighting of takes, a made pattern that leaves two of
  each of at least two second-pattern forms it meets, more often with them than without, in fresh growths of the same

## 6. Facts used (each argued in P, "Settled by construction")

1. **S1, ties are decided at birth.** Under a law in which every birth either receives a fresh distinction or (parity,
   IMPLIED) adds a rule already in the span, every allowed state before a round extends, whether or not any knot passes,
   so the allowed states restricted to the distinctions existing after round t are the same after every later round. It
   excludes LITERAL, FLOOR0 and ONE-FEWER.
2. **S2, not-all-equal.** Every birth with a fresh distinction: a fresh distinction f of a birth u is tied to another
   distinction exactly when u has one fresh distinction and its inputs are all tied equal before its round; f is then
   tied unequal to each input. So, from a start with no tie, no made distinction is ever tied to another, whether or not
   knots pass. From a start with a tie it can be.
3. **S3, parity.** A birth with two or more fresh distinctions hands nothing on (this uses IMPLIED); a birth u with one
   fresh distinction f has f tied to v exactly when the sum, over the field of two elements, of u's inputs and v lies in
   the span of the rules before u's round, zero included; a passing maker of a distinction tied to v never holds v.
4. **S4, one fresh distinction.** Under MET or STEPS-1, with RELEASE or HOLD, FLOOR1 and the fresh count by distinct
   inputs, every birth other than an offshoot of a start knot receives exactly one fresh distinction (none, under parity,
   when it is implied); under STEPS-2 likewise, except also offshoots of knots whose parents are all start knots. So every
   knot such a birth makes holds **at most one** loose distinction.
5. **S5, S6.** Parity with at most two holders: ties are two-element cuts. Not-all-equal with at most two holders: every
   distinction can change alone. Record facts (survey 1.1, 1.2). The registered designs have at most two holders and so
   no tie; the starts with a third pattern give e three holders, so S6 does not cover them, and their tie classes at
   round 0 are computed (P, run G): none has a tie or a frozen distinction under either knot law.
6. **S7.** A passing knot with three or more places has no two of its own places tied (a two-place knot left by an
   implied birth has both places tied and passes).

## 7. The renaming statement (argued in PB, Settled 1)

Under a law with no taking rule (DB 1.1, with RELEASE or HOLD, either knot law), for a start S and any renaming σ of its
knots and distinctions, after each round t there is a renaming σ_t carrying the structure grown from S onto the
structure grown from σ(S), such that σ_t agrees with σ on the start's knots and on the distinctions shared or barred at
the start, and on each member's usable loose distinctions agrees with σ only up to a permutation of that member's usable
loose distinctions. The bounds on rounds and knots and the guard are counts, so σ(S) stops when S does. Every reading of
D and DB defined up to renaming (forms, descent, the conditions, the knot test, the counts) is then the same for S and
σ(S). **Version 1's wording**, "σ_t agrees with σ on the start's knots and distinctions", is false: a start member holding
loose a before b makes one join and one offshoot; σ exchanges the names of a and b; in S the join takes a, in σ(S) it
takes σ(b), so σ_1 carries a to σ(b). Nothing in ticket B uses a start distinction's name, so no reading changes; a
values reading would need the repaired wording. Under a taking rule the statement fails in general.

# Parts that meet again: definitions

## 0. Words

- **Kept with their meaning in the definitions:** start, structure, place, loose, live, dead, usable, barred, member,
  h(u), k(u), met set, common knot, joinable, part, past its start, the same, group, owing, short, waits, retires, join,
  offshoot, demand, ration, treatment, round, come apart, returns, ended (definitions lines 9–93).
- **Shared** means held by two or more knots. The number of holders is given wherever it matters.
- **Take.** A birth takes a distinction; a shared distinction it takes gains a holder. This study's own laws use no other
  verb for the move. The other thread's records call it "re-read"; the word is theirs and appears only in citations.
- **Reads** is kept for the owner's sense: a part reads another when its course depends on whether the other is there.
  Rules and engines **use** or **look at** what they need.
- **The choice** is the rule that says which of several eligible shared distinctions a birth takes (section 2, item 5).
  The owner's "index" keeps his sense: which part of another pattern is looked at. Under the laws that take from beyond
  the parent (MET and the ancestor-taking laws) the choice decides which knots the new knot meets, and so stands in for
  his index there.
- New here, each defined where it first appears: **parent step, ancestor, the descendants of g, common ancestor,
  ancestral common knot, share nothing, wholly apart, one part again, one part for the first time, eligible, taking
  rule, parent-taking, ancestor-taking, course, trace, the paired test, the four marks of a pair, way**.
- "A knot meets only its parents and its children" holds **under the earlier laws** (the definitions, with every
  distinction held once or twice) and is never used here without that qualifier.
- "Root", the owner's candidate for the knot a set of knots descends from, is not adopted: the birth record is not a
  tree, since a join has two or more parents, and "root" is already used for the root of an equation that gives a rate.

## 1. Structures in which a distinction may have any number of holders

1. A distinction has one or more holders: **loose** with one, **shared** with two or more (definitions line 12,
   extended). A knot's places are fixed when it is made. Two facts used below:
   - **holders never decrease**: a birth adds itself as a holder of what it takes, and nothing removes a holder (run
     B(ii) of the paired test, section 5, is the one construction that does, and it is defined only where stated);
   - **a barred distinction is never unbarred**, and a knot never gains a place, so a knot that is no member never
     becomes one.
2. Two knots **meet** when they hold a common distinction, whatever its number of holders; the m holders of one
   distinction meet pairwise (the definitions' line 14, read for any number of holders; as the other thread's engines
   read it, `rlaw.py` lines 372–381).
3. Live, dead, member, h, k, met set, common knot, joinable, part, groups, owing and short are worded as in the
   definitions (lines 14–40), read with this meeting. **A knot is not in its own met set**: the met set of w is the set of
   members other than w that w meets, when there are at least two.
4. **Parents** are written by each birth (definitions lines 49–51; `LOCAL-ROUND-20261123-001/definitions.md` lines
   27–31): the birth record is not a reading of the holders. A start knot has none. One **parent step** goes from a knot to
   one of its parents. The **ancestors** of a knot are the knots reached by one or more parent steps; the **descendants of
   g** are the knots that have g as an ancestor, g not included; **X and its descendants**, for a set X, includes X.
   Start knots have no ancestors. A **common ancestor** of two knots is an ancestor of both; it says nothing of meeting.
5. An **ancestral common knot** of two members u and v is a common knot of u and v that is a recorded ancestor of each and
   not the parent of both. (The owner approved the term on 24 November 2026.) The start's own common knot of two start
   members, which is no recorded ancestor of their descendants, is not an ancestral common knot and is reported as its
   own class (section 5, item 7).
6. **Share nothing and wholly apart.** Two parts of a roster **share nothing** when no member of one holds a distinction
   that a member of the other holds. Link two parts when they share something; the **classes** of a roster are the sets
   of parts connected by such links. Two parts are **wholly apart** when they lie in different classes: the parts of the
   roster can then be split into two sets, one holding each, such that no part of one set shares anything with a part
   of the other. (A third part may not link them.)

## 2. One complete round with taking

Steps 1 to 8 of the definitions (lines 34–67), with these additions and exact readings.

1. **Order of births and names.** The joins are the kept groups: groups of three or more in lexicographic order of their
   sorted member names, then pairs in the same order. Then the offshoots, in increasing name of the member; a member's
   offshoots in increasing name of the loose distinction each takes. Each member gives its usable loose distinctions, in
   increasing name, to its births in birth order (its joins first). Under HOLD a member keeps the first of its usable
   loose distinctions after those its joins take, and its offshoots take the rest (as the ring study's engine does). New
   knots are named in birth order once every birth of the round is formed; their fresh distinctions are named after the
   demand step, in birth order, each knot's usable ones before its barred ones. A start's distinctions are named by its
   stars' labels in increasing order. The swap "order of births reversed" reverses the whole list of births before any
   distinction is given out.
2. **Eligible.** A distinction is **eligible** for a take when it has two or more holders at the start of the round. A
   distinction loose at the start of the round is never eligible. Several births may take one distinction in one round.
3. **SHORT-TAKES** (rule R, A230, as a treatment of a short member, in place of the base law's treatment). No group is
   cancelled. A short member gives its usable loose distinctions to its first births in birth order and, for each further
   birth, takes one distinction it holds that is eligible, is held at the start of the round by no other parent of that
   birth, and differs from the others it gives this round; which one, by the choice (OLDEST or NEWEST, ALL-CHOICES on small
   cases). A short member owes more than it holds, so it makes no offshoots. If some short member has no allowed
   distinction for one of its births, no birth is made in the round (a **residual stall**); the structure is then
   unchanged, so the residual stall repeats for ever and the run stops.
4. **Step 5′, the taking rule.** After the round's births are formed and before any is made, the law's taking rule (section
   3) adds distinctions to some births, read on the structure at the start of the round, birth by birth in birth order.
   Every taking rule adds at most one distinction to a birth, except CARRY, under which a join takes up to one for each of
   its parents. A take never repeats a distinction the birth already takes; the fresh count uses **distinct** inputs.
5. **The choice.** Among the eligible distinctions a birth may take, sorted by name:
   - **OLDEST**: the smallest name; **NEWEST**: the largest (A231, A236). Both use the names, and so the start's numbering.
   - **IN-TURN**: defined for offshoots: the i-th offshoot of one parent in the round (counting from zero, in birth order)
     takes the i-th, cycling. With one offshoot per parent it equals OLDEST. A join under MET(IN-TURN) takes by OLDEST;
     IN-TURN is not used with SHORT-TAKES.
   - **BY-COUNT**: the taking birth's parent's number of places (for a join, its first parent's, by name), modulo the
     number of eligible distinctions, counting from the oldest at zero. It uses the structure only.
   - **BY-RECEIVED** (a named case only): the parent's number of places less its number of parents, modulo the number of
     eligible distinctions, counting from zero. It uses the birth record.
   - **ALL-CHOICES**: every choice enumerated (small cases).
6. **Step 6, demand.** As the definitions, with the met sets read from the structure after the takes and before the fresh
   distinctions, so that a knot that took a shared distinction is already met by that distinction's other holders (the
   other thread's lookahead, `rlaw.py` lines 480–494). Swap: **demand before the takes**, read on the structure in which
   each birth holds only what it took loose (and, under SHORT-TAKES, what it was given).
7. **Step 7, fresh distinctions.** The ration's formula with p read as the number of distinct distinctions the birth takes
   (A235, `ONE-PARENT-REREAD-20261123-001A/preregistration.md` line 47). For a birth that takes nothing extra this is its
   number of parents, so nothing in the definitions changes. Swap: **fresh by parents**.
8. **Closing (parity).**
   - **IMPLIED** (primary): a birth receives no fresh distinction when the parity of what it takes lies in the span of the
     rules of the knots of the structure before the round (A230's form, `REREAD-...-001A/preregistration.md` line 47).
     Each birth of the round is tested against the structure before the round, not in sequence, so that the result does
     not depend on the order of births within the round. Several births of one round may be implied. The definitions'
     clauses "it is then the round's only birth, nothing is barred and no old member keeps anything" do not apply.
   - **LITERAL**: the definitions' exception (line 63), a swap only, never in a paired test (any barred distinction
     switches it off everywhere, definitions lines 73–74, and run B(i) always bars).
   - With every distinction held at most twice, in a connected structure, the two agree (the settled statement of
     `RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001A/preregistration.md` lines 100–101). A birth that IMPLIED leaves with no fresh distinction
     and that then fails the knot test is a failure.

### 3.4 The A225 control, placed by hand

then held by exactly two knots: one that X's lowest-named member holds with one other knot, and one that Y's
lowest-named member holds with one other knot (the oldest such, by name). It receives one fresh distinction and has no

### 3.5 The HOLD arm

## 4. Coming apart, and one part again

1. **Come apart** keeps the definitions' sense (line 87): the members after round t descended from a part after round s
   lie in two or more parts.
2. **The descendants of X after round s**, X a part after round s: the knots made after round s that have a parent among X
   and its descendants.
3. Two parts X and Y after round s that are wholly apart, and that descend from one part after an earlier round, are
   **one part again** at round t > s when a member of X or its descendants and a member of Y or its descendants lie in one
   part after round t. When X and Y have no common part in their ancestry they are **one part for the first time**. The
   owner's "meet again" is quoted only in his words.

## 5. The paired test

1. **Runs.** Run A grows the start under the law. For a round s and two parts X and Y after round s:
   - **B(i), the second part barred** (primary): run A through round s; then every usable loose distinction of Y's members
     is barred; Y stays built and makes nothing more; then the same law.
   - **B(ii), the second part never made** (swap): run A through round s; then Y's members are removed with their fresh
     distinctions, and each distinction they took returns to its other holder, loose and barred. It is defined only when
     no knot outside Y holds a fresh distinction of a member of Y and every distinction a member of Y took has exactly one
     other holder; otherwise it is reported "not defined".
   - Runs are compared only through the last round both reached, with the same bounds on rounds and knots.
2. **The trace of X.** For each round t > s: the births of round t all of whose parents are in X or its descendants (a
   joint birth, with a parent outside them, is excluded; its children are included), each with its kind, its parents, what
   it takes, which of those were shared at the start of the round, its fresh count and its usable and barred fresh
   counts; the members after round t in X or its descendants with h and k; and their met sets, each met set intersected
   with X and its descendants first and then reduced to the inclusion-maximal ones. Knots made by round s and
   distinctions existing after round s keep their names; knots made after round s for X and its descendants, and their
   fresh distinctions, are renamed in order of name (an order-preserving renaming); any other new knot or distinction is
   "other". **The course of X** is its trace. The demand of X's births is recorded beside the trace and reported on its
   own line (the first round at which it differs); it is not part of the course.
3. **The four marks of a pair** (facts about run A, and comparisons; not a scale):
   - **one part again**: the first round t > s at which they are one part again (section 4);
   - **a joint birth**: the first round of run A with a birth that has a parent in X or its descendants and a parent in Y
     or its descendants;
   - **the course differs**: X's trace in run A and in run B(i) differ (the first round);
   - **the course differs with what the other is**: X's trace differs between two runs A from starts that differ only in
     the holdings of the start knot Y descends from (renaming every knot and distinction of the trace by first
     appearance, since the two starts are numbered differently).
4. **The choice is necessary** (a control, not a mark): the same start and law, with only the takes of X and its
   descendants using another choice (OLDEST, NEWEST, IN-TURN, BY-COUNT, BY-RECEIVED) while every other birth keeps the
   law's own; reported: whether the course differs under each.
5. **The way** of a "course differs" mark, by the first differing step of the first differing round, in the order of the
   round's own steps: **a group or owing** (the groups containing a member of X or its descendants, or their owing); **a
   round-wide trigger** (a stall, a residual stall or a closing differs between the runs); **a take**; **fresh** (the
   fresh counts, through the demand).
6. **Confirmation through a meeting.** A masked run M is run A in which, from round s + 1, no member of X or its
   descendants is joinable with a member of Y or its descendants (the pair is removed from the joinable relation in the
   round and in its demand step). The difference is **through a meeting** when X's trace in M equals its trace in B(i). If
   not, an isolating run M′ makes no member of Y or its descendants joinable with any other member; when X's trace in M′
   equals B(i), the difference is **through a third part**.
7. **The verdict** of each "course differs" mark:
   - **through a knot the growth made**: confirmed through a meeting (or a third part), and at the round they are one part
     again X's and Y's descendants are joinable using only met sets that have a common knot made after round 0;
   - **through a start knot** (the start's own meeting reopened): confirmed, and joinable only through met sets whose
     common knots are start knots;
   - **supplied**: the way is a round-wide trigger; or the law is FIRST(PARTS) and the way is a take (its rule looked for
     the other part); or the law is RECORD-k or ALL-JOINABLE (the record alone);
   - **unconfirmed**: none of these.
   With each verdict, the class of the knots linking the two at that round: an ancestral common knot (made, or a start
   knot that is a recorded ancestor); a knot made by the growth that is no ancestor of both; a start knot that is no
   recorded ancestor of both (the start's common knot of the founders).
8. **Pairs.** For each round s from 1 to 6: the parts after round s in order of their smallest member name; the first eight;
   every pair (i, j), i < j, in lexicographic order, that is wholly apart, at most four per round. Each pair is labelled as
   having come apart (a common part in its ancestry) or not. In the HOLD arm, and on the two-knot start's own parts, every
   pair is taken and labelled wholly apart or not.

## 6. The two hypotheses that split the laws

- **H1, parents only.** Every distinction a new knot takes is held, at the start of its round, by one of its parents; its
  other places are fresh.
- **H2, joinable from the structure.** Two members are joinable only when some knot meets both, and every birth's parents
  lie in one part of the roster its round reads.
- The earlier laws, Q and SHORT-TAKES (every choice, every start round), CARRY, and every ration, treatment, release rule,
  extra and closing of the definitions satisfy H1 and H2. RECORD-k and ALL-JOINABLE break H2. MET, STEPS-k, UP-TO-k and
  FIRST break H1. The A225 control breaks H1 once, by hand.

## 7. The knot test with three or more holders

- **Parity**, two methods that must agree on every knot: **ranks** (the definitions' line 133), which needs no limit on
  holders; and **kernel**, a basis of the allowed states projected onto the knot's places, rank |S| − 1. The definitions'
  cut method needs two holders per shared distinction and is not used. Both are checked against brute force before any run.
- **Not-all-equal**: a SAT solver for each of the 2^|S| assignments of each knot's places (definitions lines 137–139).
  Under not-all-equal with a ration that gives every birth a fresh distinction (ORD, CAP-k, FLOOR1, RATION-k), every round
  extends (a birth with a fresh distinction forbids no old word), and a knot's relation on its places does not change
  after its birth: a knot tested at any round after its birth is tested for every later round. Under FLOOR0 and ONE-FEWER
  a birth can receive nothing, and every knot is tested at every round.
- **The birth method** (not-all-equal, for knots of any size, where every birth receives a fresh distinction). A knot u
  born in round t passes, at round t and every later round, exactly when its inputs that existed before round t are
  jointly free in the structure before round t (every assignment of them extends to an allowed state). The argument: at
  the end of round t every other birth of the round can be satisfied by one of its own fresh distinctions, which nothing
  else holds yet, so the allowed states on u's places are those whose old part is allowed before round t and which are
  not all equal; and u's own fresh distinction completes any old part to one that is not all equal. Joint freedom of a
  set I is decided exactly by removing, again and again, every knot that has a distinction held by it alone among the
  knots left and not in I (such a knot can always be satisfied); splitting what is left into pieces linked by
  distinctions outside I; and testing, for each piece, every assignment of the distinctions of I it touches (half of them,
  by the symmetry of flipping every distinction). A start knot is tested directly. The method is checked against the
  direct test on every knot of at most 12 places of run A, and on drawn grown structures with failing knots.
- Each run's knots are tested under the run's own knot law. A knot that passes has, in the whole structure, exactly its
  own rule on its places: constrained as a whole, every part free, some state allowed (K1–K3 with forgetting).
- With three or more holders a birth with a fresh distinction need not pass: its inputs may be constrained together. The
  earlier laws' settled statement (`RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001A/preregistration.md` lines 100–105) needs two holders.
