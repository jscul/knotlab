# Fixed map for the two continuing seven-knot starts

The common birth with parents $U,V$ receives **two fresh distinctions with $W$ exhausted and one with $W$ live**, under parity and not-all-equal. This count is fixed across all fair orders of the completed map. Making the same birth first under the archived present-state rule gives **one in either source**. ## Admission and the complete map

Take the physical meetings $US,VS,WS,SA,AB,BW,AZ$, with nine distinct shared distinctions on each. Both sources have eight loose distinctions. $U,V$ each have two. The others belong to $Z$ in the quiet source and split two each between $W,Z$ in the shifted source. The two moved distinctions keep their names. The physical presentations are connected, every distinction has one or two holders, and every source knot has at least three places. Under parity, the support of each source knot is a bond. After deleting that knot the other holders still connect through ordinary meetings and the auxiliary exterior for loose distinctions. Under not-all-equal, fix any allowed assignment on a single source knot. Every surviving ordinary meeting has enough distinctions to display both values. The remaining ordinary knots that can lose all such meetings are $U,V$ when $S$ is deleted and $Z$ when $A$ is deleted. Each has at least two unpinned loose distinctions. Thus any allowed assignment on the chosen knot extends to the other knots under either law. The sole block of size at least two on the original live members is witnessed by $S$. It is $\{U,V\}$ in the quiet source and $\{U,V,W\}$ in the shifted one. The latter is held by $S$, so its three pairs are separate first births. The two opens at each of those three parents fund its two pairs. $Z$ has no candidate and gives only one-parent births at first. Each live pair has at most one common witness among *all* old knots. There are no first groups of size three or more, no shortage and more than one initial loose distinction. This verifies S3, S4, S0 and S6′ of the identification theorem with multiple shared distinctions. In particular, nine distinctions shared with the same knot count as one witness. The two pieces of the map of joins have no incidence cycles to unroll. In the quiet source, each of $U,V$ has one leftover distinction after its pair. This attaches one member, $a_1$ to $U$ and $b_1$ to $V$. Each attached member carries one endless chain under the rule for two initial opens. ## The unordered successors of the common birth

Write $P=\{U,V\}$ for the cluster of the marked birth. An immediate successor is a cluster formed by adding one member for which $P$ is a parent. Its parents are the one-member deletions that leave admitted clusters. Here the lists are exhaustive.

| Source | Immediate successor | Its parents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quiet | $\{U,V,a_1\}$ | $\{U,V\},\{U,a_1\}$ |
| Quiet | $\{U,V,b_1\}$ | $\{U,V\},\{V,b_1\}$ |
| Shifted | $\{U,V,W\}$ | $\{U,V\},\{U,W\},\{V,W\}$ |

In the quiet source, the next members on the two chains require their preceding attached members, so they cannot be added directly to $P$. The remote piece contributes no successor of $P$. In the shifted source, the relay member can only be added to the whole $\{U,V,W\}$ cluster, so it does not contribute a second successor of $P$. The triple shown here is assembled from three already-born pairs. It is a different event from an initial three-parent proposal on $U,V,W$, which the first roster does not admit.

The completed map gives one outgoing incidence from $P$ per listed successor. Since all listed successors take inputs from at least two parents, this count is also the demand $d$ of $P$. Its two-parent nonclosing floor is one, so $f=\max(1,d)$ gives two and one, respectively. In the identified-class formula, $h(U)+h(V)-t=4-t$ with witness block size $t=2$ in the quiet source and $t=3$ in the shifted source. A unary successor also consumes a distinction but does not add to multi-parent demand.

A bare cluster enumerator only lists the admitted sets and possible parent incidences of the map it receives. The source data and identification theorem specify which map to use, including the live-witness blocks, leftover members, chains and relays. The numerical rule specifies the least fresh count. In this class it agrees with the number of outgoing incidences of the completed map: assign a fresh distinction when its supplying cluster is born, and share it when the eventual consumer is born. The two fresh labels at $P$ in the quiet arm are allocated even if neither consumer has been born. The map is specified from the source and carries that future information.

## Partial orders and physical distinctions

Start with all seven original knots. A fixed-map event is admitted after every parent cluster exists and it occurs once. Each prospective parent-to-child incidence has its own distinction. A source allocates its loose labels to its outgoing incidences once. Every later event consumes its designated input at each parent and allocates the distinctions for its own outgoing incidences. No competing event can consume an input reserved for that event. Therefore eligibility persists, and parent precedence alone governs the structural serializations. With a fixed allocation the literal supports agree once the same events have occurred. If the allocation of indistinguishable loose slots is varied, the structural comparison is up to a renaming of those slots. Fixing the initial loose labels pointwise also fixes their allocation when asserting literal equality.

Each finite cluster has finitely many ancestors, so every fair serialization eventually builds every prescribed cluster, by induction over prerequisites. Fairness means an event that remains enabled is eventually selected. An infinite legal order may keep choosing from one piece and starve the other. Even fair orders can have different finite prefixes. In the quiet source one can build $\{U,a_1\}$, then $P$, then $\{U,V,a_1\}$ before $\{V,b_1\}$. In the shifted source the three first pairs can form $\{U,V,W\}$ before any child of $Z$. Each maximal serialization of a finite parent-closed truncation finishes after exactly its number of events.

Under either law no event in such a finite serialization takes every currently loose distinction. In the other piece a largest built cluster has a successor and that successor is still absent. Its allocated distinction is loose. Connectivity of the old physical presentation persists, so under parity a proper subset of the loose distinctions is jointly free by the parity sum argument. For not-all-equal, remove the loose inputs of the proposed event from the old holder incidence. Each old parent retains at least two places, and the remaining ordinary incidence is still connected. With any unpinned loose distinction represented by a separate degree-one tip, the resulting multigraph has a high-degree knot from the original source, so it is not an odd cycle. The archived two-colour lemma gives both values at each old knot using unpinned distinctions alone. This extends every assignment of the selected inputs. A new event has at least three places and at least one fresh distinction. Its parity or not-all-equal relation preserves every old permitted assignment and has its full intended projection on its own support. Thus the physical support construction and the fresh count agree across the two laws, whose constraints on assignments differ. These serialization statements concern eligibility from the fixed map and allocated distinctions. Under the archived present-state rule, if the common $U,V$ pair is made first, its new knot meets exactly $U,V$. No other knot currently live meets either of them, so the newborn has present demand zero. The nonclosing floor is one in both sources, under both laws. A law that first lists and budgets all the births of a complete step has another input, the full roster for that step, and can serialize that roster with its preassigned budgets.
