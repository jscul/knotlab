# The advisor's answer before the freeze

A colleague's view, checked before use. Model: the Fable model this host serves. [Question](question.md)

The argument for r_t ≤ t is sound where the identification is exact, and I think the fresh counts are where it breaks. A round-t birth descending from k has its fresh count set by d, its groups in round t + 1. So the view at round t reads one step beyond the knots within t holder steps, and your pendant-for-far-neighbour swap is carrying the whole weight. That swap is right when each far neighbour of a cut knot v costs v exactly one group. It fails when two far knots f1 and f2 form a group of three with v through holders outside the region, which needs a cycle through the far side. Then v loses one group and gains one pendant point p. But the birth B holding k and v extends, in the full growth, by f1 and by f2, two round-(t + 1) groups, and in the region by p alone. The fresh count of B drops by one, so k's round-t view differs if it records fresh distinctions. It also differs at the cut itself: the three-parent birth gets fresh max(d, 0, 1), the one-parent birth at least 2. That is the hand test I would run before freezing: a start whose cut knot has two far neighbours joined to it in one group. If your identification already folds a group of three into one extension, show that step in the proof, because it is the step a referee will ask for. Also say plainly which observable the view is. Your stop rule should distinguish a difference in fresh counts from a difference in births.

Two smaller points on H1. The region is a set of knots, but the blocks it needs belong to holders, and in a bipartite start the holders of k's component never enter k's component at all. The engine must keep the cut blocks of every holder, so on-demand needs the holder map one start step beyond the knots it computes. Say this, since "a ball of knots" hides it. And the cap on births per round in the cycle weighting is itself a global read. Whichever births it drops, it drops them by some order over the whole round, and a region sees a different round. Either cap by round depth only or compute k's descendants alone. The second is the natural on-demand computation anyway: a region grows far births you never read.

On order, region independence is not the owner's picture. H2 as written chooses the region before round 1. The owner's picture enlarges the region mid-growth: grow a region to round s, admit more knots, continue to round t, and compare with the full growth. That needs late admission of start knots and their round-1 births, which is exactly the domain of the one-at-a-time result, so you must compose the two results, not cite one. The cheapest honest test of order is a diamond check: two enlargements applied in both orders at round s, each continued to round t, compared with each other and with the from-scratch growth. Random permutations of fills add little once the diamond holds, because the earlier result supplies the rest.

On where the law reads the whole start, your two places are right and I add two. The round clock is a global read supplied by the scaffold, which the asynchronous result removes, so name it. The stall is global because the law says the whole growth stops, so a reader that checks only its own region is running a different law, which is your H3a. Under not-all-equal there is no finiteness test because the relay never closes. Under parity, a knot that is live at round t is live by its fresh count, which reads round t + 1 locally, so liveness is not a further global read. Record the cap and the clock as scaffolds in the register.

On what this does not show, your framing is fair and I would sharpen it. The result is that the far part is unread, not undetermined. A deterministic local law can be evaluated lazily, and that is what you show. Nothing here bears on co-real continuations or on anything a reader could learn beyond the chosen view, and the view is a chosen observable. Parity's implied birth is the literature's implied clause, a consequence of the whole presentation, and your earlier studies already show no reader hears a far change through it with free settings. Say so in one sentence and stop.

On known mathematics, H1 is exactly the dependence cone of a cellular automaton: the state at time t within one site depends on the radius-t neighbourhood, with one step per round. Cite that once. Lieb–Robinson is the quantum analogue with exponentially small tails, so it is an intuition, not the link. H2 is confluence, and the diamond test is Newman's lemma applied to a terminating round structure. Lazy evaluation is the computing name for on demand. For H3b the exact link is that a parity consequence has no local certificate, the unit-propagation versus Gaussian-elimination contrast you name, and Tseitin's cycle parity is its smallest instance.

One word note. "Ball", "radius" and "cone" are words of a space the program is trying to build. The glossary would say the knots within t holder steps of k, and the reach of k at round t.
