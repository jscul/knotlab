# What exact counts and count parity reveal after crossings

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

Let \(p\) be prime. A table is a set of ordered strand values \((x,y)\in\mathbb F_p^2\). Write \(d=y-x\) and \(T(d,x)\in\{0,1\}\) for its membership bit. The \(p\) states with a given \(d\) form a **difference class**. A class containing some but not all of its states is *partial*. The crossing \(\sigma(x,y)=(y,2y-x)\) adds \(d\) to the first strand and preserves \(d\). Therefore the exact counts under all crossing words and one-strand equality tests are

\[
C_k(a)=\sum_{d\in\mathbb F_p} T(d,a-kd),\qquad k,a\in\mathbb F_p.
\]

A parity answer is \(C_k(a)\bmod 2\). An existence answer is \([C_k(a)>0]\). The *missing direction* means asking directly about \(y-x=d\), with class count \(N_d=\sum_xT(d,x)\). It is an additional observation.

1. With exact counts, every partial class is fixed entry by entry. Among the \(m\) remaining classes the number \(r\) that are full is fixed, and every choice of those \(r\) is possible. The fiber has \(\binom mr\) tables. If \(b=2^p-1\), there are \(b^p+p b^{p-1}\) different exact profiles.
2. With count parity, a table can change by complementing every entry in any even number of complete difference classes. This includes classes that were partial. These are all its possible changes. Each fiber has \(2^{p-1}\) tables and there are \(2^{p^2-p+1}\) profiles, including at \(p=2\).
3. Adding missing-direction *presence* to the original exact counts distinguishes all tables. Adding missing-direction *parity* to the original parities distinguishes all tables for odd \(p\). For \(p=2\), even exact counts in the missing direction leave one exceptional kind of pair unresolved. The mixed cases are characterized below.
4. With **both existence and parity on the original queries**, one negative existence answer already makes the entire table unique, at any size. A size-\(p\) table has a distinct competitor of any size exactly when it is an entire difference class. Its fiber consists of the \(2^{p-1}\) odd unions of complete classes. With exact counts, that same table has only the \(p\) single-class possibilities.

## Nearest known result

In classical finite geometry the classes \(x+kd=a\) are lines in \(p\) directions of the affine plane. The \(p+1\) sets of line sums, including \(d=\mathrm{constant}\), are the prime-size finite Radon transform of Matúš and Flusser. Sin, Sorci and Xiang's Theorem 1.1 gives the original incidence rank \(1+p(p-1)\) over any coefficient field in which \(p\ne0\). Their Theorem 3.3 gives the kernel generated by differences of indicators of parallel lines in the omitted direction. These results apply over the rationals and, for odd \(p\), over \(\mathbb F_2\). [Matúš and Flusser, *Image Representation via a Finite Radon Transform*](https://doi.org/10.1109/34.254058) · [Sin, Sorci and Xiang, *Linear representations of finite geometries and associated LDPC codes*, Theorems 1.1 and 3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.06824)

The underlying linear kernel and full-direction inversion are classical. The question here restricts the unknown to zero or one per state and compares exact, parity and existence answers on the same crossing tests. The mixed size-\(p\) result refines the previous existence-only classification. Its disjoint ties survive and its shared-state ties disappear. The proofs below derive these statements directly and make no external priority claim for the refinements. ## Proof

**The incidence identity.** Write \(N=\sum_d N_d=\sum_a C_k(a)\), independently of \(k\). Sum the \(p\) queries through a given state \((d,x)\). This state contributes \(p\) times. Every state with a different \(d'\) contributes once, since \(x+kd=x'+kd'\) has a unique solution in \(k\). Other states in the same difference class contribute zero. Thus

\[
A(d,x):=\sum_k C_k(x+kd)=p\,T(d,x)+N-N_d. \tag{1}
\]

**Exact counts.** Suppose \(T,T'\) have the same counts, and let \(\Delta=T'-T\). Equation (1) applied to their difference gives \(p\,\Delta(d,x)=N'_d-N_d\), because their total populations agree. Thus \(\Delta\) is constant along each difference class. Its only possible nonzero values are \(+1\) on an empty class and \(-1\) on a full class. The numbers of each change agree because the population is fixed. Conversely, every measured query sees exactly one state from each difference class, so exchanging equally many full and empty classes preserves every count.

To count exact profiles, choose the \(m\) constant classes, a partial pattern in each other class, and the number \(r\) of full constant classes. Each partial class has \(2^p-2\) choices. Different choices of any recorded item give different profiles by the equivalence just proved. Hence the number is

\[
\sum_{m=0}^{p}\binom pm(2^p-2)^{p-m}(m+1)
= (2^p-1)^p+p(2^p-1)^{p-1}.
\]

**Count parity for odd \(p\).** Apply (1) modulo 2 to the XOR \(Z\) of two tables with the same parity answers. All measured counts of \(Z\) vanish modulo 2, including their sum \(N\bmod2\). Since \(p\) is odd, \(Z(d,x)=N_d\bmod2\) for every \(x\). Thus \(Z\) consists of whole classes. Each original query sees one entry per such class, so it has even count exactly when their number is even. All even choices work, giving the claimed number of possible tables and profiles. In particular an empty table and the union of two entire classes give identical count parities. Their answers to the ordinary nonemptiness question differ.

**Count parity for \(p=2\).** The two original strand measurements reduce to row and column parities of the \(2\times2\) table in its original coordinates \((x,y)\). If the parities of a difference vanish, its entries agree across each row and each column. All four entries are equal. Thus the kernel consists of the empty change and complementing the entire table, which is exactly the even subsets of its two difference classes. This proof avoids division by \(p\) in characteristic two.

### What reading the missing direction adds

For original exact counts, \([N_d>0]\) identifies which constant classes are full. This already resolves the fiber at every prime. Exact \(N_d\) also gives the familiar direct reconstruction \(T(d,x)=(A(d,x)-N+N_d)/p\).

For original parities and odd \(p\), complementing class \(d\) changes its count from \(N_d\) to \(p-N_d\), reversing its parity. Reading the class parities therefore resolves the entire table. Reading mere class presence leaves exactly the even toggles among the \(h\) partial classes, since both a partial class and its complement are nonempty. The remaining fiber has \(2^{\max(h-1,0)}\) members. This formula for presence applies also at \(p=2\).

For \(p=2\), complementing a class of size two preserves its count parity. Reading the missing parities leaves both members of each original parity fiber. Reading the missing **exact** counts leaves a pair tied precisely when each of the two difference classes contains one state. There are four such tables, forming two complementary pairs. For example \(\{(0,0),(0,1)\}\) and \(\{(1,0),(1,1)\}\) have identical original parities and identical class counts \((1,1)\). Reading exact counts in *all* directions still reconstructs every binary table by (1).

### Combining existence and count parity

Fix \(T\). Every distinct parity competitor \(T'\) complements a nonempty even set \(D\) of whole classes. If any query has no witness in \(T\), it gains one in every toggled class. Such a table is uniquely determined by existence and parity together. By symmetry, two distinct parity competitors tie under both readers exactly when **every query has a witness in each table**. The observation that a single empty query rules out every toggle was supplied by the independent referee.

Equivalently, a proposed nonempty toggle preserves existence exactly when, for every query, either a state outside \(D\) is occupied or the entries the query meets in \(D\) include both a zero and a one. In the absence of an unchanged occupied state, an original yes requires a one and a new yes requires a zero.

**Exact mixed fiber sizes.** For a fixed \(T\) and each query \(q=(k,a)\), write \(B_q=\{d:T(d,a-kd)=1\}\). Let \(b(T)\) be the number of *distinct* even-sized sets among the \(B_q\). After toggling \(D\), the occupied classes in that query are \(B_q\mathbin{\triangle}D\). The query is empty exactly for \(D=B_q\). Thus precisely \(b(T)\) of the \(2^{p-1}\) tables in the parity fiber have some empty query. Each is distinguished individually. All the others give one common mixed profile if there are any. The number of mixed profiles in that parity fiber is

\[
b(T)+[\,b(T)<2^{p-1}\,],
\]

and the fiber with every existence answer positive has size \(2^{p-1}-b(T)\). Changing the chosen representative translates all \(B_q\) by one even subset, preserving \(b(T)\). Since there are only \(p^2\) queries, at every prime \(p\ge7\) each parity fiber contains at least \(2^{p-1}-p^2\) different tables giving the same positive existence answers and the same parity answers. At \(p=7\) the uniform lower bound is 15 tables.

**Sharp size-\(p\) consequence.** Suppose \(|T|=p\) and another table gives the same existence and parity answers. Represent each state as \(f(k)=x+kd\). Since every query has a witness, at every column \(k\) the \(p\) functions take all \(p\) values. No two functions in \(T\) can agree. Two functions of different slopes agree in one column, hence all \(p\) have one slope and \(T\) is a full class.

Starting at a full class, the even toggles produce exactly the odd unions of full classes. Every such union is nonempty and gives odd counts to every original query, proving sufficiency and the mixed fiber size. With exact counts every individual query has count one, hence exactly one full class can remain. For \(|T|<p\), existence already determines \(T\) against arbitrary competitors by the single-agreement argument. Thus this also gives the first possible size of a mixed ambiguity, though some competitors in its fiber are larger.

## Validation and scope

[The checker](check.py) groups all 16 original tables at \(p=2\) and all 512 at \(p=3\) by observations generated from repeated crossings. It compares the members of whole fibers against the classifications. There are respectively 15 and 490 exact profiles, 8 and 128 parity profiles, and 15 and 399 profiles for existence together with parity. Reading the missing exact counts with the original parities gives respectively 14 and 512 profiles.

At \(p=5\) the checker obtains parity rank 21 directly from the query masks. The 16 even toggles exhaust its kernel. It tests every one of the 53,130 size-five tables against these possible competitors, of unrestricted size. Precisely the five complete classes have a mixed tie. Their common fiber contains five tables of size 5, ten of size 15 and one of size 25. At \(p=7\) the checker verifies the measured and augmented ranks, the incidence inversion, the mixed-fiber count and all 64 toggles on each of five tables. Deliberately changing the permitted class exchanges, admitting odd toggles, removing the two-label exception, or counting the wrong empty-query toggles makes the checker fail. The all-prime proof is symbolic. [Numerical results](checks.json)

These are questions about information retained by a specified reader of a supplied finite table. They charge neither constructing the table from a SAT instance nor executing a general solver. The parity collision with the empty table describes a loss of decision information under these queries. It yields no running-time lower bound for formula-only SAT.
