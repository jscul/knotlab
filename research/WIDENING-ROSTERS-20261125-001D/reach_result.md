# When an alternative took a distinction two holder steps away

The preceding-round rule reproduced **22 first meetings through made knots on 12 of the 205 matched start and growth-law settings**. All 22 used a distinction held by a start knot. The original most-holders rule alone produced first meetings with no start knot holding a linking distinction. The numbers of first meetings and their physical witnesses agreed under not-all-equal and parity. All 22 preceding-round meetings through made knots had an actual take requiring two steps that crossed from the other descent. In ten settings one first part met two distinct, separately descended second parts this way. The original rule reached exactly the same pair from the same recorded split in the same round for all 22 meetings. For 20 of the 22, the two rules had identical histories through the meeting. In its own 3,103 takes, the preceding-round rule selected a candidate tied for the most holders in 14 cases where the original rule would abstain. Every candidate it took was among the most-held on the actual offer.

The memoryless two-step-only rule chose a candidate strictly below the greatest holder count 46 times on 37 settings per knot law. A two-step take supplied an actual distinction on a common witness's arm in all 33 of its meetings. Every such meeting had a start knot among its linkers, and its recorded split already differed from the original rule's. The sole-offer rule took at least one distinction requiring two steps in 130 settings, with no first meeting. For the original rule's 1,250 meetings with no start-held linking distinction, a two-step take occurred by the meeting round in every history. None of those 1,250 had such a take on a common witness's arm or across the descents at the meeting.
