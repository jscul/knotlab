## The question

Make each distinction an endless series, a harmony, bound to the other copies by the harmony's own knots. What does that add to what
the theorems already give, and does any reach arise that is not plain connectivity, the owner's "globally influential"?

## The answer

**Bound in every order, endless blocks lock completely (by the theorems); bound in one order, a whole loop can lock every copy on it
into one state, which no open chain does.**

- **Every order (proved; exact).** A harmony has no distinction it can spare, so the pinning theorem ties every copy to every other:
  the nested base has exactly the harmony's states, all copies sharing one. With lags, the loops' net lags fold it to the states of
  matching period (502 of 502 cells as predicted), and, by the referee's rule of the twisted-pins epic, with no effect of loop length.
- **One order: loop-born locking (exact; robust).** Bind each knot's three copies by the harmony's kinds in one order only, a stitch of
  three harmonies (the reading of the bases-within-bases epic, with the harmony's own kinds as its lags). Then for 7 of 45 harmonies a
  closed loop of knots locks all the copies on it into one shared state, place by place, while the same knots in an open chain,
  unrolled three or nine times, leave the copies free to sit at different offsets. That is a tie born of the whole loop: the frozen
  criterion for reach that is not plain connectivity is met.
- **Sometimes only short loops lock.** Two of the seven harmonies lock a loop of 3 knots and not one of 4 or 6.

## What it changes

- **For the owner's "globally influential".** The first native case where a whole loop, and not a chain of local links, decides a
  value: endless patterns stitched by their own rules, closed into a loop, fall into step, while the same stitches in a line do not.
  In two of them the loop must be short. A sceptic would note what is built in: the stitch is the harmony's own kinds, so the loop's
  lock is a consistency condition on offsets around the loop (like phase locking, or a Tseitin parity), and its reach is still within
  the loop's own component; what is not built in is that an open chain of the same stitches stays free.

## In the owner's words

If every yes-or-no is itself an endless repeating pattern, and the patterns are tied to each other by their own rules taken one way
round, then a closed loop of ties can make every pattern on the loop fall into step, the same pattern at the same phase, while the
same ties in a straight line leave each free to drift. That is influence that comes from the loop as a whole. For some patterns only a
short loop does it.
