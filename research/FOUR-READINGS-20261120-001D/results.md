# How often fresh starts release lasting families

Four initially three-place knots on eight distinctions released four lasting families in a fresh random draw. Two pairs of these families each made a shared child. Indefinitely repeated release was absent in every one of the 512 tested starts, including the ones that grew without closing. A different outcome occurred inside one runaway growth: a birth inherits every knot from two parent steps earlier, so no separate lasting family can be released there. Independent referee pending.

## What ensures persistence, and what the end can read

Consider one full witnessed path with at least two holders, two loose distinctions at every intermediate holder, one or two at each end and two at a selected end. The only multi-parent groups are consecutive pairs. The selected end spends one loose distinction with its neighbour and one on a unary child. Siblings witness consecutive new holders. Each intermediate child needs two fresh distinctions. The unary end needs two. An end with only one loose place gives a binary end with one fresh. The next live part is another path of this same kind (its width stays fixed with one two-loose end and increases by one with two). The unary pure continuation has precisely its old tip as parent, at every later step. This proves indefinite survival. It uses no identified-map assumption. Actual one- and two-parent rosters distinguish a new release from the same tip issuing its next child.

Both readers get the same local answers in this class. The full reader gets live holders, their common witnesses and loose counts from all current holdings. The growing-end reader is given these three pieces at the current cut, including shared witnesses that have spent their own supply. It recognizes the two ends without assigning different names to symmetric members. A first-step release is counted relative to the supplied original start, as in the original matched path study. A later direct birth, release or output change is a claim about the observed cuts and the true offspring of families recognized there. The record of ancestry checks the claims. It never supplies an unseen origin to an observer.

There is a distinction between reading the output on actual matched ends and deciding, from the end alone, whether a one-place withdrawal would be physically admissible. In a three-holder live path put two spent witnesses between consecutive holders. Give the three live holders two loose distinctions each. Let each spent witness share one with its outer holder and two with the middle. Withdrawing a loose distinction at either outer holder would leave an inadmissible two-place support. Duplicate the sharing of the right endpoint and its spent witness. The complete witnessed end is still literally identical, with all loose counts fixed; now the right-hand withdrawal is legal under both laws. The root-free growing end cannot determine that intervention's admissibility. The larger comparator below works at both preparations. Likewise, a retrospective reading of two surviving endpoints in a path says that they exist there. It need not reveal their launch cut or their individual origins. ## What makes a later shared descendant an effect

In every observed candidate not already meeting directly, both parties launched from opposite ends of a fully funded path. If the original ends are d witnessed steps apart, both issue a unary at age one. A branch from either release can share a descendant at age d+2. At that first shared birth one parent is descended only from the first party, the other only from the second. A matched path with one loose place at the donor lacks the second release. At age d+2 the receiver has d+2 inclusive descendants with the donor versus d+1 in the comparison. One cut later there are two additional joint descendants. The receiver's pure line continues throughout. This is the path comparison established previously; the new result is its frequency and the repair of physically inadmissible withdrawals in unbuilt starts. ## Why these draws contain no endlessly releasing source

For a tree or a tree with one extra pairwise meeting, take two copies of every holder, one for its live role and one for its witnessing role. Connect the live copy of each holder to the witness copy of each neighbour. This is the alternating double cover from the identification proof. A tree gives two trees. A connected input with one cycle gives two copies of an even cycle or one cycle covering an odd cycle, with finite attachments. Removing a spent live role or suppressing a witness with at most one live neighbour cannot increase any component's number of independent cycles. Each part has at most one. The identified one-cycle/finite-chain obstruction bounds the number of disjoint indefinitely continuing families in that case. Its proof fixes a common earlier size and finds compulsory true ancestors of arbitrarily large later clusters. Width of a packet and formation size of a member are different counts. [Identified bound](../GENERATORS-20261120-001/source.md)

Every tree has unique pair witnesses and no unheld triple, since the two-copy incidence is acyclic. The minimum loose supply at its leaves gives at least two in total. Hence every funded tree in the stated weighting is covered. A first-stalled tree issues nothing. **The unbounded-emitter probability for this tree weighting is zero by proof.** In the augmented draws 20 of the 37 funded originals passed the identification gates. Each of the other 17 was followed by actual complete growth to stable witnessed paths or closure, covering every original part. For a stable path at a later cut of width k, every descendant at least k steps farther on has among its ancestors one of the two ends of the fixed cut. Along the path, actual parent intervals are consecutive and gain one position on every deletion step until they encounter an end. This holds also when one end has only one loose distinction and the opposite end keeps issuing its unary. Consequently disjoint lasting families can use at most two different anchors on each such path. There are finitely many parts, and finitely many births before this reached cut. No such draw can keep releasing disjoint lasting families indefinitely. Zero of 256 in the augmented weighting gives a one-sided 95% upper bound of 1.16%. The corresponding proof for *all* augmented starts outside the identified class remains open.

On the runaway, every new birth chooses one holder from each pair of partners. The entire set of children of any old holder x is a held choice-set one step later, and its complement is precisely the set of children of x's partner. Thus a birth two steps later avoiding all descendants of x would have as parents the entire held choice-set of x's partner. Growth excludes that choice. Every such birth therefore inherits **every** member of the two-step-earlier cohort. A lasting all-parent family must already occupy its whole cohort at every cut after launch; a separate productive source cannot continue. ## Scope of the result

The outcome's dependence on the initial sample, observer and finite horizon is measured. The ordinary-law facts include funding, the parentage of a lasting tip, the two-step ancestor obstruction in the runaway, and the closure-versus-relay difference. Calling particular descendants a family uses the declared anonymous reading. Neither finite observed survival nor the empty return-word detections alone decide what every possible reader could recognize. The rates document bounds for broader existence by treating undetermined cases as possible successes. G17 and G5 gain measured one-time releases, both-persist direct and mediated meetings, and an exact counterexample to the idea that faster growth must produce a separate enduring family. No overall weighting of realities was supplied.
