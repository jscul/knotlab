# Recovering every shrinking site through bond distance four

Let \(t\) be the total number of three-parent, two-output sites. A source has one ternary seed, three unary sites with three outputs each, and otherwise only two-parent or three-parent sites with two outputs. It has \(9-t\) loose bits. The finite frontier calculation gives \(t\le4\). We prove that every five-place junction equation is present in every reachable rival matching the whole table through \(K=5\), where \(K=D+1\) is minimum bond size. The argument also fixes the full parity space under that same correspondence of names. [Frontier calculation](frontier.md)

## Four-bit and five-bit boundaries

A shore is the ordinary side of a cut in the graph with all loose bits wired to the exterior. Call it large when it contains at least three sites. The first three selected sites in birth order have boundary at least seven. Their total arity is at least eleven, and there are at most two internal meetings by simplicity and bipartiteness. If a later selected site with \(p\) parents and \(h\) outputs has \(j\) selected parents, it changes the boundary by \(p+h-2j\). Only a shrinking site can save one, and no site saves more. Thus any large shore bounded by five bits contains at least five sites.

There is no large four-bit boundary in the source. Such a shore can be lawfully built first, ending with precisely four loose bits. Its restricted history must contain at least two unary additions, since its first two additions are unary. If it contains only two, it belongs to the minimum-width family, whose loose count never falls below five. If it contains all three, every remaining addition makes two outputs and takes at least two inputs. The completed source could then have at most four loose bits, contrary to \(9-t\ge5\). There are no large boundaries of size at most three. Thus all nonempty words through four in the source are the ternary seed and actual four-place stars. The four-bit transfer theorem makes these the complete collection of words through four in any matching rival. **Every large five-bit shore is parent-closed.** The three latest selected sites introduce at least six outputs, and at most two of those can be consumed internally. Therefore at most one input enters. If one enters, all four outgoing bits are supplied by those latest three sites.

First suppose the seed is absent. The first selected site must be unary and use the single incoming bit. Its three outputs can be taken as a new ternary seed on the restricted history. Every later selected site has all parents inside. Required pair witnesses survive restriction. The earliest ordinary common neighbor of two parents must precede the younger parent, since a later first common neighbor would have had to join the two using an earlier witness. If that earliest witness were outside, its meeting into the younger selected parent would give a second entering bit. Restriction cannot create a new common neighbor. The resulting history must use both remaining unary sites for its first two additions, leaving only two-output additions thereafter. It belongs to the minimum-width family yet has only four loose outputs. This is impossible. If there were zero entering inputs, any nonempty ancestor-closed set would already contain the seed.

Now suppose that the seed is inside and one input enters. The seed and the first selected site after it are older than the latest three, since the shore contains at least five sites. No output at either may leave. It follows that both initial unary sites are selected. For siblings both are seed children. In the chain case the seed's first child is selected, and its own unary child is selected as well. With \(u\) unary and \(s\) shrinking sites selected, net outflow counts give \(3+2u-s=4-1\). Since \(u\ge2\) and \(s\le4\), exactly four shrinking sites and two unary sites are inside. The omitted site \(A\) is the third unary addition in the source chronology. Every history reaching four shrinks puts \(A\) before all four, by the tagged frontier calculation. But a directed path from the seed to \(A\) has a first outgoing bit, supplied by one of the three latest selected sites. At most two selected sites can follow \(A\), a contradiction. [Shrink order](frontier.md)

Consequently every large five-bit shore is a lawful prefix when built first. The earliest-witness argument keeps required pair witnesses, and an additional old common witness for any replayed triple could not previously have been born later, since it would have tried to join that triple after an existing common witness. Each such shore contains the seed and at least two of the three unary sites. Its exact count is \(s=2u-2\), from \(3+2u-s=5\). Only the number of unary sites selected is needed below. ## A protected input at every five-place site

The union of two large five-bit shores again has five boundary bits. Indeed their intersection contains the seed and at least one common unary site, because each contains two or more out of the three. If the intersection contains exactly two sites, their boundary costs at least \(3+4-2=5\). If it contains at least three, the absence of any large boundary of size at most four gives the same lower bound. Their union also contains at least three sites. Cut submodularity now gives

\[
 |\partial(U\cap V)|+|\partial(U\cup V)|\ \le\ |\partial U|+|\partial V|=10.
\]

Both terms on the left are at least five, so their union has boundary five. Its two original shores may omit different unary sites.

Fix a shrinking site \(v\). If each of its three input bits occurred individually on some large five-bit boundary, union three witnessing shores. Each excludes \(v\) and includes the corresponding old holder, because all are parent-closed. The union is a large five-bit shore containing all three old holders but excluding \(v\). Adjoining \(v\) decreases the boundary to four. This contradicts the four-bit result. Hence \(v\) has an input appearing on no large five-bit boundary. If a protected input has the seed as holder, then every large five-bit shore must contain \(v\), since all contain the seed. Parent-closure then puts all its input holders inside every such shore. Thus in every case one can choose a protected input whose other holder differs from the seed. The same conclusion holds vacuously if there are no large five-bit shores.

The four-place stars have already been forced. Proceed through the shrinking sites in birth order. For the protected input just chosen, the other holder has either a forced four-place star or an earlier five-place star. The protected-bit theorem now forces \(S_v\). Its hypotheses are exactly the absence of a large five-bit cut through the selected bit, the transferred through-four words and the forced equation of the other holder. It also handles an older four-place holder adjacent to the seed. To recall the point, an extra rival five-word through this bit and a second bit of \(v\) must replace at most one member of \(S_v\) by an outside bit. Two outsiders at the other holder, or leakage through the adjacent seed-plus-four sum, would give a source word on adding one of the forced small equations. Two distinct one-bit replacements cannot both occur because their difference has size two, or size four with two bits of \(S_v\). The four partner readings must therefore be covered by \(S_v\) itself. Induction forces every source five-place site against arbitrary mixed rivals. [Protected-bit theorem](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001L/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001L-P001_binary_third/structural.md#one-protected-bit-forces-a-five-place-site)

All \(n\) independent ordinary source equations are now in the rival space. The source width is \(2n+4\). A rival with \(r\) ordinary sites and \(m\) nonseed three-output additions has width \(2r+1+m\). Its unique ternary site forces the first two additions to be unary with three outputs. The width makes \(m\) odd, hence at least three. Thus \(r\le n\), and the inclusion supplies the reverse inequality. The spaces are equal, and every allowed and forbidden triple transfers. ## Independent exclusion of the single entering input

The shrink-order claim in the first proof can also be replaced entirely by the earlier minimum-width bound. In the only remaining single-entry case the omitted third unary site \(A\) follows an outgoing crossing from one of the latest three selected sites. At least two of the four selected shrinking sites follow \(A\), since the prefix before \(A\) belongs to the two-unary minimum-width family. Thus the two later selected sites \(x,y\) are precisely these shrinking sites, and the third-latest selected site \(d\) precedes \(A\). All chronological sites through \(d\) belong to the shore, since a seed path to an earlier omission would exit earlier than its latest three sites. Their prefix \(H\) is a minimum-width prefix after two shrinking joins. It has five loose bits and no legal triple.

The shrinking site \(x\) cannot take its three parents in \(H\). Their bits would have been loose there, each first pair witness would have preceded the younger parent, and no universal witness in \(H\) could disappear. It must use the one entering input and two bits of \(H\). Site \(y\) has all parents inside and likewise cannot take all three from \(H\). It therefore uses \(x\) and two more bits of \(H\). These four older bits are distinct. The fifth is the only bit available for the first site outside \(H\) preceding \(x\). That site must be the unary \(A\), and its parent is \(d\), since only \(d,x,y\) can supply outgoing bits from the shore. No other outside site can occur before \(x\). Among the already present sites only \(A\) could supply another outside join, and no further unary addition remains. The external parent of \(x\) is consequently \(A\), with only ordinary neighbor \(d\) at that stage. Each of the two older co-parents would need to meet \(d\) in order to have a common neighbor with \(A\). This gives all three a common witness, precluding \(x\). This second argument proves the parent-closure and union results for every source with \(t\le4\), independently of the new frontier enumeration. The enumeration is needed to include every allowed width and shrink count in that conditional bound.
