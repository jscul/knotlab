# The order needed to carry later bond distances

Consider a finite positive-age stage of the complete cheapest-count parity law. A specified permutation fixes every distinction at every live junction. Let \(K(S)\) be the least cardinality of a bond containing the nonempty marked set \(S\), with infinity when no bond contains it. Readings through \(r\) specify these values for all sets of at most \(r\) distinct marks. The usual pair bond distance is \(K-1\). Count ordinary junctions by \(J\) and literal distinctions by \(n\), including the loose ones. An upper bound is uniform over stages up to the specified size. Necessity means that some stage in that budget has a specified old permutation which cannot be extended. Every separating example below uses old distinctions in its later comparison.

## The size bound

The worst-case order for all future readings of arbitrary order is \(\Theta(J)\). In literal distinctions it is at least \(c\sqrt n\) for some absolute \(c>0\) and at most
\[
\min\left(J,1+\left\lfloor(2n-1)/5\right\rfloor,1+\left\lfloor(2n+9)/7\right\rfloor\right).
\]

To see the junction upper bound, take one distinction to every distinct neighbor of an exhausted junction. Any bond crossing all those distinctions crosses every distinction on its row. Because this row is itself a bond, it is the only such bond. With one additional mark its support can be recovered as all marks that leave the minimum unchanged. An exhausted junction has at most \(J-1\) distinct ordinary neighbors. Consequently order \(J\) preserves every exhausted equation under the stipulated matching. The supplied end already carries the other rows. For the stronger distinction bounds, a changed span requires two exhausted junctions to fail. Each has at least \(r\) distinct neighbors and size at least \(r+1\). A minimum-cut argument raises the latter bound to \(2r-4\). Sum degrees over all junctions and the wired exterior. The full proof includes the minimum-cut lemma, its compatibility argument and the integer endpoints. [Full upper bounds](../../../research/BOND-READING-SCALE-20261119-001/limiting-reading.md)

For the lower bound there is a reached stage, for every \(r\ge2\), with \(J\le137r+280\) and \(n=O(r^2)\). In one complete round the stipulated map loses an old \(r\)-set while it carries all readings of order at most \(r-1\). The construction starts with a stage in which many independent attachment costs may drop. Lower the costs successively in the fixed underlying arrangement, stop at the first loss of order \(r\), and realize that single change by actual junction growth. This gives the linear lower bound in junctions even for carrying one's own order. A different construction realizes a decrease in the reading of an old pair with \(J=O(r^{3/2})\) and \(n=O(r^2)\). A shrinking part of initial separating cost \(m^2\) uses only \(8m-1\) ordinary junctions besides its root. The proof derives all parent sets, certificates for every marked comparison, and literal size counts. The square-root lower bound in distinctions holds even for meet-once starts by the unit replacement construction. ## A supplied boundary

Suppose the permutation acts in an unchanged connected part and fixes the connected changing part pointwise. With two shared attachment junctions, all present readings through \(k+1\) carry all future readings through \(k\), and the extra mark can be necessary for each \(k\). A bond that crosses both parts separates the two shared junctions within each part. The additional mark on a least-cost separating bond of the changing part reads the conditional separating cost on the unchanged part. The two possible forms of a bond internal to the unchanged part and the nonincreasing unmarked separating cost then determine its future minimum. In the lowered-cost construction one can take the first single attachment whose change loses order \(k\). This realizes sharpness with exactly two shared junctions.

For three or any prescribed larger number of shared junctions, for every finite \(r\) there is a reached example preserving the present readings through \(r\) and separating an old pair one round later. In the three-junction construction the two distinguished junctions have many redundant routes to one another and to the third attachment. A current marked cut of the requisite size can keep them together, across the entire marked domain. New junctions lower the cost of separating them on one side of the permutation. Disjoint routes certify that the number of shared junctions is necessary for the specified separation. With exactly three single crossing distinctions, all current readings through \(k\) do carry all future readings through \(k\) for the connected replacements in the statement. The change from two to three ordinary attachment junctions allows arbitrarily many distinctions at each. ## The limiting reading

For every nonempty \(S\), \(K(S)=|S|\) holds precisely when \(S\) is itself a bond. To generate an equation, subtract any bond contained in its nonempty support and repeat on the smaller support. This expresses all equations as binary sums of detected bonds. With the growing end supplied, it suffices to ask this yes/no question only for sets of shared distinctions. Every exhausted junction equation is supported on this subfamily, where its entire bond decomposition stays, and the individual live junctions supply the remaining equations. The reconstructed span and the growing end determine a consistent extension on every future round. Conversely, later equations supported entirely on old distinctions form exactly the old span. Every new nonclosing row has a private fresh distinction and the row on closing is already implied by the older rows. A missing old equation therefore has an old bond support whose equality test remains different in every subsequent round. [Reconstruction, converse and a second proper subfamily](../../../research/BOND-READING-SCALE-20261119-001/limiting-reading.md)

The theorem gives asymptotic upper and lower bounds in the stated populations. The sharp distinction exponent, the absolute finite minima from order three onward, and the sharp order in junctions for carrying only future pairs remain open.
