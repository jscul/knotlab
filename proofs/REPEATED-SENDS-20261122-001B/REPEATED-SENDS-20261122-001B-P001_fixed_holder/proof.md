# What a fixed knot can observe after it pays its last loose distinction

With the receiver, its three visible distinctions and the two source marks fixed as in the [reviewed freeze](../../../research/REPEATED-SENDS-20261122-001A/preregistration.md), one completed ordinary funding event exhausts everything observable at that receiver. This holds for every continuation under parity and under not-all-equal. It also holds when an additional source operation changes only the two original marks on a word where that change is lawful.

## Statement and prior result

Consider an append-only family of finite sets. Each element occurs in one or two sets. In each funded extension, newly appended sets receive singly occurring elements, each used exactly once, and possibly fresh elements unique to their recipients. Assignments of values extend the old assignment. Observe a fixed set through the values of designated elements and the equality pattern of their other holders. Then there is at most one positive post-enrolment change in that observation. After it occurs, the joint distribution of an observed word and any further reading assigns all its mass to identical words.

The [single-receipt experiment](../../../research/HIDDEN-SENDER-MARKS-20261122-001/decision.md) ended when the designated holder paid its three new distinctions. It exhibited all eight visible words under both sends. The additional claim concerns the **entire subsequent history**, how an actual second use of the same two source marks can affect it, and what one can infer with equal odds. The [prior-work search](../../../research/REPEATED-SENDS-20261122-001/prior_work.md) includes the different construction that carries a receiver across successive births.

## Ordinary growth

Write H for the roster of one existing knot, O(x) for all holders of distinction x and v(x) for its value on a fixed lawful history. A funded step appends knots. It adds a previously loose distinction to one new holder, so that O(x) goes from one member to two. A distinction with two holders is ineligible for payment. Every fresh distinction is issued to its newborn. No step adds an element to the roster H of an old knot. A funded step uses every available loose distinction, paying any left after the multiple-parent births to single-parent births. A stall leaves the holdings unchanged. On an ordinary extension, an old distinction keeps its value. These statements also cover a closing birth that receives no fresh distinction. At the first funded cut after a knot is enrolled, every remaining loose member of H is paid. Each previously shared member keeps its precise set of holders. From that cut on all members of H have two holders, none can be paid, and none can be replaced by a newly issued distinction. Induct on the number of further cuts. A stall changes nothing and a funded cut preserves these three assertions. This proves constancy of the values, the entire holding and the other-holder pattern at H at every finite order. Any local history made from these data, with an event only when they change, consequently has at most one event after enrolment. No count of how long the constant state persists is locally available.

If a source operation can alter values on names absent from H while preserving all other values and all holdings, it leaves this conclusion intact. An extension with a supplied value writer acting on H, a receiver that follows a new holder, or a reading of evolving properties of other holders has different premises.

## Joint law and identifiability

Let W be the ordered three-bit word originally issued to the receiver. Its local history H(W) consists of the same W first with three loose slots and then with three distinct anonymous partners. Any further snapshot has precisely the finished value. For any joint law of source choice S and W, and any fixed number of rereads m at or after the finish,
$$
 \Pr(W=w_0,W_1=w_1,\ldots,W_m=w_m\mid S=s)
 = \Pr(W=w_0\mid S=s)\,\mathbf 1\{w_0=w_1=\cdots=w_m\}.
$$
Equivalently, omitting consecutive duplicate snapshots leaves the one event. Its absence of a successor is a proved property of the offered measurement, never a locally announced timeout. The event “two distinct completed receipts” has mass zero. Accuracy conditional on that event is undefined.

With equal odds for two alternatives, an observer with the whole single-record history can score at most half the sum, over w, of the larger of the two conditional masses at w. Copying W adds no term to that sum. Under the B307 conditional birth weighting and archived common word, if exactly one of the two requests precedes payment, II and TT have exactly the original leave/exchange marginal laws. Every one of the eight words permits both. Parity gives 1/2 and not-all-equal gives 789/1568 on the intact or coherently renamed course. Diverting the contributing mark makes both scores 1/2. If both requests are early, net source values are 01 in both arms. If both are late, neither can change any name subsequently paid to R. Their marginal local laws agree for II and TT in each of those cases. A law for realized source moves conditional on the extra guard is a separate calculation.
