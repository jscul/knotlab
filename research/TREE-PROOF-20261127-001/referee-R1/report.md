# Referee report on the pair-cost theorem and the families’ tree
**Refuted. Block on recovering the families’ tree from allowed-state distances alone. The shortest-chain theorem and the tree on specified family facts are confirmed.**

Two connected 12-knot starts each complete one funded, nonclosing round. Their grown stages allow **exactly the same 4,096 states on the same 27 named distinctions**. Each start has three families. In one, two families join at cost 5 and the third joins at 8. In the other, all three join at 7. Even knowing the round, the family count and every allowed state leaves the branching undetermined. The failed step is the move from a minimum **conditioned on which family facts flip** to a claim about what the state distances alone identify. [Round-one certificate](metric_witness.json)

## What holds

1. **Lineages and funding.** The theorem applies to finite connected starts with one or two distinct holders per distinction, even parity at every knot, and complete funded rounds with fresh distinctions through round t. On 2,530 funded transitions the replay checked all 18,177 births. Each spent one loose distinction from each parent, its parents were pairwise candidates in the preceding stage, and all belonged to one starting family. Every loose distinction was spent once. All 18,177 births had fresh distinctions. This roster has no closing birth against which to test the implication. For an implied birth, the sum of old laws producing its loose inputs must cancel every shared distinction. Its two holders therefore have equal coefficients throughout the connected stage. The input exhausts the loose distinctions, leaving only this closing birth. [Replay](replay_results.json) · [Closing proof](../../ERASURE-AND-CLOSING-SIGNS-20261121-001B/closing_law.md)
2. **Lemma 2 survives wide families.** Remove the bookkeeping holder U and shorten the route to remove repetitions. A latest birth on an excursion has two earlier neighbours on the route. They are distinct parents. Their candidate witness already existed when the birth was proposed. For a first-round birth it is a starting knot. For a later birth it is a parent of both, since the two candidate knots were themselves newly born at the preceding round. Replacing the latest birth by this witness preserves length and lowers the sum of generation numbers. Deleting any repetitions only shortens the route. On eight constructed starts, all 50,634 positive-round comparisons between starting knots preserved the original step count. Of 10,473 comparisons admitting an excursion with only births in its interior, 3,221 tied the starting route and 7,252 were longer. A route through a round-15 birth tied at 30 steps. [Counts and routes](stress_wide.json)
3. **The pair cost and the odd-holder argument.** Give each family its own bookkeeping holder for its current loose distinctions. A change flipping exactly A and B has even degree everywhere except at their two bookkeeping holders. Its support therefore contains a route from A to B. Conversely, any simple such route is an allowed change with exactly those flips. Between successive distinct families on the route, the first and last starting knots reached belong to the respective families. Reaching the start costs at least t shared distinctions on each side. Lemma 2 bounds the middle by d, and the two loose distinctions give the additional 2. For the upper bound, follow a starting route of length d and one descendant per round at either end. Distinct family lineages keep the two extensions disjoint. Each segment costs d + 2t + 2. Adding the changes along a family chain cancels its intermediate flips. This gives equality. In the original pairing proof, only A and B contain odd numbers of odd holders. Pairing them on routes with disjoint links and discarding same-family returns leaves a chain joining A to B. In a nonminimum 21-distinction control, one knot held three selected loose distinctions, another held two, one paired route returned to its own lineage, and a closed three-distinction change remained. None must be charged twice. [Control](stress_wide.json)
4. **All three exceptions to the frozen direct-cost prediction are genuine.** The archive includes 2,333 starts, of which 975 supply 3,489 eligible start-rounds. The independent stage calculation reproduced **6,839 of 6,839** pair costs. In 4,265 pair rows a third family existed. Only 140 had a direct cost greater than twice the smallest possible step. Using the actual starting step counts, just three of the 3,489 stages could distinguish the two formulas. Each supplied one strictly cheaper pair. For these three starts the following enumeration tested every allowed starting state. Each minimizing change in the last column uses two loose distinctions of the intervening family. 5. **Set costs, class facts and the tree conditional on family parities.** For a nonempty proper set T, identify all bookkeeping holders on each side of the split. A flip of the sum on T contains a route joining the two sides, even if four or more individual facts flip. The route costs at least the cheapest crossing step. One segment attains it. At scale k the sum on T is kept exactly when T is a union of clusters joined at d + 2t + 2 ≤ k. Every pair flip is attained and these generate all even family tuples. The single all-family parity equation makes complementary sums equal, so r clusters give r − 1 independent sums. The components using exact pair costs and direct steps coincide at every threshold. Each direct step has an attaining change, and every step of a chain of total cost at most k costs at most k. All 6,871 set minima agreed. At each frozen scale, 8 and 12, all 6,871 individual sum classifications and all 3,465 dimensions agreed. Only 119 and 115 of those dimensions, respectively, lay strictly between zero and full. The fresh starts added 1,858 nontrivial splits and 3,427 threshold comparisons. [Replay](replay_results.json) · [Archive denominators](record_audit.json) · [Fresh checks](stress_wide.json)
6. **Which reading is needed.** Single linkage on the conditioned minima recovers the whole family hierarchy without a cutoff. Applying it directly to the 4,096 common states in the counterexample gives 4,096, 64, 16, 8, 2 and 1 components at thresholds 0, 2, 4, 5, 7 and 8. There are zero thresholds with three components. To construct the counterexample, complete the holdings with one extra even-parity row. Each distinction then occurs twice, so either of two rows can be omitted while preserving the states. Both resulting grown presentations arise by three ordinary one-parent births. A second pair of starts already has different three-family trees at round zero on the same 32,768 states. In the lower bound, after “splits into routes pairing them up,” allow leftover closed routes, discard same-family routes from the lower bound, and charge one distinct loose distinction per odd endpoint. In the set-cost paragraph replace “a proper set T” with “a nonempty proper set T” and “a cheaper chain” with “a crossing step of no greater cost”. ## What was not run

The census arm takes a seeded sample of 1,000 of 7,075 starts. The two labelled rosters reuse 900 six- to eight-knot draws and 400 nine- to twelve-knot draws. The 33 constructed entries are chosen cases. In the frozen replay, 339 next rounds stalled and 16 funded transitions passed the 300-distinction ceiling. Later pair costs on those trajectories were not run. The eight-family entries supply 24 pair-cost stages beyond the frozen six-family limit for set and scale readings. An exhaustive larger census, changed growth laws, recovery from a complete marked growth history, and a general classification of higher-layer places were not run. [Coverage](replay_results.json)
