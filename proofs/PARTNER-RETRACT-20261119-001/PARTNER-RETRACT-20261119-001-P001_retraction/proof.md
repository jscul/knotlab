# A block retraction can lose partnered growth on the third subsequent round

**Standard statement.** A retraction of live rosters is a map \(r\) sending each source block into a target block, with a section \(s\) preserving the target blocks and \(rs=1\). Blocks here include all subsets of the maximal blocks. Candidate pairs may have coincident images. Call the retraction *proper on candidates* when the two images of every candidate pair are distinct. Compare the number of junctions born in each complete round. A round is funded when every live point has at least as many open distinctions as its groups require.

**Result.** Two connected, funded starts with equal numbers of open distinctions at all six section points produce the following populations. All subsequent rounds through the first deficit are lawful, and their births have no offshoots.

| Round from the starts | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Source | 9 | 11 | 28 | **378** |
| Partnered target | 8 | 10 | 24 | **4,086** |

There is a block retraction at the starts and another at their first positive stages. At the latter observation the inequality first fails three rounds later. Two rounds of domination are proved for every funded block retraction onto a growing canonical partnered phase of order at least three. Properness on candidates together with exact section opens gives domination at every round. The example below also places both initial rosters at positive physical age with their six open counts still equal.

## The two funded starts

The partnered target has three pairs of live points \((x,\bar x),(u_0,u_1),(v_0,v_1)\). Its blocks are the twelve nonpartner pairs. Each live point has four opens. Its first eight groups are the transversals \(T\) choosing one point from each pair.

For the source add four live points \(z_{ij}\), \(i,j\in\{0,1\}\), and one point \(w\). Its seventeen maximal blocks are

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 \{x,u_i,z_{i0},z_{i1}\},&\{x,v_j,z_{0j},z_{1j}\},\\
 \{x,z_{00},z_{01},z_{10},z_{11}\},&\\
 \{\bar x,u_i\},\quad \{\bar x,v_j\},\quad \{u_i,v_j\},& \{w,z_{ij}\}.
\end{array}
\]

Keep four opens at each original point, give each \(z_{ij}\) two and \(w\) one. Send all five added points to \(x\) and use the identity section on the six originals. The first two families map to \(\{x,u_i\}\) and \(\{x,v_j\}\). The large block and the last family map to \(\{x\}\). The other pairs are fixed. Every original nonpartner pair occurs on the section and its restriction contains no further block.

The nine groups are the four \(\{x,u_i,v_j,z_{ij}\}\), the four \(\{\bar x,u_i,v_j\}\), and \(Y=\{w,z_{00},z_{01},z_{10},z_{11}\). To see completeness, a maximal candidate clique through \(w\) is \(Y\), and one through \(\bar x\) chooses one \(u_i\) and one \(v_j\). Every other maximal clique contains \(x\). Choosing both a \(u_i\) and a \(v_j\) there leaves exactly one compatible \(z_{ij}\). Choosing just one of these two families, or neither, gives one of the displayed held blocks. The nine valid cliques cover every candidate pair. Their demands exactly use the supplied opens, including those of the five additional points.

Both are literal connected starts. Take one ordinary hub for each maximal block, join it once to each of the indicated live points, and join the hubs consecutively in a cycle. Give every live point its specified opens. Every ordinary support has arity at least three. The hub cycle and the connections through the loose distinctions keep the wired graph connected after deletion of any one junction. Deleting the exterior also leaves it connected. Hence all distinctions can vary and the projection at each junction gives its full parity relation. The literal examples are meet-once, with 28 junctions and 95 distinctions in the source, 18 and 60 in the target. The full supports and independent projection checks are in the replay. ## Three subsequent rounds from a positive observation

Index eight source children by the target transversals \(T\) using the corresponding groups just listed. Write \(y\) for the ninth, with parents \(Y\). Among the eight indexed children, two are candidates precisely when their \(T\)'s are not complementary. Each original parent \(p\) gives the held block of the four \(T\)'s containing \(p\). Write \(B\) for this block at \(p=x\). The extra child \(y\) shares exactly one \(z_{ij}\) with each child in \(B\), and has no other candidate. The only additional maximal blocks are its four separate pairs with these children. This gives a retraction onto the actual first stage of the partnered target: keep the eight names and send \(y\) to any member of \(B\). Every extra pair maps into \(B\). This choice also takes each original parent image of \(Y\) into the parents of \(y\)'s image.

1. **Eleven births against ten.** In the target there are four pairs of complementary points and sixteen possible transversals. Six are held, leaving ten valid groups. Each point belongs to five of them. In the source the ten remain maximal and valid. The extra maximal clique \(Q=B\cup\{y\}\) is valid because \(y\) meets the four members of \(B\) by four different parents. It covers all extra candidate pairs. Its image is exactly the entire held target transversal \(B\). No target child has a parent set containing that image. The eight section points have six opens on \(B\) and five on its complement, while \(y\) has one. All these counts follow from the cheapest rule at their birth.
2. **Twenty-eight births against twenty-four.** The ten ordinary transversal children come in five complementary pairs. The new child with parents \(Q\) is a candidate of all ten, since the only target transversal disjoint from \(B\) is its held complement. All 32 maximal candidate cliques contain this child and one from each of the five pairs. Exactly four are held, one by each old point in \(B\). Indeed the children at any one of the eight section points select one of each complementary pair, as in the partnered recurrence. These eight selections are distinct. The old point also holds the extra child precisely if it belongs to \(B\); the old \(y\) holds only this extra child. Each ordinary candidate pair occurs in eight of the 32 cliques, and each pair using the extra child occurs in sixteen. Thus the 28 valid cliques cover every pair. Each previous child has at least four parents and positive next demand, so its fresh count funds exactly that demand.
3. **378 births against 4,086.** All 28 newborns share the parent \(Q\), so one ordinary block holds them all. Their next groups are exactly their \(\binom{28}{2}=378\) pairs. Each newborn has six parents and its exact funded open count is 27. From the real target's initial population six, WALL-READ gives birth populations \(8,10,24,4086\) at these four ages. In particular the strict deficit occurs while the source is growing.

The source subsequently closes. After the common block of 28, the successive points are all \(k\)-subsets of this set, \(2\le k\le28\). Two \(k\)-subsets are candidates if they share \(k-1\) elements. A pairwise-candidate family either shares such a common subset or lies within a single \((k+1)\)-subset: for two members \(I\cup\{a\},I\cup\{b\}\), any third omitting part of \(I\) must include \(a,b\), after which every member lies in \(I\cup\{a,b\}\). Thus the complete \((k+1)\)-subsets give precisely the unheld groups and cover all candidate pairs. The cheapest count funds the \(28-k\) uses of each point before the last level, and the unique top child takes every remaining loose distinction and closes. The target grows forever.

### Placing the exact-open comparison at positive physical age

One can also realize each of the two *initial* rosters above as the result of a lawful round. For each required block \(B\) introduce a preceding point \(p_B\). For each desired child \(i\) prescribe a parent set \(G_i\) consisting of all \(p_B\) with \(i\in B\), plus three private preceding points. Form the candidate graph by putting all pairs in each \(G_i\). Each \(G_i\) is a maximal clique because its private points have just the other members of \(G_i\) as neighbors. As predecessor blocks take all candidate pairs and every maximal clique other than the \(G_i\), discarding contained blocks. Thus the groups are exactly the \(G_i\), every candidate pair is covered, and supplying each predecessor point its exact demand makes the round lawful. The new shared-parent blocks are exactly the required ones. With at least two children, parent arity at least three, and positive next demand at every child, the cheapest opens are exactly those next demands. These are precisely the opens prescribed in both initial rosters. The same hub-cycle realization gives explicit connected starts for this extra round.

Consequently the six-point partnered **initial roster** and the eleven-point exact-open source can both be observed at positive physical age. ## Sharp two-round consequence of a funded retraction

Take any canonical partnered phase reached from order at least three, including its initial roster. Its outgoing parent groups are precisely its unheld transversals and they use its opens exactly. Let a connected source retract onto it and fund its next round.

For each target group \(T\), extend \(s(T)\) to a maximal candidate clique \(Q_T\) in the source. Any image of a candidate clique is a target clique after coincident names are identified. Since \(T\) contains one point of every partner pair, \(r(Q_T)=T\). No source block can hold \(Q_T\), as its image would hold \(T\). Thus these are distinct genuine source groups. This proves the first count inequality.

For any collection of chosen children \(Q_T\), they share a source parent if and only if the corresponding \(T\)'s have a common point. A shared parent maps into every \(T\), and a common target point has its section in every \(Q_T\). Thus on these chosen children the source candidate and held-block data exactly restrict to the next target phase. Every nonheld next target transversal extends to a maximal source clique. Its original section already lacks a common parent, so the extension is a genuine source group. Distinct target transversals yield distinct extensions because their union contains a complementary pair of noncandidates. The first source birth produced at least the whole target generation, ruling out the single-child closing clause. Its prescribed fresh counts fund all these second-round groups. This proves the second inequality. The positive observation with \(B\cup\{y\}\) disproves a third inequality under the same hypotheses.

## What properness and exact funding buy

Suppose in addition that the retraction is proper on candidates and each section point has exactly its target open count. Every full target transversal is already maximal on its literal section points: an extending point would need an image distinct from every member of that transversal and a candidate of them all. Every excluded target point is the partner of a member. Thus each genuine target group occurs in the source with just its section parents. Those groups alone exhaust every section point's opens. Funding therefore rules out any additional group touching the section, and no section point makes an offshoot.

At the next stage every chosen child has its parents in the section and every other child has its parents outside. Consequently they have no cross-candidates. Groups, demands and fresh counts on the chosen side agree with the partnered target. The latter always makes several children, so there is no global closing exception. The same disjointness and agreement persist at all later rounds. This proves numerical domination for this strengthened hypothesis.

Even here a particular parent map can fail to lift. Take two independent copies of the partnered start on a single connected realization by letting one hub cycle join the dead hubs across the copies. Their live groups and subsequent partnered populations are disjoint. At their first positive age send the first copy identically to the real target; in the second copy send each complementary pair to its own distinct member of the held transversal \(B\). Distinct members of any source block get distinct images, and all second-copy block images lie in \(B\). All opens are five. Every one of the ten genuine second-copy groups maps exactly onto \(B\), impossible as the parents of a target child. The source has twice the target population at every round. This separates transporting the specified map on parent incidences from the numerical assertion.
