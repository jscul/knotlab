# Assumptions

What the contact-first program assumes but could challenge. Each entry says what is assumed, why it is held without proof, what
rests on it and how it could be challenged, with its status and a dated history. Epics can be dedicated to challenging an entry,
and their outcomes are recorded here.

The register was started on 2026-11-07 at the owner's request: "These are things that we're assuming, but that can be challenged.
We can have epics dedicated to challenging assumptions. This list will help us keep track of what we're assuming." It adds no
evidence: results and their status come from the records each entry links.

## The rules

- **What counts.** A decision taken by default rather than proved: a reading adopted where another was available, a scope or
  measure chosen without comparing the alternatives, a premise that a line of work builds on before it is proved, or a question
  the owner records as an assumption instead of deciding it. A theorem is not an assumption, but the readings it is proved under
  can be.
- **Changes.** Any line of work may add an entry or change a status, and reports the change to the owner. An assumption that is
  part of the canonical knot test or the foundational thesis changes only through a canonical correction request to the owner. IDs
  are never reused, and entries are never deleted.
- **Challenges.** An epic dedicated to an assumption marks it Under challenge when it starts. When the epic closes, the entry
  becomes Confirmed or Overturned, or returns to Assumed, with a history line naming the epic.
- **Overturned.** Every published claim that rested on an overturned assumption is corrected, and the correction is recorded.

## Reading an entry

- **The status line** gives the status, the date it was set, and the kind.
- **Statement.** What is assumed, precisely enough to be tested.
- **Why.** Why it is held without proof: who adopted it, when, and on what evidence.
- **Rests on it.** The results, readings and lines of work that would change if it fell.
- **Challenge.** The alternative, the cheapest test that could tell them apart, and what would confirm or overturn it. For a
  settled entry, the challenge that settled it.
- **History.** Dated lines, newest first: added, challenged, confirmed, overturned, reworded.

## Statuses

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Assumed** | In force: work proceeds as if it were true, and no epic is testing it. |
| **Under challenge** | An epic dedicated to testing it is open, and the history names it. Work may still rely on it. |
| **Confirmed** | A challenge ended without overturning it: the claim was proved, or the alternative was mapped and the assumption kept for stated reasons. It can be reopened. |
| **Overturned** | A challenge showed it false or replaced it. The entry says what replaced it. |

## Kinds

| Kind | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **premise** | Part of the program's thesis or its canonical test: the theory under test. |
| **reading** | One way of reading a definition where another was available. |
| **scope** | What the program counts or studies, and what it sets aside. |
| **measure** | How the program measures a quantity it reports. |

### A1 · A native knot on an infinite system is also natively bound

**Assumed** · 2026-11-07 · reading

- **Statement.** A knot S of an infinite system is natively bound at a solution x when S is also a knot of the closure of x's
  homoclinic class: the solutions that differ from x in finitely many places. A knot that is native in every variable (A9) is
  assumed to be natively bound as well. Each knot claim on an infinite system records whether the knot is natively bound, beside
  its K1–K3 verdict and the source of each distinction, and claims of distance, locality or spacing there use natively bound knots.
- **Why.** Native variables do not say where a knot's binding comes from. In the law with Ledrappier's rule on x, z free and
  y = x + z, every variable is flipped by a finite move, yet for every k a six-variable knot joins points 2^k apart, bound through
  the hidden field x, whose openness comes only from the ends
  ([proof](../proofs/LOGIC-AXIS-20261107-001A/LOGIC-AXIS-20261107-001A-P001_rulers_without_ends/proof.md), Proposition 5).
  Requiring native binding makes every native knot of a parity law chained, so bond distance is a ruler in every parity law
  (Proposition 2), and it adds no primitive: it reuses the finite moves that already define nativity. The logic-axis epic
  recommended it, and the owner chose to record it as an assumption rather than decide it
  ([request](../research/LOGIC-AXIS-20261107-001/canonical_correction_request.md)).
- **Rests on it.** Every reading of native knots on infinite systems as local, and every claim of distance, locality or spacing
  there (question 9 of [the knot intuition](KNOT_INTUITION.md#open-questions)). Earlier verdicts keep their labels: Tseitin's
  law and the gauge law on the square grid have no ends, so their native knots are natively bound, and Ledrappier's law has no
  native knot either way. The long knots of the law above are native in every variable but not natively bound.
- **Challenge.** The alternative keeps nativity per variable and has each claim about distance or locality state only whether
  the law has ends. Whether natively bound knots are always chained is open beyond parity laws. A non-linear law whose natively
  bound knots join far points without a chain would remove the reason for the assumption, and so would a law in which the label
  discards knots that every other test treats as emergence.
- **History.**
  - 2026-11-07 · Recorded as an assumption at the owner's request, instead of a decision on the request. The canonical text of
    the source condition is unchanged.
  - 2026-11-07 · Raised as a decision request by `LOGIC-AXIS-20261107-001`, which recommended it.

### A2 · First emergence is a logical knot

**Assumed** · 2026-07-24 · premise

- **Statement.** The first structure that logic forms without presupposing space is a knot: loops that each leave a distinction
  open, bound through one contact so that the whole is constrained and every part is free (K1–K3). One knot is the first atomic
  logical topology and the minimal contact-distance. Paths, networks and extended distance are built from many.
- **Why.** It is the program's working theory, protected by the
  [foundational thesis](FOUNDATIONAL_THESIS.md#emergence-atomic-topology-and-distance). It is not a theorem: the theorems say
  which relations are knots and which constraint languages define them (A16), not that knots are where emergence begins.
- **Rests on it.** The program's reading of its results: a result about knots is read as a result about emergence, space or
  hiddenness because of this premise.
- **Challenge.** Look for structure the program would count as emergent that no knot carries. Known places to look: competing
  pairs make knots among their least-violation states (A8), pairs alone make a knot on three-valued distinctions (A10), and
  trends at infinity are invisible to the knot test
  ([holes at infinity](../research/HOLES-AT-INFINITY-20261020-001/holes_at_infinity.md)). A successful challenge goes to the
  owner as a canonical correction request.
- **History.**
  - 2026-10-19 · Made precise by the canonical knot test K1–K3, adopted by the owner.
  - 2026-07-24 · Stated in the workspace's first commit.

### A3 · A verdict depends only on the allowed states

**Assumed** · 2026-10-19 · premise

- **Statement.** A system of constraints is read as the set of joint states it allows, with witness variables quantified away.
  Whether it contains a knot depends only on that set, never on how the constraints are written or the order in which they are
  checked, and every map that preserves the allowed states preserves the verdict.
- **Why.** Adopted with the [canonical knot test](FOUNDATIONAL_THESIS.md#canonical-knot-test) after an earlier proof's knot
  turned out to depend on its executor: cross-wired two-clause guards act like a knot only if each loop's clauses are checked
  only while that loop runs.
- **Rests on it.** Every K1–K3 verdict, the knot laboratory, the census of 34 two-loop knots, and the theorem that no 2-CNF
  defines a knot (A16).
- **Challenge.** On infinite systems the source-aware reading (A9) already lets verdicts depend on more than the projected
  relation: on how the system's solutions are joined by finite changes. A challenge would ask whether something the projection
  forgets, such as a growth order (A14) or the way solutions connect, ever carries emergence that the allowed states miss.
- **History.**
  - 2026-10-19 · Adopted with K1–K3 as the canonical test.

### A4 · Nothing outside the constraints supplies an input

**Assumed** · 2026-10-19 · premise

- **Statement.** No executor, scheduler or checker may supply an input to a system. A constraint that should hold only while a
  loop runs carries the loop's presence as a literal, and formation order alone is not physical time.
- **Why.** Adopted with the [canonical knot test](FOUNDATIONAL_THESIS.md#canonical-knot-test). An earlier proof's executor
  checked a loop's clauses only while the loop ran, which supplied each loop's presence as a hidden third input and made a common
  hub of two-clause guards pass for a knot.
- **Rests on it.** The negative result for 2-CNF, which no evaluation rule can evade, and every growth result: a growth result
  belongs to the law only if it holds in every growth order (A14).
- **Challenge.** Question 4 of [the knot intuition](KNOT_INTUITION.md#open-questions) asks whether loops are better modelled as
  processes. Write a growth process as one law on space and time, and ask whether it has knots that no static law has and
  whether its order can be a variable of the law rather than an input.
- **History.**
  - 2026-11-07 · Its growth clause tested by `GROWTH-LAW-20261107-001A`. A growth order written as variables of the law never
    knots with the world's states, and existence enters as the presence literal this premise prescribes. A rule that reads a
    state does knot the order to the states, which is the input it forbids. The status is unchanged, since this is a premise.
  - 2026-10-19 · Adopted with K1–K3 as the canonical test.

### A5 · A loop is anything that leaves a distinction open

**Assumed** · 2026-10-19 · premise

- **Statement.** The thesis asks for two independently witnessed native recurrences. The knot test reads a recurrence as
  whatever leaves a distinction open: a variable, or a named pattern such as a parity, that the structure leaves free. A loop's
  presence is not a variable.
- **Why.** Settled in form with the canonical test: K1–K3 applies to open distinctions, and which structures count as native
  loops is interpretive (question 1 of [the knot intuition](KNOT_INTUITION.md#open-questions)).
- **Rests on it.** Every knot claim, since K1–K3 is checked on distinctions, not on loops.
- **Challenge.** Question 1 asks which structures should count as native loops, and it is open. Infinite systems showed that
  the source can matter: an end can open a distinction that no loop opens, and the knot test cannot tell the two apart, which
  led to A9. A challenge would look for open distinctions of different kinds in finite systems and ask whether the test should
  separate them.
- **History.**
  - 2026-10-20 · Refined for infinite systems by the source-aware reading (A9).
  - 2026-10-19 · Settled in form with the canonical test; the question of where presence comes from was retired.

### A6 · Erasure is forgetting

**Assumed** · 2026-10-19 · reading

- **Statement.** Erasing a loop forgets its distinction, so that nothing constrains it any more. A knot is then constrained as a
  whole and unconstrained in every part: forgetting any one variable, a loop or the contact, leaves the rest unconstrained (K2).
- **Why.** The owner chose it on 2026-10-19
  ([decision](../research/KNOT-FOUNDATION-20261019-001/canonical_correction_request.md)). Erasure entered the agents' gate in the
  workspace's first commit as a test of decomposability, which is the forgetting reading, and the program began from parity
  patterns, which are knots only under forgetting.
- **Rests on it.** Parity as the knot that hides the most, the 34 two-loop knots in five shapes, and every result about parity's
  knots since 2026-10-19.
- **Challenge.** The absence reading (A17) keeps only the atom. Every knot it accepts is a knot under forgetting, and both
  readings exclude the common hub and every 2-CNF pattern, so a challenge needs a question whose answer changes between them.
  The knot laboratory keeps both readings.
- **History.**
  - 2026-11-07 · Used by `GROWTH-LAW-20261107-001A`. With the variables of absent sites and pairs forgotten, no knot joins a
    growth order to the world's states. Setting them to 0 instead makes order bits carry existence, and they then knot with the
    states.
  - 2026-10-19 · Chosen by the owner, replacing the absence reading adopted earlier the same day (A17).

### A7 · Witnesses are existential

**Assumed** · 2026-10-19 · reading

- **Statement.** A state is allowed when some assignment of the witnesses satisfies every constraint. Witnesses carry no signs,
  so they cannot cancel.
- **Why.** The canonical reading since the test was adopted. On 2026-10-29 the signed reading, in which a state is allowed when
  its signed witness sum is nonzero, was adopted beside it under the owner's rule for forks between readings, and later results
  record both verdicts ([adoption](../research/INTERFERENCE-20261029-001/canonical_correction_request.md)).
- **Rests on it.** The two/three boundary as stated (A16): with signed witnesses every three-bit knot is one cancelling witness
  away from pairs, so the boundary becomes a boundary of positivity. Also "only parity survives native patterns of patterns"
  (`GEOMETRY-LAW-20261029-001`), which the signed reading changes: native gadgets then rebuild 14 of the 16 knot classes.
- **Challenge.** Both readings are mapped side by side. A challenge would ask which one the program's questions need, for
  example whether cancelling witnesses build a space that no positive law builds. Interference reproduces knots on finitely
  ramified, tree-like space but not on genuine two-dimensional space (`SIGNED-SUBDIVISION-20261029-001`).
- **History.**
  - 2026-10-29 · The signed reading adopted beside it, reopenable (`INTERFERENCE-20261029-001`).
  - 2026-10-19 · Adopted with the canonical test.

### A8 · Allowed means every constraint holds

**Assumed** · 2026-10-19 · reading

- **Statement.** A state is allowed only when it satisfies every constraint (the strict reading). When the constraints cannot
  all hold, the states that break the fewest are not allowed for that reason.
- **Why.** The strict reading keeps verdicts independent of presentation and gives pairs no knots. The competition reading
  needs a declared measure of violation. Both were mapped on 2026-10-19 and the choice was left open
  ([competing pairs](../research/KNOT-COMPETITION-20261019-001/competition.md); question 10 of
  [the knot intuition](KNOT_INTUITION.md#open-questions)).
- **Rests on it.** The theorem that pairs never make a knot, in its strict form (A16).
- **Challenge.** Under the competition reading, frustrated pairs make exactly one kind of knot, the not-all-equal triangle, and
  competition turns a triangulated layout into a chain of knots but does not make the layout. A challenge would adopt it as a
  second reading, as signed witnesses were, and record both verdicts.
- **History.**
  - 2026-10-19 · Both readings mapped (`KNOT-COMPETITION-20261019-001`); the strict reading kept and the choice left open.

### A9 · Native knots are opened by finite changes

**Assumed** · 2026-10-20 · reading

- **Statement.** On an infinite system, a knot is native at a solution only when each of its variables is flipped there by some
  change of finitely many variables. Every knot claim on an infinite system records its K1–K3 verdict and the source of each
  distinction: loop-supplied, end-supplied or fixed. Finite systems are unaffected.
- **Why.** An infinite system's ends can open distinctions that no loop opens, and K1–K3 cannot tell the two apart: the
  3-regular tree has a knot at every vertex and no loop at all
  ([holes at infinity](../research/HOLES-AT-INFINITY-20261020-001/holes_at_infinity.md)). Asked which reading gives more
  interesting results, the owner adopted the source-aware one, reopenable. Under it emergence depends on the law and on the
  solution ([frozen worlds](../research/FROZEN-WORLDS-20261020-001/frozen_worlds.md);
  [adoption](../research/HOLES-AT-INFINITY-20261020-001/canonical_correction_request.md)).
- **Rests on it.** Frozen and flexible worlds: a linear law is native everywhere or nowhere, colourings have frozen worlds
  without native knots, and Ledrappier's law has knots but no native knot. A1 adds a requirement to it.
- **Challenge.** The source-blind reading counts end-supplied distinctions too. Both verdicts are recorded for every claim, so
  reopening needs no new derivation. A challenge would look for a result that the source-blind reading makes more interesting.
- **History.**
  - 2026-11-07 · Strengthened by A1 (natively bound).
  - 2026-10-20 · Adopted by the owner, reopenable.

### A10 · The base distinction is a bit

**Assumed** · 2026-10-21 · scope

- **Statement.** A distinction has two values. The thesis is the boundary between what clauses of two and of three literals can
  express, clauses are over bits, and laws over larger domains are a different question, not a test of the thesis.
- **Why.** The owner's statement of the thesis on 2026-10-21. The base-three epic found that the threshold is three somewhere:
  three places on bits, or three values on pairs. The two/three boundary is fundamental exactly when the base distinction is a
  bit ([base three](../research/BASE-THREE-20261021-001/base_three.md)).
- **Rests on it.** The two/three boundary as the boundary of emergence (A16), and every census of knots on bits.
- **Challenge.** On three-valued distinctions pairs alone make a knot: a hidden vertex adjacent to three visible ones, under
  colouring constraints, makes "not all different" ([Gallai knots](../research/GALLAI-KNOTS-20261020-001/gallai_knots.md)). A
  challenge would ask whether the bit is forced, for example whether a three-valued distinction written natively on bits needs a
  three-place knot, which would move the boundary back to three places. The base-three epic proposed mapping all 512 binary
  relations on three values; that was deferred.
- **History.**
  - 2026-10-21 · Stated by the owner after the base-three epic.

### A11 · Hubs are a declared substrate, never contact

**Assumed** · 2026-10-29 · scope

- **Statement.** Native contact shares each distinction between exactly two knots. A distinction shared by three or more knots
  is a hub: equality fan-out, which is pairwise. Hub laws are in scope as a declared substrate, but a hub is never counted as
  contact or emergence.
- **Why.** The canonical common hub, two attachments A-k and B-k, fails K2, and a distinction in three or more knots ties its
  copies by equality. On 2026-10-29 the owner put hub laws in scope
  ([hub survivors](../research/HUB-SURVIVORS-20261029-001/hub_survivors.md)).
- **Rests on it.** Surfaces glued natively, with every shared distinction in exactly two knots (`SURFACE-GLUING-20261024-001`),
  and every result that compares a law with and without hubs.
- **Challenge.** With hubs, emergence beyond parity persists across scales (`HUB-SURVIVORS-20261029-001`). A challenge would
  ask whether some sharing among three or more knots is native after all, for example whether a law can form its own fan-out
  without an equality.
- **History.**
  - 2026-10-29 · Hub laws put in scope by the owner, as a declared substrate.

### A12 · Bond distance is a law's own spacing

**Assumed** · 2026-10-21 · measure

- **Statement.** The spacing a law sets between two distinctions is their bond distance: the size of the least knot through
  both, minus one, read from the allowed states alone. Dimension is read from how fast bond balls grow, and a circle is the
  large-scale shape of a bond ball.
- **Why.** It uses nothing but knots and allowed states, so no metric, coordinate or drawing is put in. On a thread of atoms it
  counts the loops between two points, one unit per loop, which matches the owner's reading of the integers' spacing as space
  ([logic axis](../research/LOGIC-AXIS-20261107-001/logic_axis.md)).
- **Rests on it.** The results on space from a law since 2026-11-07: the number line, the taxicab plane, the circles of
  periodic and pinwheel laws, and dimension read from ball growth (question 9).
- **Challenge.** Other measures can be read from allowed states, such as the shortest chain of knots joining two distinctions,
  each sharing a distinction with the next. On tree-shaped knot graphs bond distance is the number of knots on the path plus
  one, so the two agree there, but they need not agree elsewhere. A challenge would compare them on the laws already measured
  and ask whether any dimension or circle changes.
- **History.**
  - 2026-11-07 · Used as the measure of space from a law (`LOGIC-AXIS-20261107-001`).
  - 2026-10-21 · Defined from the allowed states of a declared substrate (`NATIVE-NETWORK-20261021-001`).

### A13 · Witness variables are part of a law, not a drawing

**Assumed** · 2026-11-07 · reading

- **Statement.** A uniform local law may use hidden witness variables, quantified away, and a space it builds with them is still
  built with nothing drawn. The number of hidden bits is reported with the result.
- **Why.** The canonical setting quantifies witnesses away (A3), so hidden variables belong to a law's constraints, unlike
  coordinates or positions written in advance.
- **Rests on it.** "With one witness bit per incidence, quantified away, a uniform local law lays out its own round sphere"
  ([sphere growth](../research/SPHERE-GROWTH-20261107-001/sphere_growth.md)), the eleven-label pinwheel rule
  (`PINWHEEL-NATIVE-20261107-001`), and A14.
- **Challenge.** Decorated matching rules force essentially every substitution tiling (Mozes 1989; Goodman-Strauss 1998), so
  with enough labels a law can carry almost any self-similar layout. A challenge would ask which spaces need no hidden bits, how
  the number of bits needed grows with the size, and whether labels that grow with the size are a drawing in disguise.
- **History.**
  - 2026-11-07 · Added when the register was started. Witness bits were first used to lay out a space in
    `SPHERE-GROWTH-20261107-001`, and how many bits roundness costs is open.

### A14 · A growing law's history is a hidden witness

**Confirmed** · 2026-11-07 · reading

- **Statement.** A growth result belongs to the law only if it holds in every growth order. Information that a growth process
  keeps from earlier steps, such as a bit carried for one lap, is a hidden witness of the world and is counted as one.
- **Why.** It follows from A4: a process may not supply an input, so a kept bit must be written into the system as a variable,
  and a variable that is quantified away is a witness. The grow-coin epic applied it: one bit kept for one lap ties a sphere's
  four equator corners together, so growth lays out a new round sphere only with a witness, just as a finished sphere is checked
  round only with one ([grow coin](../research/GROW-COIN-20261107-001/grow_coin.md)).
- **Rests on it.** "Growth does not remove the witness" (`GROW-COIN-20261107-001`), and with it the reading that a law lays out
  a new round sphere only with hidden information; "a beginning is a knot" and the reading of what growth adds
  ([growth as a law](../research/GROWTH-LAW-20261107-001/growth_law.md)).
- **Challenge.** Settled by `GROWTH-LAW-20261107-001`, which wrote growth histories into the law as variables and read them
  under K1–K3 ([proof](../proofs/GROWTH-LAW-20261107-001A/GROWTH-LAW-20261107-001A-P001_a_beginning_is_a_knot/proof.md)).
  - **The order adds no knot.** It never knots with the world's states. Existence enters only as a presence literal.
  - **The history's parts.** Its local part (which neighbour came first) is pairwise and holds no knot. Its global part, time
    without a loop, is a knot on every cycle of the world, so a beginning is a knot.
  - **What is left to carry.** What a kept history adds beyond bounded local information is only whether a world has a
    beginning.
  - **The plane.** There label-free growth by the pinwheel's own law left the pinwheel at once
    ([pinwheel growth](../artifacts/GROWTH-LAW-20261107-001B/GROWTH-LAW-20261107-001B-E001_pinwheel_grown_outward/run/interpretation.md)).
  - **For whom.** Confirmed for rules that read only the law, with absent things forgotten. A rule that reads a state is not a
    law in this sense, and fixing absent variables instead of forgetting them lets order bits carry existence.
- **History.**
  - 2026-11-07 · Confirmed by `GROWTH-LAW-20261107-001` (tickets A to C), for the reasons above.
