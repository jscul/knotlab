# The roles and next births forced by the 29-name pair readings

Supply the ordinary funded not-all-equal growth law and the complete indexed table of pair disagreement probabilities on a positive-age cut. The age, source, roles, number of words and physical holdings are withheld. At every funded cut an old loose name is consumed once, each new knot takes one input per distinct parent and gets at least one fresh name. A *loose* name has exactly one current holder. The two weightings are uniform on present words and recursive equal division of each earlier word’s mass among its extensions. The theorem below holds for each observation and **every reached rival** under its own weighting. The earlier catalogue is used only to specify which observation is tested. ## A forward check with no enumeration of the 29-name words

The selected connected start has nine names in three knots, on (0,6,7), (1,2,3,4,5,8) and (6,7,8). It has 310 allowed words. Its paid first cut has one child from inputs (0,1), with fresh 9–12, and one from each of 2, 3, 4 and 5, with four fresh names per child in consecutive groups 13–16, 17–20, 21–24 and 25–28. This construction verifies existence. The converse below uses the table alone.

Use signs +1 and −1 and write C(i,j)=1−2r(i,j). Given the old word, the four new values in a group are uniform on all 16 assignments for mixed inputs and on 15 assignments for inputs with a common sign. For the latter, the sign mean of one fresh name is minus the common sign divided by 15, and the product mean on any two different fresh names in its group is −1/15. Both means are zero for mixed inputs. Distinct groups are conditionally independent. Of the 310 old words, 154 have 759,375 extensions and 156 have 810,000, giving 243,303,750 current words. Weight the conditional answers by those extension counts for the uniform table and equally for the inherited table. This independent calculation reproduced every one of the 406 entries in each table. The two tables differ on 366 entries. ## Which roles can occur

The last-step conditional identity from the preceding study gives the exact support H around a known loose name f: its signed row minimum m is strictly negative, and its minimizers are precisely H without f. It holds under either weighting, since the two choices of f have equal conditional weight whenever both are allowed. An exterior name has a strict inequality by pinning a legal word where it differs from the unanimous companions. When testing whether f is loose, it is necessary that its companions be constant with probability −m. With two or three companions this probability follows exactly from pair correlations and complement symmetry. Every proper triple of a larger set of companions has strictly greater constant probability by full local projection. [Former conditional identity](../../../research/STATE-DISTANCE-AND-REACHED-GROWTH-20261120-001B/reading.md)

For a proposed loose f, the entire fresh set F consists of those in H whose rows agree with f’s away from the two names compared. All current fresh siblings are exchangeable. A consumed input x cannot be such a sibling. At uniform weights, exchanging x and f at fixed exterior shows that C(x,z)−C(f,z) is strictly negative at each other name of x’s earlier holder K. Its minimum is attained there. Terms for which K without x is mixed cancel. If K without x is constant, x is forced opposite that sign, and its difference from f is zero or twice that forced sign. Full projection of K and a choice of f opposite x give strict negativity. A repeated meeting at an arbitrary original source can add further minimizers and is allowed in the test.

For inherited weights, suppose f had identical outside readings to an old input x. If there is another input a in H, then C(x,a)=C(f,a)=m. Every other name y of K is exterior to H, because H uses one input per distinct previous parent. Hence C(x,y)=C(f,y)>m. But restriction to previous names preserves all their inherited pair readings, and x was then loose in K, so its signed row minimum must be attained at every such y. This is a contradiction. When x is the sole input, conditional on its value a fresh sign has expectation −s(x)/(2^|F|−1). For every y in K without x, its strictly negative previous correlation with x and its correlation with f then have opposite signs. This leaves only genuine fresh siblings in F.

On the exact observed tables the minimum, proper-triple and twin tests leave 11 possible assignments for a single current holding under uniform weights and 12 under inherited weights. For a unary or binary input set I the conditional sign formula gives, for any z outside F, the identity `|I|(2^|F|−1) C(f,z) = −Σᵢ C(i,z)`, summed over i in I.

For two inputs the sum of their signs is twice their common sign when they agree and zero when they differ. For one input the formula is immediate. This excludes the two candidates with f=0 or 8 and proposed inputs 6 and 7 under both weightings. For example, with f=0 the sides differ at z=1 under uniform weights and at z=9 under inherited weights.

The remaining false possibilities each mark just one name loose and reinterpret a group of four equal-row names as old inputs. Under uniform weights these reversals have f=2, 3, 4 or 5. For each of the four putative inputs x, the only exterior minimizers of C(x,z)−C(f,z) are 6 and 7. Every previous holder of x requires at least two other names, all exterior to H. The four inputs require four **distinct** previous holders, and neither 6 nor 7 can occur more than twice. Eight required appearances cannot fit into four available positions.

For inherited weights each of these four reversals gives, in the row of any one of its four inputs, a value −1/15 at its three other inputs. Every exterior name has a strictly higher value. All four inputs remain in the previous marginal, so no exterior companions can minimize that old loose row. For the one further reversal with f=1, the four inputs are 9–12. Each has signed correlation −77/2325 with the other three and with 0. Of exterior names, only 0 has correlation this low. An earlier holder still needs two exterior companions. Thus this reversal too is impossible. These tests allow for any of the other possible current fresh marks to be absent from the preceding carrier.

## Four verdicts from the ordinary price

Only five possible current holders remain under either weighting. Each has precisely its designated four fresh names. Every positive-age not-all-equal stage has at least one live holder. Any included one was priced at four, above its one- or two-fresh arity floor, so it must occur in four proposed multi-parent birth groups at the current cut.

Fix one live holder and delete it from each of its incident proposed groups. The resulting nonempty sets of its co-neighbors are pairwise incomparable by inclusion: the larger unheld groups are maximal and an individual pair is proposed only if no larger group covers it. Among at most three co-neighbors there are at most three mutually incomparable nonempty subsets (one may list the seven nonempty subsets of three). Therefore every included holder needs at least four co-neighbors. All five supports are forced, and each pair of them is witnessed.

If the five together had no common witness, the whole set of five would be the sole maximal unheld group and would charge each parent only once. A common witness is forced. The ordinary rule thus proposes all ten binary pairs and all four loose names of each parent are consumed. At the next lookahead the children are the ten pairs on five indices. Mutually intersecting such pairs either share one index or comprise three pairs from three indices. The former have a common witness; the latter form an unheld triple. Every pair child belongs to exactly three unheld triples, one for each choice of a third index. Its actual fresh count is three.

Hence the **loose roles** are exactly names 9–28 and the **next births** are exactly the ten unordered pairs of the five named current supports, each with three fresh distinctions. Both statements hold for the uniform table and both hold for the inherited table. A simultaneous renaming of all entries carries the unique role set and roster with it.

The old holdings can also be read in this example. Both inverse row tests bound the companions of input 0 by {6,7}, forcing K₀=(0,6,7). For each input 1–5 the only possible companions are the other members of {1,2,3,4,5,8}. The common witness of all five live knots is one previous parent, since only a previous parent can meet a newborn. It holds inputs 2–5 of the four unary children, and it cannot hold input 0 of the binary child, so it holds 1 as well. Because that binary joined it to K₀, each of the two parents needed an already shared name to meet a witness. Thus it also holds 8. All remaining old incidences force the witness to be (6,7,8). These nine-name holdings could not themselves have been a positive-age cut, since removing their live holders leaves the single (6,7,8), which could not donate both 6 and 7 to (0,6,7). Age one has therefore been deduced for this example. It was never supplied as an input to the converse. ## What the two readings identify at every size

Suppose the distinctions of one latest ordinary knot H are given, with their roles hidden. Call two names twins when their pair readings agree against every other name. The marked argument above excludes a fresh name and a consumed input being twins. Here is why two old inputs in H cannot be twins either, even if names at the source can have the same two holders.

Let u and v be inputs from two distinct earlier holders, with respective companion sets A and B. Under uniform current words, exchange u with a fresh f on the event that A is mixed. The contributions cancel in the difference C(u,z)−C(f,z). When A has one sign, u has the opposite sign. The minimum of the difference is attained at every z in A. Attaining it requires z to have the sign of A whenever A is constant and f differs from u. Every such older word can extend with f opposite to u. Apply the same reasoning to v and B. Twin rows would have the same minima on the common domain outside u, v and f, which includes both A and B. Thus A constant of a given sign forces all of B to have that sign, and conversely. Under inherited weights restriction gives precisely the previous marginal. The loose row of u in that marginal has minimum −Pr(A constant), and outside u equality requires the sign of A on every word where A is constant. This gives the same two implications.

Remove the earlier holders of u and v. Their respective loose names can restore both deleted rules to any residual solution. In the remaining system, pin A to zero. The pinning is feasible by full projection. Every member of B outside A must be zero and has at most one residual occurrence. Use ordinary not-all-equal unit propagation. At a fixed point, prescribing a value on one still-free name of degree at most one can force a value in at most one other holder at a time. Each traversed holder is permanently satisfied and every newly visited holder previously had at least two free names. This introduces no conflict. The pinned completion lemma then supplies a solution. Hence all forced zeros of B outside A already arose in the initial propagation. [Pinned completion proof](../../../research/THREE-READERS-AND-GROWTH-20261120-001B/recovery_argument.md#completing-a-partly-specified-state)

Producing each of these zeros required two earlier ones, each in turn requiring at least two earlier zeros. Each original pin occurs at most once in this residual system. Each propagated value can be used at most once more. Each target has at most one occurrence and cannot contribute to a second target. These disjoint histories therefore charge four different original pins in A outside B to each target in B outside A. Pins shared by A and B have no remaining occurrence. Interchanging A and B gives the reverse inequality, forcing A=B. The two old holders then use both occurrences of every companion and have private u and v. There is no third holder to witness their common ordinary birth. This proves the exclusion. Finite controls enumerate every such residual family with distinct rules of sizes three through six on at most six names and every feasible set of singly held or unused names pinned to zero. A seven-name example attains the factor four. [Independent controls](../../../research/LOOSE-NAMES-AND-SPENT-JOINS-20261120-001/check_forced_names.py) · [Exact totals](../../../research/LOOSE-NAMES-AND-SPENT-JOINS-20261120-001/forced_names_replay.json)

Consequently any twin pair within a known latest holding identifies its whole set of fresh names under either weighting. When H has no twins it has exactly one fresh name. Under inherited weights a false candidate u would minimize its row at a second old input inside H, strictly below every name in its former holder outside H. Preservation of the previous marginal and the old loose-row minimum exclude this. Under uniform weights the ternary case also follows: if f and u both served as the lone fresh name, their two binary-input identities above, applied to all z other than f and u including the third name of H, would give C(f,z)=C(u,z). This contradicts the lack of twins. The scientific study supplies the full argument and an independent catalogue of controls. Under uniform weights a supplied latest holding with one fresh name and at least four places remains open in general. The two named word weightings, target ordering and ordinary growth rule are premises. Identifiability for every unmarked reached table remains open.
