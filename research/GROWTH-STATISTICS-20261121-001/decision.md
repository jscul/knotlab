### Adaptive search versus independent sampling

Both arms used the same lawful starts, readings, growth limits and allowances. Four, five and six starting knots were equally weighted. At each size, labelled trees were uniform, half the draws added one meeting, and each knot independently had a half chance of one extra loose distinction. The adaptive arm retained starts with distinct early behavior and tried lawful edits. A later behavior could qualify without being named in advance. It had to be absent from the reference sample, occur on two distinct starts in one run, and survive independent replay.

| Law · 48 paired runs each | Independent types per run (total) | Adaptive types per run (total) | Independent wins / ties / losses | Mean gain, paired 95% bootstrap interval |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Parity | 3.67 (176) | 2.92 (140) | 27 / 13 / 8 | +0.75 [0.40, 1.10] |
| Not-all-equal | 3.02 (145) | 2.23 (107) | 30 / 12 / 6 | +0.79 [0.46, 1.13] |

Both methods found a qualifying type in **48/48 runs per law**. Pooled across runs, both found the same six parity types and five not-all-equal types. Each arm received 80 ms of search CPU and 50 ms for verification. Selection, edits, duplicates, stalls and work crossing the cutoff were charged. The 192 arms made **206,892 proposals** and used **16.83 CPU seconds** of the 24.96-second allowance. No run reached the cap of 192 births per round. Independent sampling also won at equal proposal counts. A post hoc comparison matched the number of *distinct lawful starts actually grown*. Its totals were 131 independent versus 140 adaptive under parity and 113 versus 107 under not-all-equal. Revisiting starts mattered to the cost result. [Search costs, uncertainty and raw records](https://github.com/jscul/knotlab/blob/main/experiments/GROWTH-STATISTICS-20261121-001B/GROWTH-STATISTICS-20261121-001B-E001_search/result.md)

**Smallest reproducible surprise.** In each start one knot met three others, with 11 distinctions in total. Their loose counts, listing the central knot first, were (0, 2, 3, 3) and (1, 2, 2, 3). Moving that one distinction preserved **five births, then five births**, but changed the third count **from three to four** under both laws. Eight loose distinctions grouped as 3+3+2. Seven grouped as 3+2+1+1. The proposed predictor, “more loose distinctions means more births,” got this pair backwards. Among 156 fresh starts, only **5 of 12,090 pairs** matched on the early counts and had observable third counts. Two tied. The predictor correctly ordered the other three, the same pairs under both laws.

The smallest type missing from the reference was simpler. A four-knot chain with nine distinctions and a closed chain with ten each produced four births in all five rounds through the same recurring pairs. The births and the three-versus-four difference follow from growth. Calling a behavior new depended on the reference sample and on reading late changes in birth counts and numbers of parents.
