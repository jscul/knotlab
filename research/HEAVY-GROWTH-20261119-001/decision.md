# What keeps growing after the recurring offshoot is removed?

**The heavy part never closes on either start. Whether its births per round grow without bound remains open on both.** From round seven onward there are at least **73 heavy births on 03** and **100 on 14** in every round. A lawful member of the same seven-place sample, **start-13**, does have heavy births growing at least exponentially. In the other direction, **02, 05 and 11** close. ## What the chain actually supplies

At a reached age remove each recurring one-parent tip and the child that joins its older tip to its older companion. Count a shared marked junction once. Every other birth counts as heavy. After the first two rounds on either start the companion has four heavy neighbors. Each spends one loose distinction to join it. Their four new children share the erased companion as a parent, and the resulting four-junction block must be kept for the next round. With those four marked junctions and that shared block, the heavy part has an exact rule of its own. Simply growing the restricted junctions gives a different result. Erasing both and recalculating the loose counts at round seven yields 28,059 round-eight births on 03. The full growing end actually produces 28,063 heavy births. The four incoming children account for the difference. [Boundary recurrence and its proof](interface.md) · [Both erasures, with both choices of loose counts](heavy_samples.md)

A fixed part of the growing end is renewed every round. All five candidate steps out from the tip are read exactly on both starts, yielding the 73 and 100 lower bounds. Among them a recurring seventeen-junction part contains eleven heavy births with at least three parents. Isolating its fifteen heavy members and funding them alone ends in three rounds. There are also connected controls with precisely four incoming children per round for which total heavy births stay at fifteen forever, and others where heavy births tend to infinity. The single-tip boundary does not decide between them. [Stationary restrictions and contrasting controls](interface.md) · [Renewed larger groups](heavy_samples.md)

On **03**, the round-ten junction with at least **2,174,695,764,692,054 parents** is heavy. It survives both forms of offshoot erasure at round eight through the two following transitions and is exactly eight candidate steps from the intact tip. Round nine has at least **2,174,695,764,693,285 heavy births**. A finite walk gives a junction at least ten steps from the tip by round fifteen. We have neither a repeating whole growing end nor a proved construction that supplies arbitrarily many heavy births per round. On **14**, the entire outermost family gives a particularly sharp test. Round nine has exactly **19,825** junctions nine candidate steps from the tip. Of these, 19,819 share one old parent. An exterior junction meets them all. Together they produce a mixed round-ten junction with at least **19,820 parents**, and no child can come solely from the 19,819. The other six make exactly one child together in round ten. By round eleven no junction descends wholly from the round-nine outermost family. The maximum candidate-step counts at rounds eight through eleven are **8, 9, 10, 10**. A later group can still combine parents from nearer and farther away. [Full outer-layer certificate](outer.md)

The general result behind this test holds at every size. Take three or more junctions held in one block, strictly inside their live connected component. Mark the pairs that receive their own child in the full next round. A set of \(j\) original junctions produces a wholly descended birth \(j-1\) rounds later exactly when **every pair in that set is marked**. Each such set gives exactly one birth. Thus the size of the largest such set minus one is the exact last round for this part growing entirely from itself. The proof allows arbitrary outside junctions and groups. For positive reached ages there are finitely many distinct reduced growing ends of a given finite width. Visiting bounded width infinitely often therefore forces a repeat. Thus a nonending single-tip start is eventually periodic with bounded heavy counts, or its heavy counts tend to infinity. Starts **03 and 14 are proved to lie in the union of these last two classes**. Assigning either to a single one remains open.
