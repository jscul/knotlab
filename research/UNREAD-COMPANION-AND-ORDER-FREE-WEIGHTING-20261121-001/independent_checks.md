# Independent reconstruction

## One physical shared distinction

The coordinating check enumerates all 32 five-bit words at each placement, assembles the accessible-word by unread-word array, and obtains the reduced matrix by contracting the unread names. At either placement the numbers of allowed full words are 8 (two parity laws), 12 (either mixed ordering) and 18 (two not-all-equal laws). On two not-all-equal knots each of the first knot's six ordinary words still has mass \(1/6\) at both placements.

The two unread superpositions overlap by \(2/3\) with a not-all-equal companion. For each value of the shared distinction there is also an unread pair that forbids its opposite value. Fix any complete history of a protocol on the accessible systems. Taking the coefficient of an exclusive unread pair isolates one initial shared value. The accessible operator acts identically whatever the unread pair is. Lawfulness therefore requires that component to retain its original physical shared value. This works even under the weaker requirement that only the actual coherent intermediate state must be lawful. When the physically retained shared name is traced out, contributions from different values cannot interfere on the independent target. This reasoning uses the unread word in the proof. No reader receives it as an answer.

If the one-place reader holds the shared name, that reader's initial source is a fixed single bit in either component. Neither component supplies entanglement between the readers. An exact sign measurement would have to leave an entangled target on input 00. Even the surviving accessible coherence before the protocol is insufficient to circumvent this constraint. For two not-all-equal knots, the reduced word matrix retains an off-diagonal element \(1/9\) between 001 and 110. Its partial transpose on the last bit has a principal minor of determinant \(-1/81\). This witnesses entanglement in the unconstrained matrix at precisely the placement where the physical protocol has zero success.

If the two-place reader holds the shared name \(s\), reading it is an actual permitted local herald. Given either value, the other two source bits occupy three words. The reader at \(s\) saves its other bit and makes the two physical names unequal. The other reader can then save and clear its bit. The state on the private bits has an unnormalized two-by-two coefficient matrix \(C\) with three ones and determinant of absolute value one. The eigenvalues of \(CC^T/3\) are \((3\pm\sqrt5)/6\). A local success filter \(kC^{-1}\), with \(k^2=(3-\sqrt5)/2\), gives a coherent binary pair on that success. The script verifies that the remaining effect is positive and checks every touched physical word for both companion laws. Concentrating the smaller weight into an equal pair bounds the success probability by \(2(3-\sqrt5)/6\) in either component, even on a protocol that couples the targets earlier. The construction attains the bound. It uses the supplied coherent preparation, private work, calibrated filter, sign measurement and ordinary records. ## Why all reveal orders determine the form of the allowed words

Let the full allowed words of a nonempty finite binary relation start with equal weights. A feasible fair coin reproduces this weighting in **every** order exactly when every partial choice of already revealed bits leaves each remaining bit either forced or equally often 0 and 1. Necessity follows by revealing the chosen partial assignment first and comparing the probabilities of its two continuations. Sufficiency follows by multiplying these same conditional probabilities in the chosen order.

Choose a maximal subset of names \(I\) on which the uniform weighting gives every bit combination equally often. For a name \(j\) outside \(I\), condition on all of \(I\) except one name. The two completions in \(I\) are equiprobable. If \(j\) were forced on one completion and free on the other, its conditional probability on their union would be \(1/4\) or \(3/4\), violating the prefix condition. Changing one of the bits of \(I\) at a time therefore shows that \(j\) is either free on every \(I\)-word or forced on every \(I\)-word. The first possibility contradicts maximality. Thus \(j=f_j(I)\). When all but two names of \(I\) are fixed, the four remaining \(I\)-words are equiprobable. The number of ones of \(f_j\) on each such choice of four must be 0, 2 or 4. Its Boolean difference when one input bit changes consequently never depends on any other input bit. This makes \(f_j\) a constant plus the parity of a fixed subset of \(I\). The cases of zero or one free input obey the same conclusion. Conversely the projections of an affine binary relation have equal-sized nonempty fibers. Fixing any prefix leaves an affine relation, so every remaining bit is fixed or fair.

For a single canonical nonempty proper knot of arity \(n\), forgetting each one of its names must give every word on the other \(n-1\) names. If the knot is affine, this forces dimension \(n-1\) and the one parity equation uses all its names. Either total parity sign qualifies. For systems, a law usable as an isolated knot must already pass this test for a theorem about *every* assembly. A particular assembly can conceal a nonaffine rule.

On a fixed growth event let \(k_x\) count lawful extensions of the old word \(x\). For an onto restriction, present-uniform weighting gives \(x\) weight \(k_x/\sum_z k_z\). Splitting the old weight \(\pi(x)\) among its extensions keeps the marginal \(\pi(x)\). For uniform old words the two agree exactly when the counts are constant. In a chain of onto affine restrictions they are constant at each step. The empty support yields no sample. Arbitrary starting weights, histories with different maps, or averaging reveal orders are distinct assumptions.

The code computes the two conditional counts at every partial assignment for all nonempty supports through four names. On five names it tests every one of the 65,536 Boolean functions of four independent inputs, of which exactly 32 pass. On one not-all-equal knot, a fixed first pair gives two words mass \(1/4\) and four mass \(1/8\); averaging the six orders restores \(1/6\) for all six. The all-size binary classification is related to Proposition 3 of Simonis and Ashikhmin. Here the theorem specifies the fair reveal experiment and its additional conditions across actual ordinary growth. ## The measured old event

The coordinating replay contracts four possible states of each pair of shared names. On the smallest spent-witness arrangement it also sums over every old word directly. With two internal holders, the marked pair has equal bits in 936 of 2,540 old words and in 1,404 of 3,812 grown words. Giving each grown word equal weight changes the event by \(-117/605155\). Preserving each old word's weight keeps the original ratio. With four and six internal holders the differences are \(397/32477767\) and \(-53505/15678042403\), respectively. These finite checks use the declared two-input not-all-equal birth. Its identification in the ordinary engine and the all-length proof belong to the scientific leaf. The directly joined controls have separate unary births and nine completions for each old assignment.
