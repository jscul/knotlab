# What a merge preserves

Each reader contributes one term `(A,B)`. Here `A` and `B` are the two holders of its first and second settings. All four knots are distinct. Equal holder lists on two settings still represent two distinct settings. We keep the original knot labels and count repeated terms with their multiplicity.

## Commutation, sign and minimality

The contribution of `(A,B)` to commutation is the binary vector with a one at each of the four pairs of knots in `A × B`, treating each pair of knots as unordered. The checks commute exactly when the vectors sum to zero. At a knot `w`, the holders of the partners of its settings must therefore occur an even number of times each. A single setting there is impossible. With two settings, their partners have the same two holders, so a forced merge exists.

Suppose two terms are `(A,{w,r})` and `(A,{w,s})`. Their vectors sum to that of `(A,{r,s})` when `r ≠ s`, and to zero when `r = s`. In the former case, the two new holders are distinct and lie outside `A`. Giving the new setting a fresh identity keeps the reader settings disjoint. The other cases and the settings outside the selected readers require no additional identifications.

For any fixed order of the knots, let `t(v)` be one if `v` lies strictly between the two holders in `A`, and zero otherwise. The contribution of `(A,{u,v})` to the closing sign bit is `t(u) + t(v)` modulo two. The two occurrences of `t(w)` cancel. This proves preservation for each order separately and also proves that a doubled pair closes even. Swapping adjacent knots changes the closing sign by their commutation bit, so commuting choices have the same closing sign in every order. The checker independently multiplies Pauli checks in two orders at each reachable remainder. [Calculation](merges.py)

A fewest strict odd word has no proper nonempty commuting subcollection. Otherwise that collection and its complement both commute, and their signs add to odd. The odd part is a smaller birth word on the same allowed pairs. At an ordinary merge a subcollection of the new terms lifts to old terms by selecting both consumed terms if it uses the new one, and neither otherwise. The sum of its commutation vectors is unchanged. A nonempty proper subcollection lifts to a nonempty proper subcollection, so minimality persists through every ordinary merge. A doubled pair in an odd word is a proper nonempty commuting subcollection. Consequently **every** sequence starting at any minimal strict odd choice avoids doubled pairs.

Write `c` for the number of knots holding selected settings minus the number of terms. An ordinary merge drops one term. Both holders in `A` and the other holders `r,s` continue to hold settings. Only `w` can become empty, exactly when the merge is forced. Thus `c` stays fixed for a forced ordinary merge and rises by one for an ordinary merge through a knot holding other settings. Both base games have `c = 1`. Any decoding without doubled pairs uses exactly `1 − c` merges through knots holding other settings, with `c` taken at the start. The ordinary-merge qualification matters. Removing two equal terms on four otherwise unused knots removes all four knots at once. [Doubled-pair control](merge_fixtures.json)

## Why the search is complete

Two labelled realizations of the same multiset of typed holder terms differ by a bijection of active settings within corresponding terms and a permutation of the readers. That bijection carries each legal merge to a legal merge, preserving its through-knot, forced status, doubled status and resulting holder multiset. Either choice of a surviving copy at the repeated meeting gives the same result under this bijection. It also preserves both base tests. The lifetime of a fresh setting and distinctions outside the readers are immaterial to these tests. At the closure grown from census index 786, for example, the two fewest words both reduce through knot 4 and introduce a setting held by knots 1 and 2. Those knots share no distinction in the original closure. The new term belongs to the abstract reduced game.

Each move strictly decreases the number of terms. Remembering the exact multiset therefore preserves all reachable endpoints. At an unrestricted state the independent search tries each repeated holder pair between two terms and each shared knot of their other holdings. These are all possibilities from the definition. It records every resulting remainder through to exhaustion. The original search also has a sound key and can settle the existence of a successful order, provided its cap is absent or inactive. Its first successful return alone cannot establish uniqueness of the base kind or equality of the labelled terminal remainders. For the frozen words, even charging every possible outgoing move of each visited multiset, plus the initial call, costs at most 17 calls against the recorded 20,000-call cap. Forced merges alone have a unique terminal multiset at fixed knot labels on every finite strict commuting pattern. Consider two possible first forced merges. If they consume disjoint terms, each through-knot uses exactly the two settings of its own terms, so both orders remain legal and give the same multiset. If they consume the same two terms, either the moves coincide or the terms are equal and both moves delete them.

If exactly one term is shared, call the two distinct through-knots `w,v`. Neither can lie in the common partner holding `A` of the other merge, since both terms in that merge would then use the knot's two settings. Thus the three terms have other holdings `{s,w}`, `{w,v}` and `{v,t}`, with the same partner holding `A`. Either first merge leaves the other through-knot with exactly two settings. The second merge gives `(A,{s,t})` if `s ≠ t`, or deletes the last two terms if `s = t`. Every local choice can thus be joined. Induction on the strictly decreasing number of terms gives uniqueness of the forced remainder. A fresh setting or the retained one among two original copies can have a different name.

The eleven-pair fixture separates three uses of uniqueness. Its six maximal routes give three labelled remainders. All have nine pairs and no merge, and none is a single base. One partitions into the three original three-reader games. Each other one contains the untouched three-reader game and a six-pair even part that has neither a merge nor a partition into base games. This witnesses the dependence of remainders and partitions on general merge order. Under the study's definition of decoding to a single base, every order on this fixture fails. ## Three pairs and the nine-knot witness

A single term cannot commute on its own. Two commuting terms must have the same four met knot pairs. These determine their four holders and which two pairs of holders lie on the same setting, so the terms agree up to exchanging the settings. Their signs cancel. An odd choice of three terms therefore has no knot carrying exactly two active settings, since a forced merge there would leave at most two terms and preserve oddness. Each active knot must carry three. The twelve appearances of settings give exactly four such knots. A repeated term would leave one noncommuting term after cancellation. The three distinct terms on these four knots consequently use all three ways of pairing the four holders. This is the three-reader game.

As a check, enumerating the three-term multisets with at least two settings per active knot gives 10, 270 and 405 multisets on four, five and six fixed knot labels, respectively. Exactly one is commuting and odd, on four knots. For the nine-pair witness stated in the study every knot carries four settings. The word is commuting and odd, the test of every proper nonempty subcollection finds none that commutes, there is no move, and the active meetings admit no odd ring around any knot. ## What the encoding supplies

For the Pauli realization, duplicate the affected reader by the map `|j⟩ → |jj⟩`. On its image either copy's Z gives the original Z, their product gives the identity, and the product of the two X settings gives the original X. If the through-knot also reads a product `R` of other readers' settings, its enlarged check is `Z₁Z₂R`, which restricts to `R` on the image. Thus an inverse ordinary merge supplies one encoded realization also in the shared case.

By itself the shared check fixes a product. More generally, in a sector with copy parity `(−1)ᵇ`, the basis states are `|j, j⊕b⟩`. Both X settings together still give the logical X. The second Z and the through-knot check now acquire the sign `(−1)ᵇ`. The explicit five-reader fixture duplicates two distinct readers of the three-reader game through the same knot. There that knot checks the product of the two copy parities. With the full check signs fixed to minus at knot 0 and plus elsewhere, the common eigenspace has dimension two. Each eigenvalue of the first copy parity occurs in one dimension, even with the same full signs. Superpositions are possible across those two values. An ordinary repetition-code subspace is therefore a chosen realization. The holder calculation certifies the reduction of the active word.
