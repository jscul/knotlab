# Why a growing excess rules out an every-law bond

Let a finite not-all-equal stage have d distinctions and h laws. Count only distinctions occurring in a law. On three disjoint agreement carriers Cᵢ, let k equal the sum of |Cᵢ| − 1 over the three carriers. If deleting each law enlarges the joint image of the readings, then d ≤ h + k. For the studied carriers k ≤ 6. Different deletions may restore different words.

To see this, specify for each carrier which of its places equal its first place. This uses k signed two-place not-all-equal requirements in total. They can express either equality or disagreement. All the ways of specifying the relative values use the same sets of places. An agreement word occurs if at least one of its specifications is feasible.

Suppose d > h + k. Among the sets of distinctions choose a minimal nonempty set S that occurs in fewer requirements than its size, counting both stage laws and the added requirements. The whole set qualifies. Write N(S) for the requirements touching S. By minimality, N(S) has size |S| − 1. Choose any r in S. Hall's matching theorem assigns each member of S except r a distinct requirement touching it. Every requirement in N(S) is assigned.

From each assigned distinction, allow a move to any *other* member of S in its assigned requirement. Every member can reach r. Otherwise let T be the nonempty set that cannot reach it. The requirements assigned to T avoid S ∖ T, leaving at most |S ∖ T| − 1 requirements for that proper nonempty subset. This contradicts minimality.

Now fix any signs in the added requirements. Give r either value. In increasing order of the fewest moves to r, give each remaining distinction the value making its signed literal differ from a literal one move closer in its assigned requirement. Thus every requirement touching S holds for every completion outside S. The same set S works for every specification because its choice used only which distinctions each requirement holds. Some stage law touches S, since every distinction occurs in a stage law. Deleting that law cannot add a specification. Retain the values outside S from any solution after deletion and replace those on S by this assignment. It follows that deletion cannot add a reading word either.

The frozen round-four count also has a shorter justification after the fact. Every recorded bond then misses one word. If all stage laws were needed for that one word, the collapsed signed system would contain a minimal inconsistent subsystem containing all those laws. Its number of occurring distinctions is at most its number of requirements by the same matching argument. The reduced word uses at most six mergers or added requirements in total. With 19 distinctions and ten knots this is impossible. For the separate, sharper census inequality tested *for each missing word*, joint essentiality alone does not say that each deletion restores that particular word. Its recorded verdict is an empirical count. The unsigned minimal-system count appears in [Seymour's paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/qmath/25.1.303).
