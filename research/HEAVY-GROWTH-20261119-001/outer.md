Count one step when a candidate pair shares a holding block. Write \(B_t(d)\) for the set within \(d\) such steps of the recurring tip and \(R_t\) for the farthest occupied layer. Every pair on a shortest candidate path belongs to some child. Consecutive children on a path share an old parent. This gives the distance formula in the [boundary proof](interface.md). Equivalently, the children with at least one parent in \(B_t(d)\) are **exactly** \(B_{t+1}(d+1)\). This includes the new tip. Iterating,

\[
B_{t+k}(d+k)=\{Y:\text{some age-}t\text{ ancestor of }Y\text{ lies in }B_t(d)\}.
\]

In particular, \(R_t\le R_{t+1}\le R_t+1\). Each farthest junction has a child and all that child's parents lie within one step of each other. A new farthest layer appears precisely when a child draws all its parents from the old farthest layer. A permanently surviving family with all its ancestry outside the tip at some fixed old age exists exactly when there are only finitely many pauses \(R_{t+1}=R_t\). Indeed, avoiding the tip at fixed old age \(s\) amounts to \(R_{s+k}>k\) for every \(k\). The integer \(t-R_t\) increases by one at each pause and stays fixed otherwise. For the reverse implication choose \(s\) larger than its eventual bound and at or past the chosen initial single-tip age. This characterizes that particular proof of heavy divergence. It leaves room for widths to increase through repeated mixing with the offshoot.
