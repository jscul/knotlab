# Independent referee report on the whole-round response and signed weightings
**Refuted** in part. **Block** on part B's claim that the record contains no sign capable of weighting configurations.

Part A's fixed-address maxima, 7/3 and 5/2, survive independent reconstruction. Allowing the sender to act on its shortage makes no-signalling infeasible. Hiding the shortage gives a maximum of 2, with scores as low as 1 under not-all-equal. Part B misses a preserved sign on two classes of 16,384 legal states and a counting identity that actually sums signed coefficients over legal states. Neither record shows ordinary growth selecting such a weighting for the four free addresses.

## A. The earlier construction and the rebuilt programs

I used the original names of the three terminals. Each preparation has 56 old knots and 95 distinctions. In the archived supports, a pair of ternary knots with two shared distinctions gives completion counts `[[2,0],[0,2]]` under parity and `[[3,2],[2,3]]` under not-all-equal. I followed the actual terminal names through eight such pairs on each of the three correspondences and counted the legal assignments of the four device knots at each end. For each law all 256 entries (four preparations, eight words at each reader) agreed as integers with the original receipt. The result is 32 positive records under parity and 256 under not-all-equal. Three preparations carry indices identically. When both preparation bits are 1 the destination indices are (1,2,0). Per preparation the uniform old weighting counts 549,755,813,888 parity assignments or 187,907,248,735,431,700,781,250 not-all-equal assignments. At each end, both addresses and the detector each participate in two proposals. The short address has one loose distinction and the other address has two. The edit supplies one at the chosen address. Across the 32 combinations of law, preparation and address pair, all eight matched choices funded six births and the other 24 stalled. Both marked distinctions gained a holder exactly on funding. Writing the preparation as $(u,v)$, the addresses as $(x,y)$ and the terminal triples as $(a,b)$, the two output bits are $(F a_0,F b_0)$ with $F=[x=u][y=v]$. With four equally weighted preparations and uniform old assignments within each, I obtained parity correlations $(1,1,1,3/4)$ and CHSH $9/4$. The not-all-equal control gave $21480051994324169922/14320015907287890625$.

**Reduced and complete records.** At fixed own address the program retains the own preparation bit, three terminal values and the funding flag. The earlier complete record includes all holdings and values at both addresses, the center, detector, and six guard knots, plus the edit and local births. Each reader sees 22 old distinction values on ten knots, 23 values after a stall, and 26 on thirteen knots after funding. A parity birth with old inputs 00 has fresh value 0 with certainty. With not-all-equal, old inputs 00 force 1 and old inputs 01 allow either value. The earlier uniform-weight proof of equality for the complete records uses local conditional distributions. The entire old record at each reader is then a function of its own preparation and terminal triple. Under parity the guard terminals follow the local triple. Under not-all-equal one can copy each local triple through its nearest guard because a pair with different terminal values still has a completion. The original local edit and birth extension rules consequently give the same conditional local distribution whenever those inputs and the old local values agree. **Why the policy test works.** Fix a receiver address and record event, and partition the pre-edit preparation by the sender's visible input $s$. Write $D_s$ for the mass of that event together with input $s$ at sender address 1 minus the corresponding mass at address 0. Relative to the constant-zero policy, the change for policy $f$ is $\sum_{s:f(s)=1}D_s$. Testing the policy that switches at just one input gives necessity of each equality $D_s=0$. Summing gives sufficiency, also for an independently randomized choice with coefficients in [0,1]. This identity uses unnormalized joint masses and works for signed weights as well. A free message selects the policy before the edit. In addition to the argument for all weightings, I compared all 65,536 visible-label policies or all 256 terminal-only policies on three exact example weightings in both directions at both receiver addresses. All 24 comparisons agreed with the joint-mass criterion (789,504 policy checks). [Policy check](check_policies.py) · [Counts](results_policies.json)

**Exact bounds.** Under fixed addresses, observing funding at each receiver forces every preparation to have mass 1/4. For parity the three identity correspondences force a common terminal distribution and the fourth makes it invariant under cyclic permutation. In either three-element orbit of nonconstant triples, the designated bits disagree in two of three cases. Thus only the fourth setting can have disagreement, with conditional probability at most 2/3, and its correlation is at least 2/3. Three one-hot triples equally weighted at every preparation attain 7/3. Under not-all-equal a stall always yields 00, so every fixed-address correlation is in [1/2,1]. The maximum odd-minus sum is at most 5/2. A fair bit copied to all of Alice's terminals and to all of Bob's, with Bob's bit flipped only at preparation 11, attains the bound. This argument uses the stall fraction *at every address pair*.

With visible shortage, a sender choosing the short address or its opposite gives receiver funding probabilities $P(u=x)$ and zero at fixed receiver address $x$. Equality at both addresses would make the total preparation mass zero. With the shortage hidden, let $p_{uv}(a,b)$ be the unnormalized input mass, including zeros for unsupported pairs. The funded record on Alice's side with Bob's visible terminals fixes $p_{u0}(a,b)=p_{u1}(a,b)$. Reversing sides fixes $p_{0v}(a,b)=p_{1v}(a,b)$. Equality on stalls then follows by subtraction. Thus $p_{uv}=q/4$ for any terminal distribution $q$ on the intersection of the four supports. Parity allows both $(000,000)$ and $(111,111)$ and every mixture. Under not-all-equal, all 64 pairs qualify. All four correlations equal $1-\Pr_q[a_0\ne b_0]/2$, giving the maximum CHSH over the eight sign choices $2-\Pr_q[a_0\ne b_0]$. For example, $(000,111)$ in all four preparations gives 1. Uniform weight on all 256 records gives 3/2. Uniformly sampling $(a,a)$ gives 2 while every terminal varies.

At any one fixed address pair the two reduced records *together* disclose $(u,v,a,b)$. Their joint probabilities therefore enforce nonnegativity of every individual reduced weight, including in the hidden-policy test, where only the sender's policy input is restricted. Finer assignments within a record may carry cancelling signs, but the exposed net weight must be nonnegative. This explains why the signed programs give the same bounds.

### Corrections to part B

5. **Narrowing — signed sums and what a value read erases.** [Row 3](../README.md) says “with phase-sensitive reads it gives every three-reader probability” and “as an amplitude (squared at a value read, every phase vanishes and the unsigned table returns) or as a weight summed directly (a reading no record adopts)”. The benchmark is one 64-entry table. The proposed second variant also extends the even-word phase prescription to odd not-all-equal words. Squaring each distinguished final word erases its phase, but summing indistinguishable witnesses first gives $(1-1)^2=0$ where the unsigned sum gives $(1+1)^2=4$. Thus resolving the final value does not by itself remove the effect of unread witnesses. The earlier working reading explicitly uses a signed sum to decide whether a state is allowed. Directly normalizing signed marginal sums *as outcome probabilities in the proposed census* is the additional prescription. ## Reproduction and limits

I read the handoff, decision, note, advisor's question and answer, register entry A290, the earlier construction, proof and cited records, and the repository's foundation, discipline and guardrails. The new programs use the repository interpreter. Each full check followed a timed prefix with the same inputs, limits and settings as that part of the full run. At most one numerical process ran at a time, with one solver thread and an address-space cap of 2,000,000,000 bytes for the linear programs or 1 GiB for the small checks.

The ten-case program pilot took 0.11 s and estimated 0.62 s for all cases. The 96-case rebuild took 0.38 s and peaked at 105,976 KiB. All 66 returned feasible solutions passed exact rational checks. The other 30 were numerically infeasible, with the mathematical bounds and contradictions in part A supplying exact reasons. The rebuild covered eight CHSH sign choices at fixed and terminal-only addresses for both ordinary and signed models, visible-shortage feasibility, and the two laws at all seven original signed thresholds (2 through 4, including 2.001 and √8). The four other full checks together took about one second. From the repository root, run each linked script with `.venv/bin/python -B`, first adding `--pilot`, then omitting it. For the program rebuild set `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 MKL_NUM_THREADS=1` before launch and give `--out <receipt-path>` on each invocation. The recorded linear-program cap corresponds to `ulimit -v 1953125` in Bash.
