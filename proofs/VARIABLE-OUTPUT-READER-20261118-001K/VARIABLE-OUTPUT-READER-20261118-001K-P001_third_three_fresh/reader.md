# Reading the actual triple choices from a current table

Let \(m\) be the number of distinctions and \(n=(m-4)/2\) the number of ordinary sites in a source from the specified family. Write \(K=D+1\). All table procedures use ordinary distinction names only as indices. A permuted table carries the marked answer by the same permutation.

## Which of the three cases applies

Every nonzero equation of size at most six is a bond. Otherwise its support splits into at least two disjoint bonds of size at least three, which would require two disjoint ternary stars. The ternary seed is unique, and any reachable competitor matching the low readings has that same single ternary star. The closure permits at most two shrinking joins. Thus every ordinary shore with at least three sites has boundary at least five by the chronological estimate. The only three- or four-bit bonds in the source are physical stars. In the graph that links pairs with \(K\le3\), read the three-place star. At \(K\le4\), the four-cliques are exactly the four-place stars. Indeed a set of four pairwise incident bits in a simple triangle-free ordinary graph has a common holder, with each loose tip given a private leaf for this incidence argument. The number of shrinking joins is therefore \(n-2-n_4\), where \(n_4\) is the count of four-place stars. The reader never receives the count of shrinks or the positions of their sites. [Low sites and rival rank](low.md) · [Unbounded move closure](closure.md)

With at most one shrink, all physical sites other than the one six-place site agree in every rival. There is a direct way to list them. When there is a shrink, link the pairs with \(K\le5\). For each four-place site adjacent to the seed, discard the pairs going between their two sets of unshared bits. A bond on more than two ordinary sites costs at least six. The discarded pairs came from sums of the seed and an adjacent four-place site; their two unshared bits could not lie together on an ordinary site by simplicity and bipartiteness. The maximal five-cliques of the remaining graph are precisely the five-place sites. Here there is just one. [Short-equation analysis](low.md#all-nine-high-input-output-pairs-require-the-extra-rank)

In the two-shrink case the answer is always the empty triple menu, against all reachable rivals, including those whose third three-output site uses just one or two parents. This conclusion uses the separate minimum-cut proof. [Two-shrink theorem](low.md#every-two-shrink-source-has-its-empty-menu-forced)

## A finite list of all possible readings at any supplied width

Remove just the six-place site, keeping every bit and every known low site. A bit with no low holder must join the high site to the exterior. Let \(q\) count these bits. The remaining bits with exactly one low holder have one available end each and must end at the high site or the exterior. There are \(12-2q\) such bits at one shrink and \(13-2q\) at zero. Select exactly \(6-q\) for the high site and put the rest at the exterior. Two low holders on the high site must be distinct and have the same ordinary color. These colors are known up to a simultaneous exchange from the connected graph of low sites alone. To see connectivity, the ordinary graph from its fourth site onward begins with a four-cycle, and every subsequent site joins at least two previously present sites. Deleting the high site therefore leaves it connected. Every pair on the proposed high site must have \(K\le6\). At one shrink the proposed exterior also has six bits and must satisfy that same test, since all ordinary sites on its opposite shore are connected. This gives at most 924 candidate supports at one shrink. [Why the low sites agree even when the high equations differ](high.md)

For each candidate check if a serial history exists. The initial site is the ternary seed. The first unary site must neighbor the seed and the second must neighbor the seed or the first. There are at most fifteen ordered choices of these first two four-place sites. For a chosen pair the incoming count at every ordinary site is known. It is zero at the seed, one at those two, three at the high site, and the arity minus two at every other site. In an acyclic orientation with these counts each source of remaining indegree zero sends every remaining ordinary incidence outwards. Iteration determines the orientation or detects a contradiction.

A single topological order then suffices. For any two parents, their earliest common witness was already present when the younger parent was born. A later *first* witness would itself have required an earlier witness to consume those two parents. Thus a lawful orientation supplies a common witness preceding the new site in every topological order. If a second site shared all three parents of a triple, it would either already forbid the addition or would itself later have had to join the same three with a witness present. Thus a lawful orientation has no such second site. Concrete replay checks the original loose-bit rules on each surviving ordered support list. Remeasure every pair through \(K=6\) and grade every three-parent mark directly from actual incidence. If the source has one shrink, all reachable competitors on the supplied names occur within these at most \(924\cdot15=13{,}860\) support and startup tests, independent of width. The time to check each candidate still depends on width. The procedure produces every distinct attainable menu, including explicit legal histories for any disagreement, should one exist. [Executable reader and controls](one_shrink.py) · [Exact answers and renamings](one_shrink.json)

At one shrink there is also a shorter decision procedure. Apply the distinct-holder, color and pair tests on the six-place junction and the six loose distinctions. If exactly one assignment survives, read the actual junctions and test the marked triple's holders. If several assignments survive, return the empty menu. Every source with a legal triple has exactly one surviving assignment by the all-depth finite proof. The true assignment always survives. This procedure even works on a table that conceals the actual parity equation. [Uniform uniqueness at positive menus](terminal_cuts.md)

## Two ways to share the same low holder

Suppose a high input or output is attached to a low holder that also has a still-loose bit. Swapping the two bits in the high star preserves all low-site supports. A differing true triple must use a swapped bit, since the physical ordinary adjacency itself stays the same.

For an input-side swap, mark the spare at the birth of the high site and follow every legal future in which it stays loose. Track its holder in the exact loose-holder record. If a later join uses a different spare at that same holder, keep the mark. At zero total shrinks there are 22 records in which the marked bit can join. At one total shrink there are none, whether the shrink precedes or follows the high site. The calculation closes the finite move graph from all eligible input-side starts. For an output-side swap, mark a newly born low child of the high site and a spare there. At one shrink the spare can join in 64 records for each order of the high site and shrink. [Tagged fixed point and literal markers](marked_spare.py) · [Counts](marked_spare.json)

There is a direct test for every output-side swap. Let \(b\) be the child, \(f\) its input from the high site, \(q\) a still-loose bit at \(b\), and \(i\) any input of the high site. Then \(K(i,f)=6\) and \(K(i,q)>6\). An ordinary shore containing the high site and crossing one of its inputs cannot cost six or less on three or more sites. If the high site occurs among the first three selected sites, its arity raises their bound from seven to at least nine, since the other two have combined arity at least seven. Otherwise its positive contribution is at least two on top of the first-three lower bound. At most two shrinking joins can lower these estimates. A two-site shore containing the high site costs at least seven. Thus the sole short equation containing the high site in its shore and crossing \(i\) is its star. A low-space shore through \(i,q\) omits the high site but contains \(b\), since \(q\) is loose, and would also cross \(f\). The low-space cross-pair lemma excludes such a shore. Hence distance five distinguishes a lone output-side swap at every finite size. [Rank and cross pairs](low.md)

More generally, with one shrink any two competitors giving the high site the *same ordinary neighbors* have identical marked triple menus. Some true input holder has no current spare. If the high site was born after the shrink, three spares at birth would let the same three holders use complementary legal triples of the six old bits, excluded by the predecessor's closure. If the high site was born first, reserving one spare at each of its three parents leaves four usable bits immediately after birth. Three of these are at the high site and only one is elsewhere. Keeping those three reserves permits at most one binary addition. After it the only usable bits are on the high site and its child, which are adjacent and cannot supply a legal pair. Thus a later shrink requires at least one input holder to exhaust its spare. At the time of comparison a rival with the same set of high neighbors must keep the high bit at that holder.

To finish, call a pair critical when its observed minimum through six is smaller than the minimum in the common low space. Every input-output pair of either high site is critical, and every critical partner of a true high input is on its six-place star, by the preceding shore bound. If an input of one high site belongs to the outputs of the other, all three rival inputs are therefore on the true high site. Either there is also a shared input and both halves of the rival site are on the true site, or all three rival inputs equal the true outputs and force all true inputs onto the rival site as well. Both cases give the same support. For unequal supports a common input therefore has the input role on both sites. Its critical partners force their three output bits to coincide. Under equal ordinary neighbor sets the remaining changes only exchange a consumed input with a spare at that input holder. At one shrink every such spare is ineligible in each presentation by the tagged fixed point above. All other marks retain their holder incidence and verdict. ## Recurrent histories and controls

The all-depth graph of loose holders, with the high holder distinguished while available, has 104 frontier records with a live one-shrink menu. Exactly 400 records from the full construction can reach any such record. Their directed graph has 390 strongly connected components. Only six contain a directed cycle, and all six lie after the high site and the shrink. On one literal history per positive record the low-holder and short-pair candidate test kept exactly one support every time. These 104 examples alone do not identify the short-pair readings for every history reaching a given record. [One-history controls](one_shrink.json)

A separate check walked every internal transition of each of the six recurrent components in a returning walk, repeated that walk 1, 3, 12 and 24 times, and reached a genuine positive menu afterward. On all 24 resulting histories the candidate support was unique. The largest example had 616 distinctions. Each pair of high or loose bits was graded by separating its endpoints in both possible orientations using disjoint unit-capacity paths. A cut of size at most six is always a bond in these sources as above, so this independently measures precisely the required small pair minima. These are stress tests of recurrent histories.
