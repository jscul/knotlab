# Does adding the spent pasts restore a live knot’s three-reader score?

**It changes the loose readers’ chances, but it cannot restore a score above two on any single newborn knot.** Every newborn has at least one paid distinction. If it also has three loose distinctions to read, its not-all-equal score is at most **25/13** when spent alternatives add coherently and **12/7** when the old words remain physically distinct. Ordinary parity gives zero. An isolated original not-all-equal ternary scores **3** before growth, when all three places are loose. For a loose word with \(n\) spent completions, ordinary uniform whole words give chance \(n/N\), while adding the alternatives first gives \(n^2/W\), where \(N=\sum n\) and \(W=\sum n^2\). On a connected parity structure all eligible loose words have equally many pasts. Their ordinary chances therefore coincide. Their change answers can differ: after an original ternary’s first parity growth, changing one fresh mark at each of two children has mean **0** with old words retained and **1** after clean erasure. A diagonal population drawn by matching the complete loose words of two independent copies also gives \(n^2/W\) on acceptance, while a change question requires its own preparation. **The distant test.** In one arm two distant parents jointly pay one binary birth. In the other, an extra remote ternary, which gives each old full word the same number of completions, changes their offers into unary births. The complete old ordinary reduced state on the two central names agrees. At five holder meetings the chance that the first fresh marks of the two local children agree is **1486832/2981835** versus **3962383/7946541**. Giving each old word the same total weight across its remote continuations removes the difference. An all-size calculation gives a nonzero ordinary difference with alternating sign as the number of intervening holders increases. Under squared ways, the reached chances differ too, but the old *clean* central densities already differ. On one fixed preparation all unannounced complete remote instruments preserve the local chances. Thus the observed differences are weights of two constructions or of globally selected outcomes; they demonstrate no signal. **Which readers can stay unchanged?** Under ordinary counting a record stays stable exactly when the mean number of extensions conditional on each of its values agrees. Only complete old-word access forces every word’s extension count to be constant. For coherent addition a record remains stable when every occupied record block’s squared norm changes by the same factor. For every preparation and every untouched record this holds exactly when the transition rows are mutually orthogonal and have a common positive squared norm. On the actual original NAE ternary all six words have **27** extensions. A copy of “the first two bits agree” keeps ordinary chance **1/3**, but its clean chance becomes **7/33**. Copy both bits and then group the equal outcomes, and the clean chance is **3/13**. What the physical record distinguishes matters.
