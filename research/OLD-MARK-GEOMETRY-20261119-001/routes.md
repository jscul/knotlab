## Why the length stays fixed and the route sets eventually stop changing

An ordinary incidence hop follows a distinction to one of its actual holders or the reverse. Two incidence hops make a mark step. Original shared path distinctions keep their two original holders. A new knot can lie between two old parents only if the parent pair has a common earlier holder. The two ordinary knot steps through the new knot can then be replaced by the same number through that holder. A return through just one parent could already be deleted. Repeating the substitution at the most recent knots on a proposed route reduces it to an original route of no greater length. The original route is still present, so the least length stays fixed. New tied routes can coexist with the old one. This is a statement about ordinary incidence, with no bond reading. A route touching a knot born at age $t$ needs at least $2t+1$ incidence hops from each original shared mark. The first hop goes to an original holder, and each later shared distinction accounts for at most one layer change. Thus a shortest route of $2(j-i)$ hops between `ei` and `ej` can use only knots born through age $\lfloor(j-i-1)/2\rfloor$. A reached age seven fixes the entire route set of every old path pair at all future completed ages. The same bound fixes its midpoint set. Knots can continue to enter the surrounding balls at later ages.
