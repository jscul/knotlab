# Smallest identical-present collisions

The two interpretations have identical physical holdings and identical whole allowed-state tables under each law. The continued interpretation retains the correctly funded births of an older complete layer. The restarted interpretation treats every currently written knot as original and calculates a fresh complete layer. Both are lawful. The **remaining cohort** consists of the unfinished earliest ordinary layer. The **full ready roster** also allows a reference event of greater depth as soon as its assigned parents and input slots exist. ## Literal witnesses

- **Two knots, five distinctions.** Start with `{0,1,2}` and make its unary child `{0,3,4}`. In the ordinary reference the original knot still needs two unary births. Its existing child gives its future distinctions to pair births with the two unwritten siblings, so it has no ready unary. As a restart the two holders each make two unary births with two fresh distinctions. The complete present permits 8 states under parity and 18 under not-all-equal.
- **Two knots, seven distinctions.** Start with `{0,1,2,3}` and make `{0,4,5,6}` as the first unary child. Mark the surviving loose distinction `1` at the original knot, which is original in both interpretations. The continued unary on `1` takes three fresh distinctions. A restart gives the same unary two fresh distinctions. The whole present permits 32 parity states and 98 not-all-equal states. The same known-original pointer and the same marked input are available under both interpretations.
- **Three knots, seven distinctions.** From `{0,1,2}` make the two siblings `{0,3,4}` and `{1,5,6}`. Their reference pair on loose distinctions `3,5` is ready and takes one fresh distinction. The third original unary is still pending in the older cohort. As a restart the two leaves make the same pair, now with one unary sibling through each leaf, so the pair takes two fresh distinctions. The present permits 16 parity states and 54 not-all-equal states. Both marked parents are children in the continued history.
- **Four knots, nine distinctions.** Take the three supports of the preceding example as the **original** path. Its two leaves have one pair birth and one unary each. Its center has one unary. Precompute the complete layer and write just the center's unary, `{2,9,10}`. The original leaf pair on `3,5` is still in that unfinished layer with two fresh distinctions. As a restart there are now three live leaves and their common holder is spent. Their three pair births give a single next ternary group, so the same original leaf pair takes one fresh distinction. The present permits 32 parity states and 162 not-all-equal states.

For each changed count, the checker writes both proposed continuations on exactly the displayed old input distinctions, verifies their whole allowed-state projections, and checks that every previous state is retained. The added knot has different immutable arity in the two results. Each fixed input can acquire only one second holder. A later history preserving the input and its old holder therefore cannot erase this difference by writing more knots. Joint renamings permute all the current holders and distinctions. The checker separately replans the recoded complete predecessors used for the continuation budgets and the recoded present used as a restart. It checks the whole current allowed-state table under the same renaming.

## Lower bounds and complete search

A one-knot present has no distinct positive-growth interpretation, because every birth adds a written knot. With two current knots a positive-growth interpretation must come from a singleton of arity `a >= 3`. Its first complete layer has `a` unary children. They are jointly held by the singleton in the next plan, so each needs `a-1` fresh distinctions. After one birth the two supports both have arity `a` and share one distinction, for `2a-1` distinct positions. In the continued layer a surviving original loose distinction gets `a-1` fresh distinctions. In the restart each parent has `a-1` loose distinctions, so its first unary gets `max(2,a-2)` fresh distinctions. The case `a=3` gives the roster obstruction at five distinctions with equal common counts. `a=4` is the first different count and gives seven distinctions. The argument covers unrestricted arity at the smaller knot counts.

A restarted two-knot presentation has no pair with a common holder. At three knots and fewer than seven distinctions, the only connected single-meeting possibility with at least three places at each holder would have every pair sharing one distinction and every support ternary. Such a present cannot have a positive history. From a connected two-knot origin there is no first multi-parent birth. From a singleton there are at least three first unaries, and each uses all its outputs for pair children with those siblings, so two additions can only be two original unaries. Both possible positive histories give a path with seven or more distinctions.

These same arguments rule out a common multi-parent event in an unfinished cohort at **every** width through three current knots. One- and two-knot origins have only unary births in their still unfinished first cohort. They also rule out a common multi with all parents original in the full ready roster at these knot counts. The only possible multi at three knots comes from the two children of a singleton. For the four-knot sharp comparisons the finite search excludes every smaller width. For the all-ternary statements no smaller knot count has an excluded larger width, since a connected presentation of `J` ternary knots has at most `2J+1` distinct positions.

The search enumerates connected simple holder incidences on one through four knots and all loose allocations up to nine distinct positions. A type is the ordinary meeting relation and the loose count at each holder, minimized over all permutations of the holders. Each shared distinction is uniquely determined by its two holders, and permutations of loose slots at the same holder give equivalent types. Every candidate's allowed table is enumerated directly under each law, and each written support must have exactly its canonical projected relation before it can be an origin or restart. The archived ordinary operator supplies completely funded reference layers and their literal allocations. A finite parent-closed set of births determines a partial present. The checker verifies actual old-state conservation at every traversed addition and uses sufficient future-layer lookahead to count next events even when they would extend outside the search bounds.
