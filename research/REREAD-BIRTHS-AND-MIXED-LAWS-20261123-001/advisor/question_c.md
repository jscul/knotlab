Cold design review before one small follow-up study is frozen. Give (1) the strongest objection to each arm, (2) one concrete change to each, (3) anything the predictions get wrong by plain reasoning, (4) a cheaper test that would decide the same question, if one exists. At most 350 words in all. Do not praise.

Setting. Structures: two-valued distinctions; knots hold 3 or more of them (even parity, odd parity, or not-all-equal). Ordinary growth, per round: a knot is live if it holds a loose distinction (held by it alone); live knots form groups by a fixed local rule; each live knot owes one loose distinction to each group containing it; if any knot owes more than it has, the whole round stalls; each group gives one birth that takes one loose distinction from each parent and gets at least one fresh distinction; every remaining loose distinction gives a one-parent birth. No distinction ever gets a third holder. Births never depend on values.

Bell-type reading (all supplied): Alice has x1,x2,x3, Bob y1,y2,y3; setting a adds a not-all-equal knot on x1, x(2+a) and one fresh distinction, likewise b; every allowed word of the finished structure counts equally (so a not-all-equal setting birth doubles the count of old words in which its two inputs differ); "hidden excess" = CHSH above 2 with each side's complete record independent of the far setting, exact arithmetic. A census found 292 small structures (7 to 18 knots, both laws mixed) with a hidden excess; in all of them the observers' distinctions are already shared when the setting birth takes them.

Two studies just finished.
Study B (ordinary growth from parts): for each target, every connected proper subset of its knots holding all six observer distinctions was a start (49,961 starts), grown 3 rounds with every law assignment to newborns. Counting argument: a birth with a fresh distinction forbids no old word; only a not-all-equal birth with two or more parents re-weights old words (2^f : 2^f - 1 for inputs not all equal). Results: allowed observer words never change (0 of 17.6 million tables). 34 histories gain a hidden excess their start lacked (smallest: 7-knot start at exactly 2, one not-all-equal birth on Bob's loose y1, y2 gives 55/27); every one of those 34 starts has a distinction with three holders. Of the 12,147 starts with at most two holders, 74 already contain a target and none of the rest gains one; 6,312 of them stall at round 1. All 27,887 stalls are at round 1.
Study A (rule R): as ordinary growth, except a knot owing more than its loose distinctions pays the rest with distinctions it shares (which gain a holder); conventions O (oldest shared) and N (newest), or every allowed choice. Results so far: every old word still extends (18.5 billion words); the recorded sources never have a short knot, so R changes nothing there; of 744 sparse random starts that stall at once, about 85% grow under R; payments happen only at round 1; holders reach 4.
Seen before this freeze: ten of the two-holder starts (7 knots, hidden, exactly 2) lack one knot nae(y3,k1,k2,k3) where k1,k2,k3 each already have two holders; giving that knot one fresh distinction (so it forbids nothing) lifts them to 271/135 or 541/270, hidden.

Proposed study C, two arms.
C1 (census of study B under rule R). Population: the 27,887 founder starts that stall at round 1 under ordinary growth (6,312 with at most two holders per distinction). Growth under R with study A's engine: conventions O and N on all; on the 6,312 two-holder starts also every allowed round-1 choice when there are at most 64, else 62 more drawn by seed. Up to 3 rounds or until past 20 distinctions. Each newborn even, odd or not-all-equal, at most 1,024 assignments per history (drawn beyond). Test at every stage: hidden excess on the target's six observer distinctions.
C2 (one free birth). Population: the 12,073 two-holder starts with no hidden excess. Add one not-all-equal knot that takes a set T of 2, 3 or 4 existing distinctions and one fresh distinction; every T. Classes: how many of T's distinctions are already shared (0, 1, 2 or more). Test: hidden excess on the target's observers.
Predictions. G: code checks (R engine reproduces study B on starts with no short knot; tables agree with word-by-word enumeration on drawn cases; allowed observer words never change; the ten known cases reproduce). P1: no two-holder start without a hidden excess gains one under R. P2: some three-holder start that stalls under ordinary growth gains one under R. P3: in C2 no birth taking only loose distinctions gives a hidden excess. P4: in C2 some start other than the ten known gains a hidden excess from a birth that takes a shared distinction. Reported beside them: the least T that works, the largest score, and for each C2 gain whether rule R makes that birth at round 1 of that start.
