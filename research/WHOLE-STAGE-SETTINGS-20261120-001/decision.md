## Which ordinary signal can still be sent?

On a fixed known presentation a deterministic, locally visible change caused by the far setting has TV one at each pivotal own choice. The two flag values identify the far choice. On a mixture, opposite changes can cancel in the observer's record. They cannot cancel for a monotone spare addition with a fixed proposal roster and a persistent local gain that reads funding. The addressed edit is different: whether address 0 or 1 supplies a missing opening changes with the prepared start. That change of sign and the preparation's terminal correlations both matter.

In the four-source experiment, revealing both labels raises the full-record TV to **1/2** on either side at either setting. Observing the bit triples bracketing one otherwise concealed remote interchange already gives TV **1/4** under parity. Biasing Bob's label to probability 3/4 for one value gives Alice's single-bit TV **1/8** and full-record TV **1/4**. The weights, terminal correspondences and extent of local access are scaffolds. Ordinary growth supplies the actual six births or their common cancellation.

There is a second way to read the signal even with the original holdings. In a *supplemental* test defined after the freeze, let Bob use an independent message bit to add one spare at his visibly deficient holder or at his other holder. Alice keeps a fixed address. The probability of her birth changes from 0 to 1/2, and her selected outcome from 0 to 1/4. If Alice also supplies her known short holder, her birth flag reads Bob's message perfectly. This uses the same ordinary growth, source and allowed one-spare edits as the primary test. It changes what the external setting instructs the sender to do. The three primary definitions and their tables were retained as frozen.

More generally let the locally readable ordinary funding flag be H=f_x(U)g_y(V), with each side able to distinguish its own two readiness values. The maximum difference in the receiver's probability of seeing a birth between two sender policies equals Pr(f_x=1 and g_0 differs from g_1). It is attained by comparing the sender's most-ready and least-ready edits. If both directions are non-signalling under every such policy, then every setting pair has the same H almost surely. With a common old state and conditionally local record generation given H, a common distribution on all four local answers follows. This gives a classical bound for the stated class. The four fixed-address tables evade that stronger hypothesis. **Quantum test.** For two binary quantum measurements per observer, E00=E01=E10=1 forces E11=1. Each unit correlation forces equality of the corresponding unit vectors from the two measurements acting on a purification of the state. The three equalities link all four vectors. Here E11=3/4. Thus this all-trial excess answers the stated four-setting existence question, and fails the stronger test of quantum entanglement. It also fails non-signalling under the full choice of ordinary local edit policies.
