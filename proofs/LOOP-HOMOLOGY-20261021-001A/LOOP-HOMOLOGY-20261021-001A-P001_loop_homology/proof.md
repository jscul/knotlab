# Loop memory and cycle-like composition

Status: Proved

Ticket `LOOP-HOMOLOGY-20261021-001A`, 2026-10-21. It uses:
- `KNOT-LOOPS-20261019-001B-P001` ("the ring theorem"), for two-valued
  witnesses;
- `HOLES-COHOMOLOGY-20261019-001B-P001` ("the nerve proof"):
  - a network allows a pattern exactly when its holonomies share a fixed
    point;
  - free contact keeps one H¹ class per loop;
  - selectors bind loops as the nerve of the fixed-point sets;
- `THOMSEN-KNOTS-20261021-001A-P001` ("the 3-net proof"):
  - Theorem 2, every circuit's top image is a Latin hypercube;
  - Theorem 4, a 3-net law composes like cycles exactly when its square is
    isotopic to an abelian p-group;
- `AFFINE-TOPS-20261021-001B-P001` ("the affine-tops proof"): Theorem 1 and
  Corollary 2;
- `KNOT-CIRCUITS-20261020-001B-P001`:
  - its definitions;
  - a Boolean law composes like cycles exactly when it is affine;
  - affine laws over finite fields compose like cycles.

It also cites:
- **Protasov and Voynov's theorem** on families of nonnegative matrices without
  positive products (Linear Algebra Appl. 437 (2012) 749–765), in the
  combinatorial form of
  [Blondel, Jungers and Olshevsky](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.0729),
  Theorem 12;
- **Jordan's theorem:** a transitive permutation group on two or more points
  has an element with no fixed point;
- **periodic Markov chains:** an irreducible chain with period h converges,
  along each residue class of times modulo h, to its stationary distribution
  restricted to one cyclic class;
- **the Galois connection** between polymorphisms and pp-definable relations,
  Schur's lemma, and Wedderburn's little theorem;
- **Zaslavsky's frame matroids** of gain graphs.

## Definitions

- **Links and transfers.** A link is a knot with two of its variables chosen
  as witnesses, in and out, whose ranges are the same set W. Its other
  variables are its middle distinctions, with joint range M.
  - For μ ∈ M, the transfer T_μ ⊆ W × W is the set of (in, out) pairs that the
    knot allows with the middle distinctions at μ.
  - **What K2 gives.** Forgetting out leaves in and the middles free, and
    likewise for in. So every transfer is left-total and right-total, M is the
    product of the middles' ranges, and the transfers together cover W × W.
- **Rings.** A ring of k copies passes each copy's out-witness to the next
  copy's in-witness, and the last copy's back to the first.
  - It forgets the witnesses and allows a pattern (μ₁, …, μ_k) exactly when
    the composite T_{μ₁} ∘ ⋯ ∘ T_{μ_k} has a fixed point (the ring theorem's
    setting).
  - e_k is the share of M^k that the ring excludes.
- **Memory.** A link keeps a **lasting record** when lim sup e_k > 0, and
  **forgets** when e_k → 0.
- **Group-like.**
  - A link is **group-like** when every transfer is a permutation of W (the
    ring theorem's permutation tables).
  - It is **group-like modulo a partition** when every transfer maps each
    block into a single block.
- **The knot's law.** "A knot composes like cycles" means that the law
  generated by its relation does.
- **Networks.** Let G be a graph with vertices V and oriented edges E.
  - A network places a copy of a link on each edge (u, v): in-witness at u,
    out-witness at v.
  - Vertex witnesses are hidden; the middle distinctions on the edges are
    visible.
- **Tops** are those of the affine-tops proof.
  - For a knot range B, Ā = B/μ_B is an elementary abelian p-group.
  - Polymorphisms act on it by affine maps. R is the ring generated by their
    linear parts.

## Standard statement

1. **The dichotomy** (Theorem 1). For every link, exactly one of these holds.
   - **(a) A full product.** Some product of transfers is W × W. Then rings
     forget: e_k ≤ (1 − |M|^{−ℓ})^{⌊k/ℓ⌋}, for a full product of length ℓ.
   - **(b) Blocks.** The transfers map the blocks of a partition of W into
     r ≥ 2 blocks onto blocks, permuting them. Then the record lasts:
     lim sup e_k ≥ 1/|Γ|, where Γ is the group of block permutations.
   - So a link keeps a lasting record exactly when it is group-like modulo
     some partition into two or more blocks.
   - With two witness values, the only such partition is into points. This is
     the ring theorem, and Theorem 1 extends it to any number of values.
2. **Two values** (Corollary 2). For a link of a Boolean knot, five properties
   coincide:
   - it is group-like;
   - it keeps a lasting record;
   - it has no full product;
   - its knot composes like cycles;
   - its knot is a parity relation.
3. **Networks in laws that compose like cycles** (Theorem 3). Let a law
   compose like cycles, and take a link of one of its three-variable knots
   whose witnesses share a knot range B.
   - **(a) Group-like modulo the tops.** Modulo the tops, every transfer is
     a ↦ φa + t on Ā.
     - φ is one gain for the whole link, a nonzero scalar of the finite field
       D = End_R(Ā).
     - t runs over Ā as the middle distinction runs over its top.
   - **(b) Memory is twisted cohomology.** Modulo the tops, a network on G
     remembers exactly H¹(G; Ā_φ), the first cohomology of G with
     coefficients in Ā twisted by the gains. That is
     |Ā|^{|E|−|V|} · |H⁰(G; Ā_φ)| classes.
   - **(c) Free sets.** Modulo the tops, a set of edges is free exactly when
     each of its components is a tree or has one cycle, of gain other than 1.
     This is the frame matroid of the gain graph (G, φ).
   - **(d) Untwisted links.** When φ = 1, every independent loop carries one
     class of Ā, and the free sets are the forests: the nerve proof's free
     contact.
     - A ring of k copies remembers |Ā| classes when φ^k = 1, and nothing
       otherwise.
     - So the record is periodic and never fades.
   - **(e) Twisted loops bind in pairs.** Two loops of gain other than 1 that
     meet, or are joined by a path, form one circuit: a handcuff. Neither loop
     alone is a hole.
4. **The owner's claim as stated** (Theorem 4). The claim reads: "group-like
   knots are exactly the ones that both remember loops and compose like
   cycles". It holds for two values, by Corollary 2. Beyond two values it
   fails in three ways:
   - **group-like and remembering, but not composing like cycles:** the 3-nets
     of Z₆ and S₃;
   - **composing like cycles and remembering, but not group-like:** a dressed
     parity knot;
   - **group-like and composing like cycles, but forgetting at some lengths:**
     x + y + z = 0 over F₃, and other twisted affine knots.

   Networks alone cannot tell the first case apart. Networks of the Z₆ and S₃
   3-nets remember |G|^{b₁} classes and have the forests as free sets, just
   like abelian p-groups.
5. **The corrected claim** (Corollary 5).
   - **Memory.** Remembering a loop requires contact that is group-like
     modulo a partition into blocks; permutation transfers are not required.
   - **Laws that compose like cycles.** There, every three-variable knot whose
     witnesses share a range is group-like modulo the tops, with a gain in a
     finite field. Its networks remember twisted first cohomology over that
     field, modulo the tops.
   - **Latin knots.** A Latin knot composes like cycles exactly when it is
     isotopic to an abelian p-group. Its holes are cohomology with
     coefficients in that group, which is a finite field only modulo the tops.
   - **Binding.** Binding loops through fixed points, as the nerve proof's
     selectors do, leaves cycle-like composition. Within it, loops bind only
     linearly, as frame-matroid circuits.

## Nearest known results

- **Primitive families of matrices.** Theorem 1's dichotomy is Protasov and
  Voynov's theorem.
  - **The general setting.** For nonnegative matrices without zero rows or
    columns, an irreducible family has no positive product exactly when the
    matrices permute the blocks of a partition. Blondel, Jungers and Olshevsky
    also show that, without the no-zero-rows assumption, deciding whether a
    positive product exists is NP-hard.
  - **The links here meet the assumptions,** by K2.
  - **What is added** is the reading as rings of knots and the two memory
    bounds.
- **Gain graphs.**
  - Solvability of f(v) − φ f(u) = b_e on a graph is classically measured by
    first cohomology with twisted coefficients.
  - The free sets are Zaslavsky's frame matroid of the gain graph (Biased
    graphs II, 1991). Its circuits are balanced cycles, handcuffs and thetas.
- **Thomsen's condition** characterizes the abelian 3-nets. This is the 3-net
  proof's Theorem 4.
- **The program's own predecessors.** The ring theorem covers two values; the
  nerve proof covers free and selector contact.

**Classification.**
- **Theorem 1** applies a known theorem. The folklore risk is high for the
  mathematics; new to the program is the reading as rings of knots, which
  extends the ring theorem to any number of values.
- **Theorem 3** combines the affine-tops proof with classical gain-graph
  cohomology. New to the program:
  - the gain is a field scalar;
  - twisted loops bind in pairs;
  - the qualifier "modulo the tops" is needed. In the 3-net of Z₄, the tops
    hide a second layer: Z/4 rather than Z/2.
- **Theorem 4 and Corollary 5** test the owner's claim.

## Theorem 1 (the dichotomy)

Let a link have witness values W with |W| ≥ 2 and middle values M. Exactly one
of (a) and (b) holds.
- **(a)** Some product of transfers, of length ℓ, is W × W. Then
  e_k ≤ (1 − |M|^{−ℓ})^{⌊k/ℓ⌋} for every k.
- **(b)** There is a partition of W into r ≥ 2 blocks such that every transfer
  maps each block into a single block. The induced maps are permutations of
  the blocks, generating a group Γ. Then lim sup e_k ≥ 1/|Γ|.

*Proof.*
1. **Protasov and Voynov apply.** As 0/1 matrices, the transfers have no zero
   row or column. The family is irreducible: for any proper nonempty U ⊂ W,
   some transfer maps a point of U outside U, since the transfers cover
   W × W. So by Theorem 12 of Blondel, Jungers and Olshevsky, either some
   product is positive, which is (a), or the transfers have a common block
   permutation structure with at least two blocks, which is (b).
2. **Not both.** A product of block-permuting relations permutes the blocks
   too. Such a product maps each block into one block, so it is never W × W.
3. **The induced maps are permutations.** If a transfer mapped two blocks
   into one, some block would receive nothing, against right-totality.
4. **(a) forgets.**
   - Let ν = (ν₁, …, ν_ℓ) give a full product.
   - Composing a full relation on either side with a left- and right-total
     relation gives a full relation. So every product that contains ν as
     consecutive factors is full. It has fixed points, so the ring allows its
     pattern.
   - A uniformly random pattern of length k has ⌊k/ℓ⌋ disjoint windows of
     length ℓ. Each equals ν with probability |M|^{−ℓ}, independently.
   - So e_k ≤ (1 − |M|^{−ℓ})^{⌊k/ℓ⌋}.
5. **(b) lasts.**
   - **Γ is transitive.** For blocks i and j, pick w in block i and w′ in
     block j. Some transfer contains (w, w′), and its block permutation sends
     i to j.
   - **A deranging element.** By Jordan's theorem, Γ has an element δ that
     fixes no block.
   - **Such patterns are excluded.** If a pattern's block holonomy fixes no
     block, its composite relation maps every block into another block, so it
     has no fixed point.
   - **The random walk.** The block holonomy of a uniformly random pattern is
     a random walk on Γ. Its steps are uniform on the multiset of the
     transfers' block permutations, which generate Γ.
     - The walk is an irreducible Markov chain on Γ, with some period h and
       cyclic classes C₀, …, C_{h−1}. Its stationary distribution is uniform,
       since the walk is on a group.
     - Started at the identity, it lies in C_{k mod h} at time k. Along
       k ≡ j (mod h) it converges to the uniform distribution on C_j.
   - **The bound.** δ lies in some C_j. Along k ≡ j (mod h),
     e_k ≥ Pr[holonomy = δ] → 1/|C_j| ≥ 1/|Γ|. ∎

**Two values.** With |W| = 2, the only partition into two or more blocks is
into points. So (b) says every transfer is a permutation: the ring theorem's
statement 4. Case (a) is its fading.

**What the record is.** The record is the block holonomy. It is exact when the
transfers are themselves permutations (blocks are points). Otherwise it is a
lower bound, since a holonomy that fixes a block can still exclude a pattern
inside that block.

## Corollary 2 (two values)

For a link of a Boolean knot, the following are equivalent:
- **(i)** the link is group-like;
- **(ii)** it keeps a lasting record;
- **(iii)** no product of transfers is full;
- **(iv)** the knot composes like cycles;
- **(v)** the knot is a parity relation.

*Proof.*
- **(i), (ii) and (iii).** They are equivalent by Theorem 1, since with two
  values blocks are points.
- **(i) and (v).** They are equivalent by the ring theorem's statement 4.
- **(v) implies (iv).** Parity is affine over F₂, and affine laws over finite
  fields compose like cycles.
- **(iv) implies (v).**
  - A Boolean law composes like cycles only when it is affine. So the knot is
    a coset of a subspace of F₂ⁿ.
  - **Every constraint is all-ones.** A nonzero linear constraint whose
    support missed a variable would survive forgetting that variable, against
    K2. So every nonzero constraint has full support.
  - Over F₂, two such constraints are equal: their sum would have smaller
    support. So the knot is Σ xᵢ = b, a parity relation. ∎

## Theorem 3 (networks in laws that compose like cycles)

Let a law compose like cycles. Let K be one of its three-variable knots, with
in-witness x, middle y and out-witness z. Let x and z have the same range B, a
knot range with top Ā.

- **(a)** There are φ ∈ D^×, with D = End_R(Ā) a finite field, and a bijection
  t from the top of y onto Ā, such that modulo the tops K is
  z̄ = φx̄ + t(ȳ). Ā is a vector space over D.
- **(b)** Modulo the tops, a network of copies of K on a graph G remembers
  exactly coker δ_φ = H¹(G; Ā_φ). Here (δ_φ f)_e = f(v) − φ f(u) on edges
  e = (u, v). That is |Ā|^{|E|−|V|} · |H⁰(G; Ā_φ)| classes, where H⁰ is the
  set of f with f(v) = φ f(u) on every edge.
- **(c)** Modulo the tops, a set Y of edges is free exactly when each
  component of (V, Y) is a tree, or has exactly one cycle and that cycle has
  gain other than 1.
  - The gain of a cycle is the product of φ along forward edges and φ⁻¹ along
    backward ones.
  - These sets are the independent sets of the frame matroid of (G, φ).
- **(d)** If φ = 1, the free sets are the forests and the network remembers
  |Ā|^{b₁(G)} classes, one class of Ā per independent loop. A ring of k copies
  remembers |Ā| classes when φ^k = 1, and one class (nothing) otherwise.
- **(e)** Two cycles of gain other than 1 that share a vertex, or are joined by
  a path, form a circuit. Each cycle alone is free.

*Proof of (a).*
1. **The top image is a graph.** By the affine-tops proof (Theorem 1(d)), K's
   top image is a coset c + H of a subgroup of Ā ⊕ B̄_y ⊕ Ā. By the 3-net
   proof's Theorem 2, it is a Latin hypercube: any two coordinates determine
   the third. So H is the graph of (x̄, ȳ) ↦ φx̄ + ψȳ, with φ and ψ bijective
   homomorphisms, as in the affine-tops proof's Corollary 2. Set
   t(ȳ) = ψȳ + c.
2. **φ commutes with R.**
   - A polymorphism G acts on Ā by an affine map with linear parts L₁, …,
     L_n ∈ R. This action is the same at x and at z, which have the same
     range.
   - G preserves c + H, and x̄ and ȳ can vary freely in it. Comparing how the
     z-coordinate depends on the j-th x̄ gives L_j φ = φ L_j.
   - So φ ∈ End_R(Ā).
3. **Ā is a simple R-module.**
   - Let N be an R-submodule. The relation "x̄ − ȳ ∈ N" on B is preserved by
     every polymorphism, so it is pp-definable.
   - It is an equivalence containing μ_B. Since the top is maximal, N = 0 or
     N = Ā.
4. **D is a field.** By Schur's lemma D = End_R(Ā) is a division ring. It is
   finite, so it is a field (Wedderburn). φ is invertible, so φ ∈ D^×. ∎

*Proof of (b).*
1. **Blurring commutes with the network.**
   - Every solution of the network blurs to a solution of the blurred network,
     in which each copy is replaced by its top image.
   - **Conversely,** take a blurred solution and choose any elements of B in
     its vertex classes.
     - For each edge, K2 gives a middle value y between the chosen witnesses.
     - Modulo the tops, K is functional at y. So y lies in the class that the
       blurred solution assigns to the edge.
   - So, modulo the tops, the network's visible relation is that of the
     blurred network.
2. **The blurred network.** It allows ȳ exactly when t(ȳ) ∈ im δ_φ, entrywise
   over the edges.
3. **The count.** t is a bijection, so the allowed share is
   |im δ_φ| / |Ā|^{|E|}. The network therefore remembers
   |coker δ_φ| = |Ā|^{|E|} |ker δ_φ| / |Ā|^{|V|} classes, and
   ker δ_φ = H⁰(G; Ā_φ). ∎

*Proof of (c).*
1. **Freeness is a kernel count.** Modulo the tops, Y is free exactly when the
   restriction of δ_φ to the edges in Y maps onto Ā^Y, that is, when its
   kernel has |Ā|^{|V|−|Y|} elements. The kernel is a product over the
   components of (V, Y).
2. **One component.** On a component with V_c vertices and Y_c edges, a
   section is fixed by its value a at one vertex. It exists exactly when
   g a = a for the gain g of every cycle.
   - The gains are scalars of D^×. So the count is |Ā| if every cycle has gain
     1, and 1 otherwise.
3. **Comparing counts.**
   - **A tree** (Y_c = V_c − 1) has kernel Ā, as required.
   - **One cycle** (Y_c = V_c) needs a kernel of size 1, that is, a cycle of
     gain other than 1.
   - **More edges** (Y_c > V_c) would need a kernel smaller than 1, so the set
     is constrained. ∎

*Proof of (d).*
- **Untwisted.** With φ = 1 every cycle has gain 1. So the free sets are the
  forests, H⁰ = Ā on a connected graph, and the count is
  |Ā|^{|E|−|V|+1} = |Ā|^{b₁}.
- **Rings.** A ring of k copies is a k-cycle oriented around, with gain φ^k.
  So H⁰ is Ā or 0, and the count is |Ā| or 1. ∎

*Proof of (e).* The union of the two cycles and the path has one more edge
than it has vertices, so by (c) it is constrained. Removing any edge leaves
either a tree, or one cycle of gain other than 1 with trees attached. Either
is free. ∎

**The circuit is a knot.** In the checks, two triangles of x + y + z = 0 over F₃
share a vertex. Their six middle distinctions are constrained together, while
every five of them, and each triangle, are free. This is a knot on six
distinctions (K1–K3) binding two loops that are not holes.

## Theorem 4 (the owner's claim as stated)

**The claim.** "Group-like knots are exactly the ones that both remember loops
and compose like cycles."

**For two values it holds:** Corollary 2. **Beyond two values it fails in three
ways.**

1. **Group-like and lasting, but not composing like cycles.**
   - **The examples.** The 3-net of a group G links by c = a · m. Its
     transfers are right multiplications, which are permutations with no
     fixed point except the identity. So it keeps a lasting record.
   - **Z₆ and S₃ do not compose like cycles,** by the 3-net proof's
     Theorem 4(e): only abelian p-groups do.
   - **Failing systems.**
     - **Z₆.** In c = a + m mod 6, x ≡ e (mod 2) is defined by e = x + u + u,
       and e ≡ y (mod 3) by y = e + v + v + v. These are two ties. Their union
       without e is free, by the Chinese remainder theorem.
     - **S₃.** Thomsen's configuration, doubled, violates elimination at two
       variables.
2. **Composing like cycles and lasting, but not group-like.**
   - **The knot.** Dressed parity has domain Z₂ × Z₂. It is the set of
     triples whose first coordinates sum to 0, with free second coordinates.
   - **It composes like cycles.** Every system's solutions are those of a
     parity system on the first coordinates, times arbitrary second
     coordinates. So its free sets are those of an F₂-linear system, which
     composes like cycles.
   - **It keeps one bit at every length.**
   - **Its transfers are not permutations.** Each maps a value to both
     elements of a block. They permute the blocks "same first coordinate",
     which are its tops.
3. **Group-like and composing like cycles, but forgetting at some lengths.**
   - **The knot.** x + y + z = 0 over F₃ is affine over a field, so it composes
     like cycles.
   - **Its transfers are reflections** a ↦ −a − m, with gain −1.
   - **So by Theorem 3(d),** rings of odd length forget, and rings of even
     length keep log₂3 bits.
   - **The same happens** for z = 2x + y over F₅ (gain of order 4) and for
     z = ωx + y over GF(4) (order 3).
   - **This does not contradict Theorem 1.** That theorem promises a lasting
     record in the lim sup, not at every length.

**Networks alone cannot tell the first case apart.** Take a 3-net of a group G,
linked by right multiplication, on a connected graph.
- **What it allows.** It allows a pattern exactly when every cycle's holonomy
  is the identity.
- **The count.** Each assignment of group elements to the vertices fixes the
  edges, and |G| assignments give each allowed pattern. So it remembers
  |G|^{|E|−|V|+1} classes.
- **Free sets.** A forest is free, and a set containing a cycle is not.
- **For every group.** This holds for Z₆ and S₃ as for any group. The failures
  in case 1 appear only in systems that are not networks.

**Binding through fixed points.** The nerve proof's selector law uses the
transpositions (03), (13) and (23). Its three loops are allowed together
exactly when not all three are selected.
- That relation is not affine.
- Two such triangles sharing one selector violate elimination. So that law
  does not compose like cycles.
- In a law that does, Theorem 3 allows loops to bind only as frame-matroid
  circuits. ∎

## Corollary 5 (the corrected claim)

1. **Memory needs block-group-like contact** (Theorem 1). A link remembers a
   loop exactly when its transfers permute the blocks of some partition into
   two or more blocks. Permutation transfers are the special case of point
   blocks, and the only case for two values.
2. **Cycle-like composition makes memory homological.**
   - In a law that composes like cycles, every three-variable knot whose
     witnesses share a range has, modulo the tops:
     - one gain φ in a finite field D;
     - networks that remember H¹(G; Ā_φ), with the frame matroid of (G, φ) as
       free sets (Theorem 3).
   - By the affine-tops proof, every other system of the law is also linear
     over finite fields modulo the tops.
   - **This is the sense in which holes behave like homology over finite
     fields modulo the tops.**
     - The coefficients may be twisted.
     - Below the tops the coefficients can be a larger p-group. In the 3-net
       of Z₄, networks remember Z/4 classes, which are Z/2 classes modulo the
       tops.
3. **Latin knots.** For a Latin three-variable knot, composing like cycles
   holds exactly for isotopes of abelian p-groups (the 3-net proof's
   Theorem 4).
4. **Binding.** Fixed-point binding of loops into atoms (the nerve proof's
   selectors) leaves cycle-like composition. Inside it, loops bind only
   linearly: two twisted loops into a handcuff, or larger frame-matroid
   circuits. ∎

## What this does not show

- **No protected structure.** No native recurrence, composed topology in the
  protected sense, path or distance is claimed.
- **The law recognizes, it does not create.** The networks' graphs are laid
  out by hand. The law recognizes their cycle space or frame matroid; it does
  not make the graph.
- **The matroid statement is modulo the tops.** In every case checked, the
  network's own free sets agree with those of its top image. That is not
  proved.
- **Only part of the setting is covered.** Theorem 3 treats three-variable
  knots whose witnesses share a range, and networks of copies of one link.
- **Theorem 1's bound is asymptotic.** It promises a lasting record in the
  lim sup. Exact memory at a given length depends on the link: the ring
  theorem's counting formula for two values, and Theorem 3(d) in laws that
  compose like cycles.

    propagation count, and also the network's own free sets.
  - **Z₄.** The 3-net of Z₄ remembers twice as many bits as its top image.
  - **Z₆ and S₃.** Their networks remember b₁ log₂ 6 bits, with the forests
    as free sets.
- **Theorem 4.**
  - **Failing systems.**
    - Z₆: the ties {x, e} and {e, y}, with {x, y} free.
    - S₃ (16 variables): the circuits {x₁, x₂, x₃} and {x₁, x₂, x₃′}, with
      failures at x₁ and x₂.
    - The selector law: its three loops are "not all three", and the doubled
      system fails at the shared selector.
  - **Random systems.** 120 random systems each of dressed parity and three
    twisted affine laws (over F₃, F₅ and GF(4)) satisfy elimination.
    Dressed parity's free sets are those of its parity projection, in all
    120.
  - **Rings of x + y + z = 0 over F₃.** They exclude shares 0, 2/3, 0 and 2/3
    at lengths 1 to 4.
- **Controls,** each rejected:
  - "every group-like knot composes like cycles" (Z₆, S₃);
  - "a knot that composes like cycles has permutation transfers" (dressed
    parity);
  - "a group-like knot that composes like cycles remembers a loop at every
    length" (F₃);
  - "networks alone tell cycle-like composition apart" (Z₆ and S₃ networks);
  - "binding loops through fixed points keeps cycle-like composition" (the
    selector law);
  - "in a law that composes like cycles no two loops bind" (two triangles
    over F₃).
