> [The check](checks/what_decides_the_class/README.md).

The earlier studies asked whether one pattern's course depends on another it meets, and varied only whether the
other was there. This study holds the place fixed and changes the form.

1. **It differs with the form, through the link.** With a first seed and the place of its link held fixed, what the
   first seed's own descent makes falls into three or more classes over 60 held-out second seeds in 87 studies of 90,
   and 59% to 87% of pairs of forms are told apart. With the two seeds in one start and not linked, nothing differs.
2. **Not by the first round alone.** Some second seeds that leave the same record at every round-1 birth with a
   first-seed ancestor are told apart later: 422 to 1,309 pairs a row where short knots retire or make offshoots.
3. **Not by the held knot alone; often by a count profile.** The class is never a function of the held knot alone.
   In 49 of the 90 held-out studies it is a function of the held knot together with the second seed's own births by
   round, and in 41 it is not.
4. **Fate: not shown.** The prediction that the first seed's line would show two or more of the fates ends, steady,
   grows in at least 40 studies of 90 fails at 37.
5. **It sees the near end, and what it sees depends on the first seed.** With no taking rule, chains that differ two
   or more steps from the held knot are told apart in at most one pair in ten; with the take of a sole candidate a
   chain of four is told from one of five in 20% to 79% of pairs under four laws. With the held-out first seeds and
   no taking rule, where short knots retire or make offshoots, only a single knot is told from a longer chain.

Nineteen of twenty predictions hold, the four exact replications of the drafter's probe among them, and the thread's
own program agrees exactly with the study's on the held-out material.

## Final report as sent (2026-11-26, about 01:00 UTC)

The registered study is closed, referee pending. **On starts built by hand, what a first seed's own descent makes differs with the form of the second seed, and the link carries the difference. It held on first seeds and forms nothing had grown, in two independently written programs that agree exactly. Two things temper it: in more than half of the held-out studies the class is decided by a count profile of the second seed, and my prediction that the first seed's fate differs with the form failed.**

- **Nineteen of twenty predictions hold.** The four exact replications are among them: a reading written from the frozen text alone reproduces my probe in all 270 studies.
- **The link carries it.** With both seeds in one start and not linked, every form gives exactly what the bare start gives: 90 studies of 90.
- **It differs with the form.** On the held-out material the forms fall into three or more classes in 87 studies of 90, and 59% to 87% of pairs of forms are told apart.
- **Not by the first round alone.** In each row, 422 to 1,309 pairs are told apart only after round 1 although every round-1 birth with a first-seed ancestor is the same.
- **Not by the held knot alone, but often by a count profile.** The class is never decided by the held knot alone (0 of 90). It is decided by the held knot together with the second seed's own births by round in 49 of 90. In those, "form" is too large a word.
- **The failed prediction.** The first seed's line shows two or more fates in 37 studies of 90; I predicted at least 40. Under most laws most second seeds leave it growing. So "its fate differs with the form" is not shown as registered, and what I told you last time about the line that ends, idles or grows holds on the probe's material only.
- **What it sees depends on the first seed.** With the held-out first seeds, no taking rule and short knots retiring or making offshoots, the first seed tells a single knot from a longer chain and nothing else.
- **Second count.** My own program, run on the held-out material only after the study's outputs were in, agrees exactly on all 360 studies and all 162 cells of the depth table.

Cuts and faults: the knot test was cut to half after it had started, against the rule of recording a cut first; no knot failed in the 263 runs tested. The swap to parity tested little, since only 28 of 5,490 runs differ.
