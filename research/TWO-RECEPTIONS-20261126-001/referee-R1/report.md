# Independent referee report on two receptions and the source of shortage
**Confirmed with fixes. Block on the parity extension and on the unrestricted claim that an ordinary-count addition cannot reach the receiver.**

The stopping reason holds for the shortage studied in the census. Under its not-all-equal law, every newborn gets at least its demand, can pay in its first active round, and spends every usable loose distinction. The 808 recorded second additions include no case that kept the first response and added a later one within the common observation window. The late hearing under the ration that drops demand is reproducible. Two counterexamples limit what can be retired on these grounds.

## The argument, checked first

In the recorded engine, all takes consult the structure before the round and can take only distinctions already shared. The engine assigns the births their inputs, adds them as holders, and constructs one demand roster containing every birth plus every older member still holding a usable loose distinction. Only then does it give each birth its fresh distinctions, each with a single holder. Thus each positive-funded birth has exactly its fresh count available, and fresh allocation leaves all meetings unchanged. Even if a ration allows zero, the literal condition **actual funding ≥ demand for every birth** forces each zero-funded birth to have zero demand. Every joinable pair belongs to some group, so that birth has no joinable partner. Omitting it from the next roster leaves the groups unchanged.

Consequently a newborn member owes its demand and can pay. Under RELEASE it pays the kept groups and gives every remaining loose distinction to a one-parent birth, including holdings released when a waiting start knot cancels a group. It leaves membership. Holders never decrease, and undisturbed growth gives an old knot no new place. This proves the claim for its entire lifetime. ## Late and early hearings

The four-knot receiver has three mutually sharing knots and a fourth sharing with just one of those three. At the fourth knot, which holds two loose distinctions, the census attached the meeting. With the one-knot sender and FLOOR1 (just enough for three places), **all three** eligible far additions after round 3 were seven sharings away. ## Fixes to the record

1. **Block — parity.** [README, argument](../README.md#b-does-the-model-make-the-shortage-itself) says “(Parity's implied births receive nothing and hold no usable distinction, so they are never members: outside the argument and harmless.)” In an unchanged eight-knot parity start with the ordinary count and the oldest eligible take, an implied birth has demand one and receives zero. The one five-member demand group becomes six pairs among the four remaining newborns. **Each owes three while holding one.** Every start member could pay. Changing only to not-all-equal gives zero shortages. Enumerating all 256 allowed states before and after the births verified the full even-parity projections of all eight and then all thirteen knots. The canonical K1–K3 checks passed at all three arities. Restrict the ordinary-count conclusion to the tested knot law or explicitly require every birth to receive its demand after all exceptions. [Exact start, growth and projections](argument_witnesses.jsonl) · [Canonical checks](argument_knot_checks.json)
2. **Narrowing — where an addition lands.** The [same paragraph](../README.md#b-does-the-model-make-the-shortage-itself) says “An added loose distinction raises what a knot holds and makes no sharing, so it makes no knot short either; a barred one can.” This holds at a **current member**, as tested. In a two-knot no-take start, one knot made three offspring, each owing two and holding two. Adding to the other, now spent, start knot brought it back into membership and made all three offspring owe three. Adding instead to an active offspring left all three owing two. 3. **Block — hearing under the ordinary count.** The [decision's long-term-goal row](../decision.md#position-and-next-directions) says “Under the model's count a pattern's own knots cannot be reached after the start except by a removal”. The [note's argument](../README.md#b-does-the-model-make-the-shortage-itself) says “So under the ordinary count a pattern's own knots can be reached after the start only through shortages made by the start itself, or by a distinction removed.” In a three-knot start under the oldest eligible local take, an addition at a current far member after round 1 changes the **same receiver-owned join** in round 2 from 9 to 10 fresh distinctions and from 12 to 13 places. Both examples set the addition earlier than the census. In the compact after-round-3 search, 209 of 695 comparisons finished their windows with no hearing. Another 429 lacked an active end and 57 reached a computational bound. An independent implementation and the study engine agree on both positive witnesses. A funded, no-take meeting has a separate locality argument. Extending that argument to takes would require the same locality premise.
