**In the generator study's 21-knot start, grown under the ordinary count, what is made on the first family's side
does not depend on the second family at all: change the second family's own original and every knot that avoids it
is there as before, at the same price. That follows from the price formula the generator study proved, and it is
checked here through size eight under both knot laws. What does carry the second family is the joint line: each
step along it costs what the second family's word says at that step. Leaving one original a loose distinction short
changes nothing in this. A cap on what a birth may receive does: with a cap of 4 to 8 the first family's side makes
something else when the second family's original is changed, in every case read; with a cap of 2 or 3, or with no
cap, it does not.**

## The words

A **live original** is a start knot with loose distinctions; two are a **candidate pair** when a start knot
witnesses both. The start has nine live originals, v, r1, r2, r3, s, t, b0, b1, b2, twelve candidate pairs, each
with its own witness, and each live original holds as many loose distinctions as it has candidate pairs: its
**degree**. In the generator study the two families released together differ by one original: the first family's
word passes b1 and never b2, the second's passes b2 and never b1. A made knot's **cluster** is the set of originals
it descends from, each counted as often as the generator study's identification counts it; here it is carried
along the births. Its **price** is the number of fresh distinctions it receives. **The first family's side** is
read by descent: the knots that have b1 among their original ancestors and not b2. **The change** gives b2 two more
candidate pairs, with three new originals in a ring of four, and two more loose distinctions: degree 4 for 2. It
touches no original of the first family's word.

## 1. Under the ordinary count: argued

The generator study proves that in this start a knot with cluster C receives
f(C) = 2 + the sum over the originals x of C of (degree of x − 2) fresh distinctions, that its parents are the
clusters with one original fewer and its children those with one more. So:

- **A knot's making is its own.** Which knots exist with a given cluster, and what each receives, depends on the
  start only through the degrees of the originals in that cluster. Nothing about an original outside the cluster
  enters.
- **So the first family's side is untouched by the second family.** Every member of the first family, at every
  size, exists and receives the same whatever the second family's own original is like. This holds for any change
  of the start that keeps the start in the class of the identification theorem and keeps the degrees along the
  first family's word. (Taking the second family's original away altogether is another kind of change: it lowers
  the degrees of s and t, which the first family's word passes, and the generator study gives the prices then.)
- **The joint line carries the second family's word.** A knot on the joint line has a parent on the line and a
  parent in the second family, and its cluster has one original more than the parent on the line: the next
  original of the second family's word. So its price exceeds that parent's by the degree of that original less
  two. Step by step the line's prices spell the degrees along the second family's word.

In this class, then, nothing a family makes responds to what it meets. A meeting adds a joint line, and takes
nothing from either party and gives it nothing.

## 3. One original a loose distinction short: nothing changes (expectation failed)

Each of the nine live originals in turn was given one loose distinction fewer than its degree, so that it owes
more joins than it can pay; the law's treatment of a short knot then acts (it retires, makes offshoots, or waits).
Expected beforehand: with s or t short, the first family's side would differ with the change at b2 under some
treatment. Found: in 60 comparisons of 60 (nine originals and none, three treatments, two knot laws, seven rounds)
the births on the first family's side are the same with b2 changed. The short original is short in round 1 only.
After that every made knot receives as many fresh distinctions as it is asked for joins, which is what the
ordinary count gives, so nothing made is ever short.

## 4. A cap: the first family's side then depends on the second family's original

Under a cap c a birth receives at most c fresh distinctions, and a made knot can be asked for more joins than it
holds. The recorded capped rations came first (the cap of three; just enough for three places). Expected
beforehand: a difference. Found: none in 12 comparisons of 12. Under so low a cap every knot that holds b2 is short
in both starts, and the growth nearly stops.

The fourth part was predicted with a reason. A knot Y that holds b2 is asked for two more joins in the changed
start. Under a cap between the two numbers Y is paid in full in one start and short in the other; a short knot's
joins are cancelled; and a knot of the first side that shares a parent with Y keeps the distinction it would have
given to their join and makes something else with it. Predicted: a difference for every cap from 4 to 8 under each
treatment, none at 2 or 3, none without a cap.

## What this shows and does not show

- **Shown.** Under the ordinary count on this start a family's own making is closed to every other family, and the
  record's proved formula is why. Scarcity made at the start (one original short) does not open it. A bound on
  what a birth may receive does, as soon as the bound falls between what the met side asks for in the two cases.
- **So the two things the fold of emergence needs have not yet been seen together.** Lasting families that the
  growth releases are proved for starts of the identification theorem's class under the ordinary count, where they
  cannot act on each other. One part's making depending on another's is seen in two other places: on this start
  under a cap; and under the ordinary count itself on starts outside that class, where knots hold more loose
  distinctions than they are asked for or one knot witnesses several members (the cone study's matched starts, and
  the built starts of [the form study](../../FOLLOWS-THE-FORM-20261125-001A/results.md)). In neither place has
  anyone shown that a start releases lasting families. Making one original a single distinction short, as in part
  3, does not take this start far enough out of the class to show it.
- **Not shown: families under a cap.** "The first family's side" in parts 3 and 4 is a reading by descent on a
  start grown seven rounds. In the generator study the families are released at size 16, far beyond what can be
  grown, and its proofs are for the ordinary count.
- **Not shown: form.** The change is one number, the degree of one original. What the first side then makes
  depends on how much the other side asks. Whether anything beyond such counts matters under a cap is not tested.
- **The argument of part 1 is not refereed**, and it is a consequence of a proved formula, not a new theorem. The
  checks are one program on one start, with one change.
- **Supplied**: the start, the change, the cap, the reading by descent. **Made by births**: everything after
  round 0.
