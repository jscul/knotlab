# When the equation space identifies the actual junctions

Let the source have one ternary seed, three unary additions making three outputs each, and otherwise only two- or three-parent additions making two outputs. The rival can be any reachable presentation under the full serial law. Transport its bit names by the given correspondence. Equal homogeneous parity spaces mean equality of allowed states on these names, including every minimal allowed flip set and every bond. We use \(K=D+1\) for the size of a smallest bond through a pair.

**Physical-support classification.** Equal parity spaces force all actual junction supports whenever a current three-parent choice is legal. This also holds for every empty menu except one explicit type of final addition. The exception requires six loose distinctions at a prefix, partitioned into two legal triples. Attaching the same two fresh distinctions to either of the two triples gives the same states and changes exactly the last junction support. Width 32 is the minimum, including comparisons with unrestricted rivals. When a scalar comparison has already forced the parity space, these conclusions apply under that same scalar correspondence. In every case the legal three-parent menu itself agrees by the [equal-state theorem](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/inverse.md).

## A cyclic ordinary graph has at most one leaf outside its block

Regard physical sites as the vertices of their ordinary meeting graph. Each shared distinction gives one ordinary edge, and each loose distinction can be wired to the exterior. Every input of a multiple-parent addition lies on a four-edge ordinary cycle. Indeed its parent and one other parent already have a common ordinary neighbor. Those two parents, the common neighbor and the new site form the cycle. All inputs of the new site belong to the same maximal two-connected block, since each pair belongs to such a cycle. An ordinary bridge therefore comes from a unary addition.

In any cyclic block take the earliest multiple-parent addition with an input in that block. Such an addition exists because the youngest site on an ordinary cycle has two distinct older neighbors. In the four-cycle just constructed, the two edges from the common neighbor to the two parents already existed. They belong to this very block. If either came from an earlier multiple-parent addition it would contradict the choice of the earliest one. Hence the block already contains two different edges introduced by unary additions. Distinct blocks share no edges. The source has just three unary additions in total. Consequently it has at most one cyclic block \(B\), and any cyclic source consists of \(B\) alone or \(B\) with a single additional edge to a leaf \(q\). A source without cycles has no multiple-parent addition at all.

A triple that may join has three distinct parent holders with three distinct ordinary pair witnesses. These six sites form a cycle. The holders and their witnesses thus all lie in \(B\). This observation alone does not identify all physical sites outside \(B\). The following argument recovers the leaf even when the menu is empty.

**Wider equal-state consequence.** For this paragraph alone, allow two or three outputs at every addition, including the unary ones, and any number of higher-place sites. If the source has at most three unary additions in total and a legal triple, equality of the full states with any reachable rival still forces all actual junction supports. The block count used only the number of unary additions. For the block carrying a legal triple, the [labeled block recovery](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/inverse.md#locating-the-exterior) identifies the block with its exterior, all its internal bits and the group of remaining bits attached at each block vertex. It also identifies the direct loose bits in each group, since these are precisely the single-edge intersections of the group with a crossing simple cycle. If a leaf \(q\) attaches at \(v\), its group also contains one ordinary bit \(e=vq\) and at least two loose bits at \(q\). This is true for a seed leaf and for a unary child making either two or three outputs. Those loose bits form a named parallel class, identified by their two-bit allowed flip sets. Equal total graphic rank leaves exactly one vertex outside the recovered block and exterior in the rival. The parallel class must join that vertex to the exterior, since a pair of ordinary vertices meets only once and all direct block-to-exterior bits are already identified. The remaining bit \(e\) must join the outside vertex to \(v\). This identifies the entire actual presentation. Thus a live triple and a changed physical support under an equal-state correspondence in the full law require at least **four unary additions** in the source. The wider conclusion assumes equality of states and gives no scalar cutoff for sources with later unary two-output sites.

## All supports at a source with a bridge

Let \(e\) join the leaf \(q\) to \(v\in B\). There are two ways the actual root can be placed.

* If \(q\) is a unary child, delete it and its three loose outputs and restore \(e\) as a loose output of \(v\). Exactly two unary three-output joins remain. The original seed and the remaining chronological order give a history in the minimum-width family of 001J.
* If \(q\) is the original seed, it has two loose distinctions and \(e\) as its only ordinary connection. Its sole child \(v\) must be the first added site, hence a unary site making three outputs. Remove \(q\) and all three seed distinctions. Take the three former outputs of \(v\) as the new ternary seed. Again the other two unary three-output joins remain, giving a history in 001J.

In either restriction the removed site had at most one ordinary neighbor at every stage, so it could never have witnessed a pair of distinct retained parents. The remaining parent sets and their meeting tests are intact. In particular the [001J fixed point](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J-P001_seven_to_six/family.md#all-histories-have-at-most-two-shrinking-joins) bounds the total number of three-parent, two-output additions in the original by two. Thus a unary-third history with three or more such additions already has a two-connected ordinary graph.

**The clipped pair table through \(D=4\) forces the entire parity space here.** In fact this step works in every source in this study with at most two shrinking joins. For a shore with at least three sites, its first three in birth order have boundary at least seven. Only the seed has degree three, and at most two ordinary edges can run among these three sites. Later insertions change the boundary by \(p+h-2j\), where \(j\) of the site's \(p\) parents are already inside and it made \(h\) outputs. The only negative contribution is minus one at a fully internal shrinking join. Thus a shore of at least three sites cannot have size four, and one of size five must contain both shrinking sites and all their input parents. No input bit of either shrinking site crosses that shore.

Hence the [short-equation theorem](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F-P001_finite_interval/structural.md#recovering-every-equation-through-size-four) forces the actual equations of arity at most four in the rival. Each five-place source site is a shrinking site. Use two of its inputs whose parents differ from the seed and at least one of whose parents avoids all ordinary contact with the seed. If the seed is a parent, the other two have its color and avoid it. Otherwise a meeting of all three parents with the seed would have forbidden the triple, so the required pair exists. A support of size at most five through either chosen input is a physical star or the sum of the seed and an adjacent four-place star, by the preceding boundary count. Away from the target star, a bit close to that input must therefore meet its other parent or, if that parent meets the seed, the seed. The two parents are distinct and have the same color, so no outside bit meets both tests. The two inputs have minimum five. A rival five-bit bond through them must be exactly the target star. This is the [001J two-input argument](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J-P001_seven_to_six/family.md#the-two-possible-shrinking-junctions-still-reveal-themselves).

All \(n\) independent source-site equations now hold in the rival. The through-three equations also locate the unique ternary site, which is the seed. A reachable rival with any later unary two-output addition would have an additional ternary equation. Its first two additions are necessarily unary and must therefore make three outputs. If \(r'\) rival additions make three outputs, the common width satisfies \(2n+4=2n'+1+r'\). Consequently \(r'\) is odd and at least three, and its rank \(n'\) is at most \(n\). Inclusion of the source space forces rank equality and equality of the full spaces.

We can now apply the [exact scalar separator theorem](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/separator.md), since equality of the spaces gives every exact pair reading. It recovers the actual bridge \(e\) and the same two sides, retaining \(e\) in each. On the leaf side there are just three or four bits including \(e\). The restricted rival side is connected and begins with a local site of arity at least three. Each additional local site would make at least two fresh distinctions, so this side in the rival is a single site on the very same bits. In the four-bit case the unique original ternary is on the main side. Both main sides are rooted mixed histories with \(e\) restored. Their allowed-state relations agree by projection onto the retained main bits including \(e\), since each side permits both values of \(e\). The 001J inverse recovers all physical sites and the holder of \(e\).

In the three-bit leaf case that physical leaf is the unique original ternary in both histories. On both sides fix all three seed bits to zero and then remove them. This realizes the second reduction above at the level of allowed states. An assignment on the remaining bits extends by these three zeros exactly when it satisfies the shortened equation on \(v\) and every other retained equation. The reduced source is in 001J, and the reduced rival is a lawful mixed history. The 001J inverse identifies all sites, including the new ternary \(v\), under the same names. Restoring \(e\) to \(v\) and reinstating the known original seed recovers every physical site. Simply forgetting \(e\) in the four-place equation would instead allow every assignment to its other three bits.

If the original graph is acyclic, all \(n-1\) ordinary edges are bridges. Equal rank gives the rival the same \(n\) sites. Its ordinary graph is connected and has these same \(n-1\) bridges, so it too is a tree. Retaining the boundary copies on the two sides of all recovered bridge partitions leaves one site per piece. Its named support is the set of its incidences. Equivalently, two distinct bits share a site precisely when no other bridge separates them, and the maximal pairwise intersecting groups are the site stars in an ordinary tree. Thus acyclic sources also have all their actual supports identified, with an empty menu.

## The exterior is the only remaining choice in a two-connected source

If the ordinary graph is \(B\), its wired graph stays connected after deleting any two vertices. When one deletion is the exterior this follows from two-connectivity of \(B\). For two ordinary deletions it is the general lawful [two-vertex deletion lemma](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/inverse.md#two-elementary-source-restrictions). Its augmented vertex stars, including the exterior star, can therefore all be recognized from the common states. They are precisely the bonds whose deletion leaves the remaining bits linked by chains of surviving cycles. Deleting a vertex leaves a two-connected graph, while a bond with two nontrivial shores leaves surviving edges on both sides and no cycle joining the sides. In any reachable rival, a bond passing this test must thus be a vertex star. Equal graphic rank gives the same number of vertices, hence the same entire augmented collection on the same bit names. [Augmented star recognition](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J-P001_seven_to_six/family.md#locating-the-ordinary-junctions-from-their-equations)

Suppose the source exterior \(o\) changes to a vertex \(u\) in the rival. No ordinary pair of sites has parallel edges, so every parallel class must meet both \(o\) and \(u\). The latest source site has at least two loose outputs. It is therefore \(u\). The latest rival site must for the same reason be \(o\). That latest source site has at least two ordinary neighbors in \(B\), so it did not enter by a unary addition. Every other source addition makes exactly two outputs. Hence the two latest sites in the two presentations have the identical two parallel fresh bits. The remaining loose distinctions of the source have to be the distinct input bits of the rival latest site. A source with six or more loose distinctions thereby rules out the change of exterior outright.

Deleting \(o\) and \(u\) leaves the literal same prefix on both sides, after all its edges to either deleted vertex are read as loose. The last junction in each history takes a complementary group of those prefix bits. Both groups must be lawful on that common prefix. Their respective orderings of earlier sites do not affect the current parent and witness tests. Conversely, two legal complementary groups permit two actual histories with the same prefix and two identical fresh names. The two last parity equations differ by the sum of all prefix site equations, which is exactly the prefix frontier. The two enlarged parity spaces are equal.

The [unary-third frontier fixed point](frontier.json) gives at least five loose distinctions at every depth. Five loose bits require a rival latest site with three inputs. If the source latest took two, its prefix would already have had five loose bits and would have admitted the rival triple, giving a lawful four-loose history. The lower bound excludes that possibility. Hence a change of exterior requires **two three-parent final choices at a prefix with six loose distinctions**. Conversely each such choice belongs to the unary-third family and realizes the changed support on the supplied names. The source's unique holder of a parallel loose pair is the only candidate for the alternative exterior. The two realizations therefore give exactly two possible collections of physical supports. The current menu of each is empty, since another three-parent choice at five loose bits would again reach four.

The fixed point has exactly one six-loose record with complementary legal triples. The three available holders have two loose bits each, so there are eight named choices, paired into four complementary pairs. One earliest prefix has width 30. Its sites 10, 11 and 12 hold \(\{24,25\},\{26,27\},\{28,29\}\). Their pair witnesses are 9 for 10 and 11, 8 for 10 and 12, and 7 for 11 and 12. No site witnesses all three. Use \(\{24,26,28\}\) on one side, \(\{25,27,29\}\) on the other, and make 30 and 31 in both histories. All 496 pair readings agree under the identity because the complete allowed states agree. The two resulting physical five-place supports differ. The fixed point reaches no five-loose history before width 32. Every smaller source consequently has either a bridge, where every physical site was identified, or at least six loose bits, where a change of exterior is impossible. This gives the minimum against every reachable equal-state rival. The width-32 example also works for exact scalar tables. A lower bound against arbitrary scalar-table rivals uses the scalar-to-state recovery result for the source widths being compared.
