# What the independent comparisons establish

## A phase carried by a word does not fix the phase of an idle pulse

For a not-all-equal knot of arity $m$, write $s$ for the number of ones. The first-root amplitude $1-\exp(2\pi i s/m)$ is $-2i\exp(i\pi s/m)\sin(\pi s/m)$. The sine is positive on every allowed word. Multiplying over knots therefore gives one constant phase and a separate turn at every named distinction. Its angle is the sum of $\pi/m$ over its not-all-equal holders. Parity contributes a positive factor on its support. This works with any overlap and with a separate conjugate choice at each holder. A reader with an independently adjustable question for every named distinction can absorb all those turns. Its comparison preparation must have the same magnitudes. For example, one four-place knot gives normalized probabilities $1/20,1/10,1/20$ per allowed word with respectively one, two or three ones. Uniform weighting would give $1/14$ to each word.

Multiplying the amplitude of word $x$ by a phase $D(x)$ transforms a transition $x\to y$ by $D(y)\overline{D(x)}$. Around any sequence that restores the word, these factors multiply to one. At an idle word they equal one on every individual transition. In the earlier compensated three-word experiment all three register words even have the same count of two ones. Thus the proposed weighting supplies no phase to a genuinely idle exchange. A separate rule that accumulates amplitudes along a history would be a different hypothesis. ## The other fifth root gives a different test

The prospective alternative at winding $k$ replaces $\pi/m$ by $\pi k/m$. For $k$ coprime to $m$, the support is still the full not-all-equal relation. On weights $s=1,\ldots,m-1$ the residual real sign is $(-1)^{\lfloor ks/m\rfloor}$. A turn attached separately to each bit can absorb these signs on a single knot only if that sequence is geometric. Indeed symmetry among the weight-one words forces the same turn at every position. The consecutive signs can then either always agree or always alternate. Of the $m-2$ successive comparisons exactly $k-1$ reverse. Thus only $k=1$ and $k=m-1$ can always be absorbed in this sense. The argument includes every input weight, and a single five-place knot already gives a counterexample at $k=2$. A measurement allowed to act jointly on the whole word can always absorb a diagonal rephasing. This distinction is about what the readers can access. [Scoped phase proof](../KNOT-COUNTS-AND-ONE-EXTRA-MEETING-20261121-001A/results.md)

In the supplemental test take two independent copies of that five-place knot. The first exterior is 1000 and the second is 1100. All four choices of the two remaining bits extend these specified exteriors. The ordinary exchange of the two bits is a lawful word permutation on this restricted set. Supplying its coherent two-valued measurement is a further operation. It can be written with the literature's swap test. For two separately prepared one-bit states it gives a positive answer with chance $(1+\mathrm{tr}(\rho_1\rho_2))/2$. The identity follows by expanding the two-bit exchange in the ordinary value basis. It compares two different conditional states, and is invariant under **any common unitary** on the two unpinned bits. A standard use is due to [Buhrman, Cleve, Watrous and de Wolf](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.87.167902). Here the exchange is checked on the actual pinned allowed words and separates two weightings with exactly the same ordinary value probabilities.

Put $r=2\cos(\pi k/5)$. After removing the same turn on both last bits, the first conditional vector is proportional to $(1,r)$ and the second to $(1,1)$ at $k=1$ or $(1,-1)$ at $k=2$. For the matched positive magnitudes the second vector is $(1,1)$ in both cases. Hence at $k=2$ the positive exchange chances are **$3/4-1/(2\sqrt5)$** for the phases and **$3/4+1/(2\sqrt5)$** for their positive magnitudes. At $k=1$ the two agree at the larger value. Conjugating both roots preserves each answer. The two full sets of conditional word probabilities, including symmetric and reversed word components of zero probability, are obtained in the independent script.

The probability of obtaining the two specified exteriors on the independently drawn full words is $1/242+\sqrt5/3025$ for $k=1$ and $1/242-\sqrt5/3025$ for $k=2$, identical for the matched moduli. All other exteriors are outside this condition. Each pair of incoherent inputs with the same ordinary probabilities gives positive answer $3/4$. A positive uniform pure preparation gives one with certainty, with joint selection probability $1/225$. In an ordinary word-wise exchange the unequal words trade places and the equal words stay. The probability of a change is $1/2$ in these not-all-equal preparations because the second bit is fair. That record alone has the same law for both phase choices at fixed magnitudes. Under parity the two pins occur with joint probability $1/256$ and force opposite bits. Their raw exchange violates both old rules and the coherent sign test on this sector is unavailable.

Keeping a common but unknown phase frame preserves this comparison. If an extra relative phase $\delta$ is physically inserted between the two independently prepared bits, the positive chance becomes $3/4\mathbin{\pm}\cos(\delta)/(2\sqrt5)$. An unknown independent calibration can imitate the original difference. No ordinary source, native cross-holder gate or phase record is inferred from sector legality. ## The seven questions

The original seven-setting script scanned two different relative signs in a sum of four three-reader correlations. Enumerating the 64 possible deterministic answer lists gives a bound of two for the relative minus and four for the all-plus expression. Both the positive preparation and the proposed ternary phases attain $7/2$ only on the bound-four expression. Both attain **3** on the bound-two expression in the same menu. Keeping just the ordinary value and unturned change settings fixed gives respectively **3** and **3/2** for the bound-two comparison. Adding phases changes individual measured tables but gives no larger maximum on this menu. The independent calculation sums the contributions of the actual six allowed words for each setting.
