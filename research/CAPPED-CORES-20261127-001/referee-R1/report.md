## Why the two certificates work

**Subset finish.** Work with all the births of one rank of a piece. If the same parent belongs to all \(m\) births, every pair shares and no family of three or more qualifies. The next births are therefore exactly the pairs. Give the original births distinct singleton labels. Suppose a later rank consists of the \(k\)-subsets, each having all its one-deletion subsets as parents, with \(2\le k<m\). Two distinct labels share a parent exactly when they are \(D\cup\{a\}\) and \(D\cup\{b\}\) for a common \((k-1)\)-set \(D\). Any third label sharing with both either contains \(D\), or is \((D-\{d\})\cup\{a,b\}\). A third label of the first kind that adds a new member cannot share with one of the second kind. Thus a pairwise-sharing family either has a common parent or lies in the deletion family of \(D\cup\{a,b\}\). That full deletion family has no common parent, is maximal, and covers the given pair. It gives precisely one child for each \((k+1)\)-subset. At size \(m\) there is a single birth and nothing follows. The additional count is \(\sum_{j=2}^m\binom mj=2^m-m-1\). The proof allows several parents shared by all \(m\) births. [Independent finite controls](controls.json)

The nine-knot `tree+` start drawn 66th has rank widths **13, 18, 25, 29, 33, 53, 146, 216, 162, 84**. I rebuilt all ten ranks from its shared and loose distinctions. All 84 births at rank 10 have 29 parents in common. There are 779 births through rank 10 and exactly \(2^{84}-85\) still to come, giving **19,342,813,113,834,066,795,299,510** non-start births in total. [Rebuilt certificate](comparison.json)

**Repeat.** A renaming that preserves the two roles, current birth and previous parent, preserves pairwise sharing, common parents, containment, maximality and uncovered pairs. It therefore sends each next birth to precisely the birth with the renamed parent set. Iteration proves periodicity from two isomorphic nonempty incidences. Distinct births with identical parents must remain distinct. Previous parents with no current children can be omitted. The implementation uses a hash to find candidates, then checks exact isomorphism with the two roles distinguished. Its output field `births per round` gives the width of the matching rank. A period can have several different widths. Equal widths or even equal sharing pairs alone do not suffice. Three births with one common parent and three births holding the parent sets 01, 12, 02 have the same sharing pairs and respectively produce three children and one. ## Why 127 starts never finish

At one rank, mark the pairs that share a parent with zeros and ones. Require an even sum on every mutually sharing triple and an odd sum around one closed walk. Here is why these two checks suffice for **every subsequent rank**.

For each next birth \(C\), choose a member \(a_C\) of its parent family. Set \(f_C(u)=w(a_C,u)\), taking \(w(u,u)=0\). Inside \(C\), the triple condition gives \(w(u,v)=f_C(u)+f_C(v)\) modulo two. If next births \(C,D\) share a member \(u\), mark their pair by \(f_C(u)+f_D(u)\). Choosing a different shared member \(v\) changes this mark by \(w(u,v)+w(u,v)=0\). For three pairwise-sharing next births, choose a shared member for each of their three pairs. Those three members pairwise share at the earlier rank, or coincide. The sum of the new marks equals the sum around that earlier triple, hence is even.

Every earlier sharing pair belongs to a next birth, either a maximal group or the uncovered pair itself. Around the odd closed walk, choose such a birth for each consecutive pair. Consecutive choices share the intervening member. Summing the new marks along this closed walk gives the old sum, since the two terms in each chosen birth add to the mark of its earlier pair. Consecutive identical choices contribute zero. An odd closed walk therefore survives in every rank. The core never finishes. A five-member cycle of constant width passes this test too, so width growth requires further evidence.

All **131 positive pieces** already have witnesses at rank 1, in **127 distinct starts**. The saved evidence lists the parent sets, marked pairs, an odd walk, and its transfer to rank 2. I checked every mutually sharing triple, independence of the chosen shared member for each transferred mark, and the transferred odd sum. The other 53 unresolved starts have no positive result in this bounded test. [Witnesses](comparison.json) · [Checker](certificates.py)

## A capped core with an exact growth constant

The twelfth seven-knot `tree+` draw has five knots on a cycle and two more sharing a distinction with the same cycle knot. All ten first-rank groups are pairs. The numbers of shared distinctions at the seven knots are \((4,1,2,1,2,2,2)\), and the group demands are \((2,3,2,3,2,4,4)\). Their sums give the drawn knot sizes exactly. Thus there are no surplus distinctions or offshoots. No two knots have two common neighbours. These facts verify all the hypotheses of the earlier cluster-identification theorem. Its parent-set induction applies to the pure node core at every rank. Here rank \(t\) counts connected sets of \(t+1\) points on the unrolled map, identifying translations by one period. The piece has **one** independent cycle under either the raw incidence count or the study's adjusted count. [Seeded redraw](redraw.json) · [Cluster-identification proof, Lemma 4](../../../proofs/RING-IDENT-20261119-001A/RING-IDENT-20261119-001A-P001_identification/proof.md) · [Exact counter](one_cycle.py)

One period visits the knot labels \(0,2,5,6,4\). Every block adjoining 5 and 6 also holds copies of 1 and 3. Any connected set meeting the recurring five labels contains a consecutive interval of them. Either additional point can be included independently at every such block meeting the interval. An interval has five possible starting labels up to translation. Extending it by a period multiplies its size-generating polynomial by \(z^5(1+z)^2\). Sets missing the recurring labels contribute just two singleton types and one pair type. Summing over the five initial lengths and five starting labels therefore gives a polynomial plus a positive numerator divided by \(1-z^5(1+z)^2\). Its smallest positive pole is simple and is the unique pole of that modulus, by strict triangle inequality away from the positive real axis. The consecutive-width ratio tends to the real solution \(\lambda>1\) of **\(\lambda^7=(\lambda+1)^2\)**. The exact interval count agrees with separate core enumeration through rank 17 and with all 30 widths saved by the study. The independent redraw reaches width **13,455 at rank 32**.
