# What a read or a change returns on one knot

With the supplied right to measure a change and the supplied squared-weight probabilities, one three-place not-all-equal knot gives three readers a table with no common explanation. The corresponding parity knot admits one. A previous study found a common explanation when two readers used different local questions on two split not-all-equal knots. The number of readers, the physical marks they hold and the questions they can ask all matter. ## 1. The rule and its observable consequences

With the positive coherent weighting on the ordinary allowed set $S$, take $f(x)=1_S(x)/\sqrt{|S|}$. A read $Z_i$ multiplies the ordinary word $x$ by $(-1)^{x_i}$. A change $X_i$ flips its $i$th distinction. Its recorded outcome is supplied by the same two-valued projection rule as the ordinary read. For independent freely chosen questions $O_1,\ldots,O_n$ at distinct readers, the chance of outcomes $a_i=\pm1$ is $\|\prod_i(I+a_iO_i)f/2^n\|^2$. Every free setting has weight $1/2$ per reader and every trial is counted. Distinctions outside the named readers are unread in their ordinary basis. The ordinary growth law supplies holdings and allowed words. It does not turn a flip into a question or give it these chances.

With three readers each holding one distinction, a common explanation is one random list $(x_1,z_1,x_2,z_2,x_3,z_3)$ prepared independently of all questions. The observed answer is the entry for the question actually asked. All 64 binary lists are admissible candidates. Matching their mixture means reproducing every probability, including zeros. For an infeasible table the receipt records integer coefficients of a separating inequality, its highest value over the 64 lists and the target's exact rational value. For a feasible table it records rational nonnegative weights and directly verifies every entry. Outcome bits are ordered little-endian by reader in the receipts, with 0 meaning plus. All one-reader marginals are given for each local choice and are independent of the other questions. ## 3. Why the laws differ

With the supplied squared-weight rule, the signed moment of change set $T$ and disjoint ordinary read set $U$ equals
$$
 \frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{x\in S,\,x+T\in S}(-1)^{U\cdot x}.
$$
Under not-all-equal the isolated three-place source consists of the six nonconstant ordinary words with equal positive signs. Complementing every mark permutes them, so $\langle XXX\rangle=1$. Flipping just the first mark connects two allowed words precisely when the other two marks differ. They contribute four of the six words and the ordinary signs on those two marks have product $-1$. Thus $\langle XZZ\rangle=-2/3$ on each of the three choices and $M=3$. A predetermined list gives $M=\mathrm{Re}\prod_i(x_i+\mathrm{i}z_i)=\pm2$, hence every common explanation has $|M|\le2$. The feature that matters is that a single flip can connect two allowed words only when the two observed ordinary values disagree, while flipping all three always connects two allowed words.

With all $n\ge3$ marks of a single not-all-equal holder separately accessible, the full tables still lack a common explanation even when this score falls within its bound. A common ordinary list lacks both constant words. When all marks but $i$ are read and their answers already contain both ordinary values, both values at $i$ occur with equal amplitude. Its change answer must then be plus. At most one mark in a nonconstant ordinary list can have all the other marks equal. Consequently a common list with at least two minus change answers would violate one of those zero-probability requirements. Reading all changes together has product plus, because complement preserves the weighting. The chance of the all-plus word under that question is $(2^n-2)/2^n$, so the chance of at least two minuses is $2/2^n>0$. The stored linear witness is the latter event minus the sum of the impossible events just used, including the ordinary constant word. Its bound is zero on every one of 64, 256 and 1,024 lists at the three tested widths.

With $p$ places and the real-part convention $M_p=\mathrm{Re}\langle\prod_i(X_i+\mathrm{i}Z_i)\rangle$, each deterministic factor has magnitude $\sqrt{2}$ and phase an odd multiple of $\pi/4$. The largest real part is $2^{\lfloor p/2\rfloor}$. At even $p$ we separately checked $\mathrm{Re}[(1-\mathrm{i})\langle\prod_i(X_i+\mathrm{i}Z_i)\rangle]$, whose bound at four places is also four. For not-all-equal the all-change term is one. For a mixed word containing a positive even number of ordinary marks, subtracting the four excluded words $0,1,T,1+T$ from the signed sum gives $-4/(2^p-2)$. Here $1$ is the full all-one word. An all-ordinary word of even width has expectation $-2/(2^p-2)$. An odd number of ordinary marks has zero moment by complement symmetry. With three of $p>3$ marks read separately, the all-three-change term instead equals $(2^p-4)/(2^p-2)$, so $M_3=(2^p+8)/(2^p-2)$.

## 4 through 6. Surrounding holdings, growth and separation

With outside marks unread, [the four comparisons and proof](holders.md) characterize a central three-place not-all-equal knot at any finite size. [The fresh starts and fixed explosive controls](sampling.md) quantify each case under the frozen placements. With either law and any finite number of separated readers, suppose each written holder contains marks from at most one reader. Fix all unread ordinary distinctions $h$. Each rule now constrains at most one reader's local word; rules on unread marks alone can remove the entire value $h$. At a surviving value the local allowed set is a product $S_1(h)\times\cdots\times S_n(h)$, and the positive weighting factors. Forgetting $h$ gives a mixture of positive product preparations with weights $\prod_i|S_i(h)|/|S|$. First draw $h$, then draw the outcomes of each reader's possible whole questions from its own local conditional distribution. The resulting single common explanation works simultaneously at all settings, including arbitrary local measurements. A closed chain of unread constraints only changes the distribution of $h$. If relative signs jointly depend on different readers the stated positive preparation assumption is lost. The three single-place readers of a single knot are jointly held and do not satisfy the separation condition. ## Complete classification on trees with one meeting per pair

With single-mark change reads, squared-weight probabilities and every outside distinction unread, **on a tree of knots meeting just once per neighboring pair, a three-place not-all-equal knot admits a common explanation exactly when all three of its distinctions are shared and its three neighboring arities are $(3,3,3)$ or $(3,3,4)$, in any order**. An isolated knot also fits the rule and violates. Further branch lengths and arities do not affect that answer.

To see this, remove the central knot. A branch attached through a single distinction has equal numbers of completions for either value, by complementing all its names. At its immediate neighboring $q$-place knot, the other $q-1$ marks lead to independent further branches, each giving equal counts for either value. Thus the common-completion factor for changing that one central bit is exactly $\lambda_q=(2^{q-1}-2)/(2^{q-1}-1)$. A loose mark has $\lambda=1$. Every central minority position has mass $1/3$. The one- and two-change moments are respectively $2\lambda_i/3$ and $2\lambda_i\lambda_j/3$. The three-change moment is $\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3$. It follows that each two-plus or three-plus probability increases with every overlap that it involves. The [four comparisons](holders.md#four-comparisons-from-the-physical-holdings) therefore reduce the infinite set of arities to five cases. With $(3,3,3)$ and $(3,3,4)$ all four excesses are negative, the largest being $-13/216$ and $-19/1512$ respectively. With $(3,3,5)$, $(3,4,4)$ and $(3,3,\text{loose})$ the three-plus excesses alone are $7/1080$, $145/3528$ and $5/216$. Every other arity pattern dominates a violating case after reordering. This argument includes knots with more than one loose distinction. The conclusion by nearest arities uses the unique branch decomposition of this class.

Direct enumeration of each of the five cases, each with and without one deeper attachment, checked all 64 common-completion counts up to scale. The 354 physical ternaries in the fresh tree sources also agreed entry by entry with the formula and with the whole-table locality verdict. The $(3,3,5)$ knot scores only $52/27$ although its full table violates.
