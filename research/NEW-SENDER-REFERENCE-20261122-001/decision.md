# Can a new sender recognize its own reference?

Yes for an exchange relative to its own prior values. Ordinary growth can give a newborn an unordered pair of unlike private marks, and the sender can know a requested transposition will reverse both payments. The first internally observable new coholders appear at the funding event that spends both marks. Their anonymous identities do not by themselves name the enrolled target. On the tested unprepared launches a fixed later knot cannot decode hold against exchange with better than even odds, even though the action really changes its payment. ## Final report as sent

Recorded 2026-11-22.

**Ordinary growth can give a sender a relative reference under both laws.** A newly grown knot with exactly two private marks can recognize when their values differ. Using the supplied exchange control, it knows both recipients will receive the opposite of what holding would have paid. Its local history still cannot tell it **which value will reach a particular recipient** before payment.
