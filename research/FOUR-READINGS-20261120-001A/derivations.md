# Derivations for signed holdings

The [results](results.md) state the model, the supplied readings and their denominators. These arguments are presented for independent review.

## A direct finite certificate for either operator verdict

List each shared distinction once, with both owners. The usual incidence matrix over the two-element field has rank n−1 on a connected sharing of n holders, since a sum of rows vanishes exactly for the empty or the full collection of holders. Indeed, a nontrivial division has at least one distinction counted only once. Summing the full system gives its total sign. If one rule is removed, every remaining row is independent and any assigned signs can be met. Choosing a spanning tree explicitly constructs a solution. An even number of changed rule signs can be moved away by changing the signs of shared distinctions along paths pairing up the changed holders. These arguments hold for repeated meetings as well.

For the operator calculation, choose a spanning tree. Each remaining distinction, together with the tree route between its two holders, specifies an independent loop. For distinction e let c(e) be the binary list of those loops that contain e. At any holder the sum of its lists is zero. Any assignment of binary Pauli labels with zero sum at every holder factors through these lists, since each bit of a Pauli label describes an even incidence pattern, and the chosen loops form a basis of those patterns.

Let M be an unknown alternating binary matrix on the loop indices. The commutator sign for two labels e,f is $(-1)^{c(e)^T M c(f)}$. Set this exponent to zero for every pair in one holder. Write out the product of all holder words, in a fixed order within holders and among holders. Each distinction appears twice. When occurrences of e and f alternate, cancelling the two copies of each distinction requires their commutator sign once. When they do not alternate, that pair contributes evenly. Thus the complete product exponent is the sum of the indicated entries $c(e)^T M c(f)$. Set this sum equal to the desired total sign bit. These are ordinary binary *linear* equations for the entries of M.

Every solution actually gives operators. On one qubit for each loop index i use $X_i$ for its generator, multiplied by $Z_j$ on earlier indices j with $M_{ji}=1$. Multiply the specified generators for each c(e) and choose a scalar power of i that makes the product Hermitian. The resulting involutions commute within each holder and have a scalar product there by the zero list sum. Their total sign is correct. Changing signs along tree routes transports the scalar products to the prescribed holders. The numerical witness stores each product as $(p,x,z)$, meaning $i^p X^x Z^z$. Direct multiplication and all local commutators are checked after construction, including a deliberately corrupted sign which fails. Each census positive has a nonzero X label on every distinction. More generally, on a connected sharing with no single distinction whose loss separates holders, each distinction belongs to a loop giving two incidences at each of its holders. Tensoring a reading with diagonal sign operators for uniform even scalar assignments then makes both signs occur at every distinction while preserving all holder products. The tensor dimensions used by the search are convenient, and no minimal quantum dimension is claimed.

Failure of the binary equations alone would exclude only this Pauli construction. On every negative type a second certificate deals with **arbitrary** Hermitian involutions. Use the cyclic order of the distinctions at each holder from the returned classical certificate. Its original factors commute, so this order is legal. A word equal to a scalar permits cyclic rotation even after merging words whose factors need not commute. Take a tree of holders. To merge two current equations linked by one tree distinction e, rotate one word until e is last and the other until e is first. Multiply them and cancel $e^2=I$. At the end, cyclically cancel adjacent equal pairs until the sole word is empty. The stored list of rotations, mergers and adjacent cancellations succeeds on every one of the 58 negative types, independently of matrix dimension. The left side is I and the product of the prescribed signs is −I. The graph-based routine need only find a candidate order. The succeeding word substitutions themselves certify the exclusion. The all-size classification remains the published theorem. The independent connected-group search enumerates all disjoint nonempty groups on any subset of at most seven knots. Within each group every knot must be reachable by shared distinctions. It explicitly checks every required pair of groups, trying each partition into two threes for six groups. Each positive stores the groups and required pair list. This operational test uses the holdings alone. The comparison with the theorem is a comparison of implementations and explicit certificates. A theorem and its equivalent statement in terms of forbidden patterns do not constitute two independent theorems.

## The six holders under free settings

There are three settings per side and nine joint choices. Let P be Alice's projector for one entire output, and let Q be the corresponding projector on Bob's operator list *before* transposition. On the state $\sum_{j=1}^4 |j,j\rangle/2$, the joint probability is $\operatorname{Tr}(PQ)/4$. Each local legal triple defines a rank-one projector. Each of its three commuting Pauli signs is included in that joint measurement. For a row and a column the only shared nontrivial Pauli product is their common distinction. The trace is zero on disagreement and 1/2 on agreement. The table measurement uses integer matrices for $8P$ and $8Q$ and retains all 576 traces. There are four local words satisfying each sign. Of their sixteen ordered pairs, exactly eight agree.

For uniform local supports in the eight-holder comparison, the same counts give 1/8 on meeting pairs. For independent settings with a disjoint pair, any combination of the four local words on either side is allowed. In both cases summing over one complete output gives exactly 1/4 for every allowed local word. The local support and agreement also permit a choice of nonuniform setting-dependent weights. Doubling on shared bit 1, for example, changes the six-knot complete-record marginals by total variation 1/6. The local rules alone therefore do not determine a probabilistic quantum reading.

## Why the eight-knot table has no quantum realization

The input roles have been fixed so that each shared distinction e is in exactly one Alice setting and one Bob setting. First suppose each local setting is measured projectively on a pure preparation $\psi$. Output labels define local signs $A_e,B_e$. They square to I, signs of any one setting commute and multiply to that setting's prescribed sign on the support of the preparation. Perfect agreement implies $A_e\psi=B_e\psi$ because the squared norm of their difference is twice one minus their product expectation. In any Alice word acting on $\psi$ each last letter can be transferred to Bob, where it commutes with every remaining Alice letter. The word therefore transfers to the reversed Bob word. On the closed linear span K of all these words, a relation in Alice's letters which holds on $\psi$ holds throughout K: test it after every Bob word. Consequently the Bob holder's commutation and product relations transfer to the Alice representatives on K. All eight holders would have one operator reading.

This remains true for general quantum measurements. Purify the preparation. For a tensor-product realization, each party can use a single local enlargement with a separate ancillary register for each setting to realize all its measurements projectively, preserving all the two-party probabilities. There is also a direct argument when the two parties' measurement effects commute. The bit marginals of a joint measurement define self-adjoint contractions. Perfect agreement saturates the norm bound, so $A_e\psi=B_e\psi$ and $A_e^2\psi=B_e^2\psi=\psi$. The same reversal transfers all words, making K invariant for every bit marginal and giving $A_e^2=B_e^2=I$ there. Compress each context's joint measurement to K. Its bit marginals are now projections. Any joint measurement with projection marginals is their joint projection measurement: each positive joint effect lies beneath each corresponding marginal, and the effects sum to I. This forces commutation and gives their products. A forbidden full answer has zero effect on $\psi$, since its effect is positive with zero expectation. It commutes with the opposite party's words, so its compression vanishes on all of K. The signed products therefore hold on K for both parties. Transfer Bob's relations to Alice as before. These steps apply to a fixed preparation and fixed local measurements of the supplied settings.

Label the holders by the subsets of three labels, or binary integers from 0 to 7. Within $(0,1,3,2)$ in that order let $a_i$ be the operator between consecutive holders i and i+1 modulo four. Give the four holders $(4,5,7,6)$, in their corresponding order, the operators $b_i$ between consecutive ones. Write $c_i$ for the shared distinction between each matched pair of holders. The local signs on the two groups of four are $s_i,t_i$. Within each individual holder the three operators commute. Thus $c_i=s_i a_{i-1}a_i=t_i b_{i-1}b_i$. Multiplying in the *same order* $i=0,1,2,3$, all a's cancel and all b's cancel. This gives $\prod_i c_i=(\prod_i s_i)I=(\prod_i t_i)I$. Precisely one of the eight signs is odd. Contradiction. The nonzero cyclic space K contains the preparation itself. No assumption of commutation between different $c_i$ enters.

For the four-holder role control, give each ordered pair of distinct holder names a bit, coming from the complete list of twelve ordered pairs. Alice's u input reads the three bits whose first name is u, and Bob's v input the three whose last name is v. Require each of these eight local words to have its sign, with the same odd name for both readers. The 32 bit assignments satisfying these demands, each given equal weight, reconstruct all twelve frozen uniform tables exactly. Adding equal inputs and requiring full-word agreement instead identifies the two role copies of every local context. Perfect shared-bit agreement and word reversal then give a common operator for each unordered pair of holders. Directly, label the three distinctions among holders 0,1,2 as a,b,d. They commute pairwise, one pair at each of these three holders. The other distinction at each of those three even holders must be ab, ad, bd. Their product is I, while the fourth holder demands −I. This gives the separate synchronized obstruction. The unsynchronized twelve-table comparison alone is classical.

## Reaching a closed holding pattern

Wire each loose distinction of a connected source to one exterior holder for this calculation. At a specified ordinary holder u, any even assignment to its distinctions extends precisely when deleting u leaves the rest of the wired structure connected. One way to see this is to solve on every remaining connected component. A component without the exterior must receive an even number of the prescribed ones, since its remaining shared distinctions each occur twice. These conditions on all components reduce exactly to even total parity if there is only one component. In the connected original wiring, every component after deleting u touches u. If there are two or more, assigning one 1 into each of two components gives an even word violating the extra parity constraints. In a source with loose distinctions the exterior is unconstrained. Its component absorbs its residual, and, when all the demands at u are even, the remaining independent parity conditions are equivalent to the same connected-deletion test. This gives full K1–K3 for an old star of arity at least three.

If a connected ordinary round closes, every newborn needs at least one fresh distinction unless its inputs already have implied parity and its next demand vanishes. A relation involving only loose distinctions is a binary sum of old stars. To cancel every old shared distinction it must sum all stars or none. Thus the single closing birth consumes every loose distinction. Inverting that last birth gives a unique candidate old source for each specified final holder v. Its incident distinctions must have individually different other holders, because no ordinary parent supplies two to the same birth. The old source is connected if deleting v preserves connectedness of the closed pattern. Its old stars are lawful exactly when the pattern wired with v as exterior remains connected after deleting each old holder. The ordinary grouping rule then reduces to one condition on the set L of parents: if there are at least three, every pair has an earlier common holder and no one earlier holder meets them all. Each parent has one loose distinction, so the unique group is funded and consumes every opening. With precisely two parents there must be an earlier common holder, making the sole pair. A connected source with a single loose distinction forces that bit to zero, violating K2 of its own holder. These tests prove necessity and sufficiency even when earlier steps are unrestricted, since the successful one-step predecessor is itself an allowed start. They make no assertion about reachability from one fixed initial source.

For the smallest positive closed pattern overall, a connected-group certificate needs either ten intergroup meetings on at least five groups, or nine on at least six. With nine distinctions every meeting is needed between the six groups; no distinction is available inside a group or outside them. Connectedness leaves just three-against-three singletons. Puncturing it leaves all three parents jointly witnessed, preventing closure. Five mutually meeting holders attain ten distinctions, and deleting one makes a lawful predecessor: the four parents pairwise have earlier common holders, while no earlier holder meets all four. Re-signing any one knot after closure gives the desired contradiction.

When all closed holders have exactly three places, suppose v has parents a,b,c. The witnesses $w_{ab},w_{ac},w_{bc}$ must be distinct; a shared witness would meet all three. If c witnesses a,b, its three available distinctions meet a,b,v, and the witnesses for a,c and b,c must be b and a respectively. The resulting connected four-knot case has no operator reading. Otherwise all three witnesses are outside the three parents, and the seven knots retain just one unused place each at the three witnesses. They form a part which can replace one three-place knot while preserving the presence of an operator-positive pattern. In the literature's drawing, the six parents and witnesses alternate on a cycle, with v joined inside to the three parents. The three attachments are all accessible from the outside in either cyclic order, which permits any ordering of three attachments when replacing one three-place knot. Conversely the seven connected knots can be contracted to one, preserving the rest. This establishes equivalence of the two classical planarity verdicts, including when two attachments join each other or share an outside holder. The reduced three-place pattern needs at least six holders for the positive verdict, since five have fewer than nine distinctions. Restoring the seven adds six, giving a lower bound of twelve.

For the attaining example use $v=0$, parents 1,2,3, and witnesses 4,5,6 on the pairs (1,2), (2,3), (1,3), respectively. Join the witnesses respectively to 7,8,9. Each of 7,8,9 also meets 10 and 11. These are the eighteen distinctions. The outside part together with the contracted seven gives the six-knot positive pattern. The literal check deletes 0, certifies every old projected triple, and regrows the missing triple with zero fresh distinctions. The five-holder and the twelve-holder cases are reached after one step from built sources. If the final birth is specifically a two-place tie, suppressing it leaves an operator-equivalent pattern with one fewer holder and one fewer distinction. To attain ten total distinctions the only possibility would be splitting one meeting of the nine-distinction positive minimum. Its two parents have no earlier common holder. Splitting an arbitrary meeting of the five-knot all-pairs pattern instead leaves three earlier common holders, giving the stated six-holder and eleven-distinction minimum. The subset growth induction is in the [ordinary closure record](../BOND-CLOSURE-20261120-001/bond_radius.md). Three labels close to the eight-holder operator-negative case proved above. With four labels use the three singletons {1},{2},{3} as one side of the six connected groups. On the other side take (i) the empty subset, (ii) {12,13,23,123}, and (iii) all subsets containing 4. The strings 12,13,23,123 here denote subsets, so the middle family is connected. Each singleton shares a distinction with each of these three families. This same certificate sits in every larger subset structure. A single initial knot with four distinctions gives the four funded one-parent births and thereby the four-leaf start. With at most three starting distinctions and at least one loose, an initially lawful connected start satisfies $3n\leq 2m-1\leq5$, so has only one knot. A three-place starting knot reaches only the operator-negative eight-holder closure. This proves the initial-supply minimum.
