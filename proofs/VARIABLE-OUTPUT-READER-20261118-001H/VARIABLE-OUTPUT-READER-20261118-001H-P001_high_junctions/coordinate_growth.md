# All finite three-dimensional arrangements built by coordinate predecessors

**Theorem.** Start with a finite nonempty set \(S\) of triples of nonnegative integers that contains every coordinatewise predecessor of each of its members. At each position take one input from every immediate predecessor. Give each site one output in each increasing coordinate direction, with the following permitted change. Any site with three parents may omit one direction in which \(S\) has no next site. Choose these omissions independently, with no restriction on how many sites are affected or how close they are. The complete pair table clipped through bond distance five identifies every actual junction, state constraint and permitted next parent group against **every** reachable mixed-output rival. The rival may have no such coordinate description. The reader receives no coordinates, axes, output counts, loose flags or earlier tables.

Build in increasing coordinate sum. Two parents of a site have the predecessor lowered in both directions as a common earlier witness. For three parents, their only possible common neighbor in the ordinary unit grid is the new site itself. Every chosen input was kept because an omission is permitted only where that successor is absent. Thus the construction obeys the original serial law. Only the initial site has arity three. Arity four arises only at unary growth with three outputs. It remains to prove the short-shore condition of the [unrestricted-rival theorem](high_inverse.md). The coordinates below certify a property of this family and play no role in the reader.

Take any set \(U\) of three or more ordinary sites. In any nonempty fixed-\(z\) layer of \(U\), at least two distinctions in the \(x,y\) directions cross its ordinary boundary. If its least \(x\) and least \(y\) are positive, there is an incoming crossing in each direction. All required predecessors exist in \(S\), even when outside \(U\). If the least \(x\) is zero and the least \(y\) is positive, look along that layer's line \(x=0\). Both the incoming bit at its first member of \(U\) and the outgoing bit at its last cross, since every site on the coordinate plane \(x=0\) supplies all three outputs. The case with the minima reversed follows by exchange. If both minima are zero, there is a last occupied point on each of the lines \(x=0\) and \(y=0\). Its positive output along that line crosses. The two bits are distinct because their directions differ. Outputs at vacant positions of \(S\) count as loose bits wherever that output was supplied.

Four or more occupied layers already give at least eight crossings. Three occupied layers give six horizontal crossings, so any vertical crossing gives the required seventh. If there is no vertical crossing, no occupied vertical column can have \(x=0\) or \(y=0\). On such a column the last member of \(U\) would always supply a vertical output. Thus in all three layers the two horizontal coordinates are positive. A layer containing two or more sites then has at least three incoming horizontal crossings, one along each occupied row and each occupied column. If all three layers are singletons, each has its two incoming bits and still supplies at least one of the two horizontal outputs, giving at least nine crossings altogether.

Apply this argument in any of the three directions. If none has three occupied layers, \(U\) lies in the product of sets of at most two coordinates apiece. Relabel these levels inside the ordinary eight-site cube. Each physical meeting is a cube link, even if gaps between coordinate levels remove some links. Write \(t=|U|\). For \(t=3,4\), bipartiteness gives at most \(2t-4\) internal meetings. For \(5\le t\le8\), each excluded cube site has at least one neighbor in \(U\), because there are at most three excluded sites including itself. Thus at least \(8-t\) cube links cross. The cube degree sum \(3t\) gives at most \((3t-(8-t))/2=2t-4\) internal meetings, and the physical set can have no more. Its actual site degrees sum to at least \(4t-1\). This again leaves seven boundary bits. The threshold is attained in every such downset with at least three sites. Take the seed with two immediate successors, or, if only one direction is used, the first three positions in it. Two internal meetings among sites of degrees three, four and four leave seven boundary bits. This connected initial portion gives a bond because every remaining component can follow later outputs to the exterior.

The class contains arbitrarily many adjacent three-parent sites making two fresh distinctions. For example take all positions in \(\{0,1\}^2\times\{0,\dots,n\}\) up to the missing last corner \((1,1,n)\). At successive positions \((1,1,k)\), \(k>0\), one unused positive horizontal output may be omitted. The three remaining parents of the missing corner still supply a legal triple. With five successive omissions, the direct reader recovered all 31 junctions and 27 legal triples on 88 distinctions after an arbitrary test renaming. With six successive omissions it recovered all 35 junctions and 27 triples on 99 distinctions. The computations measured short bonds by enumerating shore assignments with an irrevocable crossing-count bound. The arguments above establish all heights, all finite downsets and all allowed omission patterns.
