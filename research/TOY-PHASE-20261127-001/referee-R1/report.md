3. **Narrowing — the square root and what an excess requires.** “Every operation on a knot's values that keeps a read is an involution, so the root does not exist among them; it exists only as a phase.” This applies to **permutations of the four states of the three-place knot fixing that answer pointwise**. On the eight words of a four-place parity knot, `F(a,b,c,h) = (a,1⊕c,b,1⊕h)` fixes a, exchanges the b and c reads with one answer reversed, and F² flips exactly b and c. All eight words pass. The example supplies F as a permutation. Each of the knot’s fixed translation masks still has order at most two. Its value reads still have an ordinary common assignment. Even on four words, exchanging the two reference outcomes allows an order-four permutation. If irreversible operations are included, resetting b to zero while fixing a is idempotent on the three-place knot. Any square root of a permutation would itself have to be bijective. This refutes “a phase of order four needs states that do not give every distinction a value” as an inference from permutation order. Also distinguish the two qubit operations. XY = iZ squares to −I, but conjugation by it sends X to −X and has order two on observables. The phase gate S sends X to Y and squares to Z. The two order-four statements describe different operations. A second toy bit likewise permits an order-four permutation keeping the reference answer fixed, as the independent one-reader table shows.
