# Where a stored state can change growth

A five-ring loaded at the source can change the very first births. If the same five original distinctions are first loaded after one funded round, all sixteen rules give exactly the same future growth as their respective quiet controls. Moreover, in the two-ring starts, changing one ring's load never changes the growth whose source ancestry lies wholly in the other ring, at any age reached in both runs. It can change the whole arrangement, the growth of mixed ancestry, and whether there is a next funded round. These statements use the frozen whole-parent activity bit, the minimum-weight extension of the relative state and the complete-round rule. [Definitions](definitions.md)

## The active layer

Write \(A\) for the live knots holding at least one distinction whose value differs from the transported reference. A proposed parent set \(H\) reads precisely whether \(H\cap A\ne\varnothing\). Every currently loose distinction is consumed exactly once in a funded round. If they all start at their reference values, every child receives zero relative input. Its unique minimum-weight extension has every fresh distinction at zero. All current parents are then spent, and every next live knot is cold. Induction proves this for every later completed round. A source ring contributes to **one layer of sensing**. Subsequent structural differences are consequences of that first action and of the fixed cold entry of the rule.

A stronger null holds on one incidence. Adding an allowed difference supported only on distinctions with two already-spent holders preserves every live parent's entire reading. No later birth can take any of these shared distinctions as an input. Couple the port assignments and lifts in the two runs. Their physical incidences, future live readings, complete-round availability and all bond balls agree at every reached age. This includes the two endpoints of the preceding switch: the identical ten-distinction pulse plus the ring on the first copy, or the same pulse plus the ring on the second. The pulse can itself make live knots hot. It cannot reveal which of those two old rings stores the additional state. A growth event inserted *during* the switch can instead meet occupied loose distinctions. Its ordering relative to a change of old values requires a separate law. Serializing an already determined funded roster supplies no such ordering. [Passing pulse and stored remainder](../MOTION-AT-EDDY-20261119-001/ring_state.md) · [Switch endpoints](../MOTION-LAW-20261119-001/exchange_proof.md)

The per-parent even-parity sum is always zero in relative values. A reader restricted to consumed loose values also sees only zero on these controls. By contrast, a four-distinction ordinary response through the two ring legs at the attachment turns on the private inputs of their one live child \(Q\), as well as two old shared distinctions. Its loose distinctions remain zero. Both the intact one-spare ring and its funded cut admit this exact preparation. Here \(A=\{Q\}\) on the standard first layer, giving the control one active layer of its own.

## Exact first-round counts

Let \(o_v\) be the old open count and \(\mathcal H\) the original multipart proposals. Write \(f_0,f_1\) for the cold and hot entries. A veto of \(p\) parents replaces one multi-parent birth by \(p\) unary births. Thus, relative to the same law on the same quiet source, the first change in the number of knots is

\[
\bigl({\bf1}_{f_1=V}-{\bf1}_{f_0=V}\bigr)
\sum_{\substack{H\in\mathcal H\\H\cap A\ne\varnothing}} (|H|-1).
\]

Every multipart proposal at the named sources is a pair, with one distinct witness for its two-step path. For one attached five-ring, exactly **eleven** pairs see its load. Five join two ring holders, one pair at each possible middle knot. Four join a ring holder and a background holder through the four-meeting attachment. Two more join the attachment itself and a background holder through either neighboring path knot. At ordinary knot-step radius one from the five loaded holders a hot–hot veto changes one nearby birth into two. A hot–cold veto leaves exactly one of its two unary births in this neighborhood, replacing the one multi-parent birth previously there. Hence cold P, hot V gives **eleven extra knots in the full round and five extra knots in this radius-one region**. The old knots remain fixed. After the first round, no still later knot can be directly adjacent to a spent original holder.

Fresh distinctions require a separate count. Let \(B\) be the ordinary graph on old parents whose surviving pairs will actually join. This is an auxiliary graph for the proof. There are \(o_v-\deg_B(v)\) unary children of \(v\), since exactly one input goes to each child. Each such child has next structural demand \(o_v-1\): its only potential partners are the other children of \(v\), and any three containing it share \(v\). A surviving pair child \(\{p,q\}\) has demand

\[
o_p+o_q-2-|N_B(p)\cap N_B(q)|.
\]

Indeed it has \(o_p-1\) other siblings at \(p\) and \(o_q-1\) at \(q\). For every \(r\) with both \(pr,qr\) present, two candidate pairs give one unheld three-parent group. Those replacements never overlap in a candidate pair. This lists all possibilities: a pairwise intersecting collection of distinct one- or two-member parent sets either has a common member or consists of the three pairs on three members. Apply the frozen action and floor to the resulting demand on the *actual* roster.

For entries P and V on these sources every \(o_v\ge2\). Put \(u_v=\max(2,o_v-1)\). The pair demand is at least \(\max(o_p-1,o_q-1)\ge1\), so summing the preceding formulas gives an exact fresh total

\[
 F(B)=\sum_v o_vu_v-\sum_{v:o_v=2}\deg_B(v)-3T(B),
\]

where \(T(B)\) counts triples with all three pair proposals present. On an isolated five-ring with \(k\) spares, veto removes all five pair proposals. Each of its \(5-k\) two-open holders accounts for two removed incidences. At an attached ring, four complete triples in the attachment's four-neighbor block are destroyed. The two two-open path partners at offsets two from the attachment add two removed incidences. Of the two old ring holders away from the attachment and its immediate neighbors, the \(2-k\) without a spare each add two. This gives the following full contrasts.

| Loaded preparation under cold P, hot V | Baseline isolated growth factor | Extra knots | Extra fresh distinctions |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Isolated five-ring, 0, 1, 2 spares respectively | 1, 1.134724…, 1.262775… | 5, 5, 5 | 10, 8, 6 |
| Same three rings attached to the path | Same respective ring-confined baseline factors | 11, 11, 11 | 18, 16, 14 |

The factors describe the *unaltered* growth as cluster size increases. The first veto response in knots is independent of those factors. The width response actually decreases as the factor increases, here accounted for exactly by the local open counts. With cold P and hot + the roster instead stays fixed. Each hot nonimplied child adds one fresh distinction, giving \(5+k\) on the isolated and \(11+k\) on the attached source. The next ordinary multipart roster has the same parent sets and funding in the two runs. Every extra distinction therefore makes exactly one extra unary knot in the second round. With cold P and hot −, every affected binary source child on these rings would have at least two fresh distinctions. It loses one below its next demand. The next all-cold ordinary round then stalls everywhere, including at remote holders with ample local funding. [Isolated and joined baseline rates](../EDDY-WORLD-20261119-001/theory_fate.md)

The age-one four-response control has an equally short calculation. The child \(Q\) has the two ring neighbors of the attachment as parents. Each used four original open distinctions, on the intact and cut sources alike. Its six other sibling incidences give two ordinary pairs and two triples. The triples come from the two background neighbors in the attachment block. A veto at \(Q\) therefore gives \(1+1+2+2=\mathbf{6}\) extra knots on **each** source. A plus at \(Q\) adds four fresh distinctions on an unchanged roster. The cut removed a different old meeting. Its surviving pulse is a valid four-distinction match. In the old shared part alone the cut is a tree, whose only even subset is empty by removing terminal knots. A loaded broken five-ring would be an inadmissible control.

On the original unmodified one-spare attachment, two occupied loose distinctions at each of the same five holders also reproduce their complete activity mask. All sixteen first-round geometries match the raw ring. This is a first-round control because the ten occupied loose inputs can be passed to new children. ## Two rings and the reach of a difference

At separations four and eight no original candidate pair or complete candidate triple in \(B\) meets both ring-holder sets. A pair has an old common witness. The three pairs of a complete candidate triple share one old witness as well: three different witnesses would give an excluded six-meeting cycle, and a witness in the triple would give an excluded three-meeting cycle. All possible common neighbors contributing to the demand of a particular pair lie in that single witness's block. Consequently one original pair's retention, action and fresh contribution can depend on at most one load. A unary child of \(v\) has demand \(o_v-1\) independent of the vetoes. Its multiplicity changes by one for each veto through \(v\). An old holder on one ring has no incident candidate touching the other; at a cold holder shared by both effects the changes in multiplicity add at a fixed cost. Thus **for all sixteen laws the two-load interaction in the first full knot count and the first full fresh count is exactly zero**, even at four steps. There can be two effects on the same cold holder at that separation.

**The remote pure growth agrees at every common completed age.** More generally, suppose the two preparations have the same activity decisions for every original proposal incident to any holder in a set \(S\), including its unary completions. All loose inputs are at the reference. Call a knot pure when all its original ancestors belong to \(S\). For the two-ring comparison take \(S\) to be the receiving ring, hold its load fixed, and change only the donor. The separation proves the hypothesis for either value of the receiver's own load.

Use comparison names formed recursively from the complete parent set for a multipart child, or from the parent and a sibling number for a unary child. The equal-valued loose distinctions permit a consistent enumeration at matched parents. These are proof coordinates. First, every original member of \(S\) has the same **entire roster of children** in the two presentations, including children with mixed ancestry. Now let \(X\) be a proposed pure child whose parents already have this property. Its birth and parent set agree. All candidates for a next group through \(X\) are siblings of \(X\) at one of its parents. Those siblings' complete parent sets are already matched. Their intersections decide every candidate, every joint common parent, and every possible extension of a group through \(X\), since any extending member must itself be a candidate of \(X\). This gives the same structural demand at \(X\), hence the same fresh count under its common action. After the first sensing layer every live group reads cold. The same surviving multipart roster through \(X\) and the same remaining slots at \(X\) therefore give its identical full roster of children. This proves the induction. It also couples physical input choices through the pure parents. The assertion about a mixed sibling is its existence and its parent set, which is all the induction needs; its own future can differ.

In general this argument compares funded nonclosing rounds. A sole birth consuming every loose distinction has a separate global closure exception. On the named doubles that exception cannot occur. At each extreme of the path the original knot has two open distinctions and one spare. With cold P or − its first ordinary pair has no ring parent, leaving one unary child with two outputs. A unary child of a two-open holder has just one other sibling, hence one structural partner, and the unary floor supplies two outputs again. Each end line continues through every funded round. With cold + every nonimplied child retains at least one spare above demand, and with cold V every input has a unary child. The two ends therefore ensure at least two births throughout every completed history. The pure-receiver equality holds on all its common completed layers. An earlier global stop in one history makes subsequent same-age comparisons unavailable.

There are two complementary bounds for mixed observations.

- At age \(t\), any two original ancestors of one knot are at ordinary source-knot distance at most \(2t\). Descend its parent links to each ancestor. Old-to-old ordinary distances stay fixed: any new knot between two different old parents on a route can be replaced by their earlier common holder at the same cost. Removing successive latest peaks returns the route to the source. Thus a knot with an exclusive source from each ring cannot occur before \(t=\lceil(d+2)/2\rceil\), irrespective of the action table. Equality at ages 3 and 5 in the comparator was proved for its particular unchanged growth. - A finite bound also applies to ordinary rooted incidence on common funded nonclosing stages. Within three old-knot steps of a live \(u\) one can enumerate all proposals containing it: candidate peers are at step two and their witnesses at step three. To compute the first count of a child through \(u\), each of its other parents \(p\) is at most two steps from \(u\). Enumerating the complete new rosters at those \(p\)'s by the same local test gives every prospective sibling and its parent set, sufficient for the child's next demand. Allow one more step to specify the boundary holder data. A radius-\(R\) ordinary neighborhood centred on a fixed original knot after one round is therefore determined by the earlier radius-\((R+6)\) neighborhood, including full incident counts, live status and relative readings. Indeed a new knot can enter this ball only through a parent already at radius at most \(R-1\), by the peak replacement above. Iteration gives the conservative source allowance \(R+6t\) for these closed inputs, up to equal-valued port renaming. This does not assert a bound on bond minima: their witnesses may use the whole attached presentation. A refusal to form a complete round is also a global test.

## Knot counts and distinction counts can change in opposite directions

The bare five-ring gives an exact sign reversal even as the **bond radius** grows on one fixed first layer. Use one original shared distinction as the root. Cold P leaves five binary children with two fresh distinctions each; hot V replaces those by ten unary children with two fresh distinctions each. The following numbers use the whole physical ball, then the union of its ordinary holders. On the P side let child \(j\) take \(a_{j-1},b_{j+1}\). On the V side give each \(a_i,b_i\) its own child. Give each child two fresh distinctions, joining each to the same auxiliary exterior for the cut calculation. The respective numbers of ordinary knots are 10 and 15. The distinction contrast at radii four, five and six is respectively \(+8,-2,+6\). At radius five the extra three holding knots occur alongside two fewer distinctions. At large radius a full-ball contrast can simply recover the total added width. Detecting a response specific to a ring's return or growth factor therefore requires a matched activity and funding comparison, original-mark readings at common ages, and a difference remaining after accounting for the physical proposals changed by the rule. The five additional knots one ordinary step from a stored state establish local state-to-growth sensitivity. They alone do not measure a bond-radius falloff, a change in an eddy's exponential factor, or a signal transmitted to the other eddy.

## What a lasting cold policy can do

These all-size observations concern funded open continuations with at least two births, so the sole implied closure is immaterial. They help separate an initial state-dependent change from the persistent effects of choosing a different cold entry.

- With **cold V** all later births are unary. A \(k\)-open knot has \(k\) children; for each such child \(g(k)=\max(2,k-1)\) fresh distinctions suffice and are assigned. Each child has only the other \(k-1\) siblings as structural candidates before veto, and the next actual round vetoes all of them. Along every lineage the open count therefore reaches two in bounded depth on a finite start. The eventual birth and fresh-distinction factors are both exactly two. The initial choice can change their coefficients. A source ring is unnecessary for that exponential factor.
- With **cold −**, at the first nonimplied child whose structural demand exceeds its floor, the rule supplies one distinction too few. The next all-cold round keeps every prospective multipart group and fails funding. In a tail that continues forever, every cold − choice must therefore have agreed with the cheapest P count.
- With **cold +**, after one completed all-cold round producing at least two children, every child has at least two outputs and at least one spare above its next demand. All subsequent rounds are funded and have at least one unary successor per current knot. If \(n_t\) denotes the number of knots in such a cohort, choose one unary child \(x_v\) and a different child \(y_v\) of each \(v\). The pair \(\{x_v,y_v\}\) must itself form a group next time: every sibling of \(x_v\) shares \(v\), so no unheld larger group can contain it. The resulting pairs are distinct for different \(v\). Together with the one unary successor per knot of the next cohort this gives \(n_{t+2}\ge n_{t+1}+n_t\). This lower bound is another consequence of the post-sensing law, independently of which old ring was active. The unaltered P rates need their own source hypotheses.
