# Exact small-stage bounds for later bond readings

With the full pair table and the live distinctions fixed individually, the least current width at which a later reading of **any order** can differ is **11**. The least marked junction count is **five** when repeated meetings are permitted and **six** when junctions meet at most once. The witnesses of width 11 attain both junction minima in their respective regimes. Their later pair readings remain matched at every age.

A later **pair** failure is a different threshold. The native census excludes immediate successor pair failures through **18** current distinctions, with arbitrary ordinary meeting multiplicities. Equation transport and the lifetime certificates below exclude even delayed pair failures through **15** distinctions. These claims include every possible ordinary-junction count at each width.

For readings through three marks, the static superset now excludes any future failure through **18** current distinctions, including failures at higher order. Every surviving marked permutation carries the entire equation span.

## What counts as a source and a failure

Use `J` for the number of ordinary junctions at the observed stage and `n` for its number of distinctions. The classical wired graph has `J+1` vertices including the outside holder. It stays connected after deletion of any one vertex and has at least three incidences at each ordinary junction. The outside has at least two incidences. Hence `J+1 ≤ floor((2n+1)/3)`. At a positive open stage the live junctions are exactly the births of its preceding complete round. They do not meet each other. Every live-to-older meeting carries one distinction. These conditions are necessary for the static search.

The native search tests reachability from an admissible start. Delete the live junctions and discard their fresh distinctions. This gives the sole candidate predecessor in an open stage. The checker tests its ordinary connectivity and the connectivity of every complement of an ordinary holder. It reconstructs all prior loose counts, exactly enumerates the maximal candidate groups and the uncovered pairs, and checks the extra one-parent births. The current live rows must have those parent sets. Their loose counts must be exactly the fresh counts from the cheapest-count rule, including lookahead. Because an open stage has at least two births, no one birth takes every prior loose distinction and the implied exception is unavailable. The remaining free multiplicities belong to old ordinary meetings, where every positive allocation within the stated width is covered. The independent Python checker also replays the literal predecessor of each residual allocation. Taking that predecessor as a start suffices for positive-age reachability.

The search enumerates every biconnected underlying simple support up to isomorphism, each eligible choice of outside holder, and every required positive multiplicity allocation. Counts of rooted allocations may include isomorphic presentations. Pair or triple preservation is exact, including absent minima. A name with identical holders to another always occurs with it in every bond. Permuting such names preserves the equations and fixes the live end when the names are free. The residual permutation search therefore normalizes the source names in each such class by requiring increasing images. This gives one representative per coset, even if the map sends members of a source holder class to different classes. Each representative is tested by exact GF(2) reduction of every transported junction row and independently by the image of every distinct bond support.

The distinction between the two outcome orders is exact. Each complete round preserves the projection of the allowed states onto all old distinctions. The equations supported entirely on old distinctions therefore keep the same span. A non-equation-preserving current map sends some old bond outside that span. The old bond stays inclusion-minimal because every proper subset of its support comprises old names, so it still attains its own size. Its image cannot attain that size in any later stage and may have no containing bond. This gives a later arbitrary-mark failure. An equation-preserving map fixing the growing end transports all equations at every later round. This argument does not assume that the minimum for an arbitrary fixed old mark set stays constant. ## Pair lifetime certificates and immediate failures

- **Two live junctions.** With no candidate their growth is independent, and each has one or two loose names. These produce fixed-cost attachments of cost one or two. With a candidate, the counts are `1,1` or `1,2`. The first case closes by the implied equation, replacing the outside by an ordinary holder. The second repeats the `1,2` renewal. Its two-terminal attachment has separating cost one at every age, witnessed by the first thin connection. The case `2,2` would consist of two offshoots of one earlier parent, which would hold exactly the only two prior loose names. Their pair is implied by the sum of prior rows and would be a proper subequation of that parent, violating admissibility. These cases give transport of every later pair under the old pair reading. [Earlier immediate-round reductions](../../experiments/GROWN-END-MEET-ONCE-20261119-001/GROWN-END-MEET-ONCE-20261119-001-E001_rounds/reach_bound.md#safe-cases-with-at-most-two-newborns)
- **Closing or independent attachments.** If one group consumes the whole live end with one distinction from each parent, it creates the implied closing row, leaving the same bond table. If the groups consist of disjoint pairs of one-parent offshoots sharing their parent, with every other live junction independent, each attachment retains its current cost one or two. Starting with two loose distinctions and at most one joining group gives a unary continuation. Its parent supplies exactly two children, so its only future candidate is the sibling, leaving one spare distinction and another unary continuation. Independent attachments have one ordinary pole each and share only the outside, so the two-terminal reductions apply to each attachment and preserve every pair reading.
- **Two fixed attachment poles.** If ordinary paths already present after the first round give the original terminal cut capacity, that capacity is retained at every age. Each path persists on ordinary shared names. A cheapest old cut extends by putting later births with the exterior, so new growth cannot raise the unmarked terminal capacity. A two-pole attachment whose unmarked cost stays constant preserves the old pair comparison. A mixed query has only one mark in the fixed interior and uses the old reading with one suitably chosen exterior minimum-cut mark. [Two-terminal substitution](../LATER-BOND-READING-20261119-001/boundary-proof.md)
- **Invariant boundary information.** For each cut of the fixed part, record the terminals on each side, their connectivity partition, and the number of components missing all terminals. Record the least cut size of every one- or two-name mark for each such state. If that complete table is preserved under a map, all future pair comparisons glue with the same minimum. The absence of a conditional minimum is part of the comparison. The certificate is sufficient and may leave maps undecided.
- **Closed equal-bundle centers.** Suppose a closed junction meets just two closed neighbors by `w` distinctions each and there are always `w` disjoint routes between the neighbors avoiding the junction. Its `2w` distinctions may be permuted freely in every later pair reading. With neighbors opposed, moving the middle junction across a cut exchanges its two bundles, leaving both shores connected and every other crossing intact. With neighbors together, a bond through a central distinction has the central singleton as its other shore. A same-bundle pair cannot beat that singleton because any other cut must also meet the `w` disjoint routes. The verifier uses persistent ordinary routes and the private unary continuations above. This closes all remaining width-15 cases.

## A smaller reached witness with a higher-order discrepancy

The classical graph has two high-arity junctions $H_0,H_1$, $a$ ordinary junctions with unit counts, $s$ fixed junctions each meeting both $H_0,H_1$ by $M$ distinctions per meeting, and two test junctions $A,B$. Every fixed and test junction meets every unit-count junction by one distinction. $A$ meets $H_0$ and $B$ meets $H_1$ by one distinction each. The permutation exchanges the two test junctions' incidence labels and fixes all other labels individually. For positive-age growth, replace one fixed unit distinction by two loose distinctions at its former holders. Their two independent renewals have a terminal separating cost of one at every age because each original holder has just one distinction to its renewal, and both renewals connect to the outside. The marked substitution formula carries all preserved readings through the replacement and fixes all its physical names. Literal support-minimal XORs check every pair and triple at both positive ages, including all missing bonds and all names of the growing end. The old quartet `{0,14,20,21}` and its image `{1,14,20,21}` read **no bond** and **24**. The same analysis with $a=2$, $s=5$, $M=7$ gives a smaller junction count, $(13,91)$ at observation. The fixed route junction used in the marked discrepancy, $X$, is different from the one used in the unit replacement. Mark every unit incidence of $X$, one of its distinctions to $H_0$, and the test distinction $H_0A$. Since the marks put both $X$ and $A$ opposite $H_0$, all the neighbors of $A$ are opposite $A$. Its shore cannot be connected. The image instead marks $H_1B$. The shore containing just $X,H_1$ gives a bond of size $sM+a+1$ through the image. Every containing bond must put $H_1$ with $X$, since otherwise $X$ is alone and both ends of $H_1B$ are on the other shore. Every fixed junction then contributes $M$ crossing distinctions to $H_0$ or $H_1$, and the other marked bundles contribute $a+1$. The exact discrepancy persists when growth stays on the complementary shore. A four-distinction allowed flip through $H_0,A$, a unit-count junction and $X$ also certifies that the image of the $H_0$ equation lies outside the equation span.

For any supplied order $r\ge3$, set $a=r+1$, choose $s\ge2r-1$, and choose $M$ with

\[
2(r-1)M+(a-2)r+s+3<sM.
\]

Only changed queries require a comparison. Each such query has at least one test mark. In the graph with $H_0,H_1$ identified, put all junctions touched at their fixed or test incidences by the query on one shore with an unmarked unit-count junction. Such a junction exists because there are at most $r$ marks. That shore is connected. At least one unselected fixed junction stays on the other shore and connects the rest of that shore to the identified junction. If the chosen shore has $q$ fixed and $p$ test junctions, the bond has size $2qM+(a-2)(q+p)+p+s+2$. Use $p\ge1$ and $q+p\le r$ to obtain the displayed bound. Any bond separating $H_0,H_1$ costs at least $sM$. A cheaper bond of the identified graph lifts with $H_0,H_1$ on the same shore, including a fixed junction connecting them. Thus the two graphs have exactly the same minima for all changed queries at the supplied order. The exchange of $A,B$ preserves the identified graph, so it preserves the full marked reading after the unit replacement as well. The discrepancy above uses $a+2=r+3$ old distinctions, with no bond versus $sM+a+1$.

For a smaller junction count, take just $a=r-1$ unit-count junctions. The previous shore works when at least one is untouched. Otherwise at most one spoke is marked. In the bipartite graph of marked unit incidences, choose a connected component whose fixed and test junctions are untouched by the marked spoke. Put its unit-count junctions on one shore with all touched fixed and test junctions outside that component. Keep the component's fixed and test junctions with $H_0,H_1$. A marked spoke outside the component then crosses. If the chosen shore has no fixed or test junction, add one untouched by the query. The chosen shore is connected, and at least one fixed junction connects the other shore. If no eligible component exists, there is one marked spoke and only one component, containing all $a$ unit-count junctions and its marked spoke's fixed or test junction. With at most $a$ marked unit incidences, connectivity forces just one fixed or test junction in the component. A changed query of this form marks the entire test junction, whose own support is a bond. This covers all changed queries. At most $r-1$ fixed junctions contribute $2M$ each, and all unit incidences and test spokes together contribute at most $a(s+2)+2$. Require $2(r-1)M+a(s+2)+2<sM$. The exposed mark then has order **$r+1$**.
