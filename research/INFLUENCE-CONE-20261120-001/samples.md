# One extra loose distinction on the sampled starts

Among 320 lawful matched draws, 72 funded the first layer in both arms
and the extra loose distinction rescued 14 funding stalls. In mutually
positive layers, every measured receiver difference occurs at or after
the first actual joint descendant of the original sender and receiver.
A matching birth can change earlier than the receiver's first joint
descendant with the added-input unary. Erasing all but exact two-parent
steps gives a different result. It puts two changed first births at
infinite separation even though both are witnessed through a ternary.

## What the score compares

At each formed layer the receiver's inclusive outputs are all births
with a parental path from its original marked holder. A parent type
keeps the multiset of recursive parent types, anchored by the shared
literal names of the source holders. Separate copies of a unary are
retained. This type comparison records a reproducible roster signature.
It can identify fewer distinctions than a coherent match of the whole
parent incidence, since repeated copies can have the same recursive
type. The data also preserve unmarked birth counts, parent and physical
arity distributions, fresh profiles, held positions and accumulated
distinctions in the receiver's written family. Old receiver loose counts
are recorded separately from that family's new births.

The primary distance uses a step between two live knots with a shared
witness. The alternate ruler allows a step only when the pair itself is
an eligible two-parent group. A pair covered by a ternary does not supply
such a step. Ordinary holder steps are recorded separately. All distances
are taken in both arms, with equality checked at source. Among the
1,321 live receiver pairs, 544 start at witnessed separation one, 135 at
two, 12 at three, two at four and 628 in a separate component. Only one
separation-two receiver has mutually positive layers. None at finite
separation above two has such a comparison. A stopped or capped
history cannot be used as an absence of an earlier local effect on a
continuing history.

## Incidence inclusion and the part outside sender ancestry

For each paired formed cohort, a constructive comparison fixes the
original holder correspondence and seeks matching births with exactly
the mapped immediate parents and at least the baseline fresh count.
A successful matching also checks an injection of actual distinction
incidences within the mapped holders, with original shared distinctions
fixed. The 72 paired first cohorts all admit this construction. At the
second cohort 26 admit it and 46 omit a necessary parent type. This
failure is independent of swapping indistinguishable unary copies.
The third cohort admits 22 successful constructions per law, allowing
actual terminal births in this count. Its missing-type count is 46
among the 68 paired parity cohorts with births and 50 among the 72
paired not-all-equal cohorts.

In `tree/4/01`, the baseline sender's lone unary child has a unary
offspring. The extra opening gives the sender two same-type unary
children. At the next layer neither has a unary offspring because
they spend a distinction on their actual joint child. This defeats
an inclusion preserving exact birth parent sets. The result concerns
ancestry-preserving inclusion of present supports.

Outside the actual inclusive descendants of the original sender, every
jointly positive comparison has equal priced parent-type rosters. The
underlying paired source histories number 72, 68 and 63 by successive
layers for parity and 72 at each layer for not-all-equal. Wherever an
exact incidence match is also constructed, it verifies no changed
matching fresh count and no extra birth outside the sender family.
Those exact positive denominators are 72, 22 and 17 for parity and
72, 26 and 22 for not-all-equal. These finite checks are conditioned on
the sampled loose-only edit and the observed continuing layers.
