# Finding a smallest static span of original factors

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

In the named degree-two calculus over \(\mathbb F_2\), each declaration has the form \(t_i=f_i g_i\). Both factors are affine in the original variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) and earlier names. Reduce the formal coefficients modulo two. A factor is *eligible* if its reduced expression uses only original variables. Write it as \(c+a\cdot x\), and call \(a\) its slope. The constants \(0,1\) cost no rank. A static span is a linear subspace \(U\subseteq\mathbb F_2^n\) containing at least one eligible slope from every supplied declaration. The choice of side may vary between declarations and is fixed across input assignments.

If every declaration has an eligible factor, a minimum such span of rank \(\rho\) and a choice for every declaration can be found deterministically in \(O(2^\rho\operatorname{poly}(L))\) bit operations for explicit input length \(L\). With a budget \(r\), the corresponding bound is \(O(2^r\operatorname{poly}(L))\), including rejection. The variable-budget decision problem is NP-complete even when the input includes a polynomial-size valid refutation of a minimally unsatisfiable strict ternary CNF, every declaration multiplies two distinct original coordinates, and each product is an actual pivot on a derivation of the final contradiction.

## Nearest known result

The optimization is **Rank Vertex Cover** on a represented binary matroid, with constants and forced singleton choices handled during extraction. Meesum, Panolan, Saurabh and Zehavi give this formulation and identify ordinary Vertex Cover as the identity-matrix instance. Banik and coauthors describe fixed-parameter tractability in the rank as folklore, including its extension to bounded-size sets. The local contribution is the exact extraction from the formal proof, a checkable choice of basis and orientations, and hardness on strict refutations with live named pivots. The product-name predecessor eliminated proof names sharing a single original factor up to complement. ## Exact extraction and search

If a gate has no eligible factor, no static span exists. Otherwise selecting one eligible factor per gate always gives some span. Its rank is the dimension of the span of the selected slopes. Conversely any covering space lets us select one of its eligible slopes at every gate. Their span lies inside the proposed space. Hence minimizing over orientations and minimizing over all subspaces give the same optimum, even when the basis vectors of a proposed space do not appear in the proof.

For the standard graph formulation, use one vertex per distinct nonzero eligible slope, represented by its column in the binary matrix. Drop gates with an eligible constant. A two-choice gate requires covering its pair, and a singleton or repeated slope forces that vertex. One may incorporate forced vertices first and pass to the quotient by their span, charging its rank. Any newly covered pair can then be removed. The vertices selected by a cover generate a covering span. Conversely the vertices lying in a covering span form a vertex cover of rank at most its dimension. Zero rank therefore means that every gate has an eligible constant. Rank at most one means either no gate remains after dropping those gates, or all remaining gates offer the same nonzero slope, possibly at different sides or with complemented constants.

For a budget \(r\), start at the zero subspace. At \(U\), find the first gate with no eligible slope in \(U\). If there is none, return \(U\). If the budget is already exhausted, this branch fails. Otherwise branch once for each available slope \(a\) at that gate and continue with \(U+\langle a\rangle\). For any covering extension \(W\supseteq U\), this gate has some eligible slope \(a\in W\), and the corresponding new subspace remains inside \(W\). Induction proves completeness. Every branch increases dimension by exactly one. Equal residuals modulo \(U\) produce the same child and may be merged. The unmerged binary tree has at most \(2^{r+1}-1\) nodes. A polynomial number of sparse Gaussian eliminations per node tests membership, extends the basis and records coefficients. Trying budgets \(0,1,\ldots\) through success adds a geometric sum, giving the two stated bounds. When every gate has an eligible side, the number of gates is also an upper bound on \(\rho\). No enumeration over all original subspaces is required.

The solver validates the entire supplied named proof, including freshness, reduced defining equations and the final empty line. A declaration-only entry point accepts pairs in the same sparse wire format. The result records independent original slopes as affine polynomials with constant zero, their originating gate indices, an orientation bit at every gate, the coordinates of its chosen slope in this basis, and its affine constant. A certificate can be checked in polynomial time by solving linear equations and comparing the chosen factors syntactically. A sparse implementation can work only with variable identifiers appearing in the factors. ## How factor count and orientation differ from rank

Let \(p,q,z_1,z_2,z_3\) be independent coordinate slopes. Offer
\[
 (z_1,p),\qquad(q,z_2),\qquad(z_3,p+q).
\]
Every orientation uses three distinct slopes. Choosing right, left, right is the unique rank-two orientation. Replacing just one of \(p,q,p+q\) by its private \(z_i\) leaves the other two independent and adds a third independent vector. Replacing two or three likewise has rank three. Always-left and always-right both return rank three. Adding constants to any displayed factor preserves these conclusions.

More generally an \(r\)-dimensional space has \(2^r-1\) distinct nonzero slopes. This upper bound on the number of different nonzero slopes a minimum-rank choice may need is sharp. For every nonzero vector \(u\) of an \(r\)-dimensional space offer \((u,z_u)\), where the \(z_u\) are independent modulo that space and each occurs just once. Every orientation uses \(2^r-1\) distinct slopes. If it chooses \(k\) private alternatives, deleting the corresponding \(k\) original slopes lowers the original span dimension by at most \(k\). The \(k\) independent private alternatives add \(k\) back. Taking every \(u\) achieves rank \(r\). At rank two there are also six possible nonconstant affine forms, allowing both choices of constant. All six can be forced syntactically by giving each of them an ineligible earlier-name alternative after a zero-rank setup declaration.

## Hardness on actual named refutations

For a finite simple graph \(G=(V,E)\), declare exactly one product \(t_i=x_u x_v\) per pair \(e_i=\{u,v\}\). Any covering space contains a set of coordinate vectors that covers every edge. Those coordinate vectors are independent, so their number is at most its dimension. Conversely the coordinate vectors of any vertex cover span a space of rank equal to its size that covers all declarations. Extra coordinates for the CNF construction do not affect this argument. Thus \(\rho=\tau(G)\), including graphs with isolated vertices or no edges.

For completeness, the strict 3-SAT source reduces directly to Vertex Cover. Use two adjacent literal vertices per variable and a clique of three occurrence vertices per clause. Connect each occurrence to the variable vertex of its *opposite* literal. An independent set of size \(n+m\) in the \(2n+3m\) vertices must choose one variable literal per pair and one occurrence per clause. The connecting edges enforce consistency, so this is equivalent to a satisfying assignment. Its complement is a vertex cover of size \(n+2m\). This proves hardness using the admitted strict 3-SAT baseline. Guessing orientations and checking rank, and checking any supplied proof, gives NP membership. [Strict 3-SAT baseline](../../EVIDENCE-001D/EVIDENCE-001D-P001_uniform_strict_3_cnf_sat_completeness_admission/proof.md)

We now realize the edge declarations on strict formulas and use them explicitly. Order the \(m\) graph edges and choose an endpoint order \(u,v\) for each. Require \(x_w=1\) for every nonisolated vertex, \(y_0=1\), and \(y_m=0\). For edge \(i=1,\ldots,m\) add two clauses with a fresh \(z_i\),
\[
 (\neg y_{i-1}\lor\neg x_u\lor z_i),\qquad
 (\neg z_i\lor\neg x_v\lor y_i).
\]
The variables \(y_j\) and \(z_i\) are original CNF variables. Replace each required unit \(L\) with four strict ternary clauses \(L\lor (\pm a)\lor(\pm b)\) over its own two private variables and all signs. These clauses yield the unit by three ordinary resolutions. Every pair of edge clauses, once its endpoints are true, propagates \(y_{i-1}\) to \(y_i\). This contradicts the terminal pin. With \(s\) incident vertices the source has \(4(s+2)+2m\) strict clauses.

It is minimally unsatisfiable. Deleting the first clause of link \(i\) lets \(y\) change from true to false at that link by setting \(z_i=0\). Deleting its second permits the same change with \(z_i=1\). Choose the other \(z_j\) consistently on their respective sides. To drop a vertex unit choose one incident link and set that vertex false. If it is the first endpoint, \(z_i=0\) permits the change. If it is the second, \(z_i=1\) permits it. Dropping either outer pin allows all \(y\)'s false or all true. In each case, when a single strict clause of a padded unit is deleted, the two private variables can falsify exactly that missing clause with the unit false. Every other clause is satisfied. The same padding works for the opposite units on one \(y\) when \(m=0\).

To see the role of the named factors in the proof, abbreviate the equation \(h=0\) by \([h]\) and write \(p=x_ux_v\), \(t=t_i\). From \([1+x_u]\), multiply by \(x_v\), then add \([1+x_v]\) and \([t+p]\) to derive \([1+t]\). Resolve the two link clauses on \(z_i\) and use the previously derived positive \(y_{i-1}\). This leaves
\[
 [x_u]\lor[x_v]\lor[1+y_i].
\]
Multiply the first selected atom by \(x_v\) and the second by \(x_u\). Contract the resulting copies of \([p]\), and add the defining equation to the retained \([p]\). The result is \([t]\lor[1+y_i]\). Cut it against \([1+t]\) on the two opposite atoms of the named variable to obtain \([1+y_i]\). After the last link use the final negative unit. All products have formal degree at most two, and all emitted deductions can be explicit ancestors of the contradiction. The formula, proof-node count and explicit encodings are polynomial in the graph input. Each output is a genuine pivot in this supplied proof. The same source also has a short ordinary resolution proof by applying the units to the links directly. ## Scope and validation

The rank measures the written, normalized declarations of one supplied proof. In particular a factor that still mentions an earlier name is ineligible even if that name is semantically constant. The search provides an upper bound on the work of finding a static certificate. The NP-completeness theorem concerns that certificate problem. Every source formula in the hardness construction has the same SAT answer and a known short proof.

The checker compares the search with explicit finite subspaces, enumerated orientations and original input assignments. It compares all 1,088 labeled coordinate graphs on four and five vertices with exhaustive vertex cover. Across 70 named refutations the accepted kernel validated 304 opposite-name cuts, and 2,340 single-clause deletions each had a satisfying witness. Reversing one certificate choice, corrupting a defining factor or supplying an illegal forward reference causes rejection. These V1 same-task comparisons support the uniform proof.
