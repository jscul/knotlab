# Independent checks on the sibling announcement and the birth record

## Pricing one named orientation

Each launch starts at one four-place knot. Its first four children have one paid and three fresh names apiece. Supply the positive uniform weighting on the complete allowed words, the two binary questions, a pre-settings announcement on the nine loose names of the other three children and, in the clean preparation, the four physical erasure tests. For definiteness the forbidden ordinary focal word is 000. The opposite orientation has the same costs by complement.

For each observed nine-bit word count the old completions with focal paid bit zero or one. Write these counts as \(a,b\). A sibling triple of zeros forces its payment to one, a triple of ones forces it to zero, and a mixed triple admits either. The original four bits must also satisfy not-all-equal. Enumerating all 512 words gives nine count pairs with multiplicities. In particular only the nine zeros have \((a,b)=(1,0)\). Eighteen words have \((2,1)\): two sibling triples of zeros and a mixed third triple. These are literal word counts, before their different probabilities are applied.

Let \(w\) be the branch’s unnormalized weight and \(j\) the same weight times its excess on the frozen inequality. When the paid names remain available, \(w=7(a+b)\) and \(2j=a-4b\). On the all-plus erasure outcome, \(w=7a^2+12ab+7b^2\) and \(2j=a^2+ab-4b^2\). To see the second formula, the amplitudes of 000 and 111 are \(b,a\), and each mixed ordinary word has amplitude \(a+b\). The mass for two or more minus change answers is \((a^2+ab+b^2)/2\). The excluded ordinary word contributes \(b^2\). The three disallowed mixed-setting events together contribute \(3b^2/2\). Direct projectors on all eight ordinary/change settings independently reproduce these formulas. The code also checks the zero deterministic bound on each of the 64 preassigned lists. A nonempty flag reaches the requested excess precisely when its accepted words contribute at least zero total \(28j-w\). The all-zero word has surplus 7 in both preparations. Every other word has a deficit. The smallest deficits are 49 retained and 31 clean, both at \((2,1)\). Thus no further whole word, or even half a word using one fair bit, can enter the deterministic optimum. Disregarding any one of the nine bits groups the sole positive word with a deficit that exceeds its surplus. The stated excess then becomes impossible for any flag.

For an independent random flag, the mass gained per unit deficit is largest on the eighteen \((2,1)\) words. Give the all-zero word full acceptance. Add a total of 1/7 of one of the eighteen words when the past is retained, or 7/31 in clean launches. To avoid preferring a label, accept each of the eighteen with probabilities 1/126 and 7/558 respectively. By exhausting the surplus on the highest ratio these flags attain the upper bound. This is a finite instance of the threshold argument in the literature’s most-powerful-test construction. The contribution here is the exact masses on a reached newborn, the clean launch cost and the distinction between literal deterministic and randomized announcements. There are \(14\cdot7^4=33{,}614\) full not-all-equal words. Retained acceptance weighs 7 or 10 in these units. Clean acceptance weighs 7 or 630/31 against \(16\cdot33{,}614\) original-launch units. The four primary optima are **1/4802**, **5/16807**, **1/76832** and **45/1190896**. The clean port itself has chance 25009/38416. Conditioning on that port produces 1/50018 and 45/775279. A deterministic flag retains the full 1/14 violation, while the optimum randomized flag reaches exactly 1/28. Raising the requirement back to 1/14 removes the benefit of randomization. Both parity preparations give zero for all these optima. The positive probabilities are exact for the stated preparations; they assign no prevalence to starts.

At the deterministic optimum, the announcement identifies the paid orientation on every accepted retained launch. On the optimum retained randomized flag its conditional chance is 9/10. The clean experiment erases the paid record. Measuring that record as well would change the preparation. The result prices a probability inequality under a supplied measurement. The growing knots furnish the names on which the rule acts.

One matched control exposes why the word restriction matters. On a clean launch, specify zeros on eight sibling names and ask the supplied change question on the ninth. Its plus outcome now combines the two focal amplitude lists with the same sign. This pays for the flag on **53/537824** original launches with excess **2/53**, exceeding the sharp randomized word-only rate. The minus outcome weighs 13/537824 and has excess −1/26. Tossing a fair coin on the same two ordinary words has identical acceptance probabilities on either individual sibling basis word, but on this preparation gives excess **1/44**, below the required 1/28. ## What physically reaches the second receiver

With ordinary grants on the four-place start, each of the four first children gets three fresh names. Each of the six pairs of children then pays a separate child receiving two fresh names. The next four three-parent children each receive one fresh name. The specific child paid by pairs 12, 13 and 23 therefore exists, pays again at the following birth, and has received three inputs from those pairs. At that stage the focal child's three original fresh names are already twice-held. Giving this new recipient the first inputs *to* its three parents would add six old values to its actual three incoming payments. [Reconstruction from the ordinary operator](keeper_check.py)

There is an ordinary receipt at the earlier stage. The first focal child itself holds the paid name by shared identity along with its three loose names. A supplied value read of that receipt distinguishes its two values on equal numbers of launches. It leaves seven equally weighted focal words for each recorded value under the retained preparation. Both values paid exactly the same birth. Access to a twice-held name and a record of the birth therefore answer different questions.

Let the second child paid by first children i and j receive the bits \(x_{ij},x_{ji}\). To see why later loose names cannot certify the earlier paid bit, fix a lawful word on the entire focal first child, including its paid bit and all three of its fresh values. At each of its three payments to the next cut give the other parent the opposite bit. At the three remaining pair payments give opposite bits to the two parents so that each of the three nonfocal first children contributes one zero and one one around their three meetings. Each first child can now retain its own law and any original four-bit word compatible with the fixed focal bit. Each pair child has both values among its paid inputs, so any fresh word at it satisfies not-all-equal. This constructs all 98 combinations of original and focal words against every second-cut loose word. Holding one such loose word fixed preserves every permitted later suffix using only the new names.

The support obstruction also admits the two paid inputs at each of any two specified second children in addition to all later new names. If the two pairs are disjoint, give opposite bits around the four unobserved meetings, including both values at every first child. If the two observed pairs meet at a first child, use the remaining payment there to supply a missing value and make its counterpart opposite. Orient the three meetings among the other children so each has both values. The one-pair and no-pair claims follow by forgetting. Allow any measured pair's own eligible fresh word. The exhaustive check constructs a simultaneous assignment for all 265 choices of zero, one or two observed pairs and all of their possible old inputs. Adding the inputs of all three pairs incident to the focal child can expose a homogeneous focal triple: 111 certifies paid zero and 000 certifies paid one. Either observation identifies the entire focal word, which precludes keeping the earlier seven-word preparation. An arbitrary coherent future measurement or a reader with the original paid name is outside the ordinary future-value obstruction.

The actual child paid by the three sibling pairs provides a third way to test the observation limit. Give it even the six inputs formerly paid by those pairs, and allow every compatible newer value. At the three remaining pair births, let the focal first child contribute the three other original bits and have each counterpart contribute the opposite of its own original bit. The focal holding satisfies precisely the old not-all-equal condition. All three other first children contain both values. This extends all 14 original words at each of 64 six-input patterns. Each unobserved pair has opposite inputs, so it accepts every newer word. The actual recipient receives only one new name from each paying parent, then one fresh name of its own. On the first three-place parity source all 32 even six-bit words occur equally often, with their three paid values retained or erased. Changing one fresh bit from each of two children always changes two of the three paid values. Its mean sign is zero in the retained mixture. The eight possible erasure records each weigh 1/8. Four have mean sign +1, including the all-plus record, and four −1. Discard the erasure record and the mean returns to zero. The next three actual pair children each receive the parity of the two names their parents supply. Each of their four attainable joint fresh words has eight distinct earlier completions, within which every marked old value varies. An ordinary read of their full new record cannot recover the earlier ordinary settings as required for a faithful transport.

At fixed holdings, any birth or stochastic read whose condition is the realized ordinary word and whose additional randomness is independent must give the full positive pair the average of its two singleton output distributions. Counting every failure is part of that equality. Two singleton readings that return fair signs still return fair signs on the pair, including after a common successful selection with positive probability. A parity closing can give a zero grant recording the joint constraint on *all available inputs*. A rival join can remove some old words. Both depend on the presented relation or grant rule; they do not make a different birth on different realized words of that same presentation. This finite linearity argument is the familiar comparison of observations under a stochastic map. Here it applies to the actual paid births, and the support proofs above say how much of an earlier ordinary value even a later word-reading receiver can recover.
