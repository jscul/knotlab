## What the ordinary law already does

The whole growing end determines the next complete round up to naming. The input is the live knots, their loose counts and the sets of live knots met by a common knot. The counts fund the required joins and determine whether a birth takes **every** loose distinction, the closing test in a connected parity arrangement. On a reached open stage the live knots are exactly the latest births. Those births meet only their parents. Peeling them recovers the previous arrangement. Old incidences are retained, every earlier allowed state extends, and the old pair bond distances can stay the same or decrease. The result about the whole end supplies the baseline for asking what a bounded part of it can read. [Next-round lemma](../EXTEND-ROUND-20261119-001/extend_round.md) · [Peeling and old bond distances](../PARENT-RULE-20261119-001/parent_rule.md) · [Allowed-state extension](../STATE-FEEDS-GROWTH-20261119-001/selection.md)

In the identified class, a birth's layer is the size of its connected cluster in the fixed unrolled map minus one. A serial construction supplied that map reaches the same completed structure in every fair order. The two tested schedules that read only the partially built arrangement made less and could give different outcomes. Across the three joined preparations tested, each part kept its own counts by size and 97% to 99.9% of the knots of size ten or more spanned the join. [Construction without complete rounds and its limits](../ROUND-FREE-20261119-001/round_free.md)

Known fates include finite closing from a lone parity knot, continued growth with five births per layer from a bare five-ring, and \(17+2t\) births at layer \(t\) from the seventeen-knot path with four specified leftovers. One and two leftovers on the specified five-ring knots give limiting successive birth-count ratios of about 1.1347 and 1.2628. A built partnered start of order at least three has counts growing faster than exponentially. Large jumps alone leave fate undecided. One closing archive preparation had a single 974-parent birth, followed by \(304{,}590\) pair births from a held set of 781. Under the historical labelled tree-plus-extra-meetings weighting at starting sizes six through ten, 14 of the 690 canonically lawful draws had a recorded threefold jump. Among those fourteen, the later proof record establishes five end, three never end and six are undecided. ## Position, structure and values

The directed meeting trace followed a recognizable succession on newly formed knots. On the seventeen-knot path the inward trace gave distances to the two old ends of \((t,16-t)\) through layer eight and \((t,t)\) afterwards. Along a parent–child chain the ordinary meeting distance to any fixed old knot eventually increases by one per layer. This makes an old landmark a poor continuing measure of position. An occupied four-distinction response can meanwhile stay on its original identities through all later births by extending with zero on fresh distinctions. Transferring the response between occupied supports, and the \(2d+10\) handover between two prepared rings, used added value-update rules. ## What the four search records changed

The proof-based slow loops rewrote a presentation during a search for one allowed state. A proved signed tie lets one substitute a single representative and recover the old assignment uniquely. Adding a proved implied knot also preserves the allowed relation. The useful rewrites changed which distinctions and knots the fast search worked through.

| Search experiment | Result |
|---|---|
| Eager merging of proved ties and addition of implied knots | On the two local patterns at 3,200 distinctions, the eager policy used about 0.24–0.30 and 0.46–0.60 of the comparison's charged work after charging the rebuild sort. The policy transferred from random and block tuning yielded no frozen win. [Eager merge study](../SLOW-LOOP-20261117-001/decision.md) |
| Budget adjusted to the actual yield of proved merges | The held-out local primary used about 0.56–0.59 of the comparison's charged work after the referee's correction. The studied structure-poor patterns incurred about 3% overhead. [Earned-budget study](../EARN-BUDGET-20261117-001/decision.md) |
| Speculative ties with undo on stall | These trial restrictions could lose allowed states. None of the tested speculative schedules paid. [Speculative merge study](../SPEC-MERGE-20261117-001/decision.md) |
| A learned fast search under the proof-based slow loop | On the named local patterns the slow loop itself reduced 3,200 distinctions to about 100–200 classes. The learned fast search took 1.9–3.8 times the CPU of Novelty+ with the same slow loop. [Learned search study](../AGENT-LOOP-20261117-001/decision.md) |

This explains the earlier changing structure. Search used an added rule to replace the current presentation. Ordinary growth changes its own growing end by adding knots and spending loose distinctions, while retaining the old incidences and allowed-state projections. A birth taking hundreds of parents still takes separate distinctions from them. A terminal knot can have no child. Replacing an earlier presentation by tied representatives, deleting old equations, or ruling out an earlier allowed state would require changing the ordinary law, the kind of change reserved for rung 2. The fixed-map reading within the identified class adds a way to read those succession and descent relations by size.

Miguel A. Pizaña's 2004 theorem gives the ordinary graph distance between distinct maximal cliques in the classical clique graph as \(1+\min d\) between their member vertices. At iteration \(n\) the exact formula is \(n+\min d\) when their recursive source sets are disjoint. It explains why lifting and projecting paths is a natural comparison. The ordinary rule additionally excludes held groups, includes offshoots and reads loose counts. Its corresponding distance and formation claims need those checks. [Distances and diameters on iterated clique graphs, Discrete Applied Mathematics 141, 255–261](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0166-218X(03)00379-2)

The partnered recurrence adapts V. Neumann-Lara's 1978 classical example. Taking the clique graph of the graph on \(n\) opposite pairs with every nonopposite pair adjacent gives the same form on \(2^{n-1}\) opposite pairs. In partnered growth, the held sets are omitted from the otherwise admissible transversals, yielding the subtraction in the recurrence. The 1978 attribution and publication details appear in the author's later coauthored paper. [On clique-divergent graphs, cited as reference 9](https://xamanek.izt.uam.mx/map/papers/rdiv07x.pdf)

Bombelli, Lee, Meyer and Sorkin's causal-set proposal takes a locally finite partial order as its starting structure. Here birth and parent relations supply a descent order within the construction. The comparison concerns that order. [Space-time as a causal set, Physical Review Letters 59, 521–524 (1987)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.59.521)
