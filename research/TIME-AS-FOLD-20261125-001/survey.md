## 1. What "returns" means in the record, and what returns

- **The definition** (SD, lines 85–88): a part Y after round s, past its start, returns at round t > s when the members
  after round t that descend from Y (a waiting member descends from itself) include a part the same as Y; the other parts
  they form have come apart, and Y gives parts off; t − s is "the period". Sameness is a renaming of members carrying h,
  k and the maximal met sets (SD, lines 29–30). TD 2 adds what this study needs: a return needs a member made after round
  s; the series of returns follows successive first returns; a series is taken with and without "past its start"; the
  period is always in rounds.
- **The ring** ([the ring study's results](../RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001A/results.md), lines 9–15; computed, two
  engines and the pilot's programs agreeing; the argument for every odd ring at lines 54–76 is not refereed): under the
  two added rules (CAP-3, CANCEL-WAIT, RELEASE; SD, lines 69–70) a ring of an odd number of knots, five or more, comes
  back to its start's roster every two rounds while n two-knot parts come apart from it (P1, P2, lines 22–23); under the
  three added rules (with FILLERS) the parts given off end and the roster is in balance (P4, line 25). Waiting, release
  and offshoots are each needed (the swap table, lines 36–52). The ring at each return is made of the knots that waited,
  and half a period later of new knots ([the ring study's decision](../RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001/decision.md), lines
  31–33).
- **The parts that copy themselves** ([the copying study's decision](../COPYING-PARTS-20261123-001/decision.md), lines
  13–24): under rations that give a join one fresh distinction (FLOOR0, FLOOR1, ONE-FEWER) a two-knot part returns beside
  a copy of itself every two rounds, doubling (retire), as the golden ratio (offshoots) or as Pell's numbers (waiting).
  "The copies never meet again, so they cannot combine or read one another: the laws rule it out. Nothing reads the count
  of copies; the one who runs the growth counts them" (line 53).
- **What says "it has returned"**: the ring study wrote "The reader that says 'the ring has returned' is ours: nothing in
  the growth reads it" ([the ring study's decision](../RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001/decision.md), lines 77–78; its word,
  quoted). Every return in the record is found by a check the study wrote. This study calls its own the study's check
  that a return has happened, and keeps "reads" for one made pattern's course depending on another's being there (TD 0).
- **How many births lie between returns depends on a supplied order** ([the order study's
  results](../RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001C/results.md), lines 9–14 and 31–46): with the parts taking their rounds one at
  a time the same structure is built in every order (726 runs and an argument), and a knot's count of its own part's
  rounds is its round; but the births between returns grow under even-handed orders, stay at 4n under largest-first, and
  stay steady under the three added rules. "Births are counted by the one who runs the growth; nothing in the growth
  counts them" (lines 37–38). Inside a part the round is still supplied: one birth at a time jams the ring in every order
  (P1, line 21).
- **This survey (the control)**: with no taking rule the engine of the study of parts that meet again reproduces these returns on every registered start:
  the ring returns every two rounds giving n parts off on Ring(5,3), Ring(7,3), Ring(9,3), D2(4,3), D2(5,3), D2(6,3) and
  DW(5,3) under RING-LAW; on Ring(5,4), whose knots hold four loose distinctions, the start's own roster never returns,
  while its ring of five returns from round 2 every two rounds giving five parts off, as the ring study's P9 reports; and
  the copies double under COPY-LAW(RETIRE) (5, 10, 20, 40, 80 two-knot parts on Ring(5,3)).
- **This survey (the ring study's census, regenerated)**: the ring study's public code (`bref.census_starts` and
  `census_stars` in `experiments/RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001B/RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001B-E001_reference/bref.py`,
  imported read-only) enumerates 86,032 starts; 316 are set aside because a knot fails the parity knot test; under the
  control RING-LAW, 10 rounds, knot bound 1,500, the series taken with "past its start" give a tick that gives parts off
  on exactly 227 starts, the same 227 as the ring study's `p3_census_detail.json` (its law "two"); taken without "past its
  start", 437. Weighting where a share is given: each start once up to isomorphism (A238).

## 2. Can parts that return be met? What the study of parts that meet again found, and what it did not ask

- **The argument for the recorded laws** ([its
  pre-registration](../PARTS-MEET-AGAIN-20261124-001A/preregistration.md), lines 320–336, "Settled by construction",
  item 1; an argument, not refereed): under H1 (every distinction a birth takes is held by one of its parents) and H2
  (joinable only through a knot that meets both), two parts that are wholly apart after a round are never one part again
  and no birth has parents in both. Rules Q and R and the A225 move satisfy H1 ([its
  decision](../PARTS-MEET-AGAIN-20261124-001/decision.md), lines 17–22). So under them, and with no taking rule, a part's
  course never depends on a part wholly apart from it (item 4, lines 350–358), except through a round-wide trigger such
  as parity's IMPLIED closing: no part can read a made clock under them.
- **On the two objects, rules Q and R undo them from round 1**: the ring never returns and nothing copies itself,
  because the would-be parts stay joined ([its results](../PARTS-MEET-AGAIN-20261124-001A/results.md),
  P3, P4, lines 28–29).
- **Laws that reach further let parts meet again** (results, lines 60–81): MET (a knot the parent meets; two holder
  steps, no record), STEPS-1 and STEPS-2 (an ancestor one or two parent steps above the parent). 216 confirmed cases in
  which a part's course differs with another part barred (that study's "stopped"), through a meeting made by births
  (lines 85–91). What crosses is one bit, by crowding or by a joint birth (lines 142–144). No case for parts that came apart survives a change of its
  ration on its own start (lines 123–125); each depends on its choice (P13, line 37).
- **The cheapest laws** (P11, line 35, and the summary's P11 row): under the three-component reading of the cost, STEPS-1
  with OLDEST or IN-TURN under "just enough for three places" (FLOOR1), and MET with NEWEST or BY-COUNT under the cap of
  three; under the four-component reading also STEPS-2(BY-COUNT) and UP-TO-2(BY-COUNT) under FLOOR1 and STEPS-2(IN-TURN)
  under the cap. The frozen text did not say which reading. They are the cheapest laws with a confirmed case of meeting;
  nothing in that study ranked laws by whether anything returns under them.
- **What that study did not ask**: whether the ring or the copier still returns under the laws that reach further.
  Its run 1 and run 3 recorded the ring's own sameness test (its reading 27), not as a prediction. Taken from its frozen
  run files by this survey (`artifacts/PARTS-MEET-AGAIN-20261124-001A/.../run1.jsonl.gz` and `run3.jsonl.gz`, read
  only), under not-all-equal through round 8: the ring returns at rounds 2 and 4 only under RING-LAW with STEPS-2 (every
  choice on Ring(5,3), Ring(7,3) and D2(5,3); only at round 2 under BY-COUNT on the two rings), and never under MET,
  STEPS-1, UP-TO-2, Q or SHORT-TAKES from round 1; the two-knot parts appear only at round 2 under STEPS-2.

## 4. Records that bear on a count held by the structure

- **A knot's holdings give its round, sometimes** ([the ring study's decision](../RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001/decision.md),
  lines 41–42): to within three values on rings with a leftover distinction on every knot, more loosely elsewhere, not at
  all on a bare ring. The ring's own members at each return are the same form, so nothing in them counts returns (this
  survey: h = 3, two parents, three fresh at every return under RING-LAW).
- **The three-valued stamp** ([the least-record study's decision](../LEAST-RECORD-20261124-001/decision.md), lines 9–26):
  a knot's count of parent steps from the start, modulo 3, written by each new knot from its parents', lets any knot find
  its parents; it is written by hand (A247), "the order of descent cut down to three values" (lines 30–32). Whether the
  structure can hold such a count itself is sub-goal B328 (A228's history, [the register](../../docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md),
  line 5094).
- **The round read locally** ([the local-round study's decision](../LOCAL-ROUND-20261123-001/decision.md), lines 9–37):
  with a record of parents a knot that waits until its brood is weaned rebuilds the rounds in every order searched; the
  record is supplied (A242).

## 5. What this survey found that the record does not have

From the drafter's scratch prototype; every number is in [the pre-registration](../TIME-AS-FOLD-20261125-001A/preregistration.md),
"Known before the freeze", and in [its exact numbers](../TIME-AS-FOLD-20261125-001A/compare.json). A "clock" below is a
series of returns with two or more members and four ticks of equal period (TD 2.5); a one-member series is the
glossary's endless chain and is not a clock.

1. **Under the cheapest laws for meeting, nothing that gives parts off keeps returning.** Over 18 rounds on the twelve
   named starts, every treatment and choice, both knot laws: no clock under STEPS-1 with FLOOR1 (L1) or MET with the cap
   of three (L2) gives parts off at four ticks, with or without "past its start", and no series of returns gives parts
   off at two ticks or more. The same holds on the ring study's 227 starts under MET with RING-LAW, where the control has
   a clock giving parts off on every start. Under L1 there is a reason (item 2); under L2 it is only seen (item 3).
2. **The count under L1** (the advisor's argument, checked here): under the laws of the count (FLOOR1 with the fresh
   count by distinct inputs, release, no FILLERS or NO-OFFSHOOT, and a taking rule under which every offshoot of a made
   knot has a candidate), a birth other than an offshoot of a start knot gains no usable loose distinction (none of the
   births of 10 rounds on the twelve starts, under STEPS-1, MET, Q and UP-TO-2, both knot laws). So the usable loose
   distinctions held by a part's descendants rise only when a start knot acts, and a series has at most as many ticks
   that give parts off, or have two or more qualifying parts, as start knots acting. It rests on one scaffold: under the
   fresh count by parents, 107,803 of the 110,291 births under STEPS-1 gain one, and the census then has clocks giving
   parts off at four ticks under Q, STEPS-2 and UP-TO-2 with FLOOR1; L1's own verdict is then seen, not argued. The first draft's argument ("no part with a member holding two
   or three is made after round 1") held on the twelve starts but is false in general: under COPY-LAW(CANCEL-WAIT) a start
   knot that waits makes an offshoot holding two in round 2 on the ring study's start with atlas index 17 and loose
   counts (0, 1, 1, 2).
3. **Under MET with the ring's law the takes begin in round 1.** In round 1 the births are the control's in their parents
   and loose inputs but not in their fresh counts (on Ring(5,3) joins receive 2 where the control's receive 3, and
   offshoots 3 where they receive 2; on Ring(7,3) and Ring(9,3) every round-1 birth receives 3, and every member is short
   in round 2). From round 2 the would-be parts stay in one part with the ring, or the takers hold a different number or
   are short.
4. **The ladder under STEPS-k.** With RING-LAW the ring (a made clock with a period of 2 rounds): in no run does the
   start's roster return at round 2k + 2 after rounds 2 to 2k (k = 1 to 8, seven ring starts, four choices, both knot
   laws); for k ≥ 2 under OLDEST and IN-TURN it returns at exactly rounds 2, …, 2k; under NEWEST and BY-COUNT not always
   (a later return after a gap, or fewer); for k = 1 it never returns, since the parts given off take from round 2. The
   ring's own offshoots first take at round 2k + 1, when the start knots are exactly k parent steps above their parents.
   k is the law's.
5. **Laws in which only joins take keep the clocks.** Under MET's join clause alone (a rule chosen after the prototype's
   census) the ring and the copier keep giving parts off at four ticks under both rations, and so does STEPS-k for
   offshoots of joins under FLOOR1; STEPS-k for offshoots of offshoots (added by hand after the census) does under the
   cap of three for k = 2 and 3. MET's offshoot clause alone keeps none.
6. **The paired test** (TD 4: Y barred after its r-th tick, r = 1, 2, 3; X's own knots counted; a lag of up to two
   periods; the test with X barred): in no case tested does X make no further own knot after Y is barred, so claim C has
   no case in the prototype (every one of its 12,901 tests is decided as failed under TD 4.6). A case in which X's course
   depends on Y's being there at each of three returns, through a meeting (C-iii), appears on the two-knot start under
   STEPS-1 with OLDEST or IN-TURN and COPY-LAW(CANCEL-OFFSHOOT): the two three-member parts made at every odd round read
   each other, under one third of the numberings of that start, each numbering weighted once (80 of 240: those in which
   the shared start distinction is the least-named place of the start knot listed second). The first draft's near
   case, a part of four whose course depends on a one-member part, appears under 112 of the 240; its Y is an endless
   chain, so it is no case under TD. In each, X goes on making knots: under (CANCEL-OFFSHOOT, not-all-equal, no FILLERS)
   "makes no further own knot" is ruled out by the treatment. Over the rest of the census there is still no case of C, and C-iii appears in two more places, both outside the primary laws: under STEPS-2 with
   COPY-LAW(CANCEL-OFFSHOOT) on seven starts (three-member parts of period 3 rounds that each return only while the other
   is there; with BY-COUNT this is one of the fuller reading's cheapest laws), and under STEPS-2 for offshoots of joins
   with COPY-LAW(CANCEL-WAIT) on the two-knot start, where X reads Y and Y's series is unchanged with X barred, X also
   reads what Y gives off, and X's own knots grow each period, so there is no fixed ratio. The first does not survive the
   registered numberings (each case fails under at least one of the 4n shifts of the order of distinctions); the second
   is found under 112 of the 240 numberings and is undecided under the other 128, so it is not shown to survive.
7. **The knot test**: every knot at every round of the ladder (2,855,335 verdicts) and of the two-knot case's comparison
   runs at every round (13,118), both knot laws, passes; in the first draft (396,365 verdicts on 856 runs of 8 rounds)
   the only failures were the parity joins of twelve parents under MET(NEWEST) with the ring's law on the two-knot start,
   the case the P18 of the study of parts that meet again already reports. The two parity methods agree everywhere, and nothing is untested.

## 6. Words: how the record already uses the candidates

The coordinator relayed, while this survey was being written, the advisor's word decisions for the guide's chapter on
time (items 1, 2, 5, 6 and 7 of that review): clock and tick (supplied or made, always said; a tick is the event); return
and period (always with a unit); parent step and holder step; history; priority; "a pattern that reads the clock at each
return" until the number of returns can be recovered from what it made, then "counting pattern"; no "counter", "tally"
or "told". The advisor's cold review of the study's design then settled: **series of returns** (the first draft's
"chain of returns" clashed with the glossary's endless chain); **makes no further own knot**, with which of the three said (ended,
all members wait, closed), for "ceases"; **Y barred after its r-th tick**; **the test with X barred**; **MET's join clause alone**; **STEPS-k for offshoots
of offshoots**; and no "dormant", "keeps time with", "falls out of step" or "yet"; this survey also drops "depth" and
"window". Its second review added: **the laws of the count**, **kinds of birth treated differently**, **shifts of the
order of distinctions**, **cut short**, **unfinished**, and "the study of parts that meet again" for "the fold study"
(the owner's "fold" is another thing). The search below is what they settle.
