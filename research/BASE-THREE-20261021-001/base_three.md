# What appears at three?

Epic `BASE-THREE-20261021-001`, closed 2026-10-21.

## The question

The owner holds that reality is emergent from logic, and that the difference
between clauses of two and three literals is where it starts. The request had
three parts:
- treat that boundary as the base level;
- build out from its smallest blocks;
- challenge workspace assumptions where that gives a more interesting result,
  including cycles that act everywhere.

**The evidence:**
- [Transmission and the three regimes](../../proofs/BASE-THREE-20261021-001A/BASE-THREE-20261021-001A-P001_three_regimes/proof.md);
- [What the regimes carry](../../proofs/BASE-THREE-20261021-001B/BASE-THREE-20261021-001B-P001_regime_carriers/proof.md).

Both come with exhaustive checks over every law generated by one relation on
at most three bits (273 laws).

## My prediction was wrong, usefully

I predicted that knots and extension coincide: pairs give neither, and
triples give both. **That is false.**
- **Two of the 34 knots are frozen.** "Not all on" and "not all off" force
  values in one direction only, toward a vacuum. Two such knots sharing a
  distinction never compose: they are emergence without extension.
- **The other 32 transmit.** They compose into one larger knot, and their
  chains grow by one per atom.

The deciding property is not how many places a relation has, but which way
its knots can push a distinction.

## The base map (theorems)

| Regime | What it adds | Knots | Composition | Lasting memory | Local reasoning (with constants) | Hard | Small laws |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **pairs** | identity: loops | none | collapses to ties | no | decides (width 2) | no | 183 |
| **frozen** | isolated emergence | "not all on" / "not all off" | never | no | decides (width 1) | no | 2 |
| **forcing** | transmission: extension | two-signed, irreversible | grows | no | decides (width 1) | no | 32 |
| **conservation** | memory: hidden global facts | parity, reversible | grows like cycles | yes | fails at every bounded width | no | 2 |
| **choice** | search | both kinds | grows | yes | fails | with constants | 54 |

**The rules behind it:**
- **The transmission rule.** Two knots sharing one distinction compose into
  one knot on all their other distinctions exactly when they can force it to
  opposite values; otherwise they are a hub. This holds in all 10,404
  placements of three-variable knots. It generalizes resolution from clauses
  to every relation on bits.
- **One-sided knots.** A knot forces in one direction only exactly when it is
  "not all on" or "not all off". That is 2 of the 34 three-variable knots and
  2 of the 742 four-variable ones.
- **Memory and opacity.** A law keeps a lasting record of loops exactly when
  it defines a parity knot, exactly when all its polymorphisms are affine.
  With constants, that is exactly when no bounded level of local reasoning
  decides it.
- **Hardness.** Every NP-complete law transmits and keeps memory. Pairwise and
  frozen laws are always easy.
- **Memory plus anything irreversible is search.** Parity with an
  implication, a Horn clause, "not all on" or a disjunction is NP-complete
  with constants. Parity with equality or disequality stays easy.

## The underlying theory (interpretation, not theorem)

The theorems suggest one picture. It is offered as the owner's base-level
reading, kept separate from what is proved.

**1. Each step up adds one capability.**
- **Pairs** give identity. A cycle of implications is a loop: one open
  distinction carried by many variables, a recurrence in the thesis's
  original sense. Chains of pairs collapse, and nothing lies between things.
- **Frozen triples** give emergence that cannot travel. Each knot is
  satisfied by retreating to a vacuum, so it never passes anything on.
- **Forcing triples** ("A and B force c") give transmission. Knots chain into
  extended structure, but information flows one way, and every global fact
  stays readable locally.
- **Conservation triples** (parity: any two determine the third) give
  reversible transmission, and with it memory. A ring keeps its bit forever,
  and the world has facts that no bounded local view can see.
- **Choice** is what irreversible and reversible contact make together:
  memory with no law that computes it, which is search.

**2. Memory is reversibility, and reversibility is hiddenness.**
- **The ring theorem:** a loop leaves a lasting record only through
  permutation transfers, that is, reversible contact.
- **This epic:** such records exist exactly where local reasoning fails.
- **So a world can hide a global fact from its parts exactly when its
  contacts are reversible.** Irreversible contact forgets, and is
  transparent.

**3. Two kinds of universal cycle.** This answers the owner's "infinite cycles
that are universally influential".
- **By identity, in pairs.** A cycle of equalities shares one distinction:
  visible at every member, changeable at none. On an infinite cycle, no
  finite change flips it. It is a fixed fact everywhere.
- **By holonomy, in conservation triples.** A ring of parity knots keeps every
  distinction locally flippable, three changes at any length. Yet it holds one
  knot on all of them, visible to no part. On infinite networks it moves to
  the ends.

A world built from both has fixed facts that are seen everywhere and hidden
facts that are seen nowhere. No selection is needed for either; they are
simply what the cycles of that world are.

**4. Localization becomes literal.**
- **The owner's view:** "we are a localized state of a local system".
- **What that means here.** It has content only where facts hide from bounded
  views: in conservation and choice worlds. In pairwise, frozen and forcing
  worlds every global fact can be read locally, so being local loses nothing.

**5. Why three, and why bits.**
- **On bits,** "determined by pairs" (majority closure) is exactly
  "definable by clauses of two literals". So the two-versus-three boundary is
  exact there.
- **On three-valued distinctions** it is not. A hidden vertex adjacent to
  three visible ones, under colouring constraints, makes "not all different",
  a knot built from pairs alone (the Gallai-knots epic).
- **The threshold is three somewhere.** It is three places on bits, or three
  values on pairs. Two values and two places give only identity.
- **This bears on the thesis.** Its boundary is fundamental exactly when the
  base distinction is a bit. This is open for test, not proved.
