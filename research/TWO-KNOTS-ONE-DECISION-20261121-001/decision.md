## What “one decision” can mean

A setting names an actual holder and its three distinctions. On a fresh successful six-rule preparation, either reader can predict the agreed bit that the other reader will obtain if it later asks the corresponding question. It still faces two equally probable full answers at that holder. While idle, the other reader has a ready keeper and receives no new write. A subsequent question can write the predicted bit locally. The two carriers admit this correlation because their initial amplitudes are tied and because each shared name denotes the same physical change with its accompanying read sign. A single ordinary writable object serving both readers would be a different, central interaction. [Shared freeze](preregistration.md)

The literal central proposal draws uniformly among the words allowed at the holders currently asked. It gives both of the earlier perfect first-round tables. It also makes immediately repeated records stable. When one reader sweeps its own holders and the other holds a question fixed, the accumulating local record reveals information about that fixed choice. In six knots, the fixed question leaves 32 of the 64 possible sweep records. A different fixed question changes 16 of those 32. In eight knots it leaves 128 of 256 and changes 64. With two equally weighted candidate settings the observer can consequently guess the fixed remote setting with probability 3/4. Within the supplied coherent interaction, the complete local record has the same distribution for either choice. Its change of setting can instead disturb an earlier answer.
