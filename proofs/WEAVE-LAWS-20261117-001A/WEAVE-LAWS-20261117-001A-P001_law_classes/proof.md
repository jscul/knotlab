# Which knot laws are determined by their pair counts, and which are gauge copies of the triangular antiferromagnet

Status: Proved

Ticket `WEAVE-LAWS-20261117-001A`, 17 November 2026, by Claude. Epic `WEAVE-LAWS-20261117-001`. `check.py` checks L1 on all 34
three-place knots, L2's sign test on the study's laws, and L3 on small weaves. The proofs are below.

## Statements

- **L1. Only the not-all-equal class is determined by its pair counts.** (Worded "pairwise" until 17 November 2026; the
  glossary's "pairwise" means defined by two-place projections, which no knot is.) Write a three-place knot's allowed set as a function of ±1 values.
  - Its degree-three Fourier coefficient is the parity imbalance of its excluded set: excluded states of even weight minus those of
    odd weight.
  - Among the 34 knots, that imbalance is zero exactly for the 4 that exclude one complementary pair (corrected from 8 after the referee), the not-all-equal class with
    its literals.
  - So these are the only knots of degree at most two. Their bases are determined by the pair counts and N
    (`DISTANCE-ORDER-20261117-001A`, statement 3(a)).
  - Degree three does not by itself make two bases of another law collide. The census decides that.
- **L2. Consistently signed not-all-equal weaves are gauge copies of the triangular antiferromagnet.** Put on each up and down
  knot of the weave a knot excluding one complementary pair {p, p̄}. Read it as an Ising triangle whose bond between two places is
  antiferromagnetic when p agrees on them and ferromagnetic when it does not. The assignment is consistent when the up and down
  knots give each bond the same sign.
  - Then flipping a set of distinctions maps the base to the uniform not-all-equal weave: the Mattis and Toulouse gauge. On the
    torus this holds up to the two holonomy twists.
  - On the endless weave such a base is the zero-temperature triangular antiferromagnet, which is critical (Wannier; Stephenson;
    Blöte and Hilhorst; Nienhuis, Hilhorst and Blöte).
  - An inconsistent assignment is not covered. Neither statement says what it does.
- **L3. Two identifications.**
  - "Not exactly two ones" on every knot allows exactly the independent sets of the weave's co-knot graph (no two co-knotted
    distinctions both 1), together with the all-ones state. That is the hard hexagon model at activity one (Baxter 1980). Its
    critical activity is (11 + 5√5)/2 ≈ 11.09, so activity one is disordered, with states per distinction κ ≈ 1.39549.
  - Parity on every knot ties each of the weave's three classes to one value, so a torus with L divisible by 3 has 4 states and
    any other has 1. It is frozen.

## Proofs

**L1.** The degree-three coefficient of the allowed set's indicator, up to a factor, is Σ over allowed states of s_a s_b s_c. Over
all eight states that sum is 0. So it is minus the same sum over the excluded states, and each excluded state contributes +1 or −1
by its parity. The excluded sets are the nonempty sets of states with no two neighbours: 8 of size one, 16 of size two, 8 of size
three and 2 of size four. A set of size one, three or four has a nonzero imbalance:
- three non-neighbours share a parity;
- four non-neighbours are a whole parity class.
Two non-neighbours at distance two share a parity, and two at distance three have opposite parity. So the imbalance vanishes
exactly for a complementary pair. A complementary pair's set is closed under flipping all values, so its degree-one coefficients
vanish too, and its degree is two. ∎

**L2.** An Ising triangle excluding {p, p̄} forbids exactly the states in which all three bonds are violated (corrected from
"satisfied" after the referee), with the bond signs
read off p as stated, so it is a frustrated triangle. If the up and down knots give every bond the same sign, the base is the
zero-energy set of one Ising model with those bond signs and every triangle frustrated. On the endless weave, two sign patterns
with the same frustrated triangles differ by a gauge transformation, a set of distinctions flipped, since the weave's cycle space
is generated by its triangles (Toulouse 1977; Mattis 1976; Fradkin, Huberman and Shenker 1978). The uniform not-all-equal pattern
has every triangle frustrated, so the two coincide after a gauge. On the torus, the cycles around the torus add two holonomy bits.
∎

**L3.** Under "not exactly two", a knot with three ones forces each knot sharing two of its distinctions to have at least two
ones, hence three. On a connected weave that spreads to all ones. Otherwise every knot has at most one 1, which says exactly that
no two co-knotted distinctions are both 1: an independent set of the weave's graph, which is the triangular lattice in the
literature. Conversely every such set is allowed. Under parity, the two knots sharing a pair force the pair's two other
distinctions equal. Those are offsets (1, 1), (2, −1) and (−1, 2), which join each of the three classes i + 2j mod 3. So each class is
constant, and the knots then require the three classes' values to have even parity: 4 states when the classes are well defined (L
divisible by 3). Otherwise the classes merge and only the all-zero state remains. ∎
