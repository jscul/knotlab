## A sign determined before the close

The old three-parent ring has eight allowed words. Each parent leaves exactly one nonzero change invisible. It flips the one shared distinction that parent does not hold, and the loose distinctions of the other two parents. The rival rule signs the change by the reading of the first parent’s own loose distinction. Each earlier change reverses the sign read at each later step. With three parents there are three such reversals. The closing sign is minus for **all eight old states and all six orders**. On the old four-parent structure in which every pair meets, the corresponding symmetry-fixed choices close even. The rule has a measured limit. [Sign construction and controls](../../experiments/BIRTH-SIGN-WEIGHT-20261127-001A/BIRTH-SIGN-WEIGHT-20261127-001A-E001_closing_sign/result.md)

The rule fixes the relative signs of the eight words up to one common sign. For instance, two old words with opposite signs give the very same three loose values 000 at the birth. Any ordinary read of those values has the same answer for both. With a coherent change measurement, the signed state reproduces all 64 probabilities of the three-reader target and wins every scored round. The best common list wins at most three quarters, which the value reads on this fixture attain. On the old allowed words each proposed change affects two distinctions outside the reader’s pair. A different set of questions works entirely on that pair, but flipping either individual bit then leaves the old allowed words. Rephasing moves the sign between the state and the questions while preserving the 64 observable probabilities. [Full independent comparison](review/report.md)

## What joint births can produce

At a not-all-equal founder fixed at 001 before any settings are chosen, two siblings receive the same value. Each has exactly three allowed words. Giving equal weight to the symmetry classes of simultaneous completions gives the siblings dependent values while preserving every allowed completion. The event that both siblings’ two fresh values agree has probability 1/3. Independent siblings with those same individual probabilities give 1/4. A second, reinforcing rule gives each named word its ordinary one-birth probability of 1/3 and also yields dependence. An ordinary shared random draw reproduces the first joint table exactly. No knot joins the two readers on this fixed support. Across all six founder values two knots join them. [Joint weighting and canonical checks](../../experiments/BIRTH-SIGN-WEIGHT-20261127-001B/BIRTH-SIGN-WEIGHT-20261127-001B-E001_joint_weights/results.md)

The two rivals use only what the current round knows. A single such rule cannot always duplicate uniform weighting of all finished completions at two different stopping rounds. On one old state a first-round outcome has probability 1/3 if growth stops there and 1/2 after one more round is counted and forgotten. The independent check found the conflict on each of the 164 old states of a different archived start. [Independent recount](review/results.json)

## A two-reader test with no fitted measurement angle

When a proposed one-bit change would be forbidden, leave the word as it was. Those two local toggles, ordinary equality, and the uniform vector they preserve determine four candidate questions. The choice of which combinations to measure was made with the target in view. Their coefficients require no fitted angle. With positive square-root amplitudes for the symmetric joint weighting, the exact CHSH score is $(2+12\sqrt2)/9$. For two readers using a common list of answers to either question, each list scores either $+2$ or $-2$ in the four-term sum, so no average exceeds 2. This holds just as well for shared classical randomness. [Question definitions and all six controls](../../experiments/BIRTH-SIGN-WEIGHT-20261127-001C/BIRTH-SIGN-WEIGHT-20261127-001C-E001_local_questions/result.md)

The ordinary one-bit toggles have direct meanings as changes of words. Replacing the first reader’s coherent change question by either one gives 2, even with all the other coherent ingredients present. The independent search gave 2 even after admitting every signed involutive permutation of the three words as a possible local question. In contrast, the fixed combined measurement gives 2.1078403054, and the two readers’ local probabilities are independent of the remote setting. The final cross-check matched all 96 probabilities from six preparations to within $6\times10^{-16}$. The symbolic calculations give exact answers. [Symbolic tables](../../artifacts/BIRTH-SIGN-WEIGHT-20261127-001C/BIRTH-SIGN-WEIGHT-20261127-001C-E001_local_questions/results.json) · [Independent full-word tables](review/reader_results.json)

The strong comparison uses *identical named-word probabilities*. Erasing all coherence takes the score from 2.1078403054 to 0. Erasing it only between equal and mixed classes takes it to 2/9. The other tested weighting with ordinary one-birth marginals gives 1.939424 with the same questions. Changing probabilities alone, changing which possibilities can combine, and changing what a reader can measure have three separately checkable consequences.
