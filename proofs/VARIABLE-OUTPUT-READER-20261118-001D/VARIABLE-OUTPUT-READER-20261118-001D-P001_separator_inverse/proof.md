# Separating a branch and reading a marked triple from bond distances

**Standard statement.** Equip a finite binary parity presentation with the minimum cardinality minus one of a minimal nonempty dual support containing each coordinate pair. Under the serial law specified below, prove or refute that these scalars recover the components after deleting one coordinate. Then ask whether every scalar isometry of the remaining matrix with an unordered three-element marking preserves legal extension at those inputs.

**Nearest known result.** The previous study proved the forward attachment formula, tested its converse at widths nineteen and twenty, and gave a promising parity test on the retained part. Its common-prefix theorem excludes one kind of equal-state ambiguity. The present study proves the separator converse and the stronger equal-state invariance theorem for triples, and gives a counterexample to the retained parity test. Exact isometries of scalar tables at all finite sizes remain the open question. The older all-size inverse assumed three fresh distinctions at every join. [Previous comparison](../../../research/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001C/all_sizes.md) · [Fixed-count inverse](../../../research/BOND-NEXT-JOIN-20261117-001/next_joins.md)

Start from a single even-parity equation on three distinctions. A new junction uses one loose distinction from each of one, two or three distinct existing parents, and two or three fresh distinctions. Every pair of parents must have an earlier common ordinary neighbor. Three parents must have no common ordinary neighbor. A loose distinction has one junction, and an ordinary neighbor shares a distinction. These rules are supplied. Only the current scalar matrix and the proposed unordered triple are supplied to the reader. A genuine counterexample requires an isometry of every entry carrying those very marks from a lawful choice to a forbidden choice.

## Which branches to remove

**Theorem 1.** For every reachable finite presentation and every distinction \(t\), the connected components obtained by linking \(x,y\ne t\) whenever
\[
D(x,y)\ne D(x,t)+D(t,y)-1
\]
are exactly the components of the ordinary incidence after deleting \(t\).

The one-distinction attachment formula already excludes links between different physical sides. For the converse, consider the distinctions incident to one junction. A failure would split a single such group between scalar components. Short parity cuts force the split to have one exceptional bit, shared with a junction of arity three, and leave all other bits together at a three-parent junction. If that junction fed the exceptional bit to the trivalent one, the three earlier parent paths force more crossings than its proposed short cut can have. In the other direction the original three-parent rule provides a four-junction cycle. The other three sites carry a scalar connection around that cycle, connecting the exceptional bit to another bit of the allegedly split junction. Ordinary incidence paths avoiding \(t\) therefore stay in one scalar component. [Complete proof, including both orientations](separator.md) · [Direct cut tests](check_separator_lemmas.py)

The wired incidence has a cycle through any two of its edges. The elementary disjoint-path argument used in the state-relation proof also justifies this step of the separator proof. [Graph argument](inverse.md#the-part-supporting-the-three-marked-inputs)

**Pruning and eligibility.** Mark every \(t\) producing more than one component. Reject a triple containing such a \(t\). It is a shared bridge and cannot be a loose input. Also reject a triple whose members fall on different sides of any bridge. The three parents of a legal triple, with one witness for each pair, form a simple six-junction cycle. Its witnesses are distinct because any repeated witness for two pairs witnesses all three. Deleting one shared distinction cannot split these parents.

For every surviving triple, intersect its sides at all the cuts, retaining the boundary distinction of each retained side. Equivalently take the ordinary connected part containing the marks after severing the bridges, and keep each bit incident to a junction in that part. A cut leaving the part retains the bit as a locally loose boundary. Restrict the full matrix to these distinctions. Repeated application of the one-distinction formula shows that this equals the bond matrix remeasured in the retained incidence.

For a surviving mark its original looseness and its parent or parents are unchanged by this operation. Any common witness for two distinct parents in the part must also be in the part. If it lay across a bridge, the path within the part between the parents and the two links to the witness would give a cycle across that bridge. This applies as well to a witness common to all three. Hence local physical eligibility is equivalent to original eligibility for each surviving triple, in both directions. Removing branches before screening the marks would admit 3,101 formerly forbidden boundary choices on the control sources. [Boundary examples](controls.json)

## What equality of an entire allowed-state relation guarantees

**Theorem 2.** Every bijection preserving the entire allowed-state relation between any two reachable presentations carries the exact legal triple menu, at arbitrary finite width. The same is true for genuine retained parts when both proposed triples have survived the preceding screen. The only marks needed inside the retained part are those three candidates.

In the classical graph representation, wire loose ends to one extra vertex. The allowed assignments are exactly the even edge sets of this graph. The proof uses the alternating-count obstruction from the preceding study to show that deleting any two ordinary vertices keeps every remaining junction connected to the outside. On the ordinary block supporting a legal triple, the cycles then identify all incidences of a reduced graph with its attachments collapsed to indexed edges. A possible change of the outside vertex reduces to two complementary additions on the same prefix. Neither can supply the proposed legal triple. In the retained case the first local junction may have four distinctions. Any ambiguous complementary cap would leave at most three old loose distinctions, all required by the legal triple. Since it survived the boundary screen, the whole pre-cap region is a genuine original prefix. This yields the same contradiction. [Proof for arbitrary histories and arbitrary renaming](inverse.md)

Equality of scalar values gives only minimum sizes through pairs. The remaining issue is whether those values can come from two different allowed-state relations with different verdicts at the same marks.

## What the scalars can certify without reconstructing a history

All junction stars in a reachable source are minimal nonzero parity supports of size three through six. The same holds within a retained part. Its first local site has arity three or four and later sites have their original inputs and two or three fresh outputs. Every realizable dual space is generated by these short stars.

For a table \(M\) and \(3\le k\le6\), form every \(k\)-element set whose internal values are at most \(k-1\). These are candidates for size-\(k\) bonds. When a pair has value \(k-1\), at least one such set through the pair must actually be a bond. Keep a binary linear space \(L\) of equations forced by the table, initially zero. Make these deductions until they stop adding equations.

1. Discard a candidate as a possible bond if it properly contains a nonzero member of \(L\).
2. If a candidate \(S\) is a valid equation, every word in \(L+\langle S\rangle\) must split into disjoint bonds. To see this, choose a minimal nonempty word contained in a given word and subtract it over the binary field, leaving the disjoint remainder. Every bond has at least three members. Therefore a nonzero word of size at most six must itself be a bond or the union of two disjoint three-member bonds. Discard \(S\) if any required short word admits neither possibility in the table.
3. If only one candidate remains through a pair **at its observed minimum size**, that support is forced. Adjoin all such words to \(L\).

These deductions use no hidden incidence. At every step \(L\) is contained in the true space of *every* realization, and every true generating star is still among the candidates or already in \(L\). When no viable star lies outside \(L\), the table uniquely determines the entire parity space. Theorem 2 then decides every surviving triple on this observation, even if competing presentations grew in larger surrounding arrangements. One can read the verdict in any realizing incidence. For a promised table, enumerating possible presentations until one realizes the forced relation and marks is a finite termination argument. The actual past is never an input. [Table-only deductions and deliberate invalid-table control](check_scalar_span.py) · [Original and retained examples](scalar_span.json)

There is a second stopping criterion. The junction equations are independent because in a dependence the last participating site has fresh outputs untouched by earlier sites. A width-\(n\) original or retained presentation has rank at most \(1+\lfloor(n-3)/2\rfloor\), since its first site has at least three distinctions and every later one adds at least two. If \(L\) reaches this rank, it is already the full dual. If only one more independent equation could fit, every competing space is \(L\) or \(L+\langle S\rangle\) for one viable star. Such candidates can be checked directly against the complete matrix.

On all 2,167 legal-triple-bearing originals of widths 19 and 20 and all 2,167 corresponding retained parts, these deductions and the rank bound identify the full parity relation. Hence a competing retained presentation of one of these tables, even inside a larger source and with all boundary roles anonymous, must give the same verdict on surviving marks. The same deduction from short supports alone succeeds on the designated branch pairs at 27 and 28 and the two marks on the width-22 example below. [Population certificates and exact rank-one alternatives](span_controls.json)

The three-member bonds are identifiable at every size. Each is a trivalent ordinary star by the short-cut bound. Conversely, in the graph linking pairs at distance two, three mutually linked bits belong to one such star. With a private leaf for every loose bit, three pairwise incident bits either share an ordinary endpoint or close an ordinary triangle. Bipartiteness excludes the triangle and simplicity makes the common endpoint unique. Hence if an isometry loses any actual parity relation, one presentation must have a **four-, five- or six-member generating star** whose image is absent from the other dual. A pair minimum forced at that support, or a linear deduction forcing its parity, excludes that possibility.

There is an all-size reason why this **particular transposition**, acting as the identity on all future distinctions, can never exchange a legal and forbidden triple in a continuation of this source. Distinction 13 is already shared. While 14 remains loose at site 6, the only neighbors of 6 are 2, 3 and 8. Sites 2 and 3 have exhausted all their distinctions, and their other neighbors 0, 4 and 5 have also exhausted theirs. Any other parent with a loose input that can share a neighbor with 6 must therefore meet 8. Any two such parents would share that very neighbor with 6, which forbids their triple. Once 14 is used, both 13 and 14 are shared. Triples fixed by the transposition retain their verdict in each case. This argument only treats that permutation. Descendants may have different isometries.

A common-witness obstruction applies beyond these finite runs. Suppose a site \(u\) has just one loose distinction left and hold it unused. Its neighbor set \(N(u)\) is then fixed. On the *other* sites currently holding a loose bit, link two if they are ordinary neighbors or have a common ordinary neighbor, allowing the common neighbor to be exhausted. Every later site belongs to the component of its parents. Inductively, parents from different components cannot join because they have no common witness. Nor can a new site acquire a neighbor or a two-step connection in a different component at birth. Its only current neighbors are its parents, and a crossing of length two would require one of these parents already to neighbor a site of the other component. This induction includes future pairs of new sites. No exhausted original site can become a new parent.

An old possible co-parent of \(u\) must already share a witness with \(u\). A newly born possible co-parent must use a still-available member of the fixed set \(N(u)\) as a parent. Consequently a component with no available member of \(N(u)\) can supply only old candidates. Two such old candidates cannot gain their first common witness later. In a component with a single available member \(j\) of \(N(u)\), suppose all old possible co-parents in that component already meet \(j\). Then any two co-parents from the component share \(j\) with \(u\). They cannot complete a legal triple. If each component passes one of these tests and the held bit had no triple initially, it has none in any continuation. Once consumed it stays shared. This suffices for **18 of the 32** state-changing scalar transpositions in the width-20 controls when each acts as the identity on future bits. The other fourteen are outside this all-depth certificate. [Per-transposition checks](active_part.json)

Now suppose a loose distinction at \(u\) really does occur in a legal triple, and it can be swapped with a shared distinction from \(u\) to \(v\) while fixing every other label. Then \(v\) needs arity at least four. If \(v\) has any loose bits, its number of ordinary neighbors is at least two plus the number of loose bits at \(u\). The two extra crossings are forced by the other two marked parents and their distinct witnesses with \(u\). At arity four both sites must have introduced two fresh bits. This excludes more candidate symmetries at every size and leaves higher arity, as well as more general permutations, open. ### A mixed-count subclass with a uniform scalar inverse

Suppose at most one junction takes two fresh distinctions, and if it does, it takes at least two parents. Within this class the full table truncated after distance five recovers every star at every width. This includes histories using both fresh counts.

Indeed any set of four or more ordinary sites has at least seven boundary bits. Its latest four introduce at least eleven outputs altogether and consume at most four among themselves, since four bipartite vertices support at most four ordinary edges. For a set of exactly three sites the corresponding lower bound is six. Equality would require the unique two-fresh site, two internal edges and no input from earlier outside sites. The seed must belong to the three. Both other sites must have just one parent because only two internal edges are available. This contradicts the hypothesis on the two-fresh site. Thus every bond of size at most six is a star or the sum of two adjacent stars. An adjacent pair's cut is minimal, because no other vertex can send both of its outputs to two adjacent sites in the bipartite graph. After deleting the two sites all remaining sites can follow an output to the outside. Starting at size three, subtract the cross-pair links supplied by previously recognized adjacent stars, and take maximal cliques in the remaining threshold graph. Triangle-freeness makes these precisely the single stars of the current size or smaller. This is the short-star reconstruction proof with its cut bound established for the stated mixed class. This subclass theorem assumes both competing histories belong to it. The table certificates above can preclude unrestricted competitors without that promise. ### Moving loose ends to a new parity junction

One possible way for the table to lose an equation would be to move a proper selection of loose ends from the exterior to a new ordinary junction. Select one from each of \(k\) distinct holders on the same bipartition side. The proposed new star is a bond because the ordinary graph remains connected to the unselected loose ends. Keeping the full matrix requires every pair in the new star already to have bond distance at most \(k-1\).

If the ordinary meeting graph is a tree, the bond through loose bits at two different holders has minimum size equal to the boundary of the unique ordinary path between them. Every connected shore of a cut with the exterior on the other side has to contain that path. Its boundary size is two plus the sum of the wired degrees minus two over its vertices. Adding vertices increases the size, since every ordinary degree with the loose ends counted is at least three. The path itself is a bond because every component off the path contains an ordinary leaf with a loose end. For \(3\le k\le 6\), a path of four or more steps already gives size at least seven. Thus every pair of selected holders would have to be exactly two steps apart. In a tree three or more such holders have one common neighbor, of degree at least \(k\). Even the path through that neighbor and two holders then has boundary at least \(k+2\), a contradiction. At finite widths 17,302 selected subsets on the 849 targeted arrangements also fail the necessary pair bound. These include 3,501 selections meeting an input of a legal triple. The sample has no six-holder selection. [Inspector and direct cut remeasurements](check_outside_split.py) · [Exact finite cases](outside_split.json)

The control comparison uses every reachable incidence type at widths 19 and 20 and both presentations in each of the known width-27 and width-28 pairs. It includes 544,019 named single-distinction deletions. All the scalar partitions equalled the independent physical partitions. Across 13,090,150 surviving triple occurrences, 8,745 were legal. [Independent controls](check_controls.py) · [Counts and actual isometries](controls.json)

For any two old ordinary sites, acquiring a first common neighbor later is impossible. That new neighbor would have had to join on the pair, which already requires a common neighbor. Thus an originally forbidden triple on old distinctions stays forbidden in a continuation of that presentation. Shared distinctions stay shared, equal parents of loose marks stay equal until a mark is consumed, missing pair witnesses stay missing, and a common witness for all three persists. A first opposing verdict in a continuation of a triple-empty source must involve a newly introduced mark.

The controls exhaust their specified populations. The separator and allowed-state theorems quantify over all finite sizes. For the unrestricted scalar inverse, one must still exclude or realize different short parity generators hidden under the same pair minima inside a retained piece. A collision of retained matrices on opposite surviving marks would refute pruning as a complete reader. To refute the original scalar inverse as well, the full pre-pruning matrices must admit an isometry taking the legal marks to the forbidden ones. Conversely, every isometry of original tables respects the proved cut test and induces an isometry of the survivors' retained tables. At width 32 two different labeled state relations already have the same matrix inside a part with legal triples. The missing implication is that every such scalar isometry still carries the input parents and their three distinct pair witnesses in a way that preserves the verdict.
