# Hand check of the order count

## The sign and the two choices of order

A failing pair uses four distinct holders. In a total order, an alternating list of those four holders has one or three descents precisely when it follows their sorted cyclic order in one of its two directions. In that case its opposite positions give interleaving holder pairs. The other lists have two descents. There are 4, 16 and 4 labelled lists with one, two and three descents respectively. Thus each failing pair contributes its interleaving bit to the count of descents. The count of forward steps has the same parity since each list has four steps. [All 24 orders](record_check.json)

With every reference pointing from earlier to later, summing the forward steps gives the closing sign. Reversing one cycle changes four contribution bits. Reversing one cross-pair reference changes as many bits as its multiplicity, which is even by commutation. Arbitrary choices of both orders therefore preserve the total parity. A different total order on the knots merely chooses different cross-pair references. Two occurrences of the same underlying cycle cancel modulo two by choosing the same cyclic order for both.

Write mₛ for the multiplicity of a cross pair, Lₛ for its number of forward listings and F for the number of failing pairs. If Lₛ = mₛ/2 at each pair, or even if equality holds only modulo two, the sign is Σₛ mₛ/2 = 2F modulo two. A two-colouring supplies another proof of even sign. With each reference pointing from the first class, every cycle contributes exactly twice. At double coverage Lₛ is odd just when the two occurrences go opposite ways. There are 2F cross pairs, so counting the ones going the same way gives the same parity.

## Separation and attachment

At a knot v, use its cross-pair neighbours as labels. Each incident cycle connects its preceding and following neighbour at v. Retain repeated connections. With double coverage every neighbour occurs twice, so these connections partition into cycles uniquely. Split v into one copy per such cycle. The two occurrences of any shared cross pair retain the same pair of copies, one at either end. Therefore separation preserves the cross-pair occurrences, their relative directions, the closing sign and orientability. Reordering or renaming the copies has no effect. A component joined through shared cross pairs already commutes by itself, so minimality forces one component after separation.

In the literature's terminology, on each resulting closed surface the dual sum crossing every edge once is a mod-two cycle, since every face has degree four. Its pairing with the first Stiefel–Whitney class counts the orientation disagreements modulo two. The sum bounds exactly when a subset of primal vertices takes one end of each edge, which is two-colourability. On the projective plane its first mod-two homology has dimension one and the orientation character is nonzero. This gives the stated equivalence there on the separated pattern. Two knots with different colours can have been identified before separation. The constructed example in the fixture audit checks this possibility. [Mohar, Simonyi and Tardos, §§1, 6](https://www.renyi.hu/~simonyi/mstquadr.pdf) · [Separation controls](fixture_audit.json)

With higher even coverage, pair up the occurrences at each cross pair in any way and preserve the holder at each end. Each paired copy inherits the reference order of its original cross pair. Keep distinct copies even when their endpoints agree. The sum of forward listings remains exactly the same. There are 2F paired copies, so the parity is also the sum of the component parities in the literature's Lemma 6.1 after splitting at knots. The literature permits parallel edges here. This derives the all-even-coverage sign from the classical double count without selecting a preferred attachment.

The glossary's orientability of the original choice is equivalent to the **existence** of an orientable pairing of occurrences. Given balanced cycle orders, match each forward listing to a backward listing at its pair. Conversely any orientable pairing supplies those balanced orders before pairing. A particular pairing can fail this test. Under the three pairings of the unique fourfold cross pair, each of the two named odd witnesses gives separated Euler count 1 twice and 0 once. Four fresh failing pairs with the same cycle admit balance, but only 45 of their 81 pairings admit orientation. All 81 have even sign. These alternatives belong to the attachment convention. ## Forced merges

For a strict choice, let the two cycles of a forced merge be listed as (p,r,q,w) and (p,w,q,s). If r ≠ s, the five labels are distinct. The new cycle is (p,r,q,s). Only the cross pairs {p,w} and {q,w} disappear and both had multiplicity two. Active knots, cross pairs and failing pairs decrease by 1, 2 and 1. The Euler count and the property of double coverage are preserved. In any balanced orders the two old cycles have opposite traversals through w. Such orders correspond exactly to orders of the new cycle. Choose the two orders to agree with the replacement and cancel on the path through w. Each removed cross pair then contributes one forward listing and the sign is unchanged, whether or not a balanced choice exists. A two-colouring of the result gives p and q the same colour and extends by giving w the other colour. At p and q, one connection between neighbours is subdivided once. At r and s the connection is retained, and w has a single two-step cycle. Thus the one-cycle condition is preserved. For double coverage a shared merge of this form becomes forced after separation because at w its two occurrences already form a complete component.

If r = s, the earlier rule deletes both failing pairs. Their sign contributions cancel, but their removal can change the Euler count, coverage and the one-cycle condition. The closure grown from census start 3997 supplies a strict, odd, nonminimal example. At knot 3, two different failing pairs have the same holder lists and exhaust its marked values. Before deletion there are six active knots, nine cross pairs and five failing pairs. After deletion these counts are four, six and three. All remaining cross pairs are covered twice. A doubled pair itself commutes, so a minimal strict odd choice cannot contain it. ## The fixture families

For the literature's Klein grids, orient the lists consistently before making the reflected identification. The n cross pairs along that identification are exactly the same-order ones, giving sign n modulo two. With an untwisted identification all are opposite. For the literature's projective grids, checkerboard parity descends under the boundary identification (i,j) ↦ (N−i,N−j), which preserves i+j modulo two. For the literature's subdivided cube modulo the antipodal map, the connected covering pattern's two-colouring is coordinate sum modulo two. The antipodal map changes this sum by 3k modulo two, so the two-colouring descends exactly for even k. The projective-plane criterion above gives odd sign for odd k.
