# Which premises give certainty and fairness?

Supply a binary change question on a finite set S of allowed words. Choose a nonzero change t. The unordered pairs {w,w+t} partition the full binary carrier. A compatible observation of ordinary parities can identify a pair without distinguishing its two members. The probability of learning that pair comes from ordinary word counts. To recover a distribution before that observation, ignoring its report must preserve the relevant question statistics. These are operational assumptions.

**A full pair.** Suppose both words are allowed and a compatible observation has exposed their pair. If the conditional preparation answers as the fresh allowed set of those two words (P1), their interchange preserves the set. Certainty for a change preserving the set (P2) gives yes. If P2 is itself required for every conditional visible set, it already includes the conclusion for full pairs. Merely specifying a fresh-preparation rule as a function of S does not imply that exposing and then forgetting a pair preserves its change statistics. The previous rule that retained the original classes of globally interchangeable words illustrates the difference. [Earlier rival](../../../research/FREEDOM-CLOCKS-AND-BIRTHS-20261120-001A/counting.md).

**A singleton.** Fix an ordinary sign z on a coordinate toggled by t when the exposed pair contains just one allowed word. Let A be the answer sign. In a literal sequential reading, P3 makes the later ordinary sign −z on every trial. The mean of the product reported on those trials is then −z E[A]. If P4 lets the initially certain z factor from that same product even on its later side, the mean also equals z E[A]. Thus E[A]=0, and a binary answer is fair. Certainty of a value only at the instant at which it is read supplies no such second equality. If P4 applies only to compatible questions, the toggle and the certain read need not belong to its domain. In an ordered-product reading the same calculation instead uses the sign reversal on exchanging a toggle and its changed read, followed by two-sided factorization. That calculation supplies an algebra of questions and identifies its products with answer statistics. A translation inside that algebra and an actual post-measurement toggle are distinct hypotheses.

Full pairs then contribute one per allowed word to the mean change sign. Singletons contribute zero. Averaging with the ordinary pair masses gives the free fraction. A compatible ordinary read constant on the pair contributes its ordinary sign. With the supplied composition rule for the completed real questions, finite inversion gives the earlier probability tables. The argument assumes that compatible conditioning preserves the moments being averaged. The result concerns an unsigned uniform preparation, and includes signed answers without any signed input probability.

**The fork.** On allowed words the rule-keeping act exchanges a complete pair and fixes a singleton. It is a permutation that preserves the uniform weighting. An answer defined as its eigenvalue on that weighting is always plus for a single change. An answer mechanism supplied independently of this act can be fair on a singleton while keeping the actual word. Conversely an unconditional toggle can always report plus. Permission for an act to reach a forbidden word therefore neither forces nor characterizes the singleton calibration in the absence of the specific product and reporting assumptions. A complete compatible context can even be read with the earlier outcome effects restricted to the allowed-word subspace, then followed by a fresh allowed preparation. It has all the original one-context probabilities and has supplied a collective measurement and a reset. This construction makes no claim about a locally implemented act.

**Outcome state.** For the particular selective real projection of the unguarded change, take amplitudes proportional to 1_S(w) ± 1_S(w+t) after the corresponding answer. Normalizing their squares gives the ordinary word probabilities. Singletons produce two opposite endpoints of equal magnitude. A plus outcome also keeps the complete pairs, with amplitude two. On one not-all-equal ternary a single flip puts conditional mass 1/10 on forbidden words after plus and 1/2 after minus. Repeating the same sign measurement preserves that outcome and its forbidden mass. The reset just described shows why the effects alone do not fix a later preparation. [Exact independent control](../../../research/CALIBRATIONS-CLOCKS-AND-WIDER-KNOTS-20261120-001/pair_answer_replay.json).

There is also a local obstruction with no new shared resource. On the isolated not-all-equal ternary, a reader operating on one mark alone must preserve the original legal support for every allowed input if this branch requires that of its instrument. With the other two marks both zero, the local value one cannot be sent to zero. With the other two both one, the reverse change is also forbidden. Each local outcome map on the binary word space therefore has only diagonal entries. Its ordinary outcome probabilities have a common explanation by sampling one allowed word and then the local response at each mark. On the two split parity knots, the two marks held by the second reader are fixed by the designated parities of the first reader’s marks. Legal-support preservation forbids that second reader from taking a word to a different local word while the first reader has stayed put. Such local effects are diagonal in the common labels. Within each label the uniform preparation is a product across the two readers, so their remaining local responses also have a common explanation. This distinguishes local support preservation from a joint table-dependent instrument on the same legal states.
