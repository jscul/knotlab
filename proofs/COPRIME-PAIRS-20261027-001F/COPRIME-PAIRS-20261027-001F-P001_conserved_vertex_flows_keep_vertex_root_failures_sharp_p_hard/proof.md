# Conserved vertex flows: class-3 pair failures blocked only by vertex-root brackets

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

**Setting.** As in `COPRIME-PAIRS-20261027-001E`:
- p ≥ 5, r | p − 1, G = N ⋊ C_r with 𝔫 graded and the action grading-preserving;
- d | r, 𝔪 = 𝔪_d the ideal generated by the nonzero characters. Here 𝔪 has class ≤ 3, and the quotient option is 𝔨 = 0.
- Take a hard pair (α, β) with slots θ₀ = α (vertex), θ₁ = β, θ₂ = γ = α + β, root sets R_i = R(θ_i), and Λ_k(i) the characters
  of R_i at which V_k is nonzero.
- V₁(Λ) = ⊕_{c∈Λ} V_{1,c}. D_i, the determined layer-2 part of leaf i, is the layer-2 part modulo γ₃ of [V₁(Λ₁(i)), V₁(Λ₁(i))].

**Flow edges.** A **flow edge** satisfies every condition of Theorem 5 in E except (V₃), and in its place:
- **(F0)** Λ₂(0) = ∅: the vertex block has no free data at layer 2.
- **(F1)** For every nonzero Δ ∈ V₁(Λ₁(0)), the map y ↦ [Δ, y] from V₁(Λ₁(1)) to the components at R₂ of γ₂/γ₃ is injective, and so
  is y ↦ [y, Δ] from V₁(Λ₁(2)) to the components at R₁.
- **(F2)** [V₁(Λ₁(1)), V₁(Λ₁(2))], [D₁, V₁(Λ₁(2))] and [V₁(Λ₁(1)), D₂] have no component at a character of R₀.

(V₃) is (F2) together with: [V₂(Λ₂(1)), V₁(Λ₁(2))] and [V₁(Λ₁(1)), V₂(Λ₂(2))] have no component at R₀. Flow edges drop that second
part.

**Theorem 6.** If there is a flow edge at some d | r, counting |Hom(π₁ S³_{d/p}(K), G)| over knot diagrams K is #P-hard under
polynomial-time Turing reductions, for each fixed G.

**What it covers.** In the census of `COPRIME-PAIRS-20261027-001C`, 121 interacting algebras are open after Theorem 5 of E. Of these,
16 (all of class 3) have an edge failing only (V₃). Flow edges exist for 8 of them (check 1):
- free class 3 on (1, 1, 2) at d = 5;
- on (1, 2, 4) at d = 6;
- on (1, 1, 4) at d = 7;
- on (1, 2, 3) at d = 9;
- on (1, 4, 6) and (2, 2, 4) at d = 10;
- on (2, 3, 4) and (2, 4, 8) at d = 12.

The other 8 fail (F1), (F0) or both. So 113 census algebras remain open.

## Proof

**What is unchanged.** Every step of Theorems 4 and 5 that does not use (V₃) holds verbatim:
- the summand lemma (Lemma 1), zero-jump blocks (Lemma 2), the gadget, Brunnian insertions (Lemma 3), steering (Lemma 4);
- Lemma 5 and its character-0 refinement. The vertex equations at layer 2 vanish by (B₀). The private equations at layer 2 define
  the variety 𝒱, a product over edges. On 𝒱 every private equation at layer 3 is local to its edge.
- Lemma 7: F(σ, σ) = F_max; 1 ≤ F(0, Δ) < F_max and 1 ≤ F(Δ, 0) < F_max for a pair-failing Δ.

Here F(σ_u, σ_v) = |S(σ_u, σ_v)|, where S is the set of private configurations solving the edge's private equations. Only the
vertex equations at layer 3 need a new argument.

### 1. The vertex equations are a conserved flow

On 𝒱, the layer-3 part of log W₀ is [ℓ₁, ℓ₂]^{(3)} plus the terms removed by (V₃′) (E, Lemma 6 and §6). For an edge e = (u, v), let
f_e be the components of [ℓ₁^e, ℓ₂^e]^{(3)} at the characters of R₀, in a fixed basis of ⊕_{t∈R₀} V_{3,t} ≅ F_p^k. Then f_e is a
function of (σ_u, σ_v, π_e).

By Lemma 4, the vertex dual s at character t⁻¹ (t ∈ R₀) receives κ_t⟨(f_e)_t, ℓ₀^e(s)⟩ from edge e. The leaf of bundle j is
ℓ_j(s) = Σ_{i<d} t^i s_{o+i} (the bundle identity, A Lemma 3.1).
- Bundle 0 holds d − 1 copies of u's reading strand followed by one copy of v's.
- The bundles move as over-strands, so at the insertion point each copy carries its own block's dual colour, and the other block's
  dual is zero there.
- All vertex blocks are the same zero-jump block, so their dual colourings at t can be normalized to the same value s* ≠ 0 on the
  reading strand.

Hence ℓ₀^e(s_u) = (1 + t + ⋯ + t^{d−2}) s* = −t^{d−1} s* = −t⁻¹ s*, and ℓ₀^e(s_v) = t^{d−1} s* = t⁻¹ s*. Dividing by κ_t t⁻¹ s* ≠ 0,
the vertex equations at v read

  Σ_{e: head v} f_e − Σ_{e: tail v} f_e = 0 in F_p^k.

Each edge carries a flow f_e out of its tail and into its head, and every vertex conserves it.

### 2. The Fourier dual

Write [x = 0] = p^{−k} Σ_{λ∈F_p^k} ω^{⟨λ, x⟩}, with ω = e^{2πi/p}. Then, with C a known power of p (free top-layer data),

  P_τ(K_Γ) = C p^{−k|V|} Σ_{σ, λ} Π_{e=(u,v)} Â(σ_u, λ_u; σ_v, λ_v),
  Â(σ, λ; σ′, λ′) = Σ_{π∈S(σ,σ′)} ω^{⟨λ′ − λ, f(σ, σ′, π)⟩}.

### 3. Off the diagonal the flow vanishes

Let σ ≠ σ′. By (F0), vertex states are layer-1 data, and the reading strand's class colouring is nonzero at every root character
(Lemma 2(ii)). So the layer-1 part Δ of ℓ₀ = log(exp(c_{σ′}) exp(−c_σ)) is a nonzero element of V₁(Λ₁(0)).

On S(σ, σ′), the layer-2 private equations say [ℓ₂^{(1)}, Δ] has no component at R₁ and [Δ, ℓ₁^{(1)}] has none at R₂, modulo γ₃.
By (F1), ℓ₁^{(1)} = ℓ₂^{(1)} = 0. Every layer-3 term of [ℓ₁, ℓ₂] has a layer-1 factor from ℓ₁ or ℓ₂, because [ℓ₁^{(2)}, ℓ₂^{(2)}] lies
in layer 4 = 0. So f = 0 on S(σ, σ′), and

  Â(σ, λ; σ′, λ′) = F(σ, σ′).

### 4. On the diagonal the flow is bilinear

Let σ = σ′. Then Y₀ = 1, so W₁ = W₂ = 1, every private equation vanishes, and S(σ, σ) is all F_max configurations.

The leaves ℓ₁, ℓ₂ depend only on the private data:
- ℓ_i^{(1)} = a_i, a bijective linear image of slot i's layer-1 data in V₁(Λ₁(i));
- ℓ_i^{(2)} = b_i + D_i(a_i), with b_i a bijective linear image of slot i's layer-2 data in V₂(Λ₂(i)), and D_i(a_i) ∈ D_i.

So [ℓ₁, ℓ₂]^{(3)} = [a₁, b₂] + [b₁, a₂] + [a₁, D₂(a₂)] + [D₁(a₁), a₂] + [a₁, a₂]^{(3)}. By (F2) the last three terms have no component
at R₀, so

  f = B(a₁, b₂) + B′(b₁, a₂),

with B, B′ bilinear. Summing the characters over b₂ and b₁ first, with r the number of remaining private coordinates:

  N̂(μ) := Σ_π ω^{⟨μ, f(π)⟩} = p^{r + dim b₁ + dim b₂} · #{a₁ : ⟨μ, B(a₁, ·)⟩ = 0} · #{a₂ : ⟨μ, B′(·, a₂)⟩ = 0}.

This is a positive integer, with N̂(0) = F_max. It does not depend on σ, and Â(σ, λ; σ, λ′) = N̂(λ′ − λ).

### 5. Hardness

By §§3–4, Â is a non-negative integer matrix on S × F_p^k:
- Â(σ, λ; σ′, λ′) = F(σ, σ′) if σ ≠ σ′;
- Â(σ, λ; σ, λ′) = N̂(λ′ − λ) > 0.

Subdivide every edge by a vertex ordered last (E §5). The middle vertex carries its own flow constraint and its own λ, so

  P_τ(K_{Γ′}) = C′ Σ_{x: V(Γ) → S×F_p^k} Π_{uv} M(x_u, x_v),  M = Â Âᵀ,

with C′ a known power of p. M is symmetric, non-negative and integral, with positive diagonal.

Take x₀ = (0, 0) and x₁ = (Δ, 0) for a pair-failing Δ. The rows of Â at x₀ and x₁ restrict on the columns {x₀, x₁} to
(F_max, F(0, Δ)) and (F(Δ, 0), F_max). These are independent by Lemma 7, so the principal minor of M on {x₀, x₁} is positive.
Moreover M(x₀, x₁) ≥ F_max · F(Δ, 0) > 0. So x₀ and x₁ lie in one non-bipartite component of rank ≥ 2, and EVAL(M) is #P-hard
(Bulatov–Grohe; Cai–Chen–Lu, Theorem 2.1). Disjoint unions, isolation by Lemma I and the total count across sectors follow as in E
§5 (Theorem 2 of `COPRIME-PAIRS-20261027-001C`). Membership in #P is standard. ∎

## Evidence and checks

`check.py`:
1. **The flow census.** Among the 121 open algebras, 16 have an edge failing only (V₃); (F0)–(F2) hold for 8 of them.
2. **Conservation**, on free class 3 on (1, 1, 2) at d = 5, p = 11, slots (1, 2, 3). The two endpoints' vertex equations are exact
   negatives on every diagonal sample (all private data solve there) and on sampled off-diagonal solutions.
3. **Off the diagonal**, the flow vanishes on every sampled solution, and no random private data with nonzero layer-1 parts solve.
4. **On the diagonal**, the flow is bilinear: exact interpolation of degree ≤ 3, validated on fresh points, uses only monomials
   (layer 1 of one slot) × (layer 2 of the other). N̂(μ), computed exactly from the form, is a positive integer for every μ.

Scoping, not evidence: for free class 4 on (1, 1) at d = 5, outside this theorem, the diagonal flow is exactly conserved too, and
its Fourier transform over all 161 051 private configurations is real and positive, though not uniform on a subspace.

## Scope and gaps

- **Proved.** Theorem 6, for each fixed G.
- **Cited.** E (Theorems 4–5 and their lemmas), A (the bundle identity), Lemma I, Bulatov–Grohe via Cai–Chen–Lu.
- **Open.**
  - The 8 algebras failing only (V₃) where (F1) or (F0) fails. There, off-diagonal flows may survive, or vertex data reach layer 2,
    so the dual weights need not be non-negative.
  - Class 4: the dual route needs class-4 locality (layer-4 shifts on passed strands are not central) as well as flows.
  - The other open mechanisms of E §8.
- **Novelty and folklore risk.** Fourier duality between flows and potentials is classical (flow–tension duality; Tutte). Its use
  here, to turn a gadget count with vertex conservation laws into a non-negative spin system, is a candidate contribution. Folklore
  risk: medium.
- **References.** [BG] A. Bulatov, M. Grohe, Theoret. Comput. Sci. 348 (2005) 148–186. [CCL] J.-Y. Cai, X. Chen, P. Lu, SIAM J.
  Comput. 42 (2013) 924–1029, Theorem 2.1.
