# Pair failures: reduction to the ideal of nonzero characters, and hardness through inert layers

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

**Setting.**
- p is an odd prime, r | p − 1, ζ ∈ F_p^× has order r, and G = N ⋊ C_r with N a finite p-group of exponent p and class c < p.
  𝔫 = log N (Lazard), with its character decomposition 𝔫 = ⊕_{j ∈ Z/r} 𝔫_j.
- For d | r, τ = σ^{r/d} has order d, 𝔤_α = 𝔫^{(d)}_α (α mod d), 𝔫′_d = ⊕_{α≢0} 𝔤_α, and the criterion (P2_d), (P3_d) is as in
  `COPRIME-PAIRS-20261027-001B` §2.
- **The ideal of nonzero characters.** 𝔪_d is the ideal of 𝔫 generated by 𝔫′_d. It is C_r-invariant, and 𝔪_d = 𝔫′_d ⊕ (𝔪_d ∩ 𝔤₀).
  M_d = exp(𝔪_d), a normal C_r-invariant subgroup of N. γ_k(𝔪) is the lower central series of 𝔪 as a Lie algebra.
- **Pinned counts.** For a knot K with exterior X and meridian μ, P^H_τ(K) = #{ρ: π_K → H : ρ(μ) = τ}. For M = S³_{d/p}(K),

  |Hom(π₁M, G)| = Σ_{e ∈ (r/d)Z/rZ} p^{dim 𝔫 − dim 𝔫^{σ^e}} P^G_{σ^e}(K)

  (`COPRIME-PAIRS-20261027-001A`, Lemma 2.1), and every P^G_υ is multiplicative over connected sums (Lemma 2.2).
- **Active characters.** For a knot K, A(K) = {θ ∈ Z/d ∖ {0} : H¹(X; F^{ζ_d^θ}) ≠ 0}, the characters at which its exterior carries
  twisted cohomology. A(K) = −A(K), and A(K₁ # K₂) = A(K₁) ∪ A(K₂).

**Theorem 1 (reduction to 𝔪_d).**
- (a) Let π be a group with H¹(π; F_p) = 0 and ψ: π → C_r a sector with image C_d = ⟨τ⟩. Every ρ ∈ Hom_ψ(π, G) lands in
  M_d ⋊ C_d.
- (b) Every ρ: π_K → G with ρ(μ) = τ lands in M_d ⋊ ⟨τ⟩. So P^G_τ(K) = P^{M_d ⋊ ⟨τ⟩}_τ(K).
- (c) The pair and triple conditions at d, and every pair failure, are the same for 𝔫 and 𝔪_d, since 𝔪_d ⊇ 𝔫′_d.
- (d) Consequently the complexity of counting over sectors of order d depends only on the C_d-algebra 𝔪_d. It can have smaller
  class than 𝔫: a trivial-character generator x of 𝔫 lies outside 𝔪_d, so brackets [x, y] become generators of 𝔪_d.

**Theorem 2 (inert transfer).** Let 𝔨 ⊆ 𝔪 = 𝔪_d be a C_d-invariant ideal, and Ḡ = (M_d/exp 𝔨) ⋊ C_d. Suppose there is a **pinned
hardness family** for Ḡ:
- a polynomial-time map from loopless multigraphs to knot diagrams Γ ↦ K_Γ, with K_{Γ⊔Γ′} = K_Γ # K_{Γ′};
- a set A ⊆ Z/d ∖ {0} with A(K_Γ) ⊆ A for every Γ;
- for every Γ, P^{Ḡ}_τ(K_Γ) = c^{comp(Γ)} λ^{n} μ^{m} Z_W(Γ), with constants c, λ, μ > 0 and a fixed symmetric non-negative matrix W
  for which EVAL(W) is #P-hard (here comp(Γ) is the number of components, n and m the numbers of vertices and edges).

If no component of 𝔨 has a character in A, then counting |Hom(π₁ S³_{d/p}(K), G)| over knot diagrams K is #P-hard under
polynomial-time Turing reductions, for each fixed G.

**Theorem 3 (instances).** Theorem 2 applies, so counting on S³_{d/p}(K) is #P-hard, in each of these cases.
- **(i) A class-2 quotient.** 𝔨 ⊇ γ₃(𝔪) is a C_d-invariant ideal, 𝔪/𝔨 has a hard pair (χ₁, χ₂) at d (χ₁, χ₂, χ₁ + χ₂ ≢ 0
  and [𝔪_{χ₁}, 𝔪_{χ₂}] ⊄ 𝔨), and no component of 𝔨 has a character in A_S = {±χ₁, ±χ₂, ±(χ₁ + χ₂)}.
  - The natural choice is 𝔨 = γ₃(𝔪). In particular, the theorem applies whenever 𝔪_d has class 2 and (P2_d) fails (𝔨 = 0).
- **(ii) A triple quotient.** 𝔫 is graded of class ≤ 4 with a grading-preserving action, p ≥ 5, 𝔨 ⊆ 𝔪 is a graded C_d-invariant
  ideal, 𝔪/𝔨 satisfies (P2_d), and it has a near triple at a with [[𝔤_a, 𝔤_{−a}], 𝔤_{±a}] ≠ 0 there. No component of 𝔨 has a
  character in R(a), where:
  - R(a) = {a, −a} if ζ_d^a has odd order;
  - R(a) = {α : ζ_d^{αk} = −1} if ζ_d^a has order 2k.

**Corollary 4 (examples).**
- **The cross-layer group** of `BEYOND-CLASS2-20261027-001A` (free class 3 on x of trivial character and y of character α,
  α² ≠ 1): 𝔪_d = span(y, [x,y], [x,[x,y]], [y,[x,y]]) has class 2, so (i) applies. The earlier proof's identity is Theorem 1(a)
  written out.
- **Free class 3 on two generators of one character a** (coprime to d, 2a ≢ 0): γ₃ has character 3a, and A_S = {±a, ±2a}. So (i)
  applies unless 4a ≡ 0 or 5a ≡ 0, i.e. unless d ∈ {4, 5}. At d = 3 the layer has the trivial character and is inert.
- **Free class 4 on (a, −a)** at d = 5 or 7: 𝔪/γ₄(𝔪) satisfies (P2_d) with a near triple at a, and γ₄(𝔪) avoids R(a) = {±a}, so
  (ii) applies, although 𝔪/γ₃(𝔪) has no hard pair.
- **A class-3 algebra whose 𝔪_d has class 2:** free class 3 on (y₁, y₂, x) with characters (1, 1, 0) mod 4, modulo
  [[y₁, y₂], y_i]. Its criterion holds at d = 2, and (P2_4) fails; 𝔪₄ has class 2.

**What is left.** An algebra of the residual family is decided by Theorems 1–3 as soon as one allowed failing order d admits an
inert quotient construction. The rest are **interacting**: at every failing d ∈ 𝒟, every quotient construction tried has a
kernel component at an active character. Examples: free class 3 on (a, a) at d = 5, free class 4 on (a, a) at d = 3, 4, 5. In
the census of `check.py` (850 free graded algebras on two or three generators, classes 3 and 4, d ≤ 12, 𝒟 = all divisors of d):
- 10 satisfy the criterion at every order;
- 511 are hard by the triple theorem at some divisor;
- 153 are residual and decided here;
- 176 are residual and interacting.

## Nearest known results

- **`BEYOND-CLASS2-20261027-001A`.** The cross-layer identity, which Theorem 1 explains and generalizes.
- **`CLASSK-DICHOTOMY-20261026-001A`, `-001C`.** The class-(K) count on gadget manifolds and its knot realization, used as a
  hardness family in Theorem 3(i).
- **`COPRIME-PAIRS-20261027-001A`.** The triple construction, used in Theorem 3(ii), and pinning.
- **Standard.** Lifting through central extensions (obstructions in H², torsors under Z¹); a homomorphism from a group with
  H¹(π; F_p) = 0 to a p-group is trivial.
- **Novelty.** The reduction and the transfer are short. Their content is the bookkeeping that makes earlier constructions apply
  to algebras with higher layers. Folklore risk of Theorem 1: high (it is the standard "maps factor through the p-residual"
  argument). Theorem 2: medium.

## Proof

### 1. Theorem 1

- **The quotient has trivial action.** Every character of 𝔫/𝔪_d is ≡ 0 mod d, since 𝔫′_d ⊆ 𝔪_d. So τ acts trivially on
  N/M_d, and (N/M_d) ⋊ ⟨τ⟩ = (N/M_d) × ⟨τ⟩.
- **(a).** Compose ρ with N ⋊ C_d → (N/M_d) × C_d. The first coordinate is a homomorphism from π to the p-group N/M_d. A nontrivial
  one would give a nontrivial homomorphism to its abelianization, an F_p-vector space, contradicting H¹(π; F_p) = 0. So it is
  trivial, and ρ(π) lies in the preimage of {1} × C_d, which is M_d ⋊ C_d.
- **(b).** The first coordinate kills μ, since ρ(μ) = τ ↦ (1, τ). The knot group is the normal closure of μ, so it is trivial.
- **(c).** A pair failure [𝔤_α, 𝔤_β] ≠ 0 has α, β ≠ 0, so 𝔤_α, 𝔤_β ⊆ 𝔫′_d ⊆ 𝔪_d. A triple [[𝔤_α, 𝔤_{−α}], 𝔤_β] lies in 𝔪_d
  likewise.
- **(d)** follows from (a) applied to π₁M (H₁(M; F_p) = 0) for the closed count, and from (b) for pinned counts. The class
  statement is the example in Corollary 4. ∎

### 2. Inert lifting

**Lemma 5.** Let 𝔨 ⊆ 𝔪 be a C_d-invariant ideal and K a knot with A(K) disjoint from the characters of 𝔨. Then reduction modulo
exp 𝔨 is a bijection from pinned homomorphisms π_K → M ⋊ ⟨τ⟩ to pinned homomorphisms π_K → (M/exp 𝔨) ⋊ ⟨τ⟩. So
P^{M⋊⟨τ⟩}_τ(K) = P^{Ḡ}_τ(K).

*Proof.*
- **A central series through 𝔨.** Put 𝔨₀ = 𝔨 and 𝔨_{i+1} = [𝔪, 𝔨_i]. These are C_d-invariant ideals, 𝔨_i/𝔨_{i+1} is central in
  𝔪/𝔨_{i+1}, and 𝔨_i = 0 for large i, because 𝔪 is nilpotent. By Lazard the same holds for the groups exp 𝔨_i.
- **One step.** Lifting a pinned homomorphism through the central extension by the C_d-module V = 𝔨_i/𝔨_{i+1}:
  - the obstruction lies in H²(X, μ; V), and lifts with ρ(μ) = τ form a torsor under the relative cocycles Z¹(X, μ; V);
  - V = ⊕_θ V_θ by characters. For θ = 0, H¹(X, μ; F_p) = H²(X, μ; F_p) = 0, because X has the homology of a circle generated by
    μ;
  - for θ ≠ 0, the boundary circle μ is F^θ-acyclic, so H^i(X, μ; F^θ) = H^i(X; F^θ). This vanishes for i = 1 because θ ∉ A(K),
    and then for i = 2 because the Euler characteristic of X is 0 and H⁰(X; F^θ) = 0.
  - So every lift exists and is unique.
- Induction over i gives the bijection. ∎

### 3. Theorem 2

- **The reduction.** Given Γ, build K_{Γ ⊔ F^{⊔j}} = K_Γ # K_F # ⋯ # K_F for j = 0, …, R − 1, with F the anchor multigraph of
  Lemma I below, and query the counting oracle on S³_{d/p} of each.
- **The sector of τ.** A(K_Γ) ⊆ A misses the characters of 𝔨, so by Theorem 1(b) and Lemma 5,
  P^G_τ(K_Γ) = P^{M⋊⟨τ⟩}_τ(K_Γ) = P^{Ḡ}_τ(K_Γ) = c^{comp(Γ)} λ^n μ^m Z_W(Γ).
- **The other sectors.** For each e, f_e(Γ) := P^G_{σ^e}(K_Γ) is a non-negative integer, multiplicative under disjoint union (by
  K_{Γ⊔Γ′} = K_Γ # K_{Γ′} and Lemma 2.2), with f_e(∅) = 1 and f_τ ≥ 1 (the trivial map, μ ↦ τ, exists).
- **Isolation.** The closed count is h(Γ) = Σ_e c_e f_e(Γ) with c_e = p^{dim 𝔫 − dim 𝔫^{σ^e}} > 0. Lemma I
  (`CLASSK-DICHOTOMY-20261026-001C` §3) gives a fixed multigraph F and recovers (Σ_{e∈I} c_e) f_τ(Γ) from h(Γ ⊔ F^{⊔j}),
  j < R, where I = {e : f_e = f_τ}. Dividing by the known constants gives Z_W(Γ).
- **Hardness.** EVAL(W) is #P-hard by hypothesis. Membership in #P is standard. ∎

The anchor F, the set I and the constants depend only on G: this is a reduction for each fixed G.

### 4. Theorem 3

**(i) The class-(K) family.** Let Ḡ = (M/exp 𝔨) ⋊ C_d. Since 𝔨 ⊇ γ₃(𝔪), its normal subgroup has class ≤ 2, so it is of class
(K), with B = 𝔪/(γ₂(𝔪) + 𝔨) and A = (γ₂(𝔪) + 𝔨)/𝔨, and (χ₁, χ₂) is a hard pair of Ḡ in the sense of
`CLASSK-DICHOTOMY-20261026-001A` (with r := d).
- **The knots.** For connected Γ, let K_Γ be the knot of Lemma R of `CLASSK-DICHOTOMY-20261026-001C` for the slot triple of
  (χ₁, χ₂). For disconnected Γ, take the connected sum over components.
- **Active characters.** S³_d(K_Γ) is a Γ-gadget manifold: d_θ = 0 for θ ∉ A_S. Restriction H^i(S³_d(K_Γ); F^θ) → H^i(X; F^θ)
  is an isomorphism for θ ≠ 0, compatible with cup products (`CLASSK-DICHOTOMY-20261026-001C`, proof of Corollary E (a)). So
  A(K_Γ) ⊆ A_S.
- **The pinned count.** Lifting through A gives
  P^{Ḡ}_τ(K_Γ) = |H¹(X; A)| · #{x ∈ H¹(X; B) : o(x) = 0}, with pinned cocycles at the trivial character 0 and
  H²(X, μ; F_p) = 0. The closed sector-1 count is
  |Hom_ψ(π₁ S³_d(K_Γ), Ḡ)| = |Z¹(A)| · |B¹(B)| · #{x ∈ H¹(B) : o(x) = 0} (`CLASSK-DICHOTOMY-20261026-001A` §1), with the
  same obstruction map under the restriction isomorphism.
  - The ratio is 1/(|B¹(M; A)| · |B¹(M; B)|), a constant independent of Γ, because B¹(M; F^θ) has dimension 1 for θ ≠ 0.
  - Theorem A of `CLASSK-DICHOTOMY-20261026-001A` gives the closed count c_1 λ_1^n μ_1^m Z_{W_1}(Γ) for connected Γ, with W_1
    positive, symmetric and of rank ≥ 2 for a hard pair, so EVAL(W_1) is #P-hard (Bulatov–Grohe [BG]; [CCL, Thm 2.1]).
- So the hypotheses of Theorem 2 hold with A = A_S.

**(ii) The triple family.** 𝔪/𝔨 is graded of class ≤ 4 with a grading-preserving action, (P2_d) holds and a near triple at a
fails (P3_d). So Theorem 2 of `COPRIME-PAIRS-20261027-001A` applies to it.
- Its gadget knots K_Γ are connected sums of summands with root characters R(a) (amphichiral two-bridge knots, or (k,1)-cables of
  T(2, p)), unknot blocks, and braid insertions whose bundle products have trivial character, which do not change H¹. So
  A(K_Γ) ⊆ R(a).
- Its pinned count is λ^n μ^m Z_f(Γ) on edge-doubled graphs, with EVAL(f²) #P-hard (that proof, §§7–8).
- So the hypotheses of Theorem 2 hold with A = R(a).

### 5. Corollary 4

- **The cross-layer group.** The characters of x, y, [x,y], [x,[x,y]], [y,[x,y]] are 0, α, α, α, 2α (mod d). 𝔪_d is spanned by
  the last four: it contains y and is closed under brackets with x. In 𝔪_d, [y, [x,y]] spans γ₂(𝔪_d), and γ₃(𝔪_d) = 0 because
  all brackets of length 4 vanish in class 3. So 𝔪_d has class 2, with the hard pair (α, α) when 2α ≢ 0.
- **Free class 3 on (a, a).** Characters a, a; 2a; 3a, 3a. 𝔪 = 𝔫 and γ₃ = L₃. 3a ∈ {±a, ±2a} iff 2a ≡ 0 (excluded), 4a ≡ 0,
  a ≡ 0 (excluded) or 5a ≡ 0.
- **Free class 4 on (a, −a)** and **the d = 4 algebra**: computed in `check.py` (part 3), which lists 𝔪, its lower central
  series, the quotient used and the kernel characters.

## Check

`check.py` imports `cd_lib` (COPRIME-DICHOTOMY-20261027-001A) read-only. It verifies:
1. **Theorem 1 on counts.** For free class 3 on x (trivial character) and y at d = 4, p = 5, exact counts of homomorphisms from
   S³_{4/5}(K) agree for G and for M_d ⋊ C_d on four knots (C₂ of T(2,5), 4₁, 5₂, 3₁, chiral ones included), and 𝔪_d has class 2.
2. **Lemma 5 on counts.** For free class 3 on (1, 1), with 𝔨 = γ₃(𝔪): on five knots whose active characters avoid the kernel
   (d = 3 with the kernel at the trivial character; d = 4 on T(2, 5) and 5₂), exact counts for G and for the quotient differ by
   exactly p^{(nonzero-character dimension of 𝔨)}. On C₂ of T(2, 5) at d = 4, whose active characters meet the kernel, they do not.
3. **Corollary 4.** The cross-layer group has 𝔪_d of class 2 and an inert hard pair at d = 3, …, 9; free class 3 on (1, 1) is decided
   by (i) exactly at d ∉ {4, 5} among d ≤ 12; free class 4 on (1, −1) at d = 5, 7 is decided by (ii) through γ₄(𝔪) but not by (i)
   through γ₃(𝔪); the three-generator algebra satisfies the criterion at 2, fails (P2_4), and has 𝔪₄ of class 2.
4. **The census.** The classification of 850 free graded algebras quoted above (10 / 511 / 153 / 176), using the quotients
   𝔪/γ_j(𝔪) (j = 3, 4, 5) and 𝔪/(𝔪 ∩ L_{≥j}) (j = 2, 3, 4).

## Scope

- **Proved.** Theorems 1–3 and Corollary 4, for each fixed G, under Turing reductions.
- **Cited.** The class-(K) count and realization (`CLASSK-DICHOTOMY-20261026-001A`, `-001C`); the triple construction
  (`COPRIME-PAIRS-20261027-001A`); Lemma I; Bulatov–Grohe via Cai–Chen–Lu.
- **Not covered.** Interacting pair failures, the subject of the next tickets. The census is finite evidence about where the
  remaining cases are, not a classification.
