# Do knots constrain states only, or also questions?

With the second binary question still supplied, this is an open choice between governing ordinary allowed words alone and also restricting the operation of asking. The proposed equivalence between a fair singleton and permission to leave the allowed words fails in both directions. A fair coin may be reported on a singleton while its only word stays unchanged. A supplied operation can certainly carry it outside and always report plus. Under the stronger requirement of an actual reversing flip with factorization of the after-order signed product, the word must leave that singleton and the fair sign follows. In the full signed-product formalism the *transport* has outside words in its domain, while its sharp sign measurement gives either the old or forbidden ordinary value when checked afterwards. Different premises answer different questions about the act. [Both derivations](principles.md)

With a rule-keeping act define $F_t(w)=w+t$ if that word belongs to $S$, and $F_t(w)=w$ otherwise. On each full pair it interchanges the two words. On a singleton it fixes the word. Therefore it is an involution on $S$ and permutes it. Its native return type here is an ordinary word. A movement flag returns yes with chance $m/n$ on a uniformly drawn word. The spectral sign of its permutation matrix has plus chance one on the positive uniform preparation because the permutation fixes that prepared vector. The all-plus conclusion applies to this sign of a pure change and to conditioning that leaves a union of complete or fixed actions. An ordinary read or a combined signed question can have a negative answer. Testing membership in $S$ to decide a purportedly local flip can require marks owned by other readers. A global permutation on allowed words need not define a local instrument.

With an independent instrument on just one mark of the isolated triple, suppose each possible outcome must keep the positive uniform ternary preparation in its original allowed word space when the other two readers do nothing. For not-all-equal, hold the other two bits at 00. The only allowed value of this mark is 1, so its transition amplitude from 1 to 0 under every local operation must vanish. Otherwise the output has positive probability at 000. The other two held at 11 exclude its transition from 0 to 1. Each possible Kraus operator is therefore diagonal. Summing over private apparatus outcomes preserves this conclusion because squared outside norms add. Parity gives the same conclusion, since the two other bits determine the remaining one. Products of these independent diagonal effects on any supported preparation are mixtures of ordinary word answers, with one common list for all local settings. The three-reader score is then at most 2. Extra correlations among readers supplied in another quantum resource fall outside this statement.

With the parity six-rule preparation on two split triples, each of Bob's two bits is determined by Alice's two corresponding ordinary bits. For every Bob input and every different Bob output one can keep Alice's full word fixed while only the first Bob word is allowed. His truly local allowed-output instrument would have to be diagonal on the four ordinary Bob words. In every joint distribution one can then reveal Bob's classical word first, condition Alice's preparation on it and sample all her whole-setting answers using independent private coins. Bob's own answers for each setting are sampled conditionally on that word. This constructs a common table even if Alice's operations are unrestricted. The same twelve off-diagonal obstructions are present for the not-all-equal split. For each desired Bob input, set both of Alice's physical bits in each triple to its opposite. Those four Alice values then force that particular Bob word. The archived unrestricted not-all-equal game already has a common explanation. Thus perfect six-rule play cannot follow from independent local instruments of this kind without an additional resource. It is stronger to impose local preservation for the actual game than simply to require fair singleton answers. With arbitrary allowed-output joint instruments, compressing each entire context gives **all** earlier one-shot probabilities while staying inside $S$. Direct integer comparison reproduces all 576 six-rule entries and all 64 isolated three-reader entries for each law. Of the 576 compressed six-rule outcome effects, 120 (parity) and 124 (not-all-equal) cease to be projectors. For the 64 compressed ternary effects these counts are 56 and 44. Noncommuting compressed single-question marginals occur in six of the nine not-all-equal six-rule trials and four of its eight ternary settings. There are zero such contexts in these parity tests, even though products of the compressed marginals need not give the joint outcomes. Implementing each joint context collectively with fixed-setting access does not establish that it could be split among independent readers. [Compressed instruments and updates](after_an_answer.md)

With the chosen reader limits, a physical local realization of the *violating* tables must relax at least one of the requirements just stated. Allowing individual questions to have effects on ordinary words outside $S$ is the earlier implementation. Allowing an additional joint response, memory, new shared resources or wider reader access changes the questions.
