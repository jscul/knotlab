# Pure descendants of a held block are counted by the cliques of its first residual-pair graph

Let \(B\) be a set of at least three junctions in a held block at a positive funded age. Suppose \(B\) is a proper subset of its live candidate component. Form the graph \(F\) on \(B\) by retaining exactly those pairs whose own two-parent child is born in the next **full** round. A later child is *pure* if all of its ancestors in the chosen generation lie in \(B\).

**At \(j-1\) rounds after the chosen generation, there is exactly one pure child for each \(j\)-clique of \(F\), and there are no others.** For \(j\ge2\), the child's parents are all its \((j-1)\)-subcliques. Every pair of those parents occurs together in just this one child, even among the children with outside ancestry. The last occupied pure layer is \(j=\omega(F)\). All outside groups and overlaps are allowed. Here the term *clique* refers to the ordinary finite candidate or residual graph specified in the statement. [Funded growth rule](../../proofs/REDUCE-BLOCKS-20261119-001A/REDUCE-BLOCKS-20261119-001A-P001_reduce/proof.md)

## Conditions on one-parent children

Every original \(v\in B\) has at least \(|B|-1\) candidates. If \(v\) is unary, its single parent holds it with every one of its candidates. Every incident pair then receives its own child, so its demand is at least two and it has no spare. If \(v\) has at least two parents, its positive demand already supplies its exact loose count. These facts exclude pure unary births on the first step.

Each live vertex has a child in any funded round, all children have parents, every old candidate pair has a common child, and children meet when they share a parent. Each old candidate component therefore gives one connected new component. Before any global terminal birth every original vertex has a descendant there. If a pure junction were isolated, it would be the whole new component and would have every original component vertex among its ancestors. The assumed outside vertex excludes this. Accordingly every later pure child of two or more parents has positive demand and no spare, by the same fresh-count rule. This justifies the induction below for arbitrary other components. It also works for \(|B|=1,2\) whenever \(B\) is proper in its component and its vertices have no initial spare.

## Classification and exclusive use of each pair

At the first step all of \(B\) is held, so a pure group of at least three is impossible. Its pure births are precisely its residual pairs. Each such pair is used by no other child. This establishes both claims at \(j=2\).

Inductively call the pure node of a \(j\)-clique \(A\) by its support \(A\). For two distinct such nodes to be candidates their complete parent lists must share a \((j-1)\)-subset. Thus they have the form

\[
Q_a=U-\{a\},\qquad Q_b=U-\{b\}, \qquad |U|=j+1.
\]

Their unique common parent is \(D_{ab}=U-\{a,b\}\). Hereafter \(D_{ac}\) means the pure \((j-1)\)-node with that support whenever it is used. Within a family of pairwise compatible \(j\)-subsets, any third either contains \(D_{ab}\) or is another \(j\)-subset of \(U\). A third containing \(D_{ab}\) but extending outside \(U\) cannot be compatible with a fourth inside \(U\) that misses part of \(D_{ab}\). Thus a candidate clique of pure vertices is either held by one common facet or consists entirely of facets of one \(U\). This is the standard subset calculation used in the isolated single-block law. [Earlier whole-stage subset proof](../../proofs/GROWTH-CONTINUES-20261119-001/GROWTH-CONTINUES-20261119-001-P001_continuation/proof.md)

**When \(U\) is a clique of \(F\), every facet \(Q_i=U-\{i\}\) exists.** We show that these \(j+1\) facets form one maximally unheld group and that their pairs are exclusive in the full roster. An arbitrary current child \(y\) compatible with \(Q_a,Q_b\), but missing \(D_{ab}\) from its parent list, must use parents \(D_{ac},D_{bd}\) with \(c,d\notin\{a,b\}\). Those two parents of \(y\) must themselves be candidates. If \(c\ne d\), the two sets miss four distinct indices between them and cannot share a \((j-2)\)-facet. For \(j=2\) there is only one choice outside \(\{a,b\}\) to begin with. Hence \(c=d\). The known pure \(Q_c\) already uses both \(D_{ac},D_{bc}\). The inductive exclusivity of that parent pair gives \(y=Q_c\), even though \(y\) was allowed outside ancestry.

Suppose a child \(y\) compatible with three distinct facets \(Q_a,Q_b,Q_c\) contains \(D_{ab}\). To meet \(Q_c\) it must also contain some \(D_{cd}\). Its two parents \(D_{ab},D_{cd}\) can be compatible only if \(d=a\) or \(d=b\), by the same four-index calculation (and by cardinality when \(j=2\)). In the first case the two parents belong to \(Q_a\), and in the second they belong to \(Q_b\). Exclusivity again forces \(y\) to equal that facet. Together the two arguments show that no child outside the facets can extend their whole clique. Three distinct facets have no common parent, so the complete facet clique is one legal birth.

Finally any other unheld clique through \(Q_a,Q_b\) must include a child missing \(D_{ab}\), since otherwise it is held. This child is one of the other facets \(Q_c\), by the first argument. Every member of the clique now belongs to the same facet family, by the test against those three facets. Maximality forces the complete family. Its pairs receive no residual children either. This proves exclusivity at the new level.

**When \(U\) is not a clique of \(F\), the two available facets \(Q_a,Q_b\) force \(ab\) to be its only missing edge.** Every other facet contains \(a,b\) and is absent. The main hazard is a residual pair through these two available facets. Choose \(c\in U-\{a,b\}\). The existing nodes \(D_{ac},D_{bc}\) are candidates. At \(j=2\) they are two original members of the single held block. At \(j\ge3\) they share the pure facet \(U-\{a,b,c\}\). Take a real \(j\)-child \(Y\) in the full roster covering their pair. A pure child containing both would have support \(U-\{c\}\), which contains the missing edge \(ab\). Consequently \(Y\) is mixed. It cannot also contain \(D_{ab}\), since \(D_{ab}\) and \(D_{ac}\) are two parents used exclusively by \(Q_a\). Now \(Q_a,Q_b,Y\) are mutually compatible, and their only possible common parent \(D_{ab}\) is missing from \(Y\). A maximal unheld group therefore covers \(Q_a,Q_b\) together with an outside child. Their pair cannot be born purely. This settles every possible pure pair, as well as pure large groups through the preceding clique classification. The induction is complete.

## Proper components, whole components and the two outer populations

For \(|B|\ge3\), if \(B\) is the entire live component its first residual graph is complete. The single-block subset calculation still holds up to the unique top birth. If this component exhausts the whole presentation, that birth is implied and closes. If another live component exists, the top is nonimplied, has zero candidate demand and receives one fresh distinction. It gives a unary child with two fresh distinctions, then two unary siblings. The descendants of this component continue forever by the earlier relay recurrence. A held pair that already contains a recurring unary tip gives another exception, even inside a larger connected component. Thus unqualified finiteness under arbitrary outside growth is false. Both exceptions have a physical source under the same growth law. [Relay recurrence](../../proofs/RELAY-IDENT-20261119-001A/RELAY-IDENT-20261119-001A-P001_relays/proof.md) · [Recurring tip](../GROWTH-CONTINUES-20261119-001/persistence.md)

For comparison, an **unheld** candidate clique \(C\) of size at least three is contained in some maximal unheld group. All of its pairs are therefore covered. A pure first group must be \(C\) itself and can be born only if \(C\) was maximal. All members have at least two parents, since one unary member would hold the entire clique at its parent. If \(C\) is proper in its component the at-most-one pure first child stays nonisolated and has no pure child. Two initial subsets with no cross candidates can never produce a pure child mixing the two subsets. At the first round all pure parent lists stay on one side, and at each subsequent round lists on distinct sides are disjoint, hence incompatible. These facts permit separate treatment of the two outer cliques of 14. For its large held clique, a single real outside group covering every internal pair makes \(F\) edgeless and immediately eliminates all pure children.

Every finite graph \(F\) on \(n\ge3\) labels can occur. Hold all \(n\) labels together. For each prospective missing pair \(a,b\), add one point adjacent only to \(a,b\), and hold its two new pairs separately. Exactly the missing pairs enter the new unheld triangles. The other pairs are residual. For a complete \(F\), attaching a leaf by one separate pair makes the original \(B\) proper and preserves every internal residual pair, so the \(|B|-1\) bound is attained even with an outside vertex in its component.

These block rosters have realizations at positive age. Give each desired block an old label. The intended parents for each desired child are its incident block labels and three further old labels private to it. Define old candidates by co-occurrence in an intended parent set. Hold every old candidate pair separately and hold every undesired maximal old clique. Each intended set is maximal because of its private labels and is unheld. Every old candidate pair belongs to an intended set. Give each old live label one loose distinction per intended group containing it. The first funded round produces exactly the prescribed child population, and its shared parent blocks are the prescribed ones. For a physical start, realize each designated old held block with a distinct dead junction, pad these with two common dead junctions joined to one another, and retain the specified loose distinctions. Each old live label occurs in at least three candidate-pair blocks. The wired graph stays connected after deleting any one junction or the exterior, so all even assignments at each individual junction extend to a joint state. This is the private-label realization from the earlier positive-age test applied to the full block family. ## Finite checks

The checker exercises all 64 labelled choices of \(F\) on four points and all 1,024 on five points. It also checks one missing edge at each size from six through nine and 80 fixed-seed arrangements with overlapping outside blocks. In every case it verifies the complete pure parent lists and counts every use of each parent pair across *all* children. All 80 extra arrangements exhibited two mixed children sharing at least two parents in some tested round. The seven-point fixture includes its positive-age physical source, the complete later parent lists, and the actual mixed child, its two earlier witness parents, the excluded common parent, and the next covering group at each missing face of sizes three through seven. A separate two-component control reproduces the pure relay after the held triple reaches its top. These checks independently exercise the finite statements using the same task's group enumerator. The all-size result follows from the induction.
