## What an observer can decode

In the parity star and the longer-route arms, the receiver can inspect the **positive event** that takes its three named inputs. That same born holder exists in both preparations. Counting its physical holdings gives three or four, or equivalently zero or one fresh distinction. This is a binary readout across preparations at the second formation step, one step after the common receiver cut. It needs neither a remote status report nor a declaration that an expected birth failed to appear. An observer limited to new *loose* holders would miss the formed terminal knot and would need a separate observation of its actual support. This is a dependence through the global closing rule. No later sender action on a fixed structure is used.

The actual allowed-state projection on the three loose receiver inputs was also checked at the common age-one boundary. ## A distinct global funding veto

Each candidate pair qualifies as a full two-parent group. The primary and alternate step graphs therefore agree. Every live knot owes exactly two openings.
