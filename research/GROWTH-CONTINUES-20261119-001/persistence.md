# What a funded round preserves about closed candidate walks

**The live candidate components and their fundamental groups persist through every nonclosing funded round.** A disconnected live candidate graph, or a nontrivial fundamental group in any component, therefore certifies nonending. A second local certificate applies to start-03 even when its candidate complex has trivial fundamental group. All graphs and complexes below are classical descriptions of the reached parent and candidate relations. [Funded growth rule](../../proofs/REDUCE-BLOCKS-20261119-001A/REDUCE-BLOCKS-20261119-001A-P001_reduce/proof.md)

## The two candidate graphs

Put the old live junctions on one side \(X\), all births on the other \(Y\), and record every parent incidence. A pair in \(X\) is a candidate pair exactly when some child contains both points. Indeed, every multi-parent birth is a clique of old candidates. Every candidate pair not used in a larger legal group receives its own child. Two children are candidates exactly when they have a common parent. A single-parent offshoot contributes its own vertex. Every old live junction has a child on a funded round, since each leftover loose distinction makes an offshoot. Thus both sides meet every incidence component. The two candidate graphs are precisely its two projections by a common neighbor.

For either projection form its *flag complex* by filling all cliques. The componentwise fundamental-group correspondence for such projections is a classical theorem of Larrión, Pizaña and Villarroel-Flores. Their Proposition 5.1 explicitly treats a cover of the old pairs by cliques. Here it applies to the actual children, including the residual pair children and repeated offshoots. [Original article, Theorem 3.1 and Proposition 5.1](https://xamanek.izt.uam.mx/map/papers/Fundame06w.pdf) · [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2007.12.006)

For a direct proof, start with the bipartite incidence graph and fill its simple cycles of lengths four and six with disks. Every alternating closed walk of length at most six then contracts by disks and deletion of backtracks. Map this common complex to the old flag complex by fixing each old vertex and sending each child \(y\) to any one parent \(p_y\). An incidence \(x\!-\!y\) maps to \(x\!-\!p_y\), allowing equality. Along \(x\!-\!y\!-\!x'\) this gives \(x,p_y,x'\), which contracts to the old edge \(x,x'\) inside the clique of parents of \(y\). The image of each attached disk boundary therefore contracts to a walk around at most one old triangle. This defines a map on fundamental groups.

Conversely, lift each old edge \(x,x'\) through any child containing both. The lift of an old triangle has length six and contracts. Lifting is therefore well defined on edge loops modulo backtracks and triangle moves. Projection after lifting returns each old edge up to its triangle inside one child. For the other composite, connect \(y\) to \(p_y\) by their incidence. The lift of the projected incidence \(x,p_y\) can use \(y\) itself, because changing the chosen witness traverses a square. Thus this composite also returns the original incidence path after the basepoint connections are inserted. The two induced maps are inverses. Interchanging old junctions and children proves the same for the other projection. Alternating paths also give the bijection of components. In particular the first homology groups agree over every coefficient field.

If a birth consumes all loose distinctions it is the sole child, and every previously live point belongs to it. The old candidate graph is then complete, including the one-point case. Its positive-degree homology and fundamental group are trivial. The sole birth can have no fresh distinctions, leaving the live roster empty. Otherwise each child receives at least one fresh distinction and enough for its computed next demand. Thus a funded stage with at least two components, or with a nontrivial component fundamental group, always has a next nonclosing, funded stage with the same witness. Isolated points and components with a single child are covered because implication is tested on the *whole* presentation.

For example, give the five-cycle candidate graph its five neighboring pair blocks and two loose distinctions at every point. Exactly its five pairs are born. They again form a five-cycle with two loose distinctions apiece. A connected physical realization uses one dead hub per pair and a further dead junction joined to those hubs to give each three places. This end has no induced partnered four-cycle. Two separated live holders with one loose distinction each also persist. Both single-parent births miss the other holder's loose distinction and both receive two fresh distinctions.

## Higher homology can disappear in one round

For every \(n\ge3\), take \(n\) pairs of live points. Write a transversal as a binary string of length \(n\). Make exactly the transversals of odd parity in their **first three choices** held blocks. Use one dead hub per such transversal, joined once to each of its members. Give each live point \(2^{n-2}\) loose distinctions. Each hub has \(n\) places and each live point has \(2^{n-2}\) incident hubs. Both odd and even transversals realize every choice on two distinct pairs, because at least one of the first three choices remains free. This also makes the physical presentation connected. The candidates are precisely the pairs of points from different pairs.

The old flag complex has dimension \(n-1\). The mod-two sum of all \(2^n\) transversals is a nonzero top-dimensional cycle. Every codimension-one subface occurs exactly twice and there are no \(n\)-simplices. The maximal legal groups are all the even transversals. They cover every candidate pair and use exactly the supplied \(2^{n-2}\) loose distinctions at each point. Two different such groups must meet, since disjoint binary transversals are complements and complementing the first three choices changes parity. No point is in every even transversal. Thus the new candidate graph is complete, with its entire set unheld. It has just one next group, and every new point has exactly one fresh distinction. The following all-parent birth consumes them all and closes. The old nonzero homology in any prescribed degree at least two has become zero after one round. The held blocks are essential to this example and differ from the canonical partnered growth. [Canonical partnered hypotheses](../../proofs/WALL-READ-20261119-001A/WALL-READ-20261119-001A-P001_partnered/proof.md)

Using only the filled held blocks can also give a false one-dimensional certificate. Three live points with their three separate pair blocks and one loose distinction each have a cycle in that complex. The same dead-hub construction just used for the five-cycle realizes these blocks physically. The entire three-point set is an unheld clique that closes them in one birth. The executable controls check this case, the separate pieces, the five-cycle and the closing parity constructions for \(n=3,4,5\). ## A local test independent of the closed walks

On a funded stage, one candidate and exactly two loose distinctions at \(x\) force one pair child and one offshoot of \(x\). The offshoot's sole parent is \(x\), and only the pair child also contains \(x\). The offshoot has one candidate, one group of demand, and two fresh distinctions by the one-parent rule. The two births rule out global implication. Induction repeats the condition without any restriction on groups elsewhere. On a positive reached stage, one parent and one candidate already imply two loose distinctions. The earlier record identifies such a junction in start-03 at age eight.
