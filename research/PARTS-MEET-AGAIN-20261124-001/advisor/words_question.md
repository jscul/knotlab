You are the word advisor for a research program on "knot growth". Its rules for words: one word for each meaning and one meaning for each word; no words from a space of two or three dimensions (point, edge, vertex, face, axis of a figure, pivot, hinge, centre, and the like) for the program's own objects, except as the literature's name given once and marked, or as the program's stated targets (space, distance, dimension); a new word must not reuse a stem the record already uses in another sense, and must not have a settled meaning in logic, graph theory, distributed computing or topology that the program might cite in another sense. The owner's own words keep the owner's sense: where the owner has already used a word for one idea, a second idea needs another word.

Background, in the glossary's words. A structure is a set of knots, each holding distinctions; a distinction held by one knot is loose, by two is shared. Two knots meet when they hold a distinction in common. A knot with a usable loose distinction is a member of the live roster; a knot with none is a dead knot. Growth is by births: a new knot takes one loose distinction from each of its parents and receives fresh ones. Two members are joinable when some knot meets both; that knot is their **common knot**, and the members a knot meets are its **met set**. A part is a connected component of the joinable relation. Under every law studied so far a knot meets only its parents and its children, so parts that have come apart never get a common knot again.

Words already in use, with their senses:
- **axis / axes** (the guide, lessons 2 and 4.6; the owner's word): the X and Y axes of a Cartesian base, each "built out of an infinite structure of logic"; a direction of extension that the rules grow. A study is named LOGIC-AXIS.
- **hub** (glossary): one variable shared by several separate relations; sharing identity is not a binding. Another study calls a distinction with fourteen holders "a common hub".
- **common knot** (definitions; lesson 8.1): any knot that meets two or more members, which are then joinable through it.
- **dead knot** (glossary): a knot with no loose distinction; lesson 8.7 says "a dead knot is the pure case: it makes nothing and others join through it" under the heading "Every join already has a reference".
- **anchor**, **reference**: the owner's earlier words (23 November) for the same family of ideas: "What if some emergent patterns need anchor points, or like a reference". The guide's subsection is "Anchors and time"; a ticket is titled "what a reference is". Elsewhere "reference" also means the engine a new engine is compared with, and "a chosen reference" is one kind of scaffold.
- **parent, child, descend, line of descent, brood, weaned**: by the birth record.
- **junction**: retired; it was an older word for knot and must not be used.

The new idea, in the owner's words (24 November): "Programming is a thing in our reality, there should be a way to reference information like joining 2 different clusters of child logic from the parent via something like an 'axis point'. So we can essentially reference chunks of logic."

The object to name: a knot g that two clusters of knots both descend from, serving as the common knot through which members of the two clusters, which are NOT its children, become joinable. For that, each of the two members must come to hold a distinction that g holds (a distinction that is already shared gains a holder). So it is (1) by the birth record, a common ancestor of the two members, and (2) by the structure, their common knot.
