# Carrying a once-loaded state into the next decision

A hot knot with two loose distinctions can give each of its two unary children a pair of occupied fresh distinctions. At the next completed layer the children themselves veto their proposed joint birth. On the bare five-ring this gives 20 births against 15 on the very same incidence after erasing only the fresh pairs. Two is the least positive fresh weight that can carry the state through one such child. The old minimum-extension rule has fresh weight zero on this preparation at every layer. ## The birth and its allowed states

A distinction is occupied when its value differs from the transported allowed reference. The relative values on each knot sum to zero modulo two. A knot is hot when at least one of its distinctions is occupied. Under cold P, hot V, a proposal through hot parents loses its multi-parent birth. Each loose distinction left over has its own unary child. Fix the complete roster, its funding and its ordinary fresh counts before writing the newborn values. A nonimplied child with $p$ inputs and ordinary demand $d$ has $h=\max(d,1,3-p)$ fresh distinctions. A valid sole implied closure keeps its original convention. [Structural law and parity projections](../STATE-FEEDS-GROWTH-20261119-001/selection.md)

Keep every old value. Use the ordinary minimum extension except for a unary birth with a hot parent and an unoccupied input. In the **exactly-two rule**, occupy the two fresh distinctions if $h=2$. In the **any-pair rule**, occupy an unordered pair whenever $h\ge2$, admitting all tied choices. Birth and the selected extension form one event. The condition uses the current parent, the consumed distinction and the fresh set already determined for that birth.

For each child the parity of its fresh values must equal the parity of its inputs. A pair has even parity. For odd input the old minimum uses one fresh distinction. Each nonimplied row therefore extends every old allowed assignment. For the full static parity system the old loose distinctions have exactly the even boundary words on a connected presentation. To extend such a word, set all shared distinctions outside a spanning tree of old knots to zero and satisfy the old rows successively from terminal knots of the tree. A proper subset of the loose inputs is thus free. Any prescribed even word on a nonimplied child extends first across the remaining old inputs and then across other children's disjoint fresh sets. Its local projection is full even parity, which is nonempty, is constrained as a whole and becomes free on forgetting any place. Old projections persist. A sole closing row uses the entire even boundary. The selected activity lifts are particular states in these parity systems.

**Strict minimum and earliest sensing.** If every loose input is zero, every child under A113 gets zero on its whole support, whatever the first roster. At the next planning stage the old knots are exhausted and every live parent reads cold. Induction gives the same conclusion at every subsequent completed stage. Stored occupied distinctions can still be present on old knots. Comparing two states *after* fixing the first-grown incidence isolates the second reading from the earlier change of roster. More generally, if two assignments on the same stage differ only on distinctions held entirely by exhausted knots, every live support and every consumed input agrees. For either fused rule the rosters can be coupled and so can all fresh lifts. Exhausted knots acquire no further distinctions. This coupling persists at every subsequent stage. Loading an old ring only after all its holders are exhausted therefore has no effect in this signature. On the once-loaded source, a fresh deposit born in the first plan can first affect a structural decision in the following plan.

**Cost.** For a child whose inherited inputs are zero, its smallest positive fresh weight is two. Writing one at each of two different children violates both rows. Once a pair at one child exists and both distinctions are loose, toggling them together is an allowed change and each singleton fails parity. The modified signature changes one local case of the extension table. The exactly-two rule adds a fresh-count condition. The new law uses the whole parent support to test activity. A law reading only the values of spent zero inputs cannot distinguish the ring load from the quiet source. The pair minimum assumes that old values stay fixed and the next structural decision reads only its own parents. ## The same-incidence comparison

Suppose a hot parent $v$ begins the round with $L\ge2$ loose distinctions. All its children are unary. Each has $v$ as its sole ordinary neighbour at birth. Its only candidates are its $L-1$ siblings, and all share $v$. No unheld larger group can contain one of these children, and no outside group uses one. The ordinary next proposals in this family are exactly the $\binom L2$ unordered pairs. Each child has
\[
 H=g(L)=\max(2,L-1)
\]
fresh distinctions, as computed before the next veto. The same sibling calculation holds at every later hot parent. The use of ordinary demand on the actual roster is the funding convention from the preceding feedback study. [Ordinary demand at unary children](../STATE-FEEDS-GROWTH-20261119-001/locality.md)

Start with every loose value zero. Every child of a cold parent stays cold, and a retained multi-parent child has only cold parents. After a fused birth round, erase the deposited pairs on the same incidence while retaining the original ring colours. Each individual erasure respects parity. If $r_v$ out of the $L$ siblings were warmed, the number of extra next births in this family is
\[
 \binom L2-\binom{L-r_v}{2}.
\]
Each refused pair replaces one joint birth by two unary births. Arbitrary remaining slots retain their unary births. The different families have disjoint proposals. In these two laws $r_v$ is either $L$ or zero on the prepared source. The exactly-two rule warms all $L$ when $L=2,3$. The any-pair rule warms all $L$.

There is also an exact count of fresh distinctions at the second birth layer. For a fully warmed family put $K=g(H)$. The warm side supplies $LHK$ fresh distinctions on $LH$ unary births. The erased side has $\binom L2$ binary births and $L(H-L+1)$ unary births. Each erased-side unary child again needs $K$ fresh. A binary child with parents $i,j$ has $2(H-1)$ sibling incidences. For each of the $L-2$ other parents $k$, the three binary children with parent sets $\{i,j\},\{i,k\},\{j,k\}$ form one unheld group, replacing two of these incidences by one demand. This lists every unheld group through the binary child. Pairwise intersecting one- or two-element parent sets with no common member must consist of precisely those three pairs. Its fresh count is $\max(1,2H-L)$. Thus the warm-minus-erased fresh count is
\[
 \binom L2\bigl[2K-\max(1,2H-L)\bigr].
\]
When $L$ is $2,3,4$, the respective additional knot counts are $1,3,6$ and the additional fresh counts are $2,9,12$. These are changes in the number of physical distinctions created. The occupied fresh count in the *first* plan comes separately from the pair assignments.

## Every later completed layer

For the any-pair rule, each of the $L$ unary children of a hot parent is hot and has occupied loose distinctions. A zero input gives two occupied fresh distinctions, a one gives one. Along such descendants the next loose counts are $g(L),g^2(L),\ldots$, reaching two after finitely many steps. Two then persists, giving two hot children per knot per layer. For a source parent with $L\ge2$ the hot family count in layer $t\ge1$ is exactly $\prod_{j=0}^{t-1}g^j(L)$. Offspring of distinct hot parents cannot join one another because a joint proposal through any hot parent is vetoed. Cold parents cannot introduce any other occupied fresh distinction in the prepared examples.

For the exactly-two rule one additional condition determines persistence. Let $w$ of the $L$ loose distinctions at the hot parent be occupied. If $L=2,3$, then $g(L)=2$ and all $L$ children are hot. If $L\ge4$, exactly $w$ children are hot. Each of them spends a one and receives one occupied loose distinction. Thus a high-count parent with $w>0$ has a continuing hot descendant until the finite decrease of $L$ brings it to $L\le3$. Once there, all its descendants are hot. A parent with $L\ge4$ and $w=0$ has no hot child under this rule. This condition prevents extrapolating persistence to every hot parent of every source. The statements assume the stage is funded. The two policies give each child at least its ordinary next demand, and next-plan vetoes can only reduce that demand. The presence of a continuing family with $L\ge2$ ensures that at least two children form and excludes the sole closure at every following stage.

On the bare ring, write $t=0$ for the source. Both fused rules give $5\cdot2^t$ hot knots newly formed in layer $t\ge1$. After same-incidence erasure in layer one, every subsequent live parent stays cold. Each of the five sibling families starts with two candidates holding two loose distinctions each. When their candidate relation is a path on $m$ knots, the $m-1$ consecutive pairs form and the two end knots each have a unary child. The new candidate relation is a path on $m+1$ knots, with fresh count two at every knot by demand and the unary floor. Thus the erased comparison has $5(t+1)$ births at every $t\ge1$. On the one-spare attached ring the exactly-two rule gives $5\cdot2^{t-1}$ hot births at $t\ge1$. The any-pair rule gives 17 at $t=1$, then $46\cdot2^{t-2}$ at $t\ge2$. The attached counts concern the hot part alone. The process keeps the original five occupied distinctions and repeatedly creates new occupied values. Its initial preparation happens once.

## Names, independent events and maximal orders

On a fixed complete-layer plan, fix the old input for each child and its fresh lift. The births use disjoint old loose distinctions and disjoint fresh sets. They can share a parent whose old values stay fixed. Therefore every permutation of the births yields identical named incidence and relative values with the prescribed names. A plan of $b$ distinct births has $b!$ such serializations. Every maximal serialization within this finite plan completes it, because any unfinished birth remains available. On the bare first layer there are $10!$ orders. Its ten unary children are individually determined by their old loose inputs and take their entire fresh pairs.

When physical input choices are free, every parent of a retained multi-parent birth is cold. At each such parent any two allocations agree after permuting its old loose zeros. A hot parent's unary children match by their actual old distinctions. Corresponding child lifts always have the same number of occupied fresh siblings. A permutation of the fresh set matches the chosen pair, singleton or empty set. At later steps a needed swap of current loose names interchanges equally coloured siblings from the preceding birth. It preserves that birth and every earlier coloured knot. Induction therefore gives an isomorphism of any two finite completed histories, allowing the initial equally coloured loose names to be transported. Fixing those names individually can distinguish allocations even at a cold source with two interchangeable loose names and both a joint and a unary role. Within a fresh set of $h>2$, no two-element subset is fixed by all permutations. For an odd input and even $h$, neither invariant constant word has odd parity. The exactly-two exception fixes its own entire fresh pair, including on the one-spare attached ring. The pre-existing odd-input rule still admits each fresh singleton. [Old-name and fresh-name comparisons](../STATE-FEEDS-GROWTH-20261119-001/selection.md)

On the loaded bare ring, its rotations and the independent swaps of the two old loose distinctions at each holder act transitively on the ten first children. Each has exactly two fresh distinctions. A single deterministic first-layer output invariant under these input symmetries must warm all ten or none, using 20 occupied fresh distinctions for any direct response. This is an exact simultaneous count at completion of that first layer before any second births. A partial realization after $b$ first-plan births has $2b$ new occupied distinctions. Arbitrary pauses between independent families at different depths need not put every first child at its growing end together. A weaker, coordinated relation could choose one of ten tied children, spend two in total, and veto that child's unique sibling pair. Such choices give one orbit of alternatives. They require selecting a winner across children and give up the deterministic invariant first-layer output. In an $L$-sibling family at least $L-1$ warm children suffice and are necessary to veto every pair, since two cold siblings retain their pairing.

**Unconstrained delayed events fail even under fairness.** Give the first ten bare unary children their minimum lifts. Permit a separate pair-writing event at each eligible child while its two fresh distinctions remain loose, and permit the next growth plan before the events. Writing first gives 20 second births. Growing first gives 15, consuming every formerly eligible fresh pair and disabling each of its events. In a single sibling family the two outcomes are four unary births, or one binary birth and two unaries. Both finite orders admit fair continuations. An event enabled only during a finite opportunity can be skipped even under strong fairness. A single-coordinate toggle would meanwhile violate parity at its holder. The two-coordinate update is an atomic allowed state change if performed after birth.

Fusion supplies the causal order between recording the child's state and using it. A stricter two-event implementation can instead make an uncommitted child's state unavailable to every dependent structural read, including computations of funding and demand. At a completed birth roster made initially with minimum lifts, the pending children on the named sources can be recognized from incidence and values. Such a child is cold, has a single shared zero leading to an exhausted hot parent, and has the eligible number of loose distinctions. Every pending child has that signature since all parents have finished and all fresh distinctions are still loose. Conversely that signature identifies an eligible unary whose extra pair has yet to be occupied. One write disables its own guard. Distinct pending children admit independent writes. On the bare ring the five sibling families have no joint future candidates between families, so whole family plans can also be interleaved independently after the common first roster. These statements compare completed local plans at the indicated depth. General partial rosters introduce the further question of whether all potential candidates and all affected funding decisions are known. The original complete-round rule supplies that information. Fairness by itself does not.

## Reading one more holder

A different structural reader can keep the strict minimum extension. In addition to its parent’s own activity, let it test whether that parent has exactly one ordinary neighbour which is exhausted and hot. All holders on each of the prepared sources are initially live, so this extra test makes no first-plan change. At the next completed layer a unary child of a hot original knot reads that exhausted neighbour, even with every fresh distinction quiet. Those sibling pairs are then refused. On the fixed first incidence, erasing the five *original* occupied distinctions changes the second decision by the same $\sum_v\binom{L_v}{2}$ as the any-pair rule. This reader needs zero fresh writes and one additional ordinary holder step. It observes the original stored mark through the child. Under the stated direct-end comparison only a changed current parent's own support supplies the second reading. The transfer cost consequently depends on which supports are available to the reader. Neither version uses a ring label, a destination, an elapsed-time unit, or a new external preparation.
