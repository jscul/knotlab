# What an individual mark and a separated part reveal

## The greatest possible distance between two members of a legal triple

In any reachable presentation, including unrestricted mixed counts, two members of a legal triple have bond distance at most **13**. Equality is attained. The bound holds as well for the operands of a legal two-parent join. It supplies a necessary test for each pair in a proposed triple.

Use the classical wired incidence graph, with junctions as ordinary vertices and all loose ends joined to one exterior vertex. Let the two distinct holders be \(a,b\) and take a common neighbor \(u\). The marked distinctions are loose at \(a,b\). The cut around the connected set \(\{a,u,b\}\) has size at most \(d(a)+d(u)+d(b)-4\le14\), since two internal meetings each remove two contributions. Keep the component of its complement containing the exterior. Move any other components of its complement to the ordinary side. Each moved component meets that connected ordinary side and has no link to the retained exterior component. The new cut is a subset of the first one and still contains both loose marks. Both sides are connected, so the cut is a bond. This gives \(K\le14\), hence \(D\le13\). The two quantified statements use only the legal common-neighbor condition. Neither requires the rest of the growth to be regular.

For equality take the finite drawing on the triples of integer coordinates in \(\{0,1,2\}\times\{0,1,2\}\times\{0,1\}\), omitting \((2,2,1)\). The drawing specifies the history. It supplies no coordinates to the reader. Build sites in increasing coordinate sum, add one input from each immediate predecessor and three fresh outputs, and dedicate one of those outputs to each increasing coordinate. Parents in two different directions have the site lowered in both directions as a witness. When there are three parents the omitted new site is their only possible common neighbor in the integer grid. This proves all steps legal. A fresh distinction from each parent of the missing site gives a legal triple. Its first two parents \((1,2,1),(2,1,1)\) are terminal six-place sites.

The 17 independent site equations give 131,071 nonzero cuts to check. Enumeration of shores with both sides connected finds 36,250 bonds. The minimum containing the designated pair has size 14. A second loose bit at the second parent has minimum six with that parent's original mark. The pair therefore changes from bond distance 13 to five if the first mark is swapped with this spare bit. The image uses the second parent twice and is forbidden. [Exact cuts, history and swapped marks](horizon.json) · [Direct evaluator](check_horizon.py)

This same swap preserves the whole table clipped through \(D=4\). Indeed a bond containing a loose bit at either terminal site contains that site on its ordinary shore. A singleton shore costs six. A two-site connected shore containing it costs at least seven. Three or more sites export at least seven distinctions, since the last three supply nine outputs and at most two of them meet another of those three. Thus every other entry in either swapped row is at least \(K=6\). All 1,275 clipped pair entries agree. The full matrix changes in 76 entries. Together with the upper theorem this proves the sharp distance cutoff five for its entire class and for its all-three-fresh subclass.

There is a smaller comparison with all nonseed sites making three outputs and maximum arity five. Use \(\{0,1\}^3\) with its last site removed. The same swap at two five-place parents changes a legal triple and agrees on every entry through \(D=3\). For sources meeting the three structural hypotheses of the upper theorem, if *both* presentations have maximum arity five, \(D=4\) suffices. Each source star is forced through \(K=5\), and any strict rival space would have an extra generating star of size at most five. The size-five case of the proof excludes it. A rival star in the same space must be an ordinary source star or the sum of the seed and a four-place star. The sum shares two seed distinctions and cannot be an actual rival star. This proves sharpness in that two-sided degree-five class. For an unrestricted rival the general theorem supplies \(D=5\). [One-sided proof and size-five case](high_inverse.md)

## Where a mixed obstruction would have to be

An isometry through \(K=6\) with opposite marks must have a five- or six-place junction in **each retained part containing the marks**. Arbitrarily many large junctions elsewhere can be left attached in the original tables. Any triple containing a separating distinction, or split by one, is forbidden on both sides.

Apply the bounded separator theorem with \(L=6\), since every site of either presentation has at most six distinctions. It identifies each single-distinction separation and both sides from the clipped table. For surviving marks keep the portion containing them after the shared separating distinctions are severed. Retain its boundary distinctions. Each remains locally loose. The one-distinction attachment law makes its remeasured table the restriction of the original. The ordinary six-cycle of any genuine triple stays in the part, and a witness joining two of its holders cannot be in a discarded branch. The local verdict therefore equals the original one. A retained part can start with four distinctions. To apply the ordinary three-seed theorem, put the two local parts on the same bit names by their induced correspondence. For each local start with four distinctions, remember its incoming original bridge bit. Every such anchor is a locally loose boundary bit on both sides, and none of the anchors is a mark. Use their union, containing at most two bits. Add a ternary junction on each chosen anchor and two new bits. On a side whose start already has three distinctions make its original local steps and then these unary additions. For a four-place start begin with the new ternary junction on its incoming bit and grow the four-place site from it on that one input. Continue in the original restricted birth order, then add the other selected ternary junction if needed. Local pair witnesses survive restriction, by the bridge argument, so both resulting histories are legal. The added junctions each meet just one site and cannot change the local verdict.

The pair minima of the two padded pieces still agree through six. For an old bit \(x\ne t\) and a new bit at an anchor \(t\), the one-bit attachment formula gives \(K_{\mathrm{new}}=K(x,t)+1\). A cap bit has minimum three with the anchor or the other cap bit. Between two distinct added caps use the same formula twice. Thus no formerly pooled high value becomes visible. If either original marked part had maximum arity four, the padded presentation on that side would also have maximum arity four. The preceding all-size small-arity theorem would then identify its entire incidence in the other padded presentation through \(K=6\). Opposite original verdicts would be impossible. ## A short-bond signature that looks like part of a large junction

The design advisor suggested that three distinctions on alternate meetings could have pair minima six with no common six-bit bond. A direct replay confirms this part. In the seven-site comparison with three-fresh unary junctions and two-fresh pair joins, all three entries are six but the least bond containing the whole group has size nine. In the capped comparison the chosen three inputs instead have a joint minimum of six. The proposed equality of the six-by-six signatures fails. On the uncapped side each loose mark has one off-diagonal cross-entry six, where the proposed capped counterpart reads eight. None of the 36 permutations matching the two marked groups identifies these six-by-six matrices. This is a real obstruction to inferring a joint bond from its three pair minima. The two histories have different widths and are used only to test that inference. [Named matrices and exact joint minima](advisor_blocks.json) · [Replay](check_advisor.py)
