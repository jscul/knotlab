# One live betweenness test cannot guarantee alignment

**Theorem.** From the eight original cut distinctions, a rule that sees only the current table of scalar bond distances and has one live betweenness test cannot guarantee the first aligned mixed round by the individual moves of the connected growth law. It can be prevented from making even its second permanent junction correctly. This holds if it may choose a replacement test from the still-live table, with automatic release of the first pair. The proof grants it recognition of every original as such, the root and its two parity outputs individually, and the temporary endpoints individually.

## Question and source

In conventional terms, the state relation on eight binary variables is \(S=\{0,1,x,\bar x,y,\bar y,z,\bar z\}\), where the coordinates index the eight points of the Boolean cube and the nonconstant rows are the coordinate cuts. These indices describe the construction and grade its answers. A controller receives arbitrary names and the minimum arity minus one of a constrained nonempty projection through each pair that becomes free on forgetting any single variable. Only arities of at least three count (K1–K3). An absent bond gives infinity. The controller's allowed actions transport under every renaming of its current observation. Every permitted resolution must terminate with one compatible literal target.

The [original attachment](../../CUBIC-FROM-A-KNOT-20261117-001/CUBIC-FROM-A-KNOT-20261117-001-P001_local_growth/cube_interface.md) reuses a chosen original \(r\) in a three-place root junction \(r\oplus u\oplus v=0\) with two fresh permanent outputs. The first other site needs a true original neighbor of \(r\) and one root port. All three next sites sharing a root port belong to the prescribed eight-site block. For the obstruction, treat **any** unused true neighbor with **any** of the three ports as a successful second move. This enlarges the success set. A commitment using a nonneighbor cannot be part of the requested aligned assembly.

The exact terminal states give a separate check on that necessity. The complete short-support census for the literal target has three full even-parity ternary projections, only one of which reuses one original. It is the root star. Of the 19 full even-parity projections on four identities, exactly four meet that first star in a single bit. Three use one additional original and two fresh bits. Their original positions are exactly 001, 010 and 100 relative to the root. The fourth uses three additional originals and supplies neither of the required fresh bits. None uses just a root port and three fresh bits. All later source moves preserve the permanent marginal, so a wrong second move cannot disappear into an equivalent presentation of the endpoint states. [Terminal projection check](intervals.json)

A test on distinct permanent identities \(a,b\) gives its fresh endpoints \(p,q\) the same two constraints as in the [successful simultaneous construction](../../BOOTSTRAP-ALIGNMENT-20261117-001/BOOTSTRAP-ALIGNMENT-20261117-001-P001_distance_start/proof.md), namely \(B(p,a,q),B(p,b,q)\). Equal ends require both middles to take their common value. Opposed ends extend every permanent assignment. A single probe, its erasure, its replacement, or one legal permanent junction is a move. The permanent junction may be chosen with the test live. The replacements include restarting the same physical test using fresh endpoints. A rule can exclude a previous choice whenever the *current observable data* make it identifiable. The physical record of the tested middles is hidden. An instrument returning their identities or comparing a proposed test to a hidden previous choice offers another operation.

## What a live test can distinguish

Before a test, all old pairs have bond distance two. After rooting, each root output has distance two to the root and the other output, and three to every other original. These are the complete permanent distances from the [initial table calculation](../../BOOTSTRAP-ALIGNMENT-20261117-001/BOOTSTRAP-ALIGNMENT-20261117-001-P001_distance_start/seed.json). Testing cannot change them. Every single test endpoint is free above the entire permanent relation, so a new minimal constrained support involving it also contains its mate. In particular, no new ternary bond can reduce a permanent-pair entry from three to two. All binary projections in the extended relation are free.

Write \(U\) for the seven unused originals. The following classifications give the **exact** scalar distances from each test endpoint to every member of \(U\).

| Tested middles | Unused originals at distance two from either end | All other unused originals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two originals \(a,b\) | The interval \(J(a,b)\) on the original cuts, intersected with \(U\) | Distance three |
| One original \(b\) and one fresh root output | Only \(b\), if unused | Distance three |
| The two fresh root outputs | None | Distance three |

For two original middles, \(J(a,b)\) fixes the coordinates where \(a,b\) agree. When the ends agree, every point in this interval takes their common value. For a point \(w\) outside the interval, both its values occur with either common endpoint value. Its three-place projection with the two endpoints is free. Take \(d\) obtained by changing \(w\) in a coordinate on which \(a,b\) differ. Then \(d\) is outside too. Whenever \(a=b\), the allowed row is constant or a cut in a coordinate where they agree. Thus \(w=d\) on those rows. The four-place projection on \(p,q,w,d\) is constrained and every deletion is free. This gives distance exactly three. The same argument applies if the root was among the tested originals.

For a probe on a fresh output and an original \(b\), the fresh output can take either value at every old row. With \(p=q=t\), precisely the old rows with \(b=t\) remain available. If \(w\ne b\), the pair \(b,w\) is free, so \(p,q,w\) is free. There is some other original \(d\) such that \(b,w,d\) is a ternary bond, because every old pair lies in one. Substituting the common endpoint value for \(b\) rules out some patterns on \(p,q,w,d\). Each deletion is free, giving exact distance three. For a test on both fresh outputs, equal ends force \(r=0\), with either value of the ends. A ternary bond through \(r,w\) supplies a \(d\) and a missing row at \(r=0\) by complement symmetry of the eight old rows. The same four-place argument applies.

Consequently **any swap of two unused originals in the same column class of the table above preserves the *entire* scalar matrix**. Their distances to the seven other originals and to both root outputs are already known individually. Their distances to both test endpoints agree. Every entry not incident to those two identities is fixed by the swap. For idle states all seven unused originals form one such class. This remains true when all the generous role markers in the theorem are supplied. The permanent neighbor relation used for grading is generally finer than these observed classes.

## A set of situations that can always recur

Fix the root for the proof. Let \(N(r)\) be its three true original neighbors and call \(r,u,v\) the three root ports. There are 45 unordered tests on the ten permanent distinctions. Put aside the nine tests consisting of a root port and a member of \(N(r)\). Give the controller success on entering any of these nine, even if the table alone would still leave it uncertain which member it tested. The other 36 tests and the idle state give 37 adverse situations.

In each adverse situation, **every nonempty observable class in \(U\) contains a nonneighbor**. Without a test all seven are interchangeable. If a fresh root output tested one unused original, that original is a nonneighbor by the definition of the adverse set, and the other six still include nonneighbors. A test confined to the root ports leaves all seven interchangeable. For a pair of old middles the interval has size two, four or eight. A two-place interval with the root and a neighbor was set aside. An interval of size two away from the root cannot contain two root-neighbors, since those differ in two coordinates. A four-place interval containing the root includes its diagonal. One excluding the root includes a nonneighbor, and its four-place complement contains the root and a diagonal. When the interval contains the root its complement also contains a nonneighbor. An eight-place interval contains every unused original. These cases cover both the interval and its complement after excluding the root.

Consider any move selected from such a table.

- For a permanent commitment, the proposed unused original can be swapped with a nonneighbor from its class, fixing the selected root port. The permuted action is allowed by the same rule and fails.
- Erasure returns to the adverse idle state. For a replacement or fresh test to enter the set-aside nine, it must choose exactly one root port and one true neighbor. For this form of move the same swap offers a nonneighbor, so the test can remain adverse. A test of any other form is adverse already. The argument permits direct replacement and correct automatic release of the actual live pair.
- Stopping before a compatible commitment fails the target. If the rule keeps taking tests instead, there is an infinite allowed play in the adverse set. This argument concerns worst-case finite termination. A record of which original choices have been tried would change the equivalences used here.

At the bare seed any old pair can be exchanged for an antipodal one in the initial scalar table. On that trial the interval is all eight, and **all 45 scalar entries on the ten distinctions equal two**. Even granting recognition of the original set and of both temporary endpoints, the controller can again be made to choose an antipodal pair. If it places its root while that test is live, the previous middles are antipodal for any choice of root, never root and neighbor. This enters the adverse set. Placing the root without a test enters its idle state directly. Thus the obstruction starts at the actual seed, and covers interleaving, fresh names, the full distance table, all three incoming roles and simultaneous replacement.

An explicit ambiguity occurs with root 000 and a test on 000 and 011. Swapping 001 with 011 preserves every current scalar entry, the root ports and both temporary endpoints. It exchanges a neighbor with a point two steps away. Committing to the latter still admits the fresh parity extension and preserves all old rows. It prevents the resulting fixed incidence from being an aligned target. Using success of the intended final placement to reject the action at execution would supply the missing neighbor test.

## Positive controls and scope

When a live test really is the root paired with a true neighbor, precisely those two originals make up its interval. The full table allows a safe second attachment in this case. From the bare root, independent uniform trials choosing one of the seven unused originals have success probability \(3/7\) each. With automatic release of a failed pair, the probability of still missing after \(n\) trials is \((4/7)^n\). This gives almost-sure discovery of a correct **second junction**. It supplies no finite worst-case bound.

Giving the controller both current tested middles and the ability to recognize the temporary endpoints changes this prefix. Test the root and an unused \(b\). A two-place interval certifies \(b\). In a four-place interval, the other two members besides \(r,b\) are both genuine neighbors. An eight-place interval identifies \(b\) as the unique opposite. Directly replace that test by testing the root with a different genuine unused original. Its interval must have size two or four. This guarantees a correct second junction with at most two tests. Requiring a new test physically to differ from a hidden former test is also extra information when those old middles cannot be selected from the present table. The established positive result for complete alignment uses the predecessor's simultaneous tests.

## Computational checks

The [literal table census](check_first.py) enumerates full binary state relations and computes all minimal nonfree supports, including entries above three and missing entries. It enumerates four hidden presentations of the cut relation, every root and every probe pair, verifies truth projections, computes exact scalar isometries, and grades possible commitments and erasures over them. It found three bare and seven rooted observation classes with a live probe. The literal unmarked table guarantees a rooted second move exactly in the root-with-true-neighbor case, three of the 45 tested pairs at a fixed root. The other six set-aside cases in the proof are deliberately overgenerous. [Tables and counterexamples](first.json)

The [adversarial reachability check](check_game.py) independently constructs the action orbits and every permitted response to a move, including incompatible commitments. It also checks the stronger version with marked probe identities against the explicit strategy above. The [truth-column check](check_intervals.py) verifies the class argument directly on each unused original. On eight originals it finds a possible adverse resolution in all 396 replacement-action orbits across the 37 adverse situations. On sixteen cut distinctions the same first-neighbor argument passes 1,622 such orbits. On four distinctions the analogous adverse set fails its own criterion. The latter two are size controls for the argument about the second commitment. [Reachability certificate](game.json) · [Size controls](intervals.json)

In the finite game there are 397 named physical positions and 19 types after symmetry of the source. The whole tables give 12 observations when the controller sees no marks, including the idle cases. Under atomic replacement, disclosing only the two tested middles makes five moves necessary and sufficient in the worst case. Disclosing the two fresh endpoints as well reduces this to four. A move here includes placing the root and committing its neighbor. Requiring erasure in a separate step defeats even that current-role information. All of these finite game outputs concern the prefix through the first correct nonroot join.

The paper's theorem concerns the connected single-junction law and the two original betweennesses per test. The earlier 28-pair construction kept many comparisons visible and prepared a bounded assembly update. The least number of live pairs sufficient for entirely serial alignment is open.
