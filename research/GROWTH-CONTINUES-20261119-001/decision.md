## What makes an entire growth close

At a nonterminal reached end with at least three live junctions, compatibility of *every* pair suffices for closure. If one old block holds the whole end of size $m$, the following layers consist of the pairs, triples, and successive subsets of those $m$ labels, ending with a single junction. If no block holds the whole end, all live junctions join together once and the new junction is terminal. A one-parent junction would force its parent to be common to the entire end in this case, so every live junction has at least two parents and exactly its needed one loose distinction. [Proof, including the two-junction exception](../../proofs/GROWTH-CONTINUES-20261119-001/GROWTH-CONTINUES-20261119-001-P001_continuation/proof.md)

The single block of nine at age 14 on 00 gives its last birth at 22 and its first empty round at 23. The block of 781 at age eight on 04 gives 304,590 births at nine, its last birth at 788 and its first empty round at 789. Its finite tail itself contains larger and larger groups that need at least three blocks and have zero replaceable parents. In a layer of $j$-subsets, a group has $j+1$ parents, each old block covers at most two of them, and a potential replacement from outside meets at most two. For $j\ge4$ the group needs exactly $\lceil(j+1)/2\rceil$ blocks and has zero replacements. This finite example shows why even repeated increases in the observed group size leave the long-term verdict open in the absence of a continuation certificate. All twelve undecided starts have a connected growing end and already made a round consisting entirely of junctions with at least two parents. Subsequent open rounds cannot restart the one-parent mechanism. For those twelve the remaining question has an exact form: **does the entire family of children ever become pairwise intersecting in its parents?** If it does, the complete-end criterion gives closure. If it never does, no closing all-parent child can form. The full finite windows are in the [archive table](starts.md).
