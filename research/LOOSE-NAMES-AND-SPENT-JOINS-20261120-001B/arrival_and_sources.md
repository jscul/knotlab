# Arrival, ancestry pace and recurring release

The rule gives a paid common descendant at step two on both of the previous equal-reading trees. It also preserves the ordinary recurring sources on seven and 21 knots. A smaller pair of lawful trees nevertheless gives **two steps or permanent separation** for the same disagreement and parity bond readings. This holds under both knot laws.

## When the spent witnesses can act

At a complete funded cut every formerly loose name was used once. A present live newborn meets exactly its actual parents, through old loose names. Distinct newborn holders of the same age have disjoint sets of distinctions. Thus a spent holder that directly witnesses a current live member must have provided it an input on the immediately preceding step. From age two onward such parents were themselves born together. Two of them cannot share any physical distinction. Consequently the new eligibility test may act on the supplied start and at age one. At every age ≥2 the active family grows by ordinary parental overlaps, although the earlier rosters and budgets may have changed. Forecasting at the supplied cut must include the age-one possibility.

This yields an exact delay test. Take two live original marks R,S. If the initial plan stalls there is no positive birth. Their first children establish a meeting at age one exactly when they share a child. Otherwise take their two complete first descendant sets X,Y. In the live age-one co-input graph H₁ join any two actual donors in a common **funded** proposal, allowing both the ordinary groups and the extra two-witness groups. Their remaining delay is the distance between X and Y in H₁, so their overall delay is `1+dist_H₁(X,Y)`; separate parts have no common descendant. For the proof of this last identity, for each paid parent set include every pair, and for the next cut use the ordinary relation of sharing a parent. Parents of all children of a set give its closed co-input neighborhood; children of all parents of a set give its next closed neighborhood. By the established inclusive-descent argument the distance decreases by exactly one per continuing cut. A sole final parity child counts when it is the first common birth. If a terminal cut has no two live queried marks, there is no such query. Counting initial co-input distance alone would miss the two-original positive-age example. ## Sharp pace for common ancestry

For a common descendant reached in t≥1 actual parent steps, two of its original ancestors are at most `4t−1` ordinary holder meetings apart in the supplied initial holdings. A first child has parent diameter at most three: two ordinary co-inputs are at most two apart, and any two on opposite sides of a new spent pair have a three-meeting route. Take the two full age-one descendant sets of the two queried originals. By the exact delay formula their age-one co-input distance is at most t−1. Choose a shortest route of at most t age-one children C₁,…,Cⱼ between them. For two consecutive children one can choose an initial parent from each such that those parents coincide or share a physical name. In an ordinary age-one group they share an actual old parent. In an added group the chosen parents are the same spent witness or the two directly meeting spent witnesses. Within each Cᵢ, travel between its two selected initial parents in at most three initial meetings, including the chosen source at the first and destination at the last. This produces a physical initial route of length at most `3j+(j−1)≤4t−1`. The t=1 case uses the single first child directly. This is an ancestry bound on a complete history, independent of a reader's ability to detect distant edits.

The bound is attained for every t. Make t separate sections of the three-meeting form `aᵢ–wᵢ–zᵢ–bᵢ`. Give each of the two spent holders wᵢ,zᵢ one further live terminal neighbor, so it is three-place. Between sections add the single shared name `bᵢ–aᵢ₊₁`. Give each live holder two loose names. The original physical structure is a tree and the separation of a₁,bₜ is `3t+t−1`. Within each section the ordinary groups are its within-side pairs, and the separating wᵢ–zᵢ group Gᵢ uses both sides. The two payments per donor fund both groups without a unary remainder. No paid child spans the old name `bᵢ–aᵢ₊₁`, which remains separating. Its formerly live endpoints each have their two live children, including Gᵢ and Gᵢ₊₁. Therefore these consecutive groups are co-input neighbors at age one. A common descendant follows in at most t steps from the start, with every new demand prepaid. The just-proved bound forbids an earlier one. Four literal preparations attained ordinary distances 3,7,11,15 at parent steps 1,2,3,4, under both laws.
