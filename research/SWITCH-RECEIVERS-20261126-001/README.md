# Which receivers switch their way of growing on what is at the far end of a line?

Epic `SWITCH-RECEIVERS-20261126-001`, direction 1 of the close of
[`SHORT-AGAIN-20261126-001`](../SHORT-AGAIN-20261126-001/decision.md), taken up under the owner's standing rule (a
selected direction is run). A check by the thread, not a registered study; computed; referee pending. Program:
[`receivers.py`](receivers.py).

> **Referee R1 (confirmed with fixes, one Block): see the notice in [the decision](decision.md).** The switch is not a
> reading of the far site's loose count in general; the counts below stand.

## Why

Under the cap of three, by a meeting through a line of link knots, one receiver of 47 (three knots each sharing a
distinction with the other two, holding one, two and two loose distinctions, sited at the knot holding one) grows
3, 4, 5, … own knots a round until round 2d + 1, and then switches to a faster way if the knot at the far end holds
two loose distinctions; with three, or with a stub, it never does. It gets in through the cap: the link side's knots
beside it come to be owed four and are given three. Is this one start's accident, or a kind of receiver?

## What is read

- **Receivers:** every sited start of one to four knots of the census of parts that copy themselves (1 + 4 + 39 + 599
  = 643), and the bench's ring, two-knot part and source: 646. Every one, not a sample.
- **Senders:** one of each kind of far end the switch study told apart: a one-knot start (its site holds three loose
  distinctions), a two-knot start sited at a knot holding two, a path of three knots sited at its middle knot holding
  one, the switch receiver itself (sited at its knot holding one), and a stub.
- **Join:** a meeting through d − 1 link knots, d = 2 to 5.
- **Laws**, a short knot waiting: the cap of three, caps of four and five, the record's "one fewer than asked", and
  the ordinary count. 16 rounds or 3,000 knots.
- **Read**, per receiver: its own births in each round with each sender; the first round they differ between two
  senders (the receiver **reads the far end** from that round); the senders that agree through the rounds read; and
  whether any own knot of the receiver was short before that round. A receiver **switches** when it reads the far end
  first at a round after 2d (late for its distance: no change can have crossed the line sooner at one step a round,
  let alone at two), with no own knot short before.

## Taken from the advisor's review

[Question](advisor_question.md), [answer](advisor_answer.md) (the Fable model named in the program's rule answered;
a colleague's view to check). Its reading of the round: each pass along the line raises what the link knots are
owed by one, and each turn at an end costs a round, so a cap of k is crossed at (k − 1)d + (k − 2); a far site with
two loose distinctions turns the pass back because it is itself short once, a site with three absorbs it. Taken:

- **A cap of five** as well, where that reading predicts rounds 11 and 15 at d = 2 and 3; and a census of the 48
  senders of the jam study against the switch receiver under the caps of four and five at d = 2 and 3, since under a
  larger cap the senders that turn the pass back may be others.
- **Three bands** for the first round r a receiver reads the far end: r < d (impossible at one step a round: a check
  of the code), d ≤ r ≤ 2d (a reading on the way out) and r > 2d (an echo, the switch). All are reported.
- **The switch by its behaviour:** no own knot short before r, own knots short in alternate rounds after.
- **The stub as common baseline:** each sender's first difference from the stub, beside the first difference
  between any two.
- **Censoring:** births compared only up to the last round both runs read.
- **Controls, for the switching receivers found:** the far end's site changed in the middle of the run (one more
  loose distinction after round t₀), read no earlier than t₀ + d; and the switch receiver as sender, both ends read.

Not taken: 22 rounds for every receiver (only for the switching receivers in the second pass); the depth of each
sender's nearest short knot before the run (read from the runs instead).

Seen in a trial after the review, before the full run: under a cap of five the switch receiver reads nothing from
the five senders at d = 2 or 3 through round 16.

## Expected (written before the full run, after two trial receivers at d = 3 under the cap of three)

Trials: the known switch receiver reads the far end at round 7 with three classes of sender (the two-knot sender;
the switch receiver as sender; the other three), no own knot short before. A four-knot receiver (a start of four
knots sited at a knot holding three) reads nothing: the same own births with every sender.

1. **Few switch.** Under the cap of three at d = 3, between 3 and 30 of the 646 receivers switch (the advisor: a
   range of ten can hardly fail; this one is narrower).
2. **They switch at an odd round, two rounds later a step, and the two-knot sender is apart.** In every receiver that
   switches the first difference is at round 7 or 9 at d = 3 and moves by two rounds a step at d = 2 to 5 (an even
   round would refute the reading of passes and turns), and the sender whose site holds two loose distinctions is in
   a class of its own or with the switch receiver as sender.
3. **The site holds one.** Every switching receiver's site holds one loose distinction.
4. **A cap of four** has fewer switching receivers than the cap of three, at 3d + 2 (checked only where 3d + 2 ≤ 16,
   d ≤ 4). **A cap of five**: among the 48 senders, some switch the switch receiver at 4d + 3 (rounds 11 and 15 at
   d = 2 and 3), the advisor's reading; I hold this weakly, since the five senders do not.
5. **Controls.** Under the ordinary count no receiver reads the far end at all by a meeting (the bench's result with a
   stub: own births are the same whatever the other is). Under the record's "one fewer", receivers that read the far
   end do so with an own knot waiting before (the short-again study's route), not as a switch.

## A rule read from the first pass, written before it was tested

The first pass ([What was found](#what-was-found), below) gave, under each cap, switching receivers that come in kinds
with the same own births with every sender. The largest kind under each cap is a start of three knots, each sharing
one distinction with the other two, two of them holding two loose distinctions and the site holding k − 2, so that
the site has exactly k places, the cap: (1, 2, 2) under the cap of three, (2, 2, 2) under four, (2, 2, 3) under five.
In each, the sender kept apart is the one whose site holds two loose distinctions.

**Rule, to be tested on caps and starts it was not read from:** under a cap of k, by a meeting, the start of three
mutually sharing knots holding two, two and k − 2 loose distinctions, sited at the last, switches at round 2d + 1
with the sender whose site holds two loose distinctions, and does not switch with the one-knot sender, the
middle-sited path or a stub. Predictions, before the run:

1. Under a cap of six, the start holding (2, 2, 4) sited at its 4-loose knot switches at rounds 5 and 7 at d = 2 and 3
   with the two-knot sender and with no other of the five; under a cap of seven, (2, 2, 5) likewise.
2. The same start under a cap one smaller or one larger does not switch with any of the five senders.
3. Changing the far site after round d (one more loose distinction) does not change the switch; changing it at or
   before round d − 1 cancels it (as under the cap of three: heard for t₀ ≤ 2, not for t₀ ≥ 3, at d = 3).

## What was found

**The census** ([`receivers_table.md`](receivers_table.md) by [`tally.py`](tally.py) on `receivers_runs.jsonl.gz`): 646
receivers, five senders, d = 2 to 5, five laws, 16 rounds or 3,000 knots; all read. "Reads the far end" is the first
round a receiver's own births differ between two senders, compared up to the last round both were read.

| law | d = 3: receivers that read the far end before d / d to 2d / after 2d / never | switch (after 2d, no own knot short before) at d = 2, 3, 4, 5 | first round of the switches at d = 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| cap of three | 0 / 1 / 6 / 639 | 6, 6, 6, 6 | 7 (all six) |
| cap of four | 0 / 2 / 10 / 634 | 9, 10, 10, 10 | 7 (all ten) |
| cap of five | 0 / 14 / 31 / 601 | 21, 21, 19, 18 | 7 (16), 8 (3), 9, 11 |
| the record's one fewer | 0 / 91 / 0 / 555 | 0 at every d | — |
| the ordinary count | 0 / 0 / 0 / 646 | 0 at every d | — |

- **No receiver reads the far end before round d**, under any law (the check of the code the advisor asked for).
- **Under the cap of three, six receivers switch, and they are one kind.** All six have the same own births with
  every sender; one is the switch receiver of the jam study (three mutually sharing knots holding one, two and two
  loose distinctions, sited at the knot holding one) and five are starts of four knots. All switch at round 2d + 1 at
  every distance (5, 7, 9, 11), with the senders in the same three classes: the two-knot sender alone, the switch
  receiver as sender alone (two rounds later), and the one-knot sender, the middle-sited path and the stub together.
  Every one's site holds one loose distinction.
- **Under larger caps other receivers switch, in kinds.** Ten receivers in three kinds under a cap of four, 21 in
  eight kinds under five; still mostly at round 2d + 1, but under the cap of five some at even rounds (8 at d = 3).
- **Under the record's one fewer** receivers read the far end only in the band d to 2d (91 at d = 3), never after
  2d with no own knot short: the waiting route of the short-again study, not a switch. **Under the ordinary count**
  no receiver reads the far end at all.

**The controls** ([`controls.py`](controls.py), `controls_runs.jsonl.gz`), on the six under the cap of three at d = 3:

- **The switch is settled by what the far end does in its first d rounds.** Giving the two-knot sender's site one
  more loose distinction after round t₀ cancels the switch (heard at round 7) for t₀ = 0, 1, 2 and changes nothing
  for t₀ = 3 to 8 (through round 16). With the one-knot sender (three loose, so four) or a stub, no change is heard at
  any t₀. The advisor's reading has a pass from the receiver turned back at the far site; but the far site acts in
  round 1 and leaves, and its slack then (what it holds less what it owes: 1, 2, 0 or 3) does not sort the 48
  senders into those that switch the receiver and those that do not ([`reflect.py`](reflect.py), `reflect.json`).
  Over those 48, what sorts them is the site's loose count: two, a switch at 2d + 1 (18 of 18).
- **The switch lasts.** To 22 rounds (or 6,000 knots) each of the six stays in the way it took: 17, 18, … 24 own
  knots a round the slow way, 113, 130, … 244 the fast way.
- **Both ends.** With the switch receiver at both ends, both leave the slow way in the same round (9).

**The advisor's reading of the round, under other caps** (`senders.py`, `senders_runs.jsonl.gz`; the switch receiver
against the 48 senders of the jam study): under a cap of four it switches at 3d + 2 (rounds 8 and 11) with 5 senders,
as that reading has it; under a cap of five at round 9 at d = 2 (7 senders; the reading said 11) and at none through
round 16 at d = 3. So (k − 1)d + (k − 2) holds for k = 3 and 4 on this receiver and fails at k = 5.

**The rule read from the first pass, tested** ([`rule.py`](rule.py), `rule_runs.jsonl.gz`; starts holding four and five
loose distinctions are outside the census):

| receiver (loose counts, site first) | cap | d = 2 | d = 3 | the senders' classes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1, 2, 2 | 3 | 5 | 7 | two-knot alone; switch receiver alone; the rest |
| 2, 2, 2 | 4 | 5 | 7 | the same |
| 3, 2, 2 | 5 | 5 | 7 | the same |
| **4, 2, 2** | **6** | **5** | **7** | the same |
| **5, 2, 2** | **7** | **5** | **7** | two-knot alone (and the switch receiver alone at d = 2) |

1. *Predicted:* under caps of six and seven the new starts switch at rounds 5 and 7 with the two-knot sender. Holds.
   Worded "with no other of the five", it fails: the switch receiver as sender is in a class of its own too, as it
   was under the smaller caps, which the wording left out.
2. *Predicted:* under a cap one smaller or one larger, no switch. Holds one smaller (no switch, or an early
   reading with an own knot short first; a cap of two was not run) and one larger for (1, 2, 2) and (2, 2, 2).
   Fails one larger for (3, 2, 2), (4, 2, 2) and (5, 2, 2): under a cap of a + 3 they switch at about three rounds
   a step (rounds 7 and 10, or 6 and 9), and the sender kept apart is then the one-knot sender, whose site holds
   three.
3. *Predicted:* changing the far site after round d does not change the switch, before it cancels it. Holds for all
   five as measured (heard at round 7 for t₀ ≤ 2, not for t₀ ≥ 3, at d = 3): a change acting in round d is heard,
   one acting in round d + 1 is not.

So the rule holds where it was meant, at two caps it was not read from: **under a cap of k, a start of three mutually
sharing knots whose site has exactly k places, the other two holding two loose distinctions, reads at round 2d + 1
whether the knot at the far end of the line holds two loose distinctions**, from what the far end does in its first
d rounds. And a second family shows: the same starts under a cap of a + 3 read whether the far site holds three, at
about three rounds a step.

## Expectations, and how they fared

1. *Few switch under the cap of three (3 to 30).* Holds: 6.
2. *An odd round, two rounds later a step, the two-knot sender apart.* Holds under the cap of three (rounds 5, 7, 9,
   11). Under the cap of five some switches come at even rounds, which the advisor said would refute the reading of
   passes and turns there.
3. *The site holds one.* Holds under the cap of three; under larger caps the site holds k − 2.
4. *A cap of four has fewer switching receivers, at 3d + 2.* Fails: ten, at 2d + 1; 3d + 2 holds only for the switch
   receiver itself. *A cap of five: some of the 48 senders switch the switch receiver at 4d + 3.* Fails.
5. *Controls.* Hold: the ordinary count reads nothing; the record's one fewer reads only with a waiting own knot.

## What this does not say

- **One kind of receiver for each cap** carries the main result; the rule is stated for starts of three mutually
  sharing knots and tested on two more caps, not on larger starts.
- **What is read is not one property of one knot** (referee's Block): on the five-sender panel the matched receiver
  tells the two-knot sender apart at round 2d + 1; another knot in the sender can reverse this reading at a fixed
  site. Not what the far pattern is as a whole either. Why two, and why the round is 2d + 1, is not explained: the reading of a pass turned back at
  the far site does not survive the check of the site's slack.
- **It rests on a cap** (A224's, here of three to seven), on the meeting and on the link knots; without a cap nothing
  is read. The model is not expected to keep a cap of a fixed size; whether a cap that the model could supply (a
  ration read from what a knot holds) does the same is open.
- **Sixteen rounds** (22 for the controls); "never" means not in the rounds read.
