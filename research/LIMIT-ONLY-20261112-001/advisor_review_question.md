SETTING. NAE on three-place knots. A nested family: definitions; a 3-port definition D may call SELF; depth t: at depth 1 SELF = the knot NAE(a,b,c); depth t: SELF = D at depth t-1. Each 3-port relation is complement-closed (16 possible). Phi maps the tuple of SELF definitions' port relations at depth t-1 to depth t (monotone). Finite truncations H_t = Phi^t(NAE). Limit: iterate from TOP (SELF dropped) to the greatest fixed point G; by compactness the infinite (bottomless) pattern allows a state iff main(G) is non-empty. Since NAE ⊆ TOP, H_t ⊆ Phi^t(TOP), so "alive at every finite depth implies limit alive" is by construction. "Limit-only" families: some finite depth dead, limit alive. A prior census found 0.63% of nested families limit-only, 58% of finitely dying families alive in the limit.

LEMMA L1. If each SELF definition's limit relation G_i ⊆ NAE (i.e. excludes the all-equal pair 000/111), then G ⊆ Phi^t(NAE) for all t (G = Phi^t(G) ⊆ Phi^t(NAE) by monotonicity), so main(H_t) ⊇ main(G) ≠ ∅: alive at every depth. Hence a limit-only family has some SELF definition whose limit relation allows its three ports all equal, the one state the holding knot forbids.

CONJECTURE L2. A family lives at every finite depth iff there is a tuple X, each X_i a non-empty-or-empty sub-relation of NAE (union of NAE's complementary pairs), not all empty, with X ⊆ Phi(X) componentwise and main(X) ≠ ∅. (⇐) is the lemma's argument (X ⊆ Phi^t(X) ⊆ Phi^t(NAE)). (⇒) is the conjecture; it is a "post-fixed point below the knot" criterion, Knaster–Tarski flavoured. Periodic orbits (period 2-4, ~0.1% of families) might break it.
