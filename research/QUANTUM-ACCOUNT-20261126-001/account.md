# What the model makes, and what a table above the classical limit needs

A one-page account of the work on the quantum question (26 November 2026), for the owner. Words: a **distinction** is a
two-valued choice, a **knot** a law on several distinctions, a **reader** someone who reads some distinctions. A table is
**above the classical limit** when no list of values, one per distinction and fixed in advance, could produce it.

## What the model makes unaided

- **The three-reader shape.** Growth closes a ring of three knots, each with one loose distinction, in one round onto
  four knots on six distinctions, every two sharing one. That is exactly the holder pattern of the literature's
  three-reader contradiction (Mermin's). With the closing knot's law odd, the three readers' tables are the quantum
  three-reader table, all 64 probabilities. Growth makes the closing law even. ([study](../NO-WHOLE-STATE-20261126-001/README.md))
- **The nine-distinction game's shape.** Six knots on nine distinctions with one odd law, where two readers with
  supplied operators win every round and a list wins 8 of 9; growth reaches 121 closed shapes of this kind, 79 of which
  admit such operators once one law is made odd. ([study](../FOUR-READINGS-20261120-001A/results.md))
- **Facts that last.** On rings, a fact held by the whole ring and by no knot; the least change that flips it grows by
  two distinctions each round; facts of three rings are bound by parity rules, one of them made by a birth.
  ([study](../THIRD-LAYER-20261126-001/README.md))
- **Allowed changes**, sets of distinctions whose flip keeps every law, as objects of the structure.
- **Weightings built law by law.** Counting every finished state once, and counting from a random start that then grows,
  are both products of one factor per listed law of the structure, under growth that reads no values. So readers whom no
  listed law joins share nothing a list cannot give. Two referees found the limits: a listed law can fail the knot test in
  the whole structure, so the factors are not always knots; and a recorded rival law that reads values as it grows (a
  newborn takes fresh distinctions according to another value of its parent) makes a weighting that does not factor at
  all, which no table has yet been tested on. ([study](../FACTOR-WEIGHTS-20261126-001/README.md),
  [argument](../THIRD-READER-20261126-001/README.md))

## What each table above the classical limit needed

| Table | Supplied |
|---|---|
| Three readers certain, on the four-knot closure | an odd closing law |
| Two readers win the nine-distinction game | operators for the values, and a shared preparation |
| Two readers whom no knot joins win the square game of certainty (Mermin and Peres's) | a state that relates allowed states (so that a change can be read), questions that read a change with a value, and a middle reader who reads changes and whose record's signs are applied |
| Three readers with no sorting | the same, plus a quarter-turn read; and the middle reader shares knots with both others |
| Two readers whom no knot joins reach CHSH 4 | a signed weighting that is not built knot by knot ([study](../SIDE-SIGN-20261126-001/README.md)) |
| Readers counting only within their parts reach CHSH 4 | nothing, but no quantum state gives it: there is no state of the whole at all |

**Which are the same piece.** They come to two pieces under several names.
1. **A relation between two allowed states.** The odd sign, a read of a change, and a state that relates allowed states
   are one piece: reading a change compares two allowed states, the relation between them is what the counts do not
   give, and the sign is its phase. The quarter-turn read and the questions that read a change with a value are the
   reads that use it.
2. **Something that ties readers no law joins.** A middle reader whose record sorts the table, and a weighting not
   built law by law, are one piece: the counts of value-blind growth factor over its listed laws, so separated readers
   share only what passes through a joined reader or a weighting that does not factor. The one recorded law that makes
   such a weighting reads values as it grows.

## The fold link, exactly

Write the closing sum knot by knot: every distinction occurs twice. If values square to one and two values either
commute or not, bringing each distinction's two occurrences together costs −1 for every pair that does not commute and
whose occurrences interleave. So **the closing law's sign is the cost of reordering the closing sum.** It holds exactly at
all 115 stored operator witnesses. With every value commuting the cost is zero and the sign even, which is what growth
assumes. Reading the reorder as time folding is a picture: nothing in the model orders the sum, and where the knots
commute the sign does not depend on the order. ([study](../NO-WHOLE-STATE-20261126-001/README.md))

## Assumptions now under challenge

- Every two distinctions have a joint value, even where no knot holds both ([A295](../../docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md#a295--every-two-distinctions-have-a-joint-value-even-where-no-knot-holds-both)).
- Growth never forbids what the old knots allowed ([A126](../../docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md#a126--growth-never-forbids-what-the-old-knots-allowed)).
- Whether knots constrain only the possible states, or also the acts used as questions ([A152](../../docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md#a152--do-knots-constrain-the-possible-states-of-a-preparation-or-also-the-acts-used-as-questions)).
- Beside them, the owner's interpretation, assumed: a contradiction among views is not a failure; one view never gives
  both answers ([A294](../../docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md#a294--a-contradiction-among-views-is-not-a-failure-one-view-never-gives-both-answers)).

## The smallest change to the law for each missing piece (none started)

| Missing piece | Smallest change to the law | Payoff | Concreteness |
|---|---|---|---|
| An odd closing law | a rule for which values commute, fixed at each birth, that keeps a state on every grown structure | 3/3 | 1/3: the rule read off the finished structure leaves no state on 290 of 300 sampled starts |
| A relation between two allowed states | a birth whose law compares two allowed states of its parents | 3/3 | 1/3: no birth's law does so now, and no candidate exists |
| A weighting not built law by law | a growth law that reads values as it grows (one is recorded, the ring-to-end study's transfer law), or one that divides weight over several births at once | 2/3 | 2/3: both exist and fail to factor; untested for tables above the classical limit |
| A middle reader whose record sorts | none needed in the law: a joined reader already exists; what is supplied is using its record | 1/3 | 3/3 |

The first two are the same piece and would give the quantum part a native source; neither has a candidate. The third
is the only concrete one, and it would give a weighting, not yet a sign: a weighting of values alone, however it is
tied, still has a list (the word itself), so it matters only together with the first piece.
