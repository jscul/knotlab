# What 10,000 uniform descents can miss on start-03

With joint design confidence of at least **96.5%**, a new mean of 10,000 independent uniform descents, at the specified ordering of the round-eight stage, has at least a **25.40%** chance of falling below one tenth of the true number of maximal mutually joinable sets. Even supplying the exact additive correction for round-nine births leaves at least a **25.39%** chance of a tenfold underestimate. The result concerns this proposal, its fixed settings, this number of draws and this one stage. It explains why a factor-ten spread remains open for the observations in hand. A different proposal or a larger sample has its own error probability. ## A deterministic lower bound from 48 actual junctions

Among the 28,065 growing junctions one can choose 24 disjoint pairs. The two junctions in each pair cannot join each other. Every two junctions from different pairs can join each other. The certificate lists all 48 names and checks every one of their 1,128 unordered pairs against the stage's 365 old-parent blocks. Its two columns belong respectively to blocks 35 and 0. To find the certificate, the search recorded, for each member of one block, its nonneighbors in a subset of the other block. It kept rows for which each selected row had a nonneighbor shared by none of the other selected rows. Adaptive selection affects how the names were found. The literal pair checks prove their property. Choose one member of every pair. The 24 chosen junctions are mutually joinable, and the finite stage lets us extend them to a maximal mutually joinable set. Two different choices cannot extend to the same set because their union contains two incompatible junctions. This gives at least 16,777,216 maximal sets. Only 203 maximal sets are already held together by an old parent, so the deterministic lower bound on round-nine births is **16,777,013**. The search establishes existence of these 24 pairs and leaves optimality open. ## A separately fixed test of rare, high-weight descents

Write M for the count of all maximal mutually joinable sets. A uniform descent selects one child uniformly at each visited branching point of the exhaustive pivoted Bron–Kerbosch search. Its weight W multiplies the branching factors by the exact terminal count. Summing the conditional expectation over the children gives E[W] = M. The ordering was stable descending original closed-neighborhood degree, with original ID breaking ties. The search used the quotient by identical closed neighborhoods, pivot pool 64 per set, exact cutoff 10, and no shuffle or population resampling. This fixes the distribution of one descent on the actual input.

Before drawing the confirmation stream, we fixed seed 202611199795, 100,000 paths, two strict thresholds and their separate error probabilities. The thresholds came from exploratory seed 20261119097. Six fresh weights exceeded the earlier maximum t = 1,479,137,034,240. Ninety exceeded s = 200,620,800. For h strict exceedances among N draws the one-sided exact binomial upper bound is BetaQuantile(1−α; h+1, N−h). The confidence accounting uses independent draws within the stream. The implementation uses the named pseudorandom seed. Let u and v denote the unrounded upper bounds for Pr(W > t) and Pr(W > s). The entire contribution from weights at most t is at most B = s + (t−s)v < 1,937,324,036. This follows by bounding weights up to s by s and allowing at most an additional t−s on the second event. Therefore at least 1−B/L > **99.736%** of the true mean comes from paths with weight above the earlier maximum t.

For 10,000 new paths the chance all their weights miss that tail is at least (1−u)^10000 > 0.26088. Conditional on that event each draw has mean at most B/(1−u). Markov's inequality bounds the chance that their mean still reaches one tenth of M. Together the two steps give

`Pr(mean of 10,000 weights < M/10) ≥ (1−u)^10000 × (1−10B/[L(1−u)]) = 0.2540036…`

For births T = M+C, the deterministic correction C is the residual pair count plus the unused-name offshoot count minus 203. It is at most 312,649,273. Using the smaller threshold L/10−0.9 × 312,649,273 in the same conditional Markov bound gives 0.2539771… for the chance that the mean plus the true correction is below T/10. The separate 99% count bound alone gives at least **734,650,722,437 births**. These observations give no comparable upper confidence limit on the count. ## Exploratory choice and replay

We inspected eight earlier named streams, four uniform and four rank-weighted. For a fixed family of eight streams, Markov's inequality gives M ≥ δ times each observed mean except on events of probability at most δ each. In each of the four uniform streams we also considered its maximum and 101st-largest weight. At the jth-largest of N independent weights, the probability of strictly exceeding that observed weight has upper bound BetaQuantile(1−δ; j, N−j+1), with coverage at least 1−δ even with ties. There are 16 events in this calculation. Taking δ = 0.05/16 gives a simultaneous nominal 95% bound for those specified comparisons. For the selected stream it would give at least 54.59% chance of a tenfold underestimate with the specified 10,000-draw mean. Allowing all ranks j≥2 in each of the four named uniform streams, with error δ × 2/[j(j+1)] per rank and δ reserved for the maximum, gives at least 54.29%. These retrospective calculations quantify the recorded comparisons. The fresh confirmation above accounts for the fact that the method, ordering and thresholds were selected after inspecting earlier data.
