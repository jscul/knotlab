# Four-distinction bonds with large shores form a chain

Take one reachable presentation of the serial mixed-count law and any one of its lawful histories. Give every shared distinction its direction from the earlier junction to the later one. A loose distinction is an output going to the exterior in the wired graph. For an ordinary vertex set \(S\), let \(\delta S\) be its boundary, including loose outputs there. The ordinary shore of a bond is the side of its minimal cut that omits the exterior.

**Theorem.** The ordinary shores of all four-distinction bonds containing at least three junctions on that shore are nested by inclusion. Fix any pair of distinctions. At most two of these shores have that pair in their boundary. If two do, the larger one adds exactly one junction to the smaller one. That junction consumes the other two boundary distinctions and makes two fresh outputs. All statements allow mixed counts and arbitrary finite width.

For any set with at least two junctions, its two latest junctions make at least four outputs between them and at most one can be consumed inside the set. It therefore has at least three outgoing distinctions. For a set with at least three junctions, the latest three make at least six outputs. At most two can be consumed within the set, by ordinary simplicity and bipartiteness on the three latest sites. Hence such a set has at least four outgoing distinctions. In particular every four-bit ordinary shore of at least three junctions has only outgoing boundary distinctions. It contains every parent of each of its junctions, and hence the seed.

A nonempty set closed under taking parents can be built by restricting the original history to it. If two parents ever have a common neighbor, they already had one by the birth of the younger parent. Indeed a first common neighbor could not be added later, since its join would already require a witness for that pair. At birth that witness is an earlier parent of the younger site, so belongs to the restricted set. All required pair witnesses are therefore present. Deleting other vertices cannot introduce a forbidden common witness. Restricting leaves the original old operands available at their actual additions.

Suppose two large shores \(U,V\) were incomparable, and put \(I=U\cap V\). Both shores contain the seed. There is no ordinary meeting between \(U\setminus I\) and \(V\setminus I\). In either possible temporal direction the earlier endpoint would have to belong to the other shore by parental closure. The ordinary cut identity now gives
\[
                |\delta I|+|\delta(U\cup V)|=8.
\]
If \(I\) has at least two sites, its boundary has size at least four. For two sites the seed and its child form a connected pair and their stars have sizes at least three each. For a larger \(I\) use the outgoing bound. The union has at least three sites and has boundary at least four, so both boundaries are exactly four.

For \(S=U\setminus I\), let \(p\) count the inputs of \(S\) from \(I\) and \(q\) its unconsumed outgoing distinctions after building \(I\cup S\). The boundary identity along this addition is \(4=|\delta I|-p+q\). The same holds for \(T=V\setminus I\). Each nonempty difference has at least two outgoing distinctions. When \(|\delta I|=4\), each must consume as many as it exports. Their input sets are disjoint, so each consumes two and exports two. The latest-two bound forces each difference to consist of a single junction. Their two-parent joins on the common restricted prefix are lawful. Either order of these two joins is lawful since pair witnesses persist and their operands are disjoint. All outputs of the prefix now go to ordinary children and the only two sinks are these two junctions. The two-sink impossibility for this growth law excludes precisely this configuration. It holds for two input parents as well as three. [Two-sink restriction and its derivation](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/inverse.md#two-elementary-source-restrictions)

If \(I\) is only the seed, \(|\delta I|=3\) and \(q=p+1\) on each side. Both sides consume at least one of its three distinct outputs. At least one side therefore consumes just one, exports two, and consists of one site by the same latest-two bound. Its shore with the seed has only two junctions, contrary to the hypothesis. This proves nesting.

For distinct nested shores \(U\subset V\) with the same two named bits in their boundaries, those bits leave \(U\) and still leave \(V\). At most two other bits of \(\delta U\) are available to \(V\setminus U\). Keeping the total boundary size four forces this difference to export exactly as many distinctions as it consumes. It must consume and export two, so again it is a single junction. Three nested shores with both named bits in their boundaries would leave at least two new sites between the smallest and largest. This proves the second assertion.

**What this contributes to the inverse.** A four-bit bond through a pair of bits may be an ordinary four-place junction, the sum of two adjacent trivalent stars, or a cut around a much larger part. The theorem restricts the last possibility to a single inclusion chain in any realization. A legal triple can use at most one distinction of any such four-bit support, by the general lower bound of four on distances between eligible marks. This latter restriction persists through every extension. Every old allowed state extends over a new junction, so no new equation supported entirely on old distinctions appears and the old four-bit bond remains minimal.
