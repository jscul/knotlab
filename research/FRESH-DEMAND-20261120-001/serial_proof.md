# What the named two-parent birth can do in every serial history

**Stage 2, all-order proof on the two seven-knot sources. Independent program referee pending.** Under the archived unrestricted rule, the exact birth on \(U,V\) can bring one or two fresh distinctions in the source where \(W\) is spent. In the source where \(W\) has two loose distinctions, every occurrence brings one. The original two-versus-one comparison occurs for some pairs of fair histories. Under joins-first the spent-\(W\) birth always occurs first and brings one. In the live-\(W\) source a different birth can permanently consume both parents under either policy. Here an occurrence is the unordered **exact** parent set \(\{U,V\}\). A later larger birth containing both is counted separately. Two loose distinctions initially held by the same parent can be exchanged. We fix neither a particular one of these positions nor an order on the start's names. The result holds for the registered \(b=2,M=9\) and for every \(b\ge2,M\ge2\) in this construction. Ordinary parallel meetings contribute one common *holder*. Their multiplicity leaves these events unchanged.

## The serial transitions and the separate activity at \(Z\)

Recompute the maximal unheld groups and the candidate pairs left uncovered by those groups among all knots currently holding loose distinctions. Remove a proposed multi-parent group if some previous child already has every one of its members as a parent. A unary from a live knot is available when it holds more loose distinctions than the number of remaining groups containing it. Joins-first excludes all unary moves whenever at least one remaining multi-parent group exists anywhere. An admitted move consumes one loose distinction per parent. With the newborn provisionally included, count **all** newly computed groups containing it among the remaining live knots and the newborn. The previous-child filter is used for move admission; the demand calculation in the archived program calls the group function directly. If the move is nonimplied, a birth with \(a\) parents and provisional demand \(d\) has \(\max(d,3-a,1)\) fresh distinctions. The knots descended from \(Z\) and those descended from \(U,V\) or live \(W\) never have a common live move. At the source there is neither a direct meeting nor a common ordinary holder between these two sets. A spent source knot never gains a new meeting. By induction each new knot meets only parents of its own set, and makes no direct meeting across the sets. An old spent witness cannot acquire new neighbours from both; a newly born witness belongs to just one set. Hence even an interleaving of birth sizes cannot create a crossing group. Both sets start with loose distinctions. A birth in either leaves the other set's loose distinctions in place and hence cannot take them all. It gets at least one fresh distinction itself, preserving the assertion by induction. A nonempty set always has a next move of its own under the unrestricted rule. If it has any available group, that is one. Otherwise a live knot makes a unary. Thus no finite history is terminal. Joins-first still applies its priority across the whole presentation.

The starting parity projections are lawful by the seven-knot source proof. Under not-all-equal they are lawful as well: the ordinary presentation has a connected four-cycle \(S,W,B,A\) with attachments \(U,V,Z\). Each of its meetings has at least two physical distinctions. To extend a prescribed nonconstant tuple at one knot, give each bundle *not* incident with that knot two opposite values. Every other non-leaf then sees both, since it has at least one such bundle. Any leaf with only a prescribed bundle has its own loose distinctions available to make its values nonconstant. All three ordinary leaves \(U,V,Z\) have loose distinctions in both preparations. If \(W\) is spent it still has two ordinary neighbours. This verifies each local allowed projection. Later a move uses one distinction at each parent, leaving at least two other places there. The actual not-all-equal nonimplication proof applies. Every serial event on these sources consequently receives the same count under the two knot laws. ## A complete criterion conditional on firing

Suppose \(P=\{U,V\}\) does fire. Each parent had just two loose distinctions initially. Each has therefore made at most one earlier child. Those earlier children, when both present, are distinct, since an earlier common child would exclude the exact pair forever. For the demand calculation the newborn meets only \(U,V\). The other old ordinary neighbour of each of these parents is the spent \(S\). Thus the only possible live candidates for \(P\) are its parents' earlier children. With none or one, the demand is zero or one and the fresh count is one. With two live earlier children \(X,Y\), one previously took from \(U\) alone of the two named parents, the other from \(V\) alone. They cannot both meet either \(U\) or \(V\), so no holder meets \(P,X,Y\) together. If \(X,Y\) have a common ordinary holder, the unique group containing \(P\) is the unheld triple. If they have none, \(P\) belongs to the two separate pairs. Hence

\[
 P\text{ brings two fresh distinctions}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 U,V\text{ each have one earlier, still-live child and those children have no common holder.}
\]

All remaining firing cases give one. This criterion includes histories of arbitrarily many intervening moves and uses the current live status of both children.

## Spent \(W\)

Initially \(U,V\) are the only pair in their part of the end. Each has one surplus for a unary. If the pair fires first, it brings one. For two, take the unary from \(U\), then the unary from \(V\), then the pair. After either unary its child has two loose distinctions; the two children meet no common holder and remain live. The named pair is still an uncovered candidate, and the conditional criterion gives two. Joins-first admits only the named pair initially: the separate \(Z\) has no initial candidate. It therefore always fires first with one.

Permanent loss has a three-move witness under the unrestricted rule. Make \(X\) as a unary from \(U\), then \(Y\) as a unary from \(X\). Before the second move \(X\) has two loose distinctions and no group. Now \(U,Y\) are candidates through \(X\), while \(V,Y\) have no common holder. Both \(U,V\) and \(U,Y\) are uncovered pairs, even though \(U\) now has only one loose distinction. The literal serial rule allows either single group. Choosing \(U,Y\) exhausts \(U\). This is a finite certificate of permanent impossibility, independently of subsequent choices.

## Live \(W\)

At the source the three roots \(U,V,W\) have one common holder \(S\); therefore the three separate pairs are the groups. Each root has two loose distinctions and no unary surplus. Before any move on these roots, moves descended from \(Z\) have no effect on them. The first local move is consequently one of the three pairs.

**Why every exact firing gives one.** If \(U,V\) are first the result follows immediately. By exchanging \(U,V\) it suffices for the other possibilities to consider a first local birth \(X=\{U,W\}\). It has no live candidate at its birth: its ordinary neighbours \(U,W\) have no live ordinary neighbours other than the new knot at this instant. Thus it receives exactly one loose distinction. For the target ever to bring two, \(X\) would have to remain live until that target fires. With only one loose distinction, this means \(X\) has made no child. Before \(V\) makes its first child it has only one ordinary neighbour, \(S\). Its only possible live candidates are \(U,W\). As long as both live, their already held triple gives two distinct remaining pairs involving \(V\), exactly using its two loose distinctions. It cannot yet make a unary.

While \(X\) stays unused and \(V\) has no child, \(W\)'s only live candidate partners are \(U,V\). Its ordinary neighbours are the spent \(S,B\) and its earlier child \(X\). The fixed neighbours of \(B\) contribute no other live knot. The only neighbours of \(X\) are its parents \(U,W\). The earlier \(U,W\) group has already fired, and the remaining \(V,W\) group reserves \(W\)'s last distinction. Thus \(W\) can neither make a unary nor exhaust itself in any other way. If \(V\) makes a child before the target, that child must be \(Y=\{V,W\}\). But \(X,Y\) then have common holder \(W\) forever, so the condition for two fails again. If \(X\) has spent its sole distinction in the meantime, the condition already fails. This accounts for all unrestricted orders, including ones mixing any birth sizes.

**Joins-first and the permanent loss.** The first choice is \(U,V\), \(U,W\) or \(V,W\). After \(U,W\) the only available groups are \(U,V\) and \(V,W\). After the symmetric two off-target pairs \(X=\{U,W\},Y=\{V,W\}\), the live part consists of \(U,V,X,Y\), each with one loose distinction. Its only available groups are \(U,V\) through \(S\) and \(X,Y\) through \(W\). The remote part still has no group because its initial unary has never been admitted by joins-first. If \(X,Y\) are joined first, their child \(C\) gets one distinction, and the pairs among \(U,V,C\) are witnessed separately by \(S,X,Y\). There is no common holder of the three. The exact two-parent group is now covered by the triple. The triple is the only possible local move and consumes \(U,V\); under joins-first it is the only possible next move anywhere. The loss is therefore permanent already when \(C\) is formed on move three. The same history is allowed under the unrestricted rule. If the archived uniform choice among currently admitted structural moves is used specifically on this live-\(W\), joins-first preparation, the target fires on moves one, two and three with probabilities \(1/3,1/3,1/6\), respectively, and is lost with probability \(1/6\). These fractions follow from the finite branching just listed.

## Postponement, fairness, and what is being compared

Under unrestricted scheduling one may perform any prescribed finite number of moves in the \(Z\) part before a displayed firing or loss witness. One may also work there forever, leaving the original pair continuously enabled and never chosen. The latter is starvation. In contrast, all the finite permanent-loss prefixes above have fair continuations, and all the finite firing witnesses can be continued fairly. For a direct existence proof, number all possible named parent sets, unary actions included, with finitely many codes below any given code and, after the prefix, always take the least code in the policy's current admissible pool. Each fixed action can fire only finitely many times because it spends distinctions at fixed finite parents which never refill. Consequently an action that remains or recurs in the pool infinitely often cannot be ignored forever. This supplies a strongly fair extension relative to the specified pool. The numbering proves existence and is absent from the growth law. Weak fairness alone also excludes starvation of a continuously admissible pair.

In fact under weakly fair **unrestricted** scheduling each specified old parent eventually spends its last loose distinction. If it stayed live forever, its finite places could acquire only finitely many children. In the union of its particular history the ordinary neighbourhood of any fixed finite radius is finite: each knot has finitely many places fixed at birth. After finitely many moves the holder's possible candidate partners within two ordinary meetings, their mutual witness data and prior children relevant to the admission test stabilize, as do their live or spent statuses. If an unretired group containing this holder exists, one fixed such group remains continuously admitted. Otherwise its loose distinction admits a unary. Either conclusion contradicts perpetual survival under weak fairness after its last spend. Thus a fair unrestricted history which misses the exact pair eventually makes its absence irreversible. This is a statement about admitted actions, not a bound on how many earlier moves a fair schedule may take.

A comparison of two orders must specify the pairing. Fire \(U,V\) immediately in both preparations to obtain \((1,1)\). Every literally identical sequence of named moves leading to a firing in both also has \(U,V\) as the first move in the root part: the spent-\(W\) part initially allows the two unary alternatives, whereas the live-\(W\) part allows the two other pairs. Their only common local first move is \(U,V\). To obtain \((2,1)\) under the unrestricted rule, perform the two *other initial siblings* before the target in each preparation. These are the two unaries in the first and \(U,W\), \(V,W\) in the second. The instructions correspond but the actual named actions differ. Both prefixes extend fairly. Under joins-first, every paired history in which both exact events occur gives \((1,1)\). An absent event has no numerical fresh count.

If instead a funded complete roster and its full-step fresh counts are **preassigned**, order only chooses the sequence in which disjoint consumed slots and distinct fresh names are recorded. The representative has seven births in the spent-\(W\) arm and five in the live-\(W\) arm. All \(7!\) and \(5!\) permutations of the individually allocated births respectively give \(2\) and \(1\). Identifying the four and two indistinguishable \(Z\)-unary positions leaves \(210\) and \(60\) ordered parent-set lists. In a hybrid which retains that first-step roster but reads provisional demand after each birth, the spent-\(W\) target gets two exactly when its two unary siblings come first relative to it. Thus \(1,680\) labelled permutations give two and \(3,360\) give one, or \(70\) and \(140\) ordered parent-set lists. The live-\(W\) hybrid always gives one. These are permutation counts under a supplied plan, not frequencies of recomputing from a changing set of possible moves.

The predecessor's other order-free rule adjoins a cluster of the precomputed unrolled map as soon as all its parents exist. A fair completed order on these identified sources gives the same target as the complete roster, even if different cluster sizes interleave. Knowledge of that map supplies future extensions; its eligible moves and its demand differ from the current-state serial rule just proved. None of these three orderings establishes a physical tick.
