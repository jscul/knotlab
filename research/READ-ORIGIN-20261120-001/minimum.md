# The first indistinguishable presents

Two three-place knots sharing one distinction suffice. A unary birth on a specified old loose distinction is required when both knots are original, and can never be a unary birth from that holder when one knot is the first child of the other. For a **different immutable fresh count on the same ready birth**, the minimum is two four-place knots sharing one distinction. Both statements hold separately under even parity and not-all-equal. The presentations are literally identical in the comparisons and their funds suffice. | What differs between histories | Fewest current knots | Fewest distinct positions at that knot count | Same marked event when applicable |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Remaining cohort menu, with a permanent physical difference | 2 | 5 | A unary on a loose distinction of the first child |
| Fresh count on a common ready birth | 2 | 7 | The next unary of the four-place original costs 3 or 2 |
| Fresh count on a common multi-parent birth when all of its own parents suffice | 3 | 7 | The two children of one original can join for 1 or 2 |
| Fresh count on a common multi-parent birth within the current cohort in both histories | 4 | 9 | The same two holders join for 2 or 1 |

The last two rows distinguish a ready mixed-depth event from one authorized within the unfinished cohort. The four-knot row uses an admissible proper subset of a funded cohort. There is no probability or scheduling-fairness premise.

## Sources, slots and local laws

Each original has at least three places and each ordinary meeting shares one distinction. Births add knots and preserve the old supports and positions. A physical birth takes one still loose distinction per parent. For the ordinary roster, two current children of a single holder are candidate partners. A maximal group of at least three pairwise candidates with no common holder is taken together. Pairs not covered by such groups each give a birth. A funded complete cohort assigns one unary to each remaining old loose distinction. A child with \(p\) parents and \(d\) next multi-parent groups ordinarily gets \(\max(d,3-p,1)\) fresh distinctions. Parity has a separate closing case. Every price comparison below concerns a proper subset of the loose inputs, so the nonclosing rule applies under either law. Certified child counts are immutable. [Fresh-count theorem](../../proofs/FRESH-COUNT-20261118-001A/FRESH-COUNT-20261118-001A-P001_free_count/proof.md) · [Not-all-equal keep and closing rules](../NAE-GROWTH-20261119-001/nae_growth.md#why-it-holds)

For parity the relation at a holder is that the sum of its bits is even. For not-all-equal it permits precisely the assignments displaying both values. In either case the relation is nonempty and proper and forgetting any one place gives every assignment on the rest. All the sources and partial presents displayed below are ordinary meeting trees. To extend a local assignment on a chosen holder, proceed away from it. Each next holder has just one already fixed ordinary distinction, so its other distinctions can be filled to satisfy it. This proves that each displayed support has its exact stated local relation in the whole presentation. For each marked multi-parent birth the selected inputs lie at leaves with at least two loose distinctions each. Any two selected values extend: first fill the other holders compatibly, then satisfy each marked holder with its remaining loose distinction. For a marked unary either value extends by the same local extension. A fresh output suffices to retain every old permitted state. These facts check the birth on its actual physical inputs.

## Two knots

Start with a lone holder \(T\) with \(m\ge3\) loose distinctions. Its first cohort consists of exactly \(m\) unaries. Every two of these children are candidates through \(T\), and every set of them is held by \(T\). Consequently every pair of children contributes a separate next join. Each child needs \(m-1\) joins and receives \(m-1\) fresh distinctions. Write just the first child \(X\), taking a distinction denoted by 0. Both holders have exactly \(m\) places and \(m-1\) loose distinctions. There are \(2m-1\) different positions.

Now take these two holders as the original source. They meet directly and have no common ordinary holder, so they make no initial joint birth. Each must produce \(m-1\) unaries. A unary child from either of them has \(m-2\) candidate siblings, all held by its single parent, and no candidate across the two parents. Its immutable fresh count is therefore \(\max(2,m-2)\).

**Five distinctions.** Use \(T=\{0,1,2\}\) and \(X=\{0,3,4\}\). With the singleton origin the current cohort still owes the two unaries of \(T\) on 1 and 2. In the fresh two-knot reading the four unaries are on 1, 2, 3 and 4. In particular a unary of \(X\) on the fixed position 3 may be written immediately. Under the singleton origin the two fresh positions 3 and 4 of \(X\) are both used for the joins with the other two children of \(T\). Neither is ever used for a unary from \(X\). Reassigning siblings at \(X\) preserves this conclusion.

This difference survives even when future parent directions are erased. In the singleton continuation let \(S\) be another unary child of \(T\), and let \(J\) be its joint child with \(X\). The ordinary route \(T,S,J,X\) connects the old holders without using distinction 0, and every later extension keeps that route. In the two-original continuation, color the future unary children of \(T\) by their parent and likewise for \(X\). Children of different colors have disjoint parent sets. At every subsequent complete layer the only witnesses between live children are their earlier parents. Thus no group can have both colors, and a unary inherits its parent's color. All later ordinary meetings stay within one color. Removing old distinction 0 separates the two holders in every such continuation. The four-knot route is a finite witness under one reading and is impossible under the other, for both knot laws.

**Seven distinctions.** Use \(T=\{0,1,2,3\}\) and \(X=\{0,4,5,6\}\). Mark \(T\)'s loose position 1. The unary on **that same input** is immediately ready in both readings. From singleton \(T\) the other three first-cohort children each join it separately, so its price is three. With both holders original only two other unaries come from \(T\), so its price is two. The newborn has four or three places immediately. Under either reading all relevant original unaries are funded.

**Minimality at arbitrary arity.** With one present knot there can have been no birth from a nonempty source, and its ordinary continuation is determined. Two knots can share at most one distinction and require at least three places apiece, so five distinctions are necessary. Any earlier nonempty origin at a two-knot present must be one knot followed by exactly one birth, hence the equal-arity pair above. At \(m=3\) every common ready unary in either interpretation costs two. Even swapping which holder was the source cannot produce unequal prices on an event admitted in both. Six distinctions cannot occur in a two-knot proper middle: \(2m-1\) is odd. At \(m=4\) the common price changes. No assumed upper arity bound enters either minimum.

## A common multi-parent birth

The same three-place holdings give both sharper examples. Keep \(T=\{0,1,2\}\), \(X=\{0,3,4\}\), and write \(Y=\{1,5,6\}\). In all comparisons the intended parents are \(X,Y\) with fixed still loose inputs 3 and 5.

**Ready as soon as its own parents exist.** In the one-original reading, \(X,Y\) are two of \(T\)'s three unary children. Their joint birth is ready under the parent-closed rule, although \(T\)'s third unary has not yet been written. In the completed next cohort there is one pair for each pair of singletons. The three resulting pair knots are pairwise candidates with no common parent. Their unique three-parent birth makes the demand of each pair knot one. In particular the marked birth costs one. This is the explicit three-element subset construction for a lone original. [Lone-knot calculation](../FRESH-COUNT-20261118-001/fresh_count.md#why-it-holds)

In the fresh three-knot source, \(X,Y\) have two loose distinctions each and their only group is their pair through \(T\). Each has one unary left and \(T\) has its own unary. The pair child shares \(X\) with the \(X\)-unary and shares \(Y\) with the \(Y\)-unary. Those unaries are not candidates with each other. The pair child has two separate joins and costs two. In the singleton-origin history a rule that finishes each cohort before releasing the next still withholds the pair at this particular prefix.

Two present knots admit no such joint birth. A two-parent birth requires a distinct common holder, and any group of three or more requires at least three parents. With three present knots and at most six distinctions, the unit-meeting and three-place bounds force exactly three ternary holders all meeting each other: the sum of arities is at least nine and at most three meetings can be shared. In any proper history for this presentation, the last-born knot must meet both remaining knots. It would have needed those two as parents when no third knot was available to witness their join. This is impossible. Thus three knots and seven distinctions are minimal for the common mixed-depth price.

**Ready within both current cohorts.** Now begin from that fresh three-knot source and write just the unary of \(T\) on position 2. Its parent has no other child in the cohort, so demand zero and the unary floor give two fresh distinctions. Write the new knot as \(Z=\{2,7,8\}\). The remaining original-cohort pair on 3 and 5 still costs two.

Take the identical four-knot presentation as a new source. The center \(T\) is spent and the three others each hold two loose distinctions. All three of their pairs are candidates held by \(T\). Each is made separately, and they exhaust each parent's supply of two. The three resulting pair knots have exactly one common three-parent successor, by the separate pair witnesses. Their price is one, including the same \(X,Y\) birth on 3 and 5. Both prices occur in the first remaining cohort under the respective readings.

For the lower bound, a source of one knot starts with at least three unaries. At up to four total knots its first cohort is either unfinished with unaries only, or has just finished. Two originals in a connected unit-meeting source have no candidate pair and at least two loose distinctions apiece, so their first cohort remains unfinished and unary-only at any proper prefix with at most four total knots. A complete boundary has the restart's continuation, as proved below. These observations rule out a smaller knot count.

It remains to consider four present knots and a proper middle from three originals. Exactly one event has been written. A connected three-knot source has either two or three ordinary meetings. With two meetings it has at most one initial multi, the pair of endpoints. For that multi still to be ready, the written birth must have been a unary. The current has three shared meetings and at least twelve total places, hence at least nine distinctions. With three original meetings each original has at least one loose distinction. All three are pairwise candidates with no common holder, so their triple is the only initial multi. Again the one birth already made must be a unary. Its original needs at least one loose distinction beyond the one owed to the triple, giving that original at least four places. The current has four ordinary meetings and at least thirteen total places, hence at least nine distinctions. This proves the four-knot, nine-distinction minimum without an arity cutoff.

## What a boundary tells us

A completed funded cohort has spent every old loose distinction. Its live knots are newborns. Distinct newborns have no meeting with one another: the inputs they spent and the fresh distinctions they supplied are disjoint. Every ordinary witness shared by two live newborns is therefore an actual parent under the preceding reading. Treating the entire presentation as a new source gives exactly those same witnessed sets, the same loose supplies and the same permitted assignments. Its next complete roster, demand, floor and implication verdict agree. This includes equal funding stalls and equal empty ends. Match names for newborns and equivalent input slots, then iterate to match all future complete layers. The same argument applies when the newborn end is already empty.

For example, the four-knot presentation just used is also the **complete** first cohort from the lone three-place \(T\), with \(X,Y,Z\) its three children. That history and the fresh four-knot reading give all three pairs price one. The remaining-cohort price of two arose from a third reading, the proper middle from three original knots. A genuine ambiguity about which source occurred can therefore have zero cost for predicting the future of a complete boundary.

At the five-distinction present, the binary question “was this a fresh start or a proper middle?” can have either answer on identical holdings. One supplied bit answers that binary question. It does not generally identify the complete remaining response. With the physical holders fixed by the query, the possibilities here are that both are original, only \(T\) is original, or only \(X\) is original. The remaining first-cohort unary menus are respectively both holders, only \(T\), or only \(X\), with two unaries at each included holder. The last two readings share the same start/middle bit and differ on the marked slot at \(X\). If the holders and the query are both unpointed and interchangeable, the latter two interpretations lie in one symmetry class. Distinguishing historical starts and predicting future births are separate information questions.
