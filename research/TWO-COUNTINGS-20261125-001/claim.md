## 2. The working choice

**Neither counting is his.** His "random" means not chosen, and the starts we pick stand in for what is not yet
known; that is about what occurs and why we pick starts, not about how much weight one allowed configuration gets
against another. By his answer on this fork, the choice is the thread's: **counting from the finish**, the working
choice of the thread, reversible; not tested here (A240).

Its reasons and their cost:

1. **Interest, not evidence.** On the settings-as-births line, it is the one of the two under which the line shows a
   hidden score above 2; with a start before the setting knots the lemma (C2) gives at most 2 (by argument, checked
   on five structures). The excess is what this choice produces, so it is not evidence for the choice. The record's
   other hidden excess, 9/4 under parity, is an excess under both countings and plays no part in this reason.
2. **It is a function of the finished structure's allowed states alone,** so it needs no start and no birth record.
   Its cost: a finishing stage we supply, and, across the four setting pairs, a weighting of histories we supply
   (TD 3.5).

The readings left open, none attributed to him: (a) every whole finished configuration equally weighted; (b) every
start equally weighted, each new value equally among what that start leaves; (c) no weighting at all, only that every
configuration can occur; (d) "starting positions" as the start's structure, a meeting pattern, not its values;
(e) the settings themselves not chosen, a weighting of histories (TD 2.3).

## 3. Words

**Counting** is a rule; a **weighting** is what it gives. **Counting from the finish**: each allowed state of the
finished structure counts once. **Counting from the start**: each allowed state of the start counts once, and at each
birth an old state's weight is divided equally among its completions. **Counting from stage s** starts at a stage
common to the histories compared. The literature's names for the two observers (Alice and Bob) are used once, here;
after this, **the first observer** and **the second observer**: each a supplied list of distinctions; nothing
observes. A picture of our rule, nothing the growth does: when a new knot forbids some values, counting from the start
tries again only the new values, counting from the finish the whole configuration.

## 6. Settled statements (no verdict)

- **No pattern the growth makes depends on the counting.** A value-blind law makes structures that are functions of
  no state, so of no weighting of states.
- **A restatement of C3**: a weighting that never changes an earlier state's weight and one that is a function of the
  finished structure alone are the same exactly under C3's condition. Whether any other weighting has both properties
  is a parked idea.
- **What each counting needs supplied.** Counting from the finish: a finishing stage (a growth that closes offers one;
  an unending one does not) and a weighting of histories across setting pairs. Counting from the start: the start and
  the birth record, which the laws keep and a hand-built structure cannot always give back.
- **Parity.** Every parity birth with a fresh distinction gives every old state the same number of completions, so the
  two countings are one along every parity history whose births bring fresh distinctions.
- **The ceiling** of THIRD-HOLDER-20261123-001A, 4(1 + ρ²)/(1 + ρ)², is 2 at ρ = 1.
- **Renaming** carries both countings.
- **Parked idea**: whether counting from the finish has a limit on an unlimited structure (A110), in exact rationals
  if ever run.

## 7. Law and pattern kept apart

The law is the rule the growth follows; structures and parts are patterns; a clock is a pattern that returns; a
counting is our rule for a weighting of states, never made by the growth. No rule of the laws counts.

## 9. What would reverse the choice

Registered readings, so that a reversal is a test and not a taste.

- **Tested, not met.** "Every structure of the line whose excess holds under counting from the finish has a rule that
  fails in the whole structure" (the excess would then belong to non-knots): not met, since the 7-knot structure
  (19/9) passes in the whole, as do the pencil 9-knot (20/9) and the 18-knot (20/9, by projection only).
- **1. An excess of the growth's own making that survives counting from the start.** A history a registered law
  makes, with the settings births the law makes (A239's reading), the start before them, both records hidden and every
  knot passing in the whole, scoring above 2 under counting from the start. Its consequence: reason 1 falls (the
  choice no longer buys the excess), not by itself a move to counting from the start. The 9/4 stands against it so:
  its cause is made by a law (all-or-none funding), but its settings are edits, not births, and its knots are
  untested in the whole.
- **2. An event that needs counting from the start**: a registered ticket whose claim is about the states of an
  unending growth (A110), where counting from the finish needs a finishing stage we would have to supply; or the
  owner's word.
- **3. Counting from the finish undefined**: a law whose growths make counting from the finish undefined on its own
  structures reverses the choice. Counting from the start being undefined confirms it.

Every count is reported under both countings, side by side; that table is what makes the choice cheap to reverse.
