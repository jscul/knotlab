# What the two remaining output rows determine

Write \(K(x,y)\) for the minimum number of distinctions in a bond containing \(x,y\). Thus the bond distance is \(K-1\). Redact entries above six. Two distinct coordinates are *twins* in this table if their transposition preserves it. Let \(m(x)=\min_{z\ne x}K(x,z)\). The following theorem holds for every finite presentation under the serial two-or-three-output law, including presentations with later three-place sites.

**Common creator theorem.** If \(x,y\) are twins and \(m(x)=m(y)=6\), then the same six-place ordinary site produced both as fresh outputs. They may be queried by name just for the proof. The hypothesis and the conclusion about a transported pair respect every renaming.

First we establish the input test underlying the theorem. It applies to shared outputs as well as loose ones.

## An earlier site's three inputs distinguish a later output

Use the ordinary graph of actual sites, with every loose distinction joined to the exterior for cut accounting. The ordinary graph is connected, simple and bipartite. Its sites have at most six distinctions each. Every ordinary star is a bond. Removing any one ordinary site still leaves a route from every other site to the exterior, by following fresh outputs and avoiding the removed site. In particular, if \(m(f)=6\), every ordinary endpoint of \(f\) is a six-place site. Every such site takes three inputs and makes three outputs. The quantity \(m(f)\) is also the least size of any nonempty cut containing \(f\). Each cut in this finite binary space partitions into support-minimal nonempty cuts, and one of them contains \(f\). Every bond has at least three bits. [Cut representation and finite bonds](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/inverse.md)

In a shore containing at least three ordinary sites, its three latest sites export at least four distinctions. They produce at least six, and at most two can be shared within those three, by bipartiteness and simplicity. Any selected site producing three contributes one more to this bound if it is among the latest three. An output crossing from a selected site earlier than those three counts separately. For a two-site shore containing two six-place sites the cut has at least ten bits.

**Input test.** Let \(u\) and \(v\) be six-place sites, with \(u\) born first. For any output \(f\) of \(v\) with \(m(f)=6\), some input \(p\) of \(u\) satisfies \(K(p,f)>6\).

Suppose every input \(p=a\to u\) of \(u\) has a six-bit bond with \(f\). Choose the shore \(W_p\) of this bond opposite the exterior. We show first that \(u\) must lie outside it. If \(u\) lay inside, \(p\) would enter. If \(v\) were inside as well, \(f\) would leave. The latest-three bound would give at least five exits, counting the third output at \(v\) if it is one of the three, or the exit \(f\) otherwise. For a six-cut there could be no second entering bit. Site \(u\) would precede the latest three, since its third output would otherwise also count. Any exit originating before the latest three would have to be \(f\). If instead \(v\) were outside, \(f\) would be shared and would enter its later ordinary recipient. The two entering bits \(p,f\) would leave room for only four exits, all from the latest three, with \(u\) strictly earlier. The two-site cases already exceed six, since both selected endpoints under discussion have six places.

In either orientation of \(f\), the other two parents of \(u\) must be inside. The outside parent \(a\) and either of those inside parents had an ordinary common neighbor before \(u\) was born. The two meetings with that neighbor give an ordinary crossing already shared strictly before \(u\). It is distinct from \(p\), from the later output \(f\), and from each of the available exits. This contradiction proves \(u\notin W_p\).

Now \(a\in W_p\). By the birth of \(u\), three edge-disjoint ordinary paths joined \(a\) to \(u\). One is their direct meeting. Each other parent \(b\) gives the path from \(a\) to an old common neighbor of \(a,b\), then to \(b\), then to \(u\). The two witnesses are distinct because the three parents of \(u\) had no common neighbor. These paths supply three different crossings already shared by the birth of \(u\), one being \(p\).

There is a selected ordinary endpoint of \(f\) after \(u\). If two selected sites followed \(u\), and these were the only two, one of them would be that six-place endpoint. Their outputs would supply at least \(3+2-1=4\) further crossings. If at least three followed \(u\), the latest three would already supply four. Thus only one selected site follows \(u\). It must be \(v\). Choosing the later recipient of \(f\) instead would make \(f\) enter in addition to the recipient's three exports and the three old path crossings. Consequently the full cut consists exactly of three old path crossings and all three outputs of \(v\). All three input holders of \(v\) belong to \(W_p\) and precede \(u\).

### Why a minimum shore becomes the entire earlier prefix

Use the actual prefix just before \(u\) was born. Write \(P,Q\) for the three input bits ultimately taken by \(u,v\), respectively. All six are distinct and loose in this prefix. In particular, all \(Q\) holders are already present, and no \(Q\) bit can occur on one of the three old paths. Such a path used ordinary meetings already shared by the birth of \(u\), while \(Q\) is consumed only at \(v\).

Remove \(v\) from \(W_p\) and call the remaining set of sites \(T_p\). It belongs to the prefix. Its prefix boundary consists of the three old path crossings, including \(p\), together with all of \(Q\). Indeed the full cut loses \(v\)'s three fresh outputs and gains its three input bits, now loose in the prefix. Other outside sites that were removed from the presentation cannot change whether a distinction from \(T_p\) crosses. Hence \(|\partial T_p|=6\).

Every prefix set \(T\) containing the holders of \(Q\) has boundary of size at least six. All \(Q\) bits are loose there. Its cut equation, obtained by summing the site equations of \(T\), continues to hold on the original coordinates in the final presentation. Adding the equation of \(v\) replaces \(Q\) by \(v\)'s three outputs, including \(f\), with the same total size. A smaller prefix cut would thus give a final equation of size less than \(m(f)\) containing \(f\).

For prefix sets the cut-size inequality \(|\partial A|+|\partial B|\ge |\partial(A\cup B)|+|\partial(A\cap B)|\) follows by counting endpoints of each distinction. If two sets contain every \(Q\) holder and have size-six boundary, so do their union and intersection. Take the union of the three \(T_p\). It contains every \(Q\) holder and, by the choice of \(T_p\) for each \(p\), every \(P\) holder. Its six-bit boundary therefore contains the six distinct loose bits \(P\cup Q\), exhausting it. There is no ordinary crossing. The ordinary graph of the prefix is connected, so the union is the *whole prefix*. The entire loose frontier is \(P\sqcup Q\).

Both triples are lawful on that prefix. For \(Q\), the earliest common neighbor of any two input holders already exists by the younger holder's birth. A later first common neighbor would have had to join those two using an earlier one. A common neighbor of all three in the prefix would still have existed at \(v\)'s birth. Moreover \(P,Q\) have different holder sets, since \(u\) would otherwise already witness all three holders when \(v\) was born. The complementary-triples theorem excludes a lawful prefix whose entire loose frontier is split into two such groups with different holder sets. This proves the input test. [Complementary-triples proof](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001C/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001C-P001_triple_inverse/complementary.md)

For the common creator theorem, suppose the creators of the twins were different. Both are six-place sites. Name the earlier one \(u\), the later one \(v\), and their respective outputs \(x,y\). The star of \(u\) gives \(K(p,x)=6\) for all three of its inputs. The transposition gives \(K(p,y)=6\) for the same inputs, although \(y\) was produced by the later \(v\). This contradicts the input test. Only entries through six were used.

## When the one-spent source supplies a minimum-six pair

Let \(H\) be the designated fourth three-output site and \(x,y\) its two remaining loose distinctions. They occur together in every cut through either, so their transposition preserves even the exact table. Their common row minimum is five exactly when the current source has five loose distinctions. It is six otherwise. Every source with a legal triple is in the latter case.

To see this, a shore containing \(H\) has boundary at least five. A one-site shore at \(H\) has size six and a two-site shore has size at least seven. For a larger shore, if \(H\) precedes the latest three, its two loose bits add to their four exits. If \(H\) is among the latest three, its third output raises their lower bound to five. Equality at five makes every boundary bit outgoing, so the shore contains the parents of each of its sites. It can be built first in original order, retaining pair witnesses by the earliest-witness observation above. The restriction contains the seed and at least two unary three-output additions before its three-parent site \(H\). If these and \(H\) were its only three-output additions, it would be in the earlier third-three-output family. At five loose distinctions every output of its three-parent, three-output site has been spent, by the exact fixed point for that family. This contradicts the two surviving loose outputs of \(H\). Thus the restriction contains all four three-output additions. All remaining steps take at least as many bits as they produce, so the full source has at most five loose distinctions. The whole ordinary shore has at least five by the same bound, and its loose frontier is a bond. This proves the assertion about the row minimum. [Five-loose fixed point](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001K/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001K-P001_third_three_fresh/closure.md#shrink-order-and-live-triples)

A legal triple on a five-loose source would allow one more three-input, two-output move. It would leave four loose bits in all, with at least one still at \(H\), because a move uses at most one bit from a holder. The latest-three bound for the whole graph is at least five whenever a three-output site still has a loose bit. This rules out that triple.

## Holder and permission consequences

In any source here with at least six loose bits, the twin class containing \(x,y\) has exactly those two elements, even after clipping the readings at six. The common creator theorem forces any additional twin to have been produced by \(H\). Its only other output meets a four- or five-place child, so that bit has row minimum at most five.

In every reachable presentation with a correspondence of the clipped tables, the images of \(x,y\) were therefore produced by **one six-place ordinary site**. If both images remain loose, their sole ordinary holder is the same. If one has been spent, its additional holder is a *later* six-place site. Since all loose outputs of a holder are actual parallel coordinates and hence twins, a common creator with one loose and one spent image can have no second loose output. If both images are spent, their two later six-place holders are distinct, since a child uses at most one output per parent. These statements apply to full-table correspondences as well. They identify a common creator without furnishing its other four distinctions.

There are two immediate table tests. Let \(S=\{x\}\cup\{z:K(x,z)=6\}\) for a source sibling of minimum six. In any matching presentation \(S\) contains the entire star of every ordinary endpoint of either image. This follows from the row minimum, the physical stars and the twin transposition. Two sites share at most one distinction, so a spent image requires \(|S|\ge11\). If both are spent, three distinct sites are present. The two children lie on the same bipartition side and have no meeting with each other, giving \(|S|\ge 6+6+6-2=16\). Thus \(|S|\le10\) certifies that both images remain loose, and \(|S|\le15\) certifies that at least one does.

In an O source there are at most two six-place sites. If \(H\) is its only six-place site, precisely its two remaining loose outputs have row minimum six whenever the source has a legal triple. This follows since every other coordinate has an ordinary endpoint of degree at most five. A matching O presentation with only one six-place site must carry this pair to that site's loose outputs.

If the source has a legal triple \(\{x,a,b\}\), the triple \(\{y,a,b\}\) is also legal with the very same holder witnesses. Any correspondence that preserves those two permissions takes the siblings to two loose bits at a common holder. A spent image would reverse one of these particular verdicts.

## A spent image would require smaller equation rank

Suppose a minimum-six twin pair in a rival consists of a loose bit \(l\) at \(u\) and a shared bit \(e\). The common creator theorem gives \(e=u\to v\) with \(u,v\) both six-place. For every output \(g\) of \(v\), its star and the twin rows give \(K(l,g)=K(e,g)=6\). Take such a minimizing shore containing \(u\). It contains \(v\) as well. Otherwise \(g\) enters a later recipient, while the three edge-disjoint ordinary paths from \(u\) to \(v\) by \(v\)'s birth already cross three times. The loose bit \(l\) and at least two exports from sites later than \(v\) give at least seven distinct crossings including \(g\).

Each of the three shores now has an export at each of \(u,v\), namely \(l,g\). Both sites made three outputs. If either is among the latest three selected sites, its extra output contributes to their basic four exports. Otherwise its specified export is earlier and counts separately. Hence all six crossings point outward. These are parent-closed shores. Their intersection has boundary six by cut submodularity, since every set containing \(u\) has cost at least \(m(l)=6\). It contains \(u,v\), and all three outputs of \(v\) leave it. Restrict the history to this parent-closed intersection. Site \(v\) can be placed last. Its only neighbors inside are its original three parents, and every pair already had an earlier witness. A later site witnessing all three would itself have had to take those three parents when \(v\) already witnessed them, contrary to the growth law. When removing \(v\) from that history, the three inputs of \(v\) replace its three outputs in the six-bit frontier. Thus there must be a lawful **six-loose prefix** in which the three inputs of \(v\) are a legal triple and \(u\) still has at least two loose outputs, \(e,l\). This prefix claim allows arbitrary intervening sites in the original rival.

A table matching O has exactly the three seed pairs at \(K=3\). Indeed a union of at least three sites exports at least four bits, and two sites have boundary at least \(3+3-2=4\). Every three-place site in the rival would therefore give three pair entries at three on its own support, which must be the original seed support. A later site introduces fresh bits, so the seed is its only three-place site. In particular the first two additions in any such rival, or in any parent-closed restriction containing a three-parent site, are unary and have three outputs. The first two sites alone have no common ordinary neighbor at which to join a pair.

The six-loose prefix above contains these two initial three-output additions and the three-input, three-output \(u\). If these were its only three-output additions, it would belong to K's closed family. We queried its exact fixed point, retaining the mark at \(u\). Consequently this prefix has at least four nonseed three-output additions, and the later \(v\) brings the total to at least five. If *both* images are spent, their common creator and the two distinct later six-place recipients already bring the total to five together with the two initial unary additions. At width \(d\), a history of \(n'\) sites and \(r'\) nonseed three-output additions satisfies \(d=3+2(n'-1)+r'\). Matching the O source, with \(n\) sites and four such additions, forces \(r'\) even. Either kind of spent image therefore requires **at least six three-output additions and at most \(n-1\) sites**. The site count equals equation rank, since the fresh terms at each birth make its equation independent of earlier ones. In particular, the siblings remain loose in *every* equal- or higher-rank rival, including all O-to-O comparisons with a source sibling of row minimum six.

There is also a direct sufficient test for every rank. Suppose \(n-2\) independent equations common to every rival avoid both siblings. Every source equation of size at most five avoids them. If an image were spent, projecting the rival equations onto just the two named coordinates would have rank two. The common creator contributes \((1,1)\) and a later recipient contributes \((1,0)\) or \((0,1)\). Thus the rival would have rank at least \((n-2)+2=n\), incompatible with the necessary rank drop. Equivalently, \(n-1\) common independent source equations suffice even if some contain the two sibling bits, since every source equation has equal entries on the siblings. This reasoning applies in particular when the source low equations of the required rank transfer. The validation for sources with a live triple is given in the [low-span argument](live_low_span.md). The holder result by itself does not identify the other four distinctions of the common six-place site, its incidences to already identified sites, or the entire legal menu.
