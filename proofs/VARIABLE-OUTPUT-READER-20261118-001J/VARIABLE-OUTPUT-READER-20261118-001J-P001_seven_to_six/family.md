# Every join can be read at distance four in the minimum-width family

**Theorem.** Start with a ternary even-parity junction. The first two additions are unary and make three fresh distinctions each. After them, any two- or three-parent addition may be made, with two fresh distinctions each time. At every finite size in this family, any renaming of the full table preserving bond distances through four identifies all actual junctions, their allowed states and the entire menu of next joins. The rival can be any reachable mixed-output presentation. It receives no promise about the number of source junctions or fresh counts. The cutoff four is necessary already at width 25 for an unordered three-parent verdict.

The family is exactly the branch of single-ternary sources with the least possible width for their number \(n\ge3\) of junctions. Write \(r\) for the number of three-output additions after the seed. Width is \(m=2n+1+r\). The first two additions must be unary, since the first two sites have no ordinary common neighbor. The single-ternary requirement forces each of these additions to make three outputs, so \(r\ge2\). Equality gives \(m=2n+3\) and every subsequent unary addition is impossible. The source at the first seven-to-six event attains this equality. An unlimited sequence of binary joins is permitted. [Minimum event](suffix_windows.md)

## All histories have at most two shrinking joins

A binary addition with two outputs keeps the number of loose distinctions. A three-parent addition with two outputs reduces it by one. Immediately after the first two unary sites the number is seven, for either placement of the second site. To obtain an exact count at arbitrary depth, keep the following data at every stage.

* For each site with at least one loose bit, retain its number of loose bits and the meetings among such sites.
* For every other site, retain its set of neighbors among the still available sites if that set has at least two members. Equal sets can be kept once.

A pair or triple of available holders has a common ordinary neighbor precisely when it is contained in one of the retained sets or in the current neighbor row of an available site. A retired site can gain no more meetings. To update, consume a bit at every parent, connect the new site to all of them, retire exhausted parents with their updated rows, and restrict every neighbor set to the new available sites. There are finitely many such records up to permutations of the available holders. With at most seven loose bits, there are at most seven available sites. Every represented transition is lawful because any realization admits exactly those parent groups. Any chosen bit at a given parent effects the same update. Starting from both possible three-site histories therefore captures every finite continuation. ## The two possible shrinking junctions still reveal themselves

Use \(K=D+1\). The graph of actual holders is simple and bipartite, and its ordinary junction supports are independent parity equations and are bonds. A minimal nonempty parity equation is the cut around a connected shore with connected complement in the representation with loose bits wired to an exterior. All comparisons below permit arbitrary relabeling. [Parity and cuts](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/separator.md)

The first three members of any ordinary shore, ordered by their births, have cut size at least seven. Their degrees sum to at least eleven, and at most two meetings are internal. A subsequent selected site of \(p\) inputs and \(h\) outputs with \(j\) parents already selected changes the boundary by \(p+h-2j\). The only negative increment is minus one, when a shrinking site has all three parents inside. There are at most two such sites. Every cut on three or more ordinary sites consequently has size at least five. A cut of size five on such a shore has to use **both** shrinking sites, strictly after the first three, with all six incidences to their respective parents inside. In particular **no input bit of either shrinking site can occur in any such five-bit cut**. [Boundary identity](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001I/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001I-P001_cycle_obstruction/nonshrinking.md#exactly-when-three-sites-can-have-a-short-boundary)

All ternary and four-bit equations are forced in any matching rival by the low-equation theorem, since the source has no four-bit bond on three or more sites. Every five-place source site \(v\) is a shrinking site. For one of its three input bits \(e\), every source bond through \(e\) of size at most five is therefore a single star or an adjacent sum of the seed and a four-place site. No other two-site sum qualifies with a unique ternary and the simplicity of ordinary meetings.

We exhibit two inputs that force the five-place star in the rival. If the seed is an input parent, choose the other two. Their endpoints away from \(v\) have the same color as the seed and neither meets it. Otherwise some one of the three input parents avoids the seed, since a common seed witness would have forbidden the original join. Choose that parent and either other one. Neither of the two chosen opposite endpoints is the seed, the two are distinct and of the same color, and at least one avoids the seed. The two input bits have \(K=5\). A smaller common star would need to contain both endpoints of those two arms away from \(v\); no such ordinary holder exists. A short two-star cut using \(v\) costs at least six and a short cut avoiding \(v\) cannot use two different same-color opposite endpoints as adjoining sites. For any outside bit within \(K\le5\) of a chosen arm, that bit must meet the arm's opposite endpoint, or possibly the seed if the endpoint is four-place and meets the seed. The two same-color endpoints do not meet one another, and the endpoint chosen to avoid the seed does not meet it either. Hence no outside bit is within \(K\le5\) of both arms. A rival bond realizing their minimum five must consist of exactly the five bits of \(v\). This proof still works when the whole frontier has already shrunk to five. We have forced \(n\) independent equations in the rival. Its ternary supports agree with the source, so it too has just one ternary. A rival with \(n'\ge3\) sites at this width must have \(n'\le n\) by the width bound. Its rank is exactly its number of ordinary sites, hence \(n'=n\), every equation agrees, and the rival also has precisely two three-output additions. In particular it too lies in this family. Equality of the full allowed states already gives every three-parent decision by the [earlier state-to-triple theorem](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/inverse.md). The next step identifies all ordinary junctions themselves.

## Locating the ordinary junctions from their equations

The seed and its one-child prefix are also recognized directly by their ternary and four-bit stars and the width bound. For \(n=3\) all sites have at most four places. The preceding rank argument forces the full space and each rival four-bit bond is itself an actual star. For \(n\ge4\), the first three ordinary sites form a length-two path. The only possible fourth site closes a four-cycle on the two endpoints. Every later site has at least two distinct ordinary parents. Adding a vertex with two different neighbors to a graph that stays connected after deleting one vertex preserves this property. Thus the ordinary graph of both presentations is two-connected. Their wired graphs stay connected after deletion of any two vertices. If the exterior is one, use the preceding ordinary property. If both are ordinary, use the [two-ordinary-site deletion lemma](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/inverse.md#two-elementary-source-restrictions).

The shared state relation determines the simple cycles of each wired graph, including parallel pairs, as its minimal nonempty allowed flip sets. The common parity equations supply the bonds. Among these bonds, all the ordinary stars and the exterior star are identifiable as an unordered collection. Delete a bond and link remaining bits whenever a surviving cycle contains both. For a vertex star, its deletion leaves a graph connected after any further one-vertex deletion. Any two incident remaining edges are in one surviving cycle, using a path between their other endpoints avoiding their common vertex, or the two-edge cycle for parallel edges. This links all edges into one component. For any other bond each of its two connected shores has at least two vertices, hence at least one surviving edge on each side. No surviving cycle links the sides. This detects precisely the complete augmented star collection using the state relation alone.

An exterior with seven or six bits is recognizable since all ordinary stars have at most five. In the remaining case the exterior has five bits and the source has reached the second shrinking join. All stars of size three or four are ordinary in both presentations, since the rival has at least five loose bits. Suppose a different five-bit star were exterior in the rival. Every parallel pair in the common augmented graph must join the two proposed exteriors, as two ordinary sites meet at most once. The physically latest site in each presentation has two unused fresh outputs to the exterior, which requires it to be the *other* proposed exterior. Its remaining three bits join older ordinary sites and are precisely its three inputs. Deleting the two proposed exteriors leaves an identical ordinary prefix in both histories, with the same six loose bits. The respective final sites consume complementary triples on that prefix, both lawful. The finite closure excludes exactly this configuration. Thus the exterior agrees, and the actual junctions, allowed states and complete next menu agree on the same names.

The theorem is a distance-only identification. For example a finite reader may enumerate all candidate reachable presentations on the supplied names, keep the ones consistent with the clipped table, and read any one's junctions. The width bounds the number of junctions in that search. The theorem proves the same answer for every surviving candidate, including ones without any supplied history or arity profile. Equivalently one can recover the low equations, include the five-bit equations forced by the pair test, and use their span and the preceding exterior test.

## Distance three can change a legal choice

A source of 11 junctions and 25 distinctions has the legal mark \(\{12,20,21\}\). Exchange distinctions 19 and 20. They came from the same four-place site, but 19 has since been used by the five-place junction and 20 is still loose. The exchange preserves every low-site support through four bits and all 300 entries through \(D=3\), yet the new mark \(\{12,19,21\}\) is forbidden. Eight entries first distinguish the exchange at \(D=4\). The checker builds the actual parity space and replays every history and operand. Thus four is sharp for the infinite family even if only the next three-parent verdict is required. [Literal supports and exact comparison](frontier.json)
