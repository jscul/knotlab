# Four ordinary holder steps suffice on the two seven-knot sources

On either registered source, the current holdings within four ordinary steps of **any one proposed parent** decide every birth that is ready, including its fresh count at the instant it is made. Every parent-closed finite part of the reference construction is admitted. No other part is admitted. The result holds under parity and not-all-equal, modulo exchange of loose distinctions held by the same knot. Under unrestricted raw fairness the rule builds the full reference construction. Under global joins-first, raw fairness still suffices and is attainable. Weak fairness applied after priority admits a counterorder on **each** source. ## Present observation and reference

One ordinary holder step crosses a shared distinction to its other knot. The view contains the proposed parent marks, the knots within the stated number of steps, their actual loose distinctions, and all shared distinctions with both holders in the view. Distinct shared distinctions at one meeting remain distinct. A boundary knot need not reveal any meeting with an unseen knot. In fact, at radius four the test uses no loose count or total place count at the outer boundary. A proposal with a parent more than two steps from another is rejected on these sources. Knot names and the names of loose distinctions carry no order. A spent knot has no loose distinctions.

The original meetings are \(US,VS,WS,SA,AB,BW,AZ\), each repeated nine times. The argument also works for any common multiplicity \(M\ge2\). There are two loose distinctions at each of \(U,V\). In the first source \(W\) is spent and \(Z\) holds four loose distinctions. In the second \(W,Z\) hold two each. The letters are for the proof. An **original** is recognized by its current meeting with another knot at least twice. Every original has such a meeting forever. A birth takes at most one distinction from each of its distinct parents, so no later knot ever has one. For an original \(s\), recover its initial loose count by

\[
h_0(s)=\text{number of places at }s
       -\text{number of distinctions in all repeated meetings at }s .
\]

Thus spending an original distinction preserves this reading. This test uses the repeated original meetings in the domain of the theorem. A source presented with unit meetings requires a separate identification argument.

The previously proved chain and relay descriptions give the following reference for verifying the rule. A two-index part has positions \((i,j)\) with nonnegative indices. Its one-step parent positions are obtained by subtracting one from a positive index. Every finite partial build is closed under these predecessors. Each written position supplies one distinction to each of its two successors. A position with exactly one positive index has one parent and two fresh distinctions. An interior position has two parents and two fresh distinctions. These indices describe the proof only. ## The physical rule

Require all proposed parents to be distinct and live. Take the union of the following permissions, forming any one permitted birth. In each case require the indicated unspent capacity. The same structural birth is counted once. All tests refer to meetings which have already been written.

**Original births.** At an original witness read the originals with positive \(h_0\) in repeated meetings with it. The only such set of size at least two in these sources has size \(t=2\) or \(3\). Each pair \(s,s'\) in it may be made once, and receives \(h_0(s)+h_0(s')-t\) fresh distinctions. An existing pair child is a common nonoriginal neighbor. At an original \(s\), subtract from \(h_0(s)\) its number of prescribed original pairs, obtained from those witnessed sets. This is its unary quota. Subtract the number of written children meeting \(s\) and no other original. If the result is positive, a unary birth may be made with \(\max(2,h_0(s)-1)\) fresh distinctions.

Thus in the first source \(U,V\) each make one unary with two fresh, and \(Z\) makes four with three fresh each. In the second source \(U,V,W\) make no original unaries and \(Z\) makes two with two fresh each. In either case \(U,V\) remain available for their pair until it is made, with respectively two or one fresh. Each new neighbor at an original is a child of that original. Counting its other original neighbors distinguishes an original pair child from a unary even after both have made further children.

**The three original pairs.** Recognize a nonoriginal three-place knot meeting two originals. Such knots are exactly the three original pair children in the shifted case. Three of them may make one birth with one fresh distinction when their original neighbor sets are the three pairs of three originals. The common child then has four places. Any live nonoriginal four-place knot meeting even one of these recognized three-place knots is that common child, since the latter three-place knot's other two neighbors are its originals. Permit this four-place knot one unary with two fresh distinctions. Its own single fresh distinction pays for the unary exactly once.

**The four children of one original.** Recognize an original \(z\) with \(h_0(z)=4\). Its single-meeting neighbors are precisely its already written unary children. Use these children themselves as singleton names. For a subset \(J\) of the written singletons, define an existing representative recursively. Its singleton representatives are those knots. For larger \(J\), a representative is an existing common neighbor, other than \(z\), of all representatives of \(J\setminus\{i\}\), for \(i\in J\). If one of those is missing there is no representative. For \(2\le|J|\le4\), when all these parents exist and the representative is absent, permit exactly this parent set with respectively two, one or one fresh distinctions. The live nonoriginal five-place knot can then make one unary with two fresh distinctions.

On a reference prefix the recursive representative is unique. A shared upper neighbor must contain the union \(J\). Meetings change subset size by one, so the upper neighbor must be \(J\) itself. For \(|J|=2\) the other common neighbor is \(z\), which is excluded. For \(|J|\ge3\) a lower common neighbor would have to contain \(|J|-2>0\) elements in the empty intersection of all one-element deletions. No knot from another part meets two of these parents. The new two-index origin only meets the full subset on this side. No labels for unborn singleton children are needed.

**Unaries on the two endless chains.** At a live nonoriginal three-place knot \(x\) exclude the original pair children recognized above. Count its distinct ordinary neighbors which are original or which have three places. Permit a unary with two fresh distinctions when this count is less than two. A noninitial position on a chain has one such neighbor behind it, and acquires its second exactly when its continuation is built. Its other neighbor, if present, is a four-place knot. At a new two-index origin the earlier four- or five-place knot contributes zero to the count. The two unary births from that origin take the count from zero to one to two. At the original starts the preceding original-quota test applies. In particular a live three-place pair in the shifted finite part cannot make a spurious unary, and none of the four-place proper subsets receives an extra unary.

**Two-parent continuations.** Require two nonoriginal parents with three or four places, excluding the recognized three-place original pair children. They must have exactly one common ordinary holder \(c\), and \(c\) must be spent. Exclude the four-open original \(z\) and every nonoriginal \(c\) reached from it by one or two single meetings with only nonoriginal knots after \(z\). The latter are precisely its subset knots of sizes one and two. The previously given subset test already handles all their children and the pairs among children of \(z\), even when \(z\) is still live. In every remaining case permit one binary birth with two fresh distinctions.

To prove this last test, let \(c\) be a spent position in a two-index part. Both its forward positions have been written. For instance, an existing predecessor \(c-e_1\) has its successor \(c\) and its successor \(c-e_1+e_2\), the latter required by \(c+e_2\). Hence that predecessor is also spent. The other direction works the same way. The only possible live neighbors of \(c\) are therefore its two forward children. These have only \(c\) and their joint successor as potential common holders. Requiring exactly one common holder says precisely that the joint successor is absent. Conversely every pending interior birth has these two live parents and their spent common predecessor. At a new origin any earlier finite parent is already spent when the origin is born.

For completeness, check the possible common holders outside this argument. An original of initial loose count zero has no nonoriginal neighbor. Each of the first source's \(U,V\) can meet exactly its pair child and its own first chain child. Their common-holder test gives precisely the intended continuation. The second source's original \(Z\) can meet its two first chain children and gives their intended continuation. The other positive originals are the excluded four-open witness or meet only the excluded three-place initial pairs. A shifted original pair has at most one nonoriginal neighbor. Its full three-parent child, once spent, has only its unary child still potentially live. In the four-branch piece, if a three-element subset is spent then the full subset is present. All two-element predecessors of that three-element subset are then spent, since both of their three-element successors had to exist. If the full subset is spent all its three-element predecessors are spent as well. Each such spent knot has at most one live neighbor. These cases exhaust the meetings across the finite parts and their continuations. They exclude any additional two-parent permission across two parts or two formation sizes.

## Four steps suffice

Fix one proposed parent \(x\). All true multi-parent groups above have every other parent within two ordinary steps of \(x\). In the finite subset case two distinct one-element deletions already share their two-element deletion, interpreted as the original for two singletons. For the three original pairs the relevant shared witnesses are their originals. Otherwise the step bound follows directly from the tests.

* For an original, its witnesses are at step one and their original neighbors at most step two. Computing \(h_0\) for all these knots sees all their incident distinctions by step three. Counting its already written unary children also fits: another original neighbor of such a child would be at most two steps away and can be recognized from its repeated meeting within three.
* Each proposed parent is at most two steps away. Its full arity is available by step three. If it is a three-place knot with two original neighbors, these neighbors are within three steps and the repeated meetings recognizing them are inside four. The shifted triple and its unary successor are therefore decidable. For the chain unary the neighbor counts require at most three steps.
* In the two-parent test every candidate common holder is adjacent to \(x\). Detecting whether that holder is the four-open original, or within one or two single meetings from it through nonoriginal knots, needs at most three steps to the original. Its repeated meetings, loose distinctions and total places are then all visible by step four. The common-holder list itself, its multiplicity and the spent condition are observed directly.
* For a proposed finite subset birth, an admissible \(x\) represents a subset \(X\) of size at most three. The original \(z\) is within three steps. All of its neighbors are visible within four steps. Its original meeting is repeated, and all its single-meeting neighbors are genuine current singletons. Every already written subset \(Y\) is reachable from \(X\) through \(X\cap Y\) in \(|X\mathbin{\triangle}Y|\le4\) ordinary steps. All intermediate subsets exist by parent closure, with the empty subset represented by \(z\). Thus every physical knot and every mutual meeting needed for the recursive test occurs in the same four-step view. Its upper representative, if present, cannot be hidden outside that view. Conversely a supposed positive certificate locating \(x\) as a proper subset representative must be genuine by the uniqueness argument. This justifies checking absence only after identifying the parents.

The construction is invariant under joint renaming. The bound also holds if all loose information on the outer boundary is erased. A one-step view about the **two** marked originals together fails at the initial pair. The visible \(U,V,S\), their internal meetings and loose distinctions agree, while the first fresh count is two versus one. With true loose counts at the outer boundary, two steps about the marked pair together suffice even at a delayed firing. Read the other originals in repeated meetings with their shared witness \(S\). In the shifted case one of them either still holds a loose distinction or has a single meeting with a nonoriginal neighbor of a mark: the only events spending \(W\)'s two distinctions are its pairs with \(U,V\). Every such newly formed neighbor and \(W\) lie in the two-step view, with the meeting visible. In the spent-\(W\) case every other original at \(S\) has initial loose count zero and can never create such a neighbor. Thus the marked pair has exact radius two in this convention. The optimal radius for the entire rule lies between two and four.

Induct on the number of written knots. The reference description applies initially. In any parent-closed reference prefix the physical tests admit exactly its pending births, with the reference number of fresh distinctions and remaining unary multiplicities. At every parent, distinct remaining obligations number exactly its loose distinctions. Choosing a loose distinction for one obligation leaves enough for every other, up to exchanging its siblings. Thus each step yields another parent-closed reference prefix. Conversely all such finite prefixes admit an order of construction. Fixed original loose names and a predetermined assignment to their future children would be an additional input for literal equality.

## Fair orders with and without global priority

Every pending reference event stays pending until it is made. Equivalent unary children of one holder use a finite quota and may be indexed for the proof. Under raw weak fairness, finite induction over the prerequisites of any given event therefore puts it into the limit. Correct structural choices at every finite prefix by themselves allow an infinite execution to omit an independent part.

Global joins-first chooses an enabled multi-parent event whenever one exists. **Any consecutive run of multi-parent events from a given finite prefix is finite on these two sources.** In each two-index part an interior birth cannot create a new maximum of either index: each index is already attained by one of its parents. The possible events without a new unary are consequently confined to a finite product of index sets. The original groups and the four-branch and three-pair parts are finite. Starting a new two-index part after a relay requires a unary. This proves the assertion even where the priority run spans both parts.

Consequently any pending multi-parent event is served eventually by every joins-first execution. While it remains pending the whole execution would otherwise have to consist of multis. An infinite joins-first execution offers infinitely many choices at which there is no multi. A raw-pending unary is in the filtered pool at every such choice until served. Strong fairness on the filtered pool suffices. A raw-fair joins-first completion exists from every finite prefix: repeatedly finish the pending and newly exposed multis, then serve the oldest pending unary. Each such finite prefix has only finitely many earlier pending unaries. The order used to witness existence is a scheduling choice external to the physical admission test.

**Weak fairness after filtering fails on both sources, including an original unary.** Leave all unaries from the original \(Z\) unbuilt. On the spent-\(W\) source start with the compulsory \((1,1)\) on \(U,V\). Then perform

\[
(2,0),(2,1),(3,0),(3,1),(4,0),(4,1),\ldots .
\]

Each first position is one chain unary. Its successor in the list is the only pending multi. On the live-\(W\) source first build the three original pairs and their common child, then its unary child and the first two chain children from this new origin. Build \((1,1)\) and follow the same displayed sequence. All these choices respect global joins-first. Infinitely many single available multis are served immediately. Every deferred unary, including the one at \(Z\), disappears from the filtered pool at each intervening multi. None is continuously admitted to that pool. Thus these executions are weakly fair after filtering and fail raw fairness. The counterorders apply to a rule admitting all physically ready reference events, with this scheduling policy. A rule restricting raw admission to an earlier formation size changes the allowed orders.

## Formation size and a check on the local tests

On correct prefixes the formation size of a knot is one plus its minimum ordinary holder-step count to the recognizable set of originals. Each birth-parent meeting changes formation size by one, meetings between originals leave it unchanged, and a descending parent chain attains the bound. This global quantity orients all birth-parent meetings. No bounded ordinary radius does that even for live knots in one of the present sources. Write a finite rectangular prefix \(0\le i\le L,0\le j\le H\) in a two-index part. At a knot \((i,H)\) farther than a proposed radius from both ends and the other chain, its live rooted view has a reflection exchanging its two neighbors \((i-1,H),(i+1,H)\). One is its earlier parent and the other its already written child. Varying \(i\) within this portion changes its formation size while giving the same view. The pending-event test above needs neither orientation nor size as an input.

As a finite consistency check, all 923 nonempty predecessor-closed subsets of \(\{0,\ldots,5\}^2\) gave exactly their missing one-step permissions under the spent-holder and chain-unary tests, including permissions extending outside the six-index range. Counted across these partial builds there were 1,846 unary and 1,849 binary permissions. All 167 predecessor-closed subsets of the four-element subset family containing the empty subset gave 300 proper binary permissions in total. Omitting the exclusion at spent one- and two-element common holders introduced 294 extra binary permissions over those prefixes. Across 992 choices of a live proper nonempty subset as root, the greatest holder-step count to another written subset was four. These are finite checks of the two components of the proof. The unrestricted formation-size and fairness conclusions follow from the arguments above.
