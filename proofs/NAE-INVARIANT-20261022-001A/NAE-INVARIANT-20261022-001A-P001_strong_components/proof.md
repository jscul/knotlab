# Strong components decide not-all-equal

Status: Proved

Ticket `NAE-INVARIANT-20261022-001A`, 2026-10-22. It uses:
- `CONTACT-CENSUS-20261022-001A-P001` ("the census proof"): not-all-equal with
  fair spokes is one of the two critical contacts, and emergence is
  −log₂ P(consistent);
- `BASE-CRITICALITY-20261022-001A-P001` ("the criticality proof"): the
  recolouring into random implications and the one-way blocking bound.

It cites:
- **The two-literal test.** A formula of two-literal clauses is unsatisfiable
  exactly when a variable and its negation share a strongly connected
  component of the implication digraph (Aspvall, Plass and Tarjan 1979). The
  check's independent solver is the propagation method of Even, Itai and
  Shamir (1976).
- **Minty's painting lemma** (Minty 1966). In a digraph every arc lies on a
  directed cycle or in a directed cut, and not both.
- **Strongly-connected percolation.** Bonds directed by fair coins are the
  random diode model. All bonds present is its percolation threshold, a
  universality class different from ordinary percolation
  ([de Noronha et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.06644);
  [Newman, Grassberger and Ziff 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24975)).
- **Torus topology of complementary configurations**, as used by Pinson (1994)
  and Arguin (2002). The check verifies the trichotomy below exhaustively on
  3 × 3 tori.

## Standard statement

Let G be a finite non-bipartite graph. Each edge e = {u, v} carries a fair bit
x_e and the constraint "not h_u = h_v = x_e".
1. **The double cover.** The implication digraph of this two-literal formula is
   a uniformly random orientation of the bipartite double cover G × K₂, among
   those that the deck swap reverses. The world is inconsistent exactly when
   some strongly connected component is mapped to itself by the swap.
2. **Cyclic edges.** Strongly connected components are the clusters of cyclic
   arcs (arcs on directed cycles). The world is inconsistent exactly when the
   projected cyclic edges have a cluster with an odd cycle. On a square-lattice
   torus that means a cluster with a cross topology or one winding in a class
   of odd length; a cross cluster always contains an odd cycle.
3. **The plane.** In a plane graph, every edge is cyclic in exactly one of an
   orientation and its rotated dual orientation.
4. **Twisted tori.** On a non-bipartite torus the same holds exactly for the
   swap-reversed orientation of the double cover. For an orientation of the
   torus itself it fails: some edges are cyclic both ways.
5. **Consequences.** On every non-bipartite square-lattice torus:
   - P(all clusters contractible) = P(a cross cluster);
   - P(consistent) = P(contractible) + P(even winding);
   - the emergence lies between −log₂(1 − P₀) and −log₂ P₀, with
     P₀ = P(contractible).

## Nearest known result

- **Item 1** combines the classical two-literal test with the bipartite double
  cover. That this ensemble is a swap-reversed random orientation is
  elementary.
- **Item 2** is elementary graph theory and surface topology.
- **Item 3** is Minty's lemma with planar cycle–cut duality. The consequence
  for random orientations is that cyclic edges form an exactly self-dual
  configuration of density 1/2 on self-dual lattices. It fits the threshold
  p_c = 1 that Newman, Grassberger and Ziff derive for the random diode model
  from the out-component coupling. A bounded search did not find the
  complementarity itself stated (moderate folklore risk).
- **Item 4** (exact duality on twisted tori, its failure without a twist) and
  **item 5** were not found in a bounded search. Newman, Grassberger and Ziff
  measure untwisted wrapping probabilities (one direction 0.75001(7), close to
  3/4). Twisted tori and not-all-equal do not appear there.

Novelty: `candidate-local-delta` for items 4 and 5. Items 1–3 are classical
ingredients in a new arrangement.

## Definitions

- **Tori.** T = Z²/Λ, with Λ spanned by w₁ = (L_h, 0) and w₂ = (s, L_v), taken
  large enough that T is a simple graph.
  - A closed walk of class c = a·w₁ + b·w₂ has length of parity
    p(c) = a·L_h + b·(s + L_v) mod 2, because contractible closed walks are
    even.
  - T is non-bipartite, or **twisted**, when p ≠ 0. The double cover
    G × K₂ is then the torus T̃ = Z²/Λ₀, with Λ₀ = ker p.
  - The swap τ is translation by any period of odd parity. It acts as the
    identity on the homology of T̃.
- **Dual.** T* is the lattice of face centres, which is T translated by
  (1/2, 1/2). An orientation D gives the dual orientation D*: each arc is
  rotated by +90°, so that (arc, dual arc) is a positive frame.
- **Cyclic.** An arc a → b is cyclic if b reaches a. For the world, ω̄ ⊆ E is
  the set of edges whose lifts are cyclic.
- **Topology.** A cluster K of edges has a homology image L_K ⊆ Λ, generated by
  the classes of its closed walks. It is:
  - contractible if L_K = 0;
  - winding in class c if L_K = Z·c;
  - cross if L_K has rank 2.

## Theorem 1: the double cover

Write (v, 1) for "h_v = 1" and (v, 0) for "h_v = 0". The constraint on
e = {u, v} with x_e = 1 is the clause ¬h_u ∨ ¬h_v, with implications
(u, 1) → (v, 0) and (v, 1) → (u, 0). With x_e = 0 it is h_u ∨ h_v, with
(u, 0) → (v, 1) and (v, 0) → (u, 1).

These are the two lifts of e to G × K₂, each oriented one way.
- **τ reverses them.** It maps (u, 1) → (v, 0) to (u, 0) → (v, 1), the reverse
  of (v, 1) → (u, 0).
- **Uniform.** The bit x_e chooses the orientation of one lift and τ fixes the
  other, so fair spokes give the uniform measure on swap-reversed
  orientations.

*Inconsistency.* By the two-literal test, the world is inconsistent exactly
when some (v, 1) and (v, 0) share a strong component C. Then:
- τC is a strong component of the reversed digraph;
- the reversed digraph has the same strong components;
- τC meets C, so τC = C.

Conversely, if τC = C and (v, h) ∈ C, then (v, 1 − h) ∈ C. ∎

## Theorem 2: cyclic edges

1. **Strong components are clusters of cyclic arcs.**
   - An arc a → b is cyclic exactly when a and b share a strong component.
   - A directed path between two vertices of one component stays in it, and
     its arcs are cyclic.
2. **Cyclic arcs are swap-invariant.** τ maps directed cycles to reversed
   directed cycles, so ω̄ is well defined on G.
3. **Odd clusters.** Let K be a cluster of ω̄.
   - **If K has an odd cycle,** its lift is connected and made of cyclic arcs.
     So it lies in one strong component containing (v, 0) and (v, 1), and the
     world is inconsistent.
   - **Conversely,** suppose C is a swap-invariant component through (v, 1) and
     (v, 0). A path of cyclic arcs from (v, 1) to (v, 0) projects to an odd
     closed walk in one cluster of ω̄.
4. **On a torus.** K has an odd cycle exactly when L_K contains an odd vector.
   For a winding cluster that means p(c) = 1. For a cross cluster it always
   holds, by the lemma.

*Lemma (cross clusters carry everything).* If L_K has rank 2, then L_K = Λ.

*Proof.*
- **Two simple cycles.** The classes of simple cycles generate L_K, so K
  contains simple cycles C₁, C₂ with independent classes. Their intersection
  number is a nonzero determinant, so they meet (at lattice vertices).
- **The union is cellular.** Let F be a face of U = C₁ ∪ C₂.
  - **Inside F, every simple closed curve γ is contractible.** If γ is
    non-contractible in T, it is disjoint from C₁ and C₂, so its class has zero
    intersection with two independent classes. That makes it zero, and γ
    bounds a disc in T.
  - **That disc lies in F.** The disc cannot contain the connected,
    non-contractible U.
  - So F is a disc.
- **Conclusion.** T is U plus 2-cells, so H₁(U) maps onto H₁(T) ≅ Λ. ∎

## Theorem 3: complementary cyclic edges in the plane

Let G be a connected plane graph with an orientation D.
- **If e is not cyclic in D,** Minty's lemma puts it in a directed cut δ(U),
  with every arc entering U.
  - **The dual arcs of δ(U) are balanced at every face.** Go around a face
    counterclockwise. Its boundary alternates between switches into U, whose
    dual arcs enter the face, and switches out of U, whose dual arcs leave it.
  - So δ(U)* splits into directed dual cycles, and e* is cyclic in D*.
- **If e* lies on a directed dual cycle Γ,** Γ is a Jordan curve. Every primal
  arc crossing it points to the same side, so e lies in a directed cut and is
  not cyclic. ∎

*Consequence.* For a uniformly random orientation, P_G(e cyclic) +
P_{G*}(e* cyclic) = 1. On a self-dual lattice, and in a translation-invariant
limit, the density of cyclic edges is 1/2. The cyclic configuration is
exactly self-dual: this is the strongly-connected percolation of the random
diode model at its threshold.

## Theorem 4: the twist makes the duality exact

Let T be twisted, and let D̃ be swap-reversed on T̃ with rotated dual D̃* on T̃*.
**At least one.** If an edge is not cyclic in D̃, the balance argument of
Theorem 3 is local to faces. It holds on any surface, so the dual edge is
cyclic in D̃*.

**Not both.** Suppose e lies on a directed cycle C of D̃, and e* lies on a
directed dual cycle Γ of D̃*.
- **Every crossing has sign +1.** At a crossing, C uses an arc f and Γ uses
  f*, and (f, f*) is a positive frame. So the intersection number is
  ⟨[C], [Γ]⟩ = #crossings ≥ 1.
- **The swapped cycle.** τ commutes with rotation and reverses D̃ and D̃*. So
  C′ = reverse(τC) is a directed cycle of D̃ with [C′] = −[C].
- **Contradiction.** ⟨[C′], [Γ]⟩ = −⟨[C], [Γ]⟩ < 0, but C′ and Γ are a directed
  primal cycle and a directed dual cycle, whose intersection number counts
  crossings and is ≥ 0. ∎

**Without a twist** there is no C′. A directed primal winding cycle can then
cross a directed dual winding cycle, and both edges at the crossing are
cyclic. The check finds such orientations in 3 × 3 tori. Without the twist,
P(contractible) and P(cross) differ: 0.09 against 0.68 in 4,000 sampled
orientations of the 3 × 3 torus.

## Theorem 5: consequences on twisted tori

By Theorem 4, ω̄* on T* is the complement of ω̄.

*Trichotomy.* For complementary configurations on a torus, exactly one holds:
- (i) ω̄ has a cross cluster and ω̄* is all contractible;
- (ii) the reverse;
- (iii) both wind, in the same class.

*Proof sketch.*
- **(i) ⇒ ω̄* contractible.** A cross cluster's faces are discs (lemma), and
  every dual cluster lies in one face.
- **Symmetrically,** (ii) holds when ω̄* has a cross cluster.
- **If neither has a cross cluster,** both have winding clusters. Primal and
  dual cycles of complementary sets never cross, so their classes are
  parallel. A configuration whose complement is all contractible has a cross
  cluster: the outside of the discs holding the dual clusters carries all of
  H₁. The check verifies the trichotomy on all 3 × 3 patterns.

*Consequences.*
- **The dual is a copy.** T* is T translated, and rotation is a bijection
  between swap-reversed orientations. So the dual configuration has the same
  law as the primal, and
  P(ω̄ contractible) = P(ω̄* cross) = P(ω̄ cross).
- **Consistency.** By Theorem 2, the world is consistent exactly when ω̄ is
  contractible or winds in an even class. Hence P(consistent) = P₀ + P(even
  winding) ≥ P₀, and P(inconsistent) ≥ P(cross) = P₀.
- **The sandwich.** So −log₂(1 − P₀) ≤ E ≤ −log₂ P₀. ∎

**The lower bound (side question).** Emergence stays bounded away from 0 at
fixed shape whenever P₀ stays bounded below. P₀ is the probability that the
twisted torus has no winding strong component. A uniform lower bound would
be a crossing estimate of Russo–Seymour–Welsh type for strongly-connected
percolation.
- **Why it is not cheap.** That model lacks the positive correlation the
  classical proofs use. The simple certificates of the criticality proof do
  not supply it.
- **Status.** The lower bound stays open, now reduced to this single estimate.

## Check

`check.py` (standard library) verifies:
- **Exhaustive, 3 × 3 tori.** All 262,144 patterns of the 3 × 3 tori with
  periods (3, 0), (0, 3) and (3, 0), (1, 3).
  - Consistency is computed exactly by marking each assignment's subcube of
    allowed patterns, and must equal the strong-component test.
  - The strong-component test must equal two-colouring of the cyclic clusters
    and the homology classification.
  - Every cross cluster's lattice must be the whole period lattice.
  - Primal and dual cyclic edges must be complementary.
  - The trichotomy must hold.
  - Contractible and cross counts must be equal.
  - Consistency must equal contractible plus even windings.
- **Sampled, larger tori.** The same on 6,800 sampled patterns of five larger
  twisted tori (both odd-side patterns and two shears), with the independent
  propagation solver.
- **Planes.** All orientations of the 3 × 3 and 2 × 4 plane grids: no edge is
  cyclic both or neither way.
- **No twist.** 20,000 sampled orientations of the untwisted 3 × 3 torus: no
  edge is cyclic neither way, and some are cyclic both ways.
- **The sandwich** on the exhaustive tori.
- **Controls (must be rejected):**
  - a "forced variable" test in place of the strong-component test;
  - a wrong rotation for north arcs;
  - the identity P₀ = P(cross) for untwisted orientations.

## What this means (interpretation, not theorem)

- **Not-all-equal's critical invariant is a wrapping probability of strong
  components.** The fair world is consistent exactly when a twisted random
  orientation of the torus has no strong component wrapping an odd class.
  Strong components are the two-way connections: a and b are in one
  component when each reaches the other.
- **The twist is what makes the problem clean.** Going around an odd cycle
  reverses every arrow. That forbids a primal and a dual wrapping cycle from
  crossing, and makes the strong components an exactly self-dual percolation
  on the torus, with P(contractible) = P(cross) as for ordinary percolation.
- **What remains unknown is the class's own torus theory.** Strongly-connected
  percolation is a known universality class. Its fractal dimension is 1.8035
  (Newman, Grassberger and Ziff), different from percolation, the backbone
  and every Fortuin–Kasteleyn model. No closed formula for its torus
  topology is known. The experiment tests candidate formulas.
