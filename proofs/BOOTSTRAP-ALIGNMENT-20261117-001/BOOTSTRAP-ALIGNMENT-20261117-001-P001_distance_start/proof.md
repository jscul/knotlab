# Getting the original eight aligned from their changing bond distances

**Theorem.** In the original common binary value convention, a rule observing only the current scalar bond-distance table can bring the actual eight betweenness distinctions to the first aligned mixed round. One such rule uses two bounded updates. It tests every old pair with fresh betweennesses, then uses the resulting table to assemble the parity extension and forget the test distinctions. Every permitted orientation succeeds. Under the inherited fresh parity attachments alone, some branches reach the same target, but no adaptive choice rule observing those tables guarantees alignment under renaming.

## Observation, operations and target

For a finite relation of allowed binary states, a bond here is a constrained nonempty projection on at least three distinctions such that forgetting any one frees the rest (K1–K3). For distinct identities the scalar bond distance is the minimum size of a bond containing both, minus one, with infinity if there is none. No pair-disagreement frequencies are observed. A renaming transports both the table entries and the rule's set of admissible actions. A rule may make arbitrary choices among indistinguishable possibilities. A guarantee covers every naming and all its admitted choices.

Write the eight truth assignments as words on the proof index set `{0,1}³`. The two constant words and the three coordinate cuts and their complements form the literal relation \(S=\{0,1,x,\bar x,y,\bar y,z,\bar z\}\). This description specifies the test fixture. The controller initially receives eight opaque identities and their scalar table. The target uses those same identities as the eight designated-direction outputs of the first two-by-two-by-two block in the 35-junction complete round \(I_4\). The placement must represent the **actual** cuts in the source's common value convention. A single common complement is harmless. Independently changing what each old bit means changes the literal target. The [original construction](../../CUBIC-FROM-A-KNOT-20261117-001/CUBIC-FROM-A-KNOT-20261117-001-P001_local_growth/cube_interface.md) specifies its legal moves. The controller knows that growth law, the attachment convention and when attachment is complete. It receives no list assigning old identities to sites.

## A uniform test with the source betweenness

Write \(B(l,m,r)\) for the original law that equal endpoints require the middle to agree. In one parallel update, for **every** unordered old pair \(a,b\), introduce a private fresh pair \(p,q\) with \(B(p,a,q)\) and \(B(p,b,q)\). All 28 updates have the same form. Either opposed setting of the endpoints is available on every original row. Equal endpoints with value \(v\) are available precisely when \(a=b=v\). Given an old row, different endpoint pairs are independent. Thus the new relation on 64 distinctions keeps exactly the original eight-row marginal.

Let \(J(a,b)\) consist of the old \(c\) that agree with \(a\) whenever \(a=b\). The projection on \(p,c,q\) is exactly the six rows of betweenness for \(c\in J(a,b)\). The constants produce both equal triples, and opposed endpoints permit either middle. Outside \(J(a,b)\) there is a violating row and its complement. Together these give the other two triples, so the projection is free. A single endpoint, with its mate absent, may be set either way above every old row. A triple with such an endpoint and two old bits is therefore free. A triple that takes endpoints from different fresh pairs without completing a pair is also free. A complete fresh pair and one endpoint of a different pair is free by the two old constant rows and the independence of distinct pairs above any old row. All binary projections are free. The old-only triples retain precisely their old projections. This exhausts all ways a triple can occur.

Consequently the distance-two neighbors of \(p\) are exactly \(q\) and \(J(a,b)\). On the literal cuts, \(J(a,b)\) fixes just the coordinates where \(a\) and \(b\) agree. If they differ in one, two or three coordinates, this gives 2, 4 or 8 old members. The counts of unordered probes of these types are 12, 12 and 4. Each old identity belongs to 13 intervals by symmetry and double-counting \((12\cdot2+12\cdot4+4\cdot8)/8\). It has seven other old neighbors at scalar distance two, plus both endpoints of each of these 13 probes, giving degree 33. The fresh endpoints have degrees 3, 5 and 9. Thus the anonymous reader recognizes the eight degree-33 identities. The degree-three endpoints name exactly the twelve neighbor pairs through their two old neighbors. This rule requires no remembered original/probe marks or identities for the pair attachments themselves. [Reader, exhaustive projections and controls](check_probe.py) · [Counts](probe.json)

The comparison of endpoints in \(B\) uses the source's literal common values. This operation breaks the independent-flip invariance used in the parity-only proof. The four translated seeds all pass the same anonymous measurement and yield four correspondingly different neighbor graphs. The controller reads just the scalar table in each case. The sensitivity comes from the **permitted operation**.

## From the measured neighbor graph to the actual aligned round

Choose any vertex \(r\) of the measured graph and any ordering \(u_1,u_2,u_3\) of its three neighbors. In the measured graph define a bit at \(v\) in position \(i\) by whether \(v\) is closer in graph steps to \(u_i\) than to \(r\). This recovers a chart of the eight from the scalar measurement. Relative to an external grading chart every choice only permutes or complements coordinate directions and therefore preserves the literal set of cut rows. There are 48 such choices. Neither a canonical origin nor a name-based tie break is required. The computed action family transports exactly under renaming.

The second bounded update uses the ordinary simultaneous local growth rule on an abstract single parity junction for four rounds. The proof above using the root and three tip distances indexes the generated target. For each of its eight first-block sites, the update reuses the measured bit with that chart value in the designated outgoing direction. It introduces the other two outputs fresh. At each of the other 27 sites it uses the outputs of the already generated parents and introduces three fresh outputs. There is no old-position list in the input. One implementation issues all of these constraints from this **one** reading of the table. A serial replay verifies the eight two-fresh attachments and the legal 27 three-fresh joins, including their original identities at both ends. Each first-block gate has two lifts of every previous assignment. Each remaining gate has four. The full relation has \(8\,2^8 4^{27}=2^{65}\) rows on 105 distinctions. It contains 35 parity junctions, 60 shared distinctions and 45 loose distinctions. The actual eight survive exactly.

To remove the temporary endpoints, let \(Q(K,W)\) be their 56 constraints and \(C(K,Z)\) the assembled parity constraints. For every \(k\in S\) one can set every probe pair opposed. Hence

\[
 \exists w\;[S(k)\land Q(k,w)\land C(k,z)] \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S(k)\land C(k,z).
\]

Forgetting the 56 endpoints gives exactly the target relation on the same eight and the 97 genuine fresh distinctions. This protocol proves an equivariant rule with two bounded updates, including temporary measurement and forgetting. The replay checks every intermediate physical junction. It does not establish a controller that must redecide each individual junction from scratch after locally erasing all probe information.

There is a reason to specify erasure. In the exact target all pairs are free. Of its 187,460 three-place projections the exhaustive short-support certificate gives 48 six-row betweennesses using only the original eight and three four-row pure parities. The latter entail no betweenness for any choice of middle. All remaining triples are free. Thus a betweenness on three distinct identities including any permanent fresh output cannot stay in the literal final system. The check enumerates the ternary supports from the accepted complete census and tests all possible middles on their actual rows. [Terminal check](check_scopes.py)

## A further test with a new scalar observation at every complete round

Here is a second implementation. It was designed after the two-update proof. It keeps the 64-distinction measured relation intact, starts a separate ordinary three-place parity junction and grows it by the local rule for four actual rounds. Until attachment this is a product of two relations. A minimal constrained support cannot cross the product, since its projection factors and all its proper projections are free. The component of the distance-two graph containing the degree-33 distinctions identifies the 64 measured distinctions. In the growing parity part only the three distinctions at the initial junction have two distance-two neighbors, and the rest have none. Using the inherited pure distance reader on the complement recovers the ordinary junctions and their loose outputs at each round. Its [short-support theorem](../../BOND-NEXT-JOIN-20261117-001/BOND-NEXT-JOIN-20261117-001-P001_distance_reader/proof.md) is already a dependency of the aligned reader.

The five successive whole-system tables have 67, 76, 94, 124 and 169 distinctions. They reveal 1, 4, 10, 20 and 35 ordinary junctions. The reader issues 3, 6, 10 and 15 ordinary joins. In each observation it finds both parts again. At the last it recovers the root and the three tips of the grown part, derives the locations of the eight shared outputs, and matches these to the frame on the measured eight. Identify the eight placeholder outputs with the corresponding original identities and forget the 56 probes. Equivalently, impose one equality per matched pair and existentially forget the placeholders and probes. Substituting these variables in the parity equations gives precisely the literal target.

This last update has a real cost. It preserves all rows on the original eight, while constraining the other component. On its 97 retained distinctions the possible assignments fall from \(2^{70}\) to \(2^{65}\). The eight placeholder columns have full rank, so their previous values are determined by the other 97.
