## A. Separated readers

**Rebuilt by argument.** Partition the distinctions into disjoint readers and an unread remainder. Each knot meets at most one reader. For a fixed unread value word $u$, collect the complex knot factors meeting reader $j$ into an unnormalised vector $v_{ju}$, and the unread-only factors into $c_u$. The amplitude at a tuple of reader words is then $c_u\prod_j v_{ju}(r_j)$. Tracing out the unread distinctions gives a mixture of normalised product states, with weights proportional to $|c_u|^2\prod_j\|v_{ju}\|^2$. Omit zero-weight records. This works for any finite number of readers, any knot laws and any nonzero normalisable product of knot factors, including factors with phases or zeros. A shared draw of $u$, followed by each reader's local response to its own setting, reproduces the joint probabilities. Settings must be independent of the preparation, and the local responses may use the full local question. No outcomes are discarded. An exact-entry check used both equal and complex knot factors with one, two and three readers. [Separation check](separation.py)

**Narrowing · Conditional independence.** The decision says “Part A is the literature's statement that a product of local factors over a factor graph makes regions conditionally independent given what separates them; here every knot is a factor and nothing separates the readers but unread distinctions, so they are independent outright.” In two even-parity knots on $(a_1,a_2,u)$ and $(b_1,b_2,u)$, both reader parities equal $u$. Each is fair, and both are even with probability 1/2, against 1/4 for independent parities. The joint state is fully separable. Under equal-positive parity counting, the vectors at fixed $u$ for each reader are sums over equal or disjoint cosets, which also gives the claimed common local basis. The conclusion that extends to arbitrary knot factors is separability. For instance, change the two laws to not-all-equal. The two normalised local vectors have supports $\{01,10,11\}$ and $\{00,01,10\}$, with inner product 2/3. Writing their projectors as $P,Q$, the joint state $(P\otimes P+Q\otimes Q)/2$ has commutator $[P,Q]\otimes(P-Q)/4$ with its first marginal tensored with the identity. A state diagonal in a common product basis would give zero. [Decision, intuition](../decision.md#L43) · [README, part A](../README.md#L13)

## C. What the contact list establishes

**Rebuilt.** Give the ring readers independent fair signs $a,b$ from a shared list. The middle, given both ring settings and its own, returns $C_sab$ when $|C_s|=1$, and an independent fair sign otherwise. This gives all 256 entries, including zeros. One setting bit even suffices for the entire table. Give B two independent signs, one per setting. The middle receives A's setting, predicts B's setting from the even-setting promise and uses B's corresponding sign in the same rule. On an odd setting B uses its other, independent sign. For a setting-local list on the loose-chain start, pre-share $a_0,a_1,b_0,b_1$ independently, and let the middle's two answers be $a_0b_0,-a_1b_0$. [Enumerated strategies](game.py) · [Loose-chain local check](controls.py)

**Narrowing · What a reader sees.** “Every probability is nonnegative and no reader or pair of readers sees another's setting.” The operational statement holds for the **answer marginals** with the retained readers' settings fixed, including the four unscored settings. On the scored settings alone any two readers can already infer the third setting from the even-parity promise. The very same table admits the explicit strategy in which the middle receives a setting. Holding a distinction in a connecting knot specifies the preparation and the support of the reads. It supplies no rule for transmitting settings during play. [README, part B](../README.md#L33)

**Narrowing · Fairness depends on when the record is fixed.** “So the second middle setting does not make the test fairer than the sorted square: the sorted square too is won by a middle that hears the row and the column.” Giving a middle both current settings permits a simulation in either arrangement. In the earlier square the raw ring-pair marginal is local by A. A simulator whose middle knows both settings and the pre-shared answers can sample the middle record from its conditional distribution given those settings and answers. However, the earlier protocol also admits a herald fixed **before** the row and column are chosen. Its two signs can be given to one reader then. With settings drawn independently of that record and the preparation, a classical list still wins at most 8/9. We recounted 4,096 valid deterministic lists, 144 attaining eight wins. The earlier referees' raw score of 2/3 and sign-corrected score of 1 were read. With private settings the new game tests the 3/4 bound without sorting. With a setting message it admits the simulation above. These facts do not give a protocol-independent ranking of fairness. ## D. Which real reads have a list

**Rebuilt by proof and complete search.** Let $S$ be the allowed words of homogeneous parity laws and $S^\perp$ their sums of laws. Use equal positive amplitudes on $S$. Each reader chooses one Hermitian product $i^k X^xZ^z$, with $k=|x\cap z|$ even. The product is real. On two copies this includes $YY$. Draw $w\in S$ and $p\in S^\perp$ independently and uniformly. Return the sign with exponent $k/2+w\cdot z+p\cdot x$. For every subset of the readers, character cancellation gives a zero product mean unless the combined change belongs to $S$ and the combined value mask belongs to $S^\perp$. Otherwise the mean is the product of the local phase signs. Directly translating the words in the coherent state gives exactly these means. All subset means determine every joint probability for any number of local settings. [Amplitude and list calculations](real_reads.py)

**Narrowing · Positivity, the read class, and the conclusion.** “Without sorting the game needs a quarter-turn read: with positive amplitudes and real reads no game of certainty exists on one or two shared triples, with two or three settings per reader (a lemma, checked on every certainty).” The full-table lemma uses the **equal-positive linear counting state and one real Pauli product per reader per trial**. For two commuting reads at the same reader, changing a distinction can reverse the other read's value sign. The one-read proof uses disjoint supports between readers and does not cover that joint question. Arbitrary real observables also fall outside the lemma. Positivity alone does not give a list reproducing every probability. For positive amplitudes proportional to $(4,2,1,4)$, local $X,Z$ reads give $XX+ZZ+ZX-XZ=79/37$, above the local bound 2. An independent third reader leaves this counterexample intact. Even on one equal-positive shared triple, a fixed middle $X$ read with outcome $+1$ prepares an equal-positive shared pair. $X,Z$ at one ring and $(Z\pm X)/\sqrt2$ at the other then give a two-reader value of $2\sqrt2$ in that record. The tilted reads are real. [Decision, item 3](../decision.md#L16) · [README, part D](../README.md#L52) · [Positive-amplitude counterexample](controls.py)

The narrower **no perfect advantage** conclusion does extend to arbitrary nonnegative amplitudes for one real Pauli read per reader. Fix any supported word $w$ and assign each local read the sign of its action at $w$. In the product of the corresponding answer projectors, the coefficient at $w$ is $2^{-n}\sum_T\psi(w\oplus x_T)>0$, summing over subsets of the $n$ local changes. The factors act on disjoint readers and each real read keeps its own sign under its own change. All summands are nonnegative and the empty subset gives $\psi(w)>0$. Thus this one list predicts an answer tuple of nonzero probability at *every* setting. Any game won with certainty by those reads accepts that list, even when its other probabilities have no local model.

## E. Does a birth compare two allowed states?

**Read in the executing code and checked on births.** The ordinary parity rule takes the current list of knots and their distinctions as input. A birth's parents and consumed loose distinctions come from the holders. Its demand counts groups involving the new knot. For input mask $d$, closing without a fresh distinction is permitted at zero demand if $d$ is a sum of old laws. Equivalently $d\cdot w=0$ for every allowed word $w$, or $d\cdot x=0$ for every allowed change $x$. For a sum of old laws to contain only loose distinctions, both holders of each shared distinction must be selected together or both omitted. The sum therefore takes all the loose distinctions of each selected component. An ordinary birth takes inputs from one component. This explains the holder test and the algebraic one. Both concern one fixed law on the entire allowed set. The birth takes no pair of actual parent words and emits no sign for a selected change.

**Not a block · Identify the executing implication test.** “"Implied" is decided from the holders alone: the birth takes every loose distinction of its component (checked by GF(2) elimination as a second route).” The original single-founder code makes this comparison. In the joined-ring run, the parity wrapper **sets** the flag from elimination before the fresh-count rule uses it. The result is the demand for an implied input parity, otherwise the maximum of demand, one, and three minus the number of inputs. The recorded ordinary not-all-equal variant asks separately whether an old word can give all zeros or all ones on the inputs. These are two single-word existence tests. From one three-place founder, all seven actual parity flags and fresh counts matched explicit enumeration through three rounds, including the last implied birth. All seven not-all-equal flags matched too, including the last non-implied birth. This finding covers the inspected birth rules.
