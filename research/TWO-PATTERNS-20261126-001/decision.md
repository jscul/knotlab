## What stands

1. **No mark is first heard late.** A mark is one more loose distinction at a start knot of the other pattern. In
   2,441 bench cases (three patterns, two joins, one to three steps, 20 rations or takes) and 17,809 census cases
   (990 pairs of starts of one to three knots, nine laws), no mark is first heard by a pattern's own knots after
   round 6, in 7 to 12 rounds read.
2. **Under the ordinary count no new knot is short, so by a meeting a pattern's own knots cannot feel the other.**
   Own births equal those with a stub in every reading of the bench (36 of 36) and of the census (1,980 of 1,980).
   Everything the two have to do with each other is in the knots that descend from both, which hear a mark one
   step a round.
3. **Under the other rations the joins between the two stop early, or nothing new crosses after round 6.** Cap of
   three and "just enough for three": the last kept join by round 5 with a meeting. The eight rations that hand a
   parent's owing on: by round 2.
4. **Joined by a bare shared distinction, own births depend on the other, early.** Knots are short at once; every
   mark heard by own knots is first heard by round 3 on the bench and round 6 in the census.
5. **What two two-knot parts make together is a table of their two lines** (n(n + 1) knots of both after n
   rounds, parents one place back, balls 1, 5, 13, 25, 41). The record's own mechanism, read by descent. A mark on
   one part gives another table; it does not stay at a place of it.
6. **The toy** at prespatial.org/playground/two-patterns shows all of this, and its engine agrees with the bench's
   on 6,229 rounds of 792 cases.

**Not established.** Anything past 12 rounds or 3,000 knots; marks deeper than the patterns are (the furthest mark
was five steps from a pattern's own knots, so "after round 6" could hardly have been met by the ordinary count's
one step a round: the successor reads distance outright); other readings of "what the other is" than a mark
(A277); starts of four knots or more.

**The census** (990 pairs of starts of one to three knots, 17,809 cases). Of 101,994 marks, a start's own knots
first hear 6,706 in round 1, 3,988 in round 2, 1,550 in round 3, 246 in rounds 4 to 6, and none later. The reason
under the ordinary count is simple: a change reaches a pattern's own knots only through a cancelled join, a
cancelled join needs a short knot, and the ordinary count gives every new knot what it is owed.

**One thing the toy showed me.** Two two-knot parts sharing a distinction make 2, 4, 6, … knots of both a round,
n(n + 1) after n rounds, each at a pair of places along the two parts' own lines, with its parents one place
back: a table of the two lines. That is the record's own mechanism (two touching knots grow a pattern of dimension
two), not a new result, but it says what "making something together" is here: each lends the other a line to
count along. Three parts give 1, 3, 6, 10, … a round. A mark on one part does not stay at a place of the table; it
gives another table.
