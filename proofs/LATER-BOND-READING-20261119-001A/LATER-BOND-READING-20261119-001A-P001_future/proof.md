# A fixed number of distinctions cannot carry every later pair bond distance

**Theorem.** For each integer \(r\ge 3\), there is a finite connected parity arrangement, reached after one complete round of the cheapest-fresh-count law, with the following property. A permutation fixes every distinction of every live junction and preserves the minimum common bond size of **every** nonempty set of at most \(r\) distinctions, including the cases with no common bond. After the very next complete round it changes a pair bond distance on two distinctions already present at the marked stage. The start can be chosen so every two ordinary junctions share at most one distinction.

We first allow multiple shared distinctions between the same two junctions. The last part removes them. In the usual wired representation of the parity equations each shared distinction joins two ordinary junctions and each loose one joins its ordinary holder to the common exterior \(O\). The sum of a set of junction equations crosses its boundary. A bond is an inclusion-minimal nonempty such equation, equivalently the crossing set of a partition with both shores connected. Denote by \(K(X)\) the smallest cardinality of a bond containing \(X\), with infinity if none exists. Pair bond distance is \(K-1\). A link of integer weight \(b\) denotes \(b\) distinct shared distinctions with the same two holders. A cut crosses all those distinctions or none.

## The two-attachment starting point

Suppose an unchanged connected part \(H\) and a changing connected part \(Q\) meet at exactly two ordinary junctions \(A,B\). All moved distinctions lie in \(H\), and the whole growing part is fixed. Write \(L_H(X)\) for the smallest connected cut in \(H\) separating the attachment junctions and containing \(X\). Write \(B_H(X)\) for the smallest bond through \(X\) with the attachment junctions identified in \(H\), and \(m=L_Q(\varnothing)\) for the separating cost in the growing part. A mixed bond carrying marks on both parts has to separate the attachments. Each of its two shores must meet its own terminal in each part, hence both restrictions are connected. Conversely two such cuts glue. For nonempty \(X\subseteq H,Y\subseteq Q\),
\[
 K(X\cup Y)=L_H(X)+L_Q(Y),\qquad
 f_X(m):=K(X)=\min\{B_H(X),L_H(X)+m\}.
\]
An anchor from a least separating cut in \(Q\) reads \(L_H(X)\) with one extra mark. A complete round confined to \(Q\) cannot increase \(m\). Placing every new junction on the old exterior shore preserves the cardinality of an old cut and the minimum ordinary terminal cut has connected shores. Thus the current three-distinction table always carries later pairs on this branch. The current table through **four** carries all later pairs and triples there. For a triple that lies entirely in \(H\), measuring its separating cost directly needs that fourth anchor. A fixed marking of a future mixed query of order at most three only requires the separating cost for at most two marks in \(H\).

There is also an exact endpoint test. For \(1\le t\le m\),
\[
 f_X(t)=\min\{f_X(m), f_X(1)+t-1\}.
\]
Expand the two minima to get the identity, including infinite costs. If the original and unit-connector tables agree under the old matching, every intermediate integer cost agrees on the same markings. In particular the 239-junction example keeps its higher-order matching on pairs and triples at every age. This also protects any inflation of its single fixed ordinary connector followed by lawful decreases, provided the starting matching passes there. For three attachment junctions, a mixed bond can choose any of three junctions to separate from the other two. The two on one shore need only be connected through one of the pieces. For larger interfaces one must match both the side of every attachment junction and which of those junctions are connected within each piece on that side. Minimizing over joint choices can conceal their individual costs. [Boundary calculation and joining criterion](../../../research/LATER-BOND-READING-20261119-001/boundary-proof.md)

## A reached family

Use \(I=r+2\) ordinary anchor junctions. One is \(v\), the others are weak anchors. Add two ordinary junctions \(h_0,h_1\). There are four distinguished middle junctions \(a,b,c,d\) and \(s=2r+1\) additional middle junctions of each of two colors, for \(N=4+2s\) middles altogether. Every middle meets every anchor.

* For a distinguished middle the link to \(v\) has weight \(L\), as does its link to its own hub, \(h_0\) for \(a,b\) and \(h_1\) for \(c,d\). All its other anchor links have weight one.
* Each additional middle meets its own hub with weight \(M\) and every ordinary anchor with weight one. At that middle install a separate growing branch to \(O\). Its minimum terminal cut is \(M\) after the first complete round and one after the second. Different branches share only \(O\). Their ordinary parts and attachment junctions are disjoint. The entire ordinary core stays exhausted.

Choose integers
\[
 L=\lfloor s(I+1)/2\rfloor+1,\qquad
 M=\max\{L,\ r(I-2)+N+1\}.
\]
In particular \(N+r(2M+I-2)<sM\), and \(2L+s(I+1)<4L\).

**A literal branch for every \(M\ge3\).** Write \(P\) for its middle attachment. Take \(M\) ordinary sites \(R_0,R_1,\ldots,R_{M-1}\), all meeting \(P\). Split \(R_1,\ldots,R_{M-1}\) into two nonempty groups. Sites in the first also meet a new site \(U\), the others meet \(V\). Join \(U,V\), and give a site \(S\) meetings with both. Give \(R_0\) exactly \(M-1\) loose distinctions, the other \(R_i\) two apiece and \(S\) one. Each other meeting is a single shared distinction.

Initially the two maximal groups of mutually backed parents are all the \(R_i\), backed together by \(P\), and \(S,R_1,\ldots,R_{M-1}\), with no common backing junction. Thus the latter joins, along with the \(M-1\) fallback pairs \(R_0,R_i\). Every loose distinction is used. The \(M\) resulting junctions are pairwise backed. The pairs of small junctions have \(R_0\) in common, and the large junction and each small one have \(R_i\) in common. No junction backs all \(M\). For a non-implied join with p parents and next demand d, the cheapest law supplies max(d, 3−p, 1) fresh distinctions. Its candidates depend only on common ordinary neighbors. Each new junction here has one demand and at least two parents, and hence receives one fresh distinction. [Fresh-count formula](../../../experiments/FRESH-COUNT-20261118-001B/FRESH-COUNT-20261118-001B-E001_free_count/freelib.py#L237) The second-round group takes these \(M\) and gives one fresh distinction. Other branches are live, so this join uses a proper subset of all loose distinctions and is not implied. Each initial parent and each later parent participates only in its own branch. A common ordinary witness would be necessary for a group to cross branches and there is none. [Complete-round law](../../../research/EXTEND-ROUND-20261119-001/extend_round.md)

At the first reached stage there are \(M\) distinction-disjoint routes from \(P\) to \(O\). Use \(R_1\) for the large child, \(R_0\) for the small child indexed 1 and \(R_i\) for its small child when \(i\ge2\). The \(M\) fresh distinctions also give a cut of that size. In the next stage the single fresh distinction gives a cut of size one. The branch connects its terminals at both stages. Adding a virtual \(PO\) retains connectivity on deletion of any one site of the starting branch: with \(P\) removed each live site reaches \(O\), and with \(O\) removed the two nonempty classes connect \(U,V,S\) to \(P\). For any other deletion every surviving \(R_i\) reaches both terminal sides, and the redundant meetings among \(S,U,V\) give the remaining sites a route to one of them. The ordinary graph without \(O\) is connected. The wired core with one virtual branch link per middle stays connected after any single deletion because its full anchor–middle incidence stays connected and every hub and the exterior have several remaining neighbors. Replacing those virtual links by the preceding branches preserves this property. Every displayed starting junction has at least three distinctions and its projection is the full local parity relation: on deleting its junction, paths in the connected remainder complete any even choice of its incidences by pairing those ends. Thus these are admissible parity starts. Each branch adds \(M+3\) ordinary sites and \(5M\) distinctions at the start, \(M\) of each at the first complete round, and one of each at the next.

**Specified renaming.** Exchange \(a,c\) on the anchor–middle links to each of these two middles. Exchange their respective hub-spoke bundles, matching the copies by any fixed bijection. Fix every other distinction, including every ordinary and loose distinction at every live junction. It is an involution. The parity equation at the first hub really changes. Flip one copy on each link of the cycle through the first hub, a, v and b. This is a permitted state difference, since it changes zero or two distinctions at every junction. It meets the image of the first hub equation only on the chosen b spoke. No position, old name or growth history is an input to a proposed reader. Names identify the counterexample and its specified matching.

## Why the entire current table agrees

For the cut calculation only, compress each growing branch to a virtual link from its middle to \(O\) of weight equal to its current terminal minimum. Whenever some mark lies outside a given marked branch, the branch terminals must be separated by any containing bond. Otherwise the shore missing both terminals could not connect its inside marked endpoint to the outside. In a crossed branch, both restrictions connect to their respective terminals. Conversely any bond of the compressed host with the marked virtual links crossed lifts by choosing connected separating cuts in those branches. For each marked branch the extra cost is its internal marked separating cost minus its unmarked separating cost, independent of the rest. This includes the case of no feasible cut. A query consisting exclusively of fixed names is preserved identically. Any query containing a moved name has a mark in the ordinary core. It therefore reduces to at most the original number of marks in this compressed host, with a fixed additive contribution (or infinity). Duplicate names from the same bundle require just one virtual crossing. The full fixed branch information, including mixed old and fresh names at the observation stage, is accounted for.

Identify \(h_0,h_1,O\) to one site in the age-one compressed host. The resulting weighted graph has the displayed renaming as an ordinary symmetry. Every set of at most \(r\) compressed marks fits in a bond of size strictly below \(sM\). Select every middle incident with one of the marks and also select one weak anchor incident with none of them. Such a weak anchor exists because there are \(r+1\) of them. The selected side is connected through that anchor. The other side is connected through the unselected middles, the other anchors and the identified site. Every requested mark crosses. Starting with the \(N\) incidences at the chosen weak anchor, adjoining one marked middle adds its degree minus two. Its degree is at most \(2M+I\). The size bound is therefore \(N+r(2M+I-2)\).

In the actual compressed host there are \(sM\) distinction-disjoint paths from each colored hub to \(O\), via the \(s\) middles of its color. Weighted links may be expanded to parallel distinctions for this counting argument. Any cut of size strictly below \(sM\) keeps the three sites together. Conversely any bond of that size after identification lifts with connected shores: an uncut path from each hub to \(O\) remains, so paths using the identified site lift inside its shore. Every smaller putative competitor also contracts. The two graphs therefore have identical minimum bond sizes on all the selected mark sets. The order-\(r\) reading on the entire literal reached stage is preserved.

## The next round changes a pair of old distinctions

Mark one spoke at \(a\) and one at \(b\). At age two the compressed shore consisting of \(h_0\) and its \(s\) additional middle junctions is connected. The complement is connected through the remaining anchor–middle incidences, and the other-color branches connect it to \(O\). This bond crosses the two \(L\)-bundles at \(a,b\), the \(I\) unit incidences at each of the \(s\) included middles and their \(s\) branches at new cost one. Its total is \(2L+s(I+1)\).

The image marks one spoke at \(c\) and one at \(b\). In the unchanged ordinary part there are two distinction-disjoint four-step cycles of heavy bundles, \(h_0,a,v,b,h_0\) and \(h_1,c,v,d,h_1\). A cut crosses an even number of bundles of each cycle. One specified crossing in each therefore forces at least two in each, a cost of at least \(4L\). The two bounds are strictly apart. Both queried distinctions were present at observation. Every possible naming of the fresh distinctions fails, already at this next pair entry. For \(r=3,4,5\), [exact costs](../../../artifacts/LATER-BOND-READING-20261119-001/LATER-BOND-READING-20261119-001-E001_future/future_reading.json) also follow by enumerating every cut up to exact independent permutations of identical middle sites and weak anchors.

## Ordinary junctions can meet once

Keep a direct distinction for the first copy in every parallel bundle of the ordinary core. Replace each extra copy separately by a closed unit connection. Its internal sites are \(P,R,U,V\), with every mutual meeting except \(PR\). Connect the first terminal to \(P\) and the other to \(R\). Each inserted site has degree three, the minimum terminal cut is one and the piece with a terminal link added stays connected after any single deletion. No ordinary pair meets twice. All these pieces remain exhausted. They change no common witness for live parents: every inserted site sees at most one original junction and the core attachments involved are exhausted. The two rounds above remain lawful.

Choose the copy bijection so the retained direct copy maps to the retained direct copy. Carry each remaining unit piece to its matched piece by the same internal identification relative to the two terminals, with orientation chosen consistently. For a query with marks in two or more pieces, the branch compression argument applies to each marked piece. The ordinary virtual distinctions required to cross number at most the number of marks; the separating surcharges match. A direct old distinction counts as an ordinary marked unit. If all marks lie inside a single moved closed piece, there are just two alternatives. With terminals together, the minimum depends only on that piece with its terminals identified. With terminals apart, add its marked separating cost to the separating cost of the rest. The latter is \(K(\{e\})-1\) in the host with that unit virtual distinction \(e\) restored. Compress every other unmarked closed unit at separating cost one. The singleton values are then the same ones already proved equal on the weighted host. This proves preservation of all readings through \(r\) for arbitrary literal marks after the replacement. The chosen failing pair uses the retained direct copies. Their next costs remain exactly the compressed values. This gives the unbounded-order obstruction even with at most one shared distinction per ordinary pair.

At each individual finite stage all bond supports can be recovered from sufficiently high readings: a nonempty set \(X\) is itself a bond exactly when \(K(X)=|X|\). These supports span the parity equation space. Its transport, together with the fixed growing end, suffices through every later complete round by the accepted equation-transport theorem. Thus the result is a worst-case unbounded requirement on the number of jointly read distinctions as allowed sizes grow. It asserts neither a common value at every width nor a growth rate for the minimum required order. [Equation transport](../../../research/EXTEND-ROUND-20261119-001/extend_round.md)

A sharper finite upper bound holds when every exhausted junction at the observed stage has a bond support of size at most d. For each such junction the image of its support is a bond, since the reading through d has the test just given. Every live junction support is fixed pointwise by hypothesis. Thus all generating equations map into the original equation space, and invertibility gives equality of the two finite spans. The known all-round transport theorem then applies.
