# Counterfactual influence and the first common descendant

**Scope and verdict.** At a continuing complete cut of an ordinary lawful finite start, the distance across current live knots is the smallest number of steps, each joining two knots that a single current knot witnesses together. The first common descendant of given releases is exactly that many parental steps away. This is an exact statement within one growth. Across two matched starts the timing depends on what was edited and on what the receiver can read. The analysis below separates a changed birth roster, a changed price on the same child, a positive closing birth, a global shortage and a change of the observer's inputs. Both parity and not-all-equal are tested. Independent program referee pending. [Observation contract](../../../research/INFLUENCE-CONE-20261120-001/preregistration.md)

## Two directions of a cone

Let I send a set of knots to **all its children**, counting a child when any parent is in the set. Let P send knots to **all their actual parents**. On a funded continuing step, PI is precisely the closed one-step neighborhood on the old cut; IP is precisely the closed one-step neighborhood on the new one. Co-parents of one birth are candidates and every candidate pair is used in a birth. At positive age two children are witnessed together exactly when they have a common parent. These identities commute with taking witnessed neighborhoods. Iterating gives PₙIₙ(A)=Bₙ(A) and IₙPₙ(Y)=Bₙ(Y), with Bₙ taken on the appropriate cut. The actual descendants Iₙ(A) can use inputs from precisely that set of original knots *collectively*. For one specified later knot its true ancestry is Pₙ of that knot; it need not contain a whole ball. The equalities concern actual descent regardless of which edit would change an output. At depth n≥1, a matching complete initial witnessed neighborhood of radius n about the receiver fixes the parent rosters of all its inclusive descendants. Radius n+1 fixes their fresh counts as well on funded, nonclosing general stages. A final one-step lookahead suffices: at a positive cut the unary remainder can be computed from the witnessed relation and the holder's known parent arity, so paying the extra radius on every step would overcount. Each bound is sharp for the indicated general observation at every n. The class with an identified fixed parent map needs only n for the fresh counts. Extra radius r at readout adds r to the respective shield. Reversing the comparison, a changed local fact must lie inside the relevant old neighborhoods to affect an output of the specified form, unless it changes a global guard. Membership in that possible dependence set is not a promise that a particular edit changes the answer. An all-parent pure packet has a further, uniform protection: agreement of its starting one-step neighborhood with matched loose supply on the packet fixes its own continuing holdings and the rosters of its immediate children at every later cut while funded and nonclosing. ## Where changes first appear

When the same knots stay live and only their loose counts at S change, the first possible different fresh count in the family inclusive of a receiver R is at d=dist(R,S)>0. Its first unmatched birth is possible at d+1. The proof matches all births with a parent outside the current sender-descendant family; births entirely outside that family have the same fresh count. No assumption of containment of *every* unedited birth inside the edited history is needed. Adding one loose distinction on a path attains both depths for arbitrary d. Measuring instead from a later released unary addresses a different sender and can give a seemingly earlier outcome. For a witness edit, match what remains after erasing its affected live set S (including all surviving restrictions of witness sets and loose counts). In the union of the old pair graphs charge every neighbor of S as well for one initial step. All groups through a knot outside this enlarged set match and the preceding boundary induction applies. At d=dist(R,S)≥2 the first possible changed fresh count is d−1, the first possible unmatched birth is d. A connected family with one altered witnessed pair realizes both at every d: the same receiver-descended knot receives three or four fresh distinctions at d−1, and at d there is one extra birth even after erasing all names. A single changed holding can implement the witnessed edit. Under both laws the first common descendant of the receiver and the affected live set is at d. If two cuts agree only about the *name of the edited holder*, a source holding can change a witness inside another part: a six-knot physical example replaces a ternary through the receiver by a binary at depth one even though the named editor remains at infinite witnessed distance. The support of the changed witness includes a site one step from the receiver. ## Global guards, holdings, and delivery

A parity birth taking every loose distinction in a single connected physical presentation needs no fresh distinction. A remote live component prevents that closure; a matched physical ternary has three written places or four at depth one, under both starts with exactly the same receiver parent group. This is a locally decodable binary preparation with no clock; every positive event exists in both arms. Its not-all-equal counterpart has four places in both. While a separated remote component remains funded and live in both preparations, its internal holdings cannot modulate the receiving part by this global exception. The second global guard, shortage anywhere at a supplied source, can cancel all births in the first step under either law at arbitrarily large witnessed and physical distance. Absence of a birth alone supplies no positive certificate of cancellation inside the stalled part. Unchanged old supports remain exactly as written. Distinct local information can become available in their child holdings or in projections of the entire stage explicitly supplied to the reader.
