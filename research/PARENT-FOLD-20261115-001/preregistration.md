# A verified local summary after one change to a larger circuit

The earlier two-output study supplied complete truth tables. Here the solver receives the actual NAND circuit and its prior local history. The practical question is whether the old summary can save a search or a reconstruction that an ordinary incremental solver would still have to perform. Both contestants pay to establish the common local history from the same source. No contestant receives a solved parent-global optimum as free history.

The original circuit takes two raw auxiliary signals in addition to one raw input per frontier signal. A shared prefix computes seven, eight or nine independently attainable frontier signals. The larger parent computes two outputs from that frontier. Its history lists multiple three-input pieces, their computed bits, and their local ports. Fresh parents vary the wiring, expression, aliasing and presentation. The two edited versions of one parent each replace one input of the same internal consumer. Solvers are called on one edit at a time. On one branch the original bit still suffices. On the other, the minimum rises to two. The generator's grade is independent of the submitter. It checks the old bit itself and the global minimum over **all triples of frontier signals essential to both outputs**, including triples absent from the history list. The raw inputs define the end-to-end verification domain.

For a fixed triple, give each of its eight assignments a signature comprising the two output truth tables as the outside inputs vary. A one-bit code exists exactly when there are at most two such signatures. The checker counts signatures and excludes every strictly narrower eligible alternative; a physical tap must intercept every path from the three selected signals to either output. A broken old bit by itself does not establish that two bits are necessary.

The ordinary solver can simplify gates, retain old nodes and their values, follow the edited connection, consider historical pieces, return a circuit already satisfying the cut, and share work between outputs. A second ordinary arm buys an exact globally optimized original at its own expense and retains its group, encoder, taps and program for the edited versions. The supplied-fold arm gets the original piece's three signal indices for free. A diagnostic gives the ordinary solver exactly that hint as well. All arms can use the same compiler and the same fully charged independent checker. A nonconstant semantically preserved bit may appear through a bypass or a different physical wire. This makes a graph edge alone insufficient evidence for either answer.

The residual-first ordinary control computes a width bound before enumerating physical cuts. It gives priority to a historical group when it is the unique one contained in the changed wire’s structural input support. For a two-bit winner it can select any two existing local wires that distinguish every pair of assignments requiring different answers. The same control also runs with the free location. A solver that follows gates first and a solver that prices the width first can each retain old expressions. Installing both controls before opening the panel guards against attributing their difference to the fold.

When the old bit fails alone, the fold may add a second bit. If each old-bit class contains at most two distinct joint residuals, a second label can distinguish the two within each class. A resulting two-bit circuit still requires exclusion of every narrower alternative. Report whether the submitted circuit actually carries the historical bit, how often it rebuilds a different encoder and which alternatives it checks. The common verifier may reuse raw truth values for a returned NAND expression exactly matching an immutable expression that it already verified in the source. Returning the same wire number with changed predecessors earns no credit.

The three seeds each supply four parents at each frontier size, balanced across the two local operations. Solvers receive public gates, history records and the **current** edit. Neither opaque identifiers nor the other edit enter search. Repeat timings on the same fixed 36 parents, starting each arm from fresh method state. Reverse whole-stream order on alternate blocks and rotate the six arms in counterbalanced order. Pin one CPU. Timing starts before importing solver code and reading the public panel. Charge establishing and validating original history once per parent, any unsuccessful attempts and fallback, constructing the actual raw-input circuit, checking the global minimum, both outputs and the physical cut, and serializing the returned program. The two branches fork the same original semantic state. Generic shape-only tables may be shared equally. Test construction, private grading, diagnostic trace storage and the extra research audit are excluded for all. Report both end-to-end CPU and the marginal cost of an edit by kind, along with actual counted work, seed-specific differences and timing variability.

Advance to a separate training experiment if the supplied fold has a lower median whole-stream total against the fastest unhinted ordinary solver and wins at least seven of eight paired timing blocks, with all edited programs correct. If the median favors the fold but repetitions are mixed, run another eight counterbalanced blocks under the same code before deciding. Count a repeatable advantage there by a lower joint median and at least twelve of sixteen paired wins. The equal-hint arm identifies savings attributable to locating the piece; it does not veto the requested follow-up. If training is reached, train only on disjoint generated rules, freeze the locator before opening a larger independent stream, give its competitors identical history and feedback, and add training, rejected candidates, policy costs and new verification to deployment CPU. Under a negative oracle result leave the training and blind panels unopened.
