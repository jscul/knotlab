Thirteen were closed initially and 61, 8 and 9 closed at parent steps 1, 2 and 3. They give 46 literal lists of holdings and 18 patterns up to renaming. An eight-dimensional witness gives both eigenvalues at every distinction. Of 16,384 ordinary words, **128 satisfy the even signs supplied by growth, and zero satisfy any single odd sign**. The predecessor already allows exactly the four even words on the closing distinctions. The follow-on study can use the grown pattern with a supplied odd reading. **How the upper bound was checked.** A completed old word has one local extension when the two presented values agree and two when they differ. Multiplying the two side tables and applying these counts gives the setting marginals. Exact proportionality tests on both sides decide hiding. The test includes empty-support and 20/9 controls.

Most hidden pairs admit a common local explanation by exchange, rank at most two, or setting-independent weights for the shared word or a group of proportional rows. At rank two every normalized row is a mixture of the two extreme normalized rows, so the one-shared-distinction argument applies. For a group of proportional rows, the counts factor given the group, and its normalized mass is checked at all four settings. Every one of the remaining **16,292** headline pairings was scored directly with integer arithmetic. Each pool contains an attaining pair. A separate implementation scored all 19,270 and 193,580 distinct normalized old tables in the smaller classes and sampled 6,000 attempted pairings per larger pool. [All pairs](hiding_counts.json) · [Integer scoring](parity_scores.py) · [Separate replay](pair_census.json)

**The two arguments hold.** With one shared bit, hiding forces its mixture weight to stay fixed wherever the two conditional record distributions differ. The informative settings therefore share one weighting. Uninformative settings give product tables and fit the same weighting. Zero-weight columns are harmless when all four setting totals are positive. For the second argument, swapping two loose distinctions of one knot identifies the two settings up to a renaming. Hiding gives one marginal on that record. Conditioning both far responses on it constructs a common explanation. With exchanges on both sides, both settings read one finished table. [One shared distinction](../../REVIEW-LEADS-20261123-001C/one_shared_distinction.md) · [Exchange argument](../../REVIEW-LEADS-20261123-001C/result.md)

**The 20/9 reference.** Its eight old record pairs give 18 finished words per setting pair. Correlations of 5/9, 5/9, 5/9 and −5/9 attain 20/9, also the exact maximum over arbitrary binary outcomes. The eighteen-knot specimen gives 512 old words and 1,152 finished words per setting pair. Its joint tables are exactly 64 times the reference counts. The old-word control gives 2. Keying Bob’s setting to his first old distinction changes Alice’s second-setting record by 1/9. [Counts and certificates](present_words.json) · [K1–K3 for the isolated laws](present_words_knot_check.json)

There is also a direct limitation specific to these eight old record pairs. Start with two allowed whole words and change one differing loose distinction toward the second word. At its knot, parity or not-all-equal can be restored by changing at most one further differing distinction. If it has a second holder, continue there. Each local occurrence changes only toward the target, so the repair terminates and changes at most one further visible loose distinction. In the reference, both (001,001) and (010,010) occur. Changing Bob's middle bit in the first pair forces his last bit to change, but (001,010) is absent. This excludes the exact reference support at any finite size under these knot laws and loose-observer assumptions. Another distribution could have a hidden score above 2. ## Fixes to the record

1. **Narrowing — closed parity parts.** “Ninety-one were met, all in the fresh draws.” The frozen definition says “A connected part with no loose distinction in which every knot is a parity knot.” All 91 are closed parity presentations for the operator test. In the ordinary even state relation, only 34 pass K1–K3 at every holding (13 initial and 21 grown). Of the remaining 57, 21 have a one-place closing rule and 35 have two places. Of the 35 remaining presentations with at least three places at every holding, one also fails. In a twelve-holding closure from the three-extra-meeting sample, distinction 5 is forced to zero, so two holdings fail K2 despite having at least three places. The exceptional eight-knot case passes at every holding. [A result](../result.md) · [A definition](../preregistration.md) · [Projected canonical tests](projected_knots.json)
2. **Narrowing — twelve steps and the source population.** “Ordinary growth keeps 247 of 280 funded fresh draws per law passing for twelve steps, where a rewiring with the same births fails within a few.” Per law, 247 passed every *reached* stage. Only 170 passing parity draws reached step 12. The other 77 closed earlier. All 247 passing not-all-equal draws reached step 12. “Ordinary growth mostly keeps a start drawable, and every structure in the record that keeps producing does not stay so.” The latter finding covers the seven named sources.
