# Which ordinary distinction survives an attempted erasure?

The three kinds of event were physically different. A paid loose distinction gained a second holder. A parity closing brought zero fresh distinctions. A merger took two distinct words to one next complete word. Each operation admits an ordinary record that can distinguish selected inputs or histories. Each gave the same ordinary output distribution when only the relative sign on a fixed pair was reversed.

## A merger with two genuine prior firing histories

The predecessor study compared a loaded donor against a receiver that was already loaded and had no events. Here both branches share the **same** earlier source, pass through different ordinary words at equal firing depth, and have a genuine next firing. For each original loading direction the first enrolled fork is reached with chance one. The two named alternatives at that fork have chances $1/2,1/2$. From each resulting word exactly one next firing is enabled, and both reach the very same complete word. The ordinary two-port indicator “arrived at this word on the next firing” therefore reads $(1,0)$ on **both singletons**. Both signs give the same port pair. No alternative next firing was removed from the denominator. Reading the sum of successes at this particular target cannot yield a calibrated eraser because the complementary port has zero singleton mass. The sharp contrast bound is zero even if an otherwise unrestricted normalized quantum effect is allowed to fit that event.

The two successful firings change different named sets of distinctions. Given a latched firing name **and** the complete post-word, reversing those named changes algebraically identifies the old intermediate word. Neither inverse firing is enabled by the actual menu at that post-word. The complete post-word alone contains no flag selecting which of the two firings arrived. The simulation transcript contains it. An accessible latch would have to store it. Keeping that transcript reveals the earlier path with certainty. Discarding it after ordinary probability weighting still gives the identical outgoing word. Both full sets of failed not-all-equal holdings are in the evidence. The archived switch words fail that other law before the test, so this particular merger is a parity result.
