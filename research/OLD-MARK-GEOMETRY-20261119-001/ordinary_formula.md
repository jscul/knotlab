# Why step counts and shortest-route multiplicities part company

Let $e_i$ be the old distinction shared by the consecutive original path knots $b_i,b_{i+1}$. For $i<j$ the original incidence route has $2(j-i)$ hops. All later pairs of old parents that share a newborn already share an old neighboring knot. A route through a latest newborn can replace it by that old neighbor for precisely the same two knot steps. Remove immediate returns and repeat until the route lies in the original stage. No route can be shorter than the original minimum. Since the old route is still available, **every completed stage preserves its length**. The argument applies to old marks already shared by two holders. A formerly loose distinction can gain a holder and needs its own test. The proof assumes the ordinary incidence graph, with no auxiliary vertex for the exterior. There is a short formula for the first-layer number of shortest routes between every pair on this background. To avoid an extra step, a route leaves $e_i$ via $b_{i+1}$ and arrives at $e_j$ via $b_j$. Every first-layer excursion on the route must replace two successive source steps along this interval: replacing all excursions produces its unique original shortest route. The newborn for the jump from $b_{v-2}$ to $b_v$ is the sole binary child of those parents. The jump exists exactly when both parents are cold. The original unit steps always remain. With $a=i+1$, put $N(a)=1$ and set out-of-range earlier counts to zero. Then

$$N(v)=N(v-1)+{\bf1}_{v\ge a+2,\ b_{v-2},b_v\ \mathrm{cold}} N(v-2).$$

The answer is $N(j)$. This counts disjoint replacements of two consecutive unit steps and counts every oriented physical route exactly once. With both loops quiet it gives the usual Fibonacci recurrence. On the longest pair its first-layer counts under neither, first-only, second-only and both loads are 610, 356, 338 and 208 at four-step separation, or 610, 356, 356 and 208 at eight-step separation. Some routes would use both affected opportunities. Thus the loss of available routes does not generally add, even when losses in the literal bond reading add. A separate literal breadth-first count verified all **9,600** first-layer pair/assignment readings across 80 designed preparations, including every one of the doubled ring's 64 physical shared-only assignments. For a once-loaded ring, every original hot parent sends all its loose distinctions to separate unary children. Their only old holder is that parent. In the next layer any pair of positive-age parents that can join must have an earlier common holder. Hence no group can join one of these families to a different first-layer family. Induction separates the positive-age descendants of each hot parent from every other such component, with their only contact to the old arrangement at that parent. Any ordinary route between two old shared marks entering those descendants must return through the same parent, which prevents it from attaining a minimum. In particular no shortest ordinary path passes through a birth descended from any of the five originally loaded ring knots, including the attachment. This does allow paths through newly formed cold background knots beside the attachment. The layer bound $\lfloor(j-i-1)/2\rfloor$ and the finite ancestry checks additionally resolve what happens at quiet rings in the named starts. [Shortest-route readout](routes.md)

On the bare five-ring, compare the two original loose distinctions at one parent with the two at a parent two knot steps away. All four pairs start at three mark steps. In the quiet first layer the four lengths are 1, 2, 2 and 3. Under load they are all 3, since both parents' new children are unary. The multiset is invariant under the old loose-name permutations. For **old shared** marks a law would have to break the replacement argument to shorten ordinary distances, for example by admitting a child of two parents more than two old knot steps apart. To lengthen a preserved old shortest route one must also remove or alter its incidence or the cost assigned to traversing it. The present law can change the joint constraints needed to relate the same old variables while retaining every original ordinary route.
