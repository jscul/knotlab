# What a two-meeting reading can decide about a paid birth

The supplied rule keeps ordinary groups. Two spent holders sharing one named distinction e may also authorize the union of their live neighbors. Every participating live holder pays one loose distinction. Include holders accessible in at most two meetings from either holder of e. Remove only e. Admit the group if the two holders become separate within the included holdings and each has a live donor. Distinct names, parallel meetings and the holding counts at the last included holders are retained. Actual demand, positive fresh grants, the parity closing exception and the complete funding veto follow the frozen policy. The previous policy performed the separation test on all holdings. ## The exact threshold

Let r be the maximum number of holder meetings read from either end of the chosen shared distinction. Among holders reached on this reading, delete that distinction. Its holders w,z can still meet by successive shared distinctions exactly if their shortest other route in the full presentation has at most 2r+1 meetings. Parallel names count individually.

If there is a route of length at most 2r+1, each of its holders belongs to the reading from at least one end. Conversely, computing distances to the nearer of w,z gives the same values before and after deleting their one direct meeting, since both are starting holders. From every included holder choose a shortest route to one nearest start. These routes avoid e. If w and z can be reconnected in the reading after deletion, some consecutive pair on that connection has different chosen starts. The routes from those two holders to their starts and the one intervening meeting give a walk of length at most 2r+1 from w to z without e. Repetitions can be removed. The same proof works when the shared name was one of several held by the same two knots.

At r=2, a five-meeting alternate route prevents the extra group. A shortest alternate route of six meetings permits it. The degree and loose annotations read at the boundary cannot distinguish every longer reconnection. The preceding paper already supplied a family with an arbitrarily distant edit. What is added here is the exact threshold, a funded difference for an explicit bounded permission, the complete named birth price, and a positive class of complete histories. Local indistinguishability is established mathematics in the literature’s distributed-computing model. The proof above works directly with the physical meetings of this program. [Linial, 1992](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.852/08/papers/Linial.pdf)

An independent calculation compares all 12,882 marked queries on 771 connected simple holdings of sizes two through five at radii zero through two, followed by 4,294 checks with a second parallel name. Pure permission is also sharp with actual funding. Take a single cycle of six or seven holders, attach one extra live terminal to each of two consecutive spent holders, and give every live holder six loose names. The six-holder cycle has an alternate route of five meetings and both rules give 32 births. The seven-holder cycle has an alternate route of six. The closer reading grants the four-parent group and gives 34 births. The whole-holdings reading gives 37. Four actual donors spend their private bits once in the group, replacing four unary births by one. Under either knot law all these presentations can realize every local word. When a holder is fixed, each other live holder retains its private names. Each of the two spent holders has two separate live neighbors as well as their mutual name. They can fill their other shared names to satisfy both constraints and then complete all live holders. The same construction with a forbidden local word after deleting that rule shows individual essentiality. Multiple children are funded in every case. ## Why every tree source keeps its whole-holdings history

Suppose the original sharing of holders is a tree, with a single named meeting per adjacent pair. At the first cut each relevant meeting really separates its holders. The new and previous rules authorize the same paid groups and have the same initial shortages, including any complete veto. At the provisional next cut, every current live child meets its actual parents. Any candidate spent pair w,z meeting current live children must have been adjacent live originals. Before their births the name they shared split the original tree into two parts.

If their name has acquired an alternate route then some child of this first cut must have taken parents from both parts. Any route from one part to the other must use such a child after the original meeting is deleted. A paid group with two originally spent witnesses cannot supply that crossing. The tree route between two of its live donors runs through one or two of its spent witnesses and contains no meeting between two originally live holders. For an ordinary group with parents in both parts, any two such parents have a common original witness. The two-meeting route via that witness crosses w,z, so one parent is w and the witness z, or the reverse. The child now provides an alternate three-meeting route via the other parent. This is already visible at r=1. Hence the actual and provisional age-one permissions agree, giving identical fresh grants on the first cut and identical age-one funding under both rules.

At ages two and above, two spent holders that each meet a current newborn must themselves have been parents on the immediately preceding cut. Those distinct parents were born together on disjoint names and can never directly share a name. There is no remaining exceptional authorization or forecast. The ordinary action now maintains the same holdings and prices inductively through every stage or common termination, including parity closing. The theorem compares the bounded and whole-holdings rivals separately under each knot law. It allows the two rival histories to differ from ordinary growth on the same tree.

More generally it suffices that each originally nonseparating shared distinction joining two live holders, or two spent holders, has an alternate route of at most five meetings. Such a route persists when births add holdings. For originally separating meetings whose two holders were live, the same two-part argument gives the three-meeting alternate route whenever first births reconnect them. Originally mixed pairs cannot have live neighbors taking input from both spent endpoints at age one. This includes trees with arbitrary parallel named meetings between adjacent holders. The seven-knot recurring preparation has one spent source holder. Each possible shared name among its original donors already has a parallel name or belongs to a three- or five-holder cycle. Any candidate on the next cut retains such a short alternate route. The 21-knot source has no eligible pair initially and its original live donors have no direct mutual meeting on which an age-one authorization could act. Thereafter simultaneous distinct parents again preclude exceptional groups. The full ordinary histories of both sources, including all grants that fund the repeating releases, are preserved. ## An identical local reading with different paid results

Take the two three-place spent holders w,z with one live terminal and one live sequence of length three incident to each. Each live original holds six loose names. Compare the preparation with no meeting between the two distal live holders to the preparation with one additional shared name between them. Those holders have separation three from their respective spent witnesses, so both complete radius-two readings coincide, including boundary degree and loose counts. Select the two opposite terminal donors, which are live in both preparations. In the open preparation both spent rules grant the four-parent group. After the distal edit, the local rule still grants it, while the whole-holdings rule forbids it.

On the same edited physical holdings, the two marked terminals acquire an immediate common child with 18 fresh names under the local rule. Suppressing just that authorization gives 42 births and 240 fresh names instead of 39 and 236. Ordinary growth and the whole-holdings rule both keep the two marked families permanently separate there. An independent replay represented every actual parent meeting by a distinct name, tested the local condition by shortest alternate length, and computed the true grants from the subsequent ordinary and extra groups. When the first children do not already overlap, their remaining delay is their co-input separation on the age-one cut, with no arrival across different parts, by the already established ordinary parent theorem. The result repeats at larger matched separations. The distinction between permission and paid growth is substantive. A bounded reading settles this particular permission and preserves every tree comparison. The global veto still depends on every donor's funding. The ordinary law and the canonical knot test are fixed. The two local records alone do not determine which of the named participation laws is supplied.

On two funded starts that differ only by one additional loose name at an already live holder X, a matched first birth whose true parents omit X keeps its own fresh grant. The extra unary child has no parent in common with that birth, and cannot make a new short alternative between its spent authorizers. Two eligible authorizations cannot charge an identical group, since an opposing pair in the first would then have an alternate route of at most five meetings through the second. Thus neither a merged charge nor a split one changes the grant. In contrast the extra name can change the grant of a birth with X as a parent, and a distant deficit can cancel all 12 births despite unchanged focal authorization and donor counts.
