# One changed join rule after the settings are supplied

This is a worked comparison for the frozen least-change question. It was derived after registration. No construction or weighting in this note was used to choose either rival law or any cohort reading.

Two readers hold disjoint ternary knots. In each, mark the first two distinctions in order. Either knot law allows every marked pair. In the parity case the pair determines the third distinction. For not-all-equal, split the weight of each pair equally over its allowed third distinctions. Every local knot consequently has its full projected law. No chain joins the marked pairs. Use the original external settings and readings. The following preparation and changed operation are additional scaffolds.

Let r, s and m be three independent fair bits drawn before the independent settings x,y. Alice's pair is (r,s). In the common preparation Bob has (r,1−s) for m=0 and (s,r) for m=1. Each reader's whole pair is uniform. With reading I the four correlations of this setting-independent preparation are (1/2,1/2,1/2,−1/2), giving CHSH 2. Now change just the rule at 00. Give Bob (r,1−s) there for **both** values of m. All eight triples r,s,m still yield answers in every setting. The first correlation becomes 1 and the other three stay as they were. Thus **CHSH is 5/2**. Alice's and Bob's complete pairs still each have probability 1/4 on every two-bit word in every context. Both recorded bits stay fair. A setting-dependent operation on Bob's pair supplies the excess. The source bits and the settings at input remain independent.

There is a second implementation specific to the question about forbidding. Keep the common preparation and the local readings unchanged. At 00 retain only states with m=0. The four retention fractions are (1/2,1,1,1). This gives the same *conditional* tables and a different distribution of retained source states at 00. The changed-operation version above produces a result on every trial. Neither rival join law reads x or y. If these contexts are instead weighted equally on one fixed set of surviving states, the common-state proof applies again.

For reading II the signed score changes from 0 to −1/2. Its maximum over the eight orientations changes from 1 to 1/2. The claim of an excess concerns reading I. Check the fairness assumption by giving r probability 3/4 of one, leaving the other two bits fair. For reading I Bob's probability of one at y=0 is then 3/4 when x=0 and 5/8 when x=1. That version signals and fails the entanglement test. These comparisons can be replayed by running the [exact checker](setting_control.py).

The fair reading-I table even has a quantum realization. For the quantum comparison alone, use the Bell state (|00⟩+|11⟩)/√2 and the Pauli observables X and Z. Take A₀=B₀=Z, A₁=(Z+√3 X)/2 and B₁=(Z−√3 X)/2. On this state the single outcomes are fair, ZZ and XX have expectation one and the two mixed expectations vanish. Multiplying gives exactly (1,1/2,1/2,−1/2), determining all four pair distributions. This agrees on a table. The added response rule has a different stated mechanism. The ordinary fair equal/opposite control with a change at 11 can reach 4, above the quantum bound of 2√2.

“Least” counts changed context rules from the declared classical preparation. Zero changes leave a common law and CHSH at most 2. One suffices. The construction also requires shared randomness and an operation able to tell whether both settings are zero. A growth law would have to derive that access, the response and its weighting to claim them as part of the model. A count of changed contexts establishes no smallest physical apparatus.
