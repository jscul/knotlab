# The question to the advisor (26 November 2026), before the hold-back laws were frozen

I need candidate growth laws, and a colleague's view of them, before I freeze a study; be skeptical.

The growth (as before): rounds; members are knots with usable loose distinctions (h); groups from met sets; a member is
short when owe > h; a short member waits; a member that is not short spends all it holds (one per kept join, the rest
on one-parent offshoots) and leaves; each new knot then receives f fresh loose distinctions. The ordinary count gives
f = max(D, 3 - p, 1), with p the distinctions the birth was given (one from each parent, plus any taken) and D its
demand: the number of groups it will be in next round. Under it no knot made after the start is ever short (proved),
and two patterns joined through a line cannot hear each other's later changes. Under rules that give less than D
(a cap of k: min(f, k); "just enough for three places": max(3 - p, 1); an offshoot given its parent's owing), knots
are short now and then, and one receiver kind hears a far change late (round 2d + 1 under the cap).

The owner's frame (G18): compare growth laws, and find what holds under every reasonable law. The coordinator asks for
"a law in which knots themselves hold back part of what they are owed", treated as a named choice, in at least two
forms, to see whether late hearing from afar survives the change of form. The rule may read only what a birth knows:
join or offshoot, p, D, its number of parents, and for an offshoot its parent's owing o.

Questions:
1. Propose three to four hold-back forms that are as natural as possible from the knot's own point of view (no number
   fixed by hand beyond those of the ordinary count), each in one line with its formula, and say for each why a knot
   might "hold back" that way. Examples I thought of: receive at most what it inherited, f = max(min(D, p), 3 - p, 1);
   receive at most one per parent; receive what is owed less what it inherited, max(D - p + 1, 3 - p, 1).
2. Which of them will make knots short in the joined starts (two small patterns joined by a meeting through two link
   knots), and which reduce to the ordinary count there? (Joins usually have two parents, p = 2, D = 2.)
3. What would count as "late hearing survives the change of form" versus "belongs to one form"? What control?
