## What stands

1. **The local taking rules carry only early changes.** Under the ordinary count with each of five local takes, in
   1,588 of 2,500 census pairs read (912 not run: slow runs stopped by their clock or memory cap), no far change is
   heard after round 6; only the newest-candidate take carries any, made after round 1 or 2 and first heard in rounds 2
   to 4 (102 of the 1,704 such changes under that take). No knot made after the start is short.
2. **Neither hold-back law gives a hearing from afar.** Under INHERIT (a new knot takes no more fresh distinctions than
   it was given) and NET-OWING (what it was given pays part of what it is owed), knots are short in nearly every pair,
   but the two patterns come apart by round 3 (4 and 0 of 505 census pairs still linked; none of the 28 pairs that the
   cap keeps linked), and no far change is heard: 0 of 6,117. The controls behave: the ordinary count hears nothing;
   the cap of three hears late (6 in the census, 124 in the linked pairs).
3. **So late hearing from afar does not survive the change of form**: it belongs to the cap of three (and, once, to
   FLOOR1 and the parent-owing rations), not to holding back in general. What the cap adds is a threshold: it binds only
   where a knot is owed more than three, so the patterns stay linked while some partners go short.

**Not established.** The advisor's other two forms; a hold-back form with a threshold the knot reads from itself; takes
combined with holding back; the 912 pairs not run under the takes.
