You are asked for a cold design review before six small studies are frozen. For each design, give (1) the strongest objection: the way the design could mislead, assume its answer, or measure the wrong thing; (2) one concrete change that would fix it; (3) anything the stated predictions get wrong by plain reasoning. Be specific and brief (at most 150 words per design). Do not praise. If a design is sound, say "no change" and name the residual risk.

Setting. A structure is a set of two-valued distinctions and rules ("knots") on them. A knot holds 3 or more distinctions and is either a parity knot (its values have even, or a fixed, parity) or a not-all-equal knot. Each distinction has one holder ("loose") or two ("shared"). "Ordinary growth" proceeds in parent steps: groups of 1 to 3 existing knots each pay one loose distinction to a newborn knot, which also receives fresh loose distinctions; holdings of an existing knot never gain a distinction; which births happen depends only on who holds what, never on values (proved earlier). A step is funded only if every proposed birth can be paid; otherwise the whole stage stalls. Under a serial variant, births happen one at a time and the order can matter. An allowed word is an assignment satisfying every knot.

Design A (a yes/no reading of growth). Reading T: a stage passes when the graph with one node per knot and per distinction, and an edge when the knot holds the distinction, is planar. Known theorem used (Arkhipov 2012): a closed parity pattern with two holders per distinction admits a signed operator solution with no ordinary assignment exactly when that graph is not planar. Populations: four benchmark constructions (one grows like a cubic lattice, two are planar tilings, one has six holders per distinction); recorded sources (structures that keep releasing new families of knots); fresh draws: uniform labelled trees on 4 to 8 knots with 0 to 3 uniform extra meetings, 256 draws per arm per law, grown 12 steps or to 5,000 knots. Per start: first failing step, a lower bound max(0, E - (3K - 6)) and a greedy upper bound on the number of shared distinctions to remove to pass, and the growth kind (stalled; bounded births per step; growing). Predictions: failing is monotone in the step (by construction); every start with bounded births per step passes at every stage and every start whose births more than double for three consecutive steps fails within the horizon; every closed parity part admits the operator solution exactly when it fails T (gate against the recorded classification of 173 small patterns). Already seen before freezing: the cubic construction passes to depth 3 and fails at depth 4; the two tilings pass.

Design B (a learner whose examples are knots). Task: 8x8 black/white images of the letters L and T; train on 64 images at two orientations (0 and 90 degrees); test at new positions, at the two unseen orientations, and on letter designs never trained. Wiring supplied: all 49 two-by-two windows, shared across positions; feature j = parity of the number of windows showing non-blank pattern j (15 features) plus a constant. Learner L1: 16 unknown two-valued memory distinctions; each rewarded training image adds one parity knot over the memory distinctions of its odd features with sign equal to its label; a test image is answered only if the accumulated knots force its label (its feature vector is in the span); otherwise unanswered. If training knots contradict each other (no allowed memory word), learner L1+ adds the next family of features from a frozen list (1x3 and 3x1 windows, then 2x3 and 3x2, then 3x3) and rebuilds, until some memory word is allowed. Learner L2: same with not-all-equal knots (each example: its odd-feature memory distinctions together with its label value must not all be equal), by exhaustive enumeration of the 2^16 memory words; answered only if every allowed word forces one label. Comparator: an earlier reward-trained reader that stores class-conditional counts of the 16 window patterns and answers by nearest class mean. Two arms on 32 fresh letter designs: narrow (train on 4 designs) and varied (train on 16); 16 designs never trained. Observable: right / wrong / unanswered by arm, learner, condition, letter and orientation. Predictions: L1 is never wrong on its training designs when training had no contradiction; the varied arm's training contradicts under L1; L1+ is wrong on a smaller share of its answers than the comparator at unseen orientations; L2 answers under 5 percent. Already seen on older, unblinded panels: L1 gave 0 wrong and many unanswered on two panels, 49 wrong of 143 answered on blind designs, and contradiction on a varied training set.

Design C (a Bell test where the weighting is over finished structures). Two observers each hold three loose distinctions x1,x2,x3 (y1,y2,y3) of an old structure of parity and not-all-equal knots. Setting a in {0,1}: a new three-place not-all-equal knot takes x1, x_{2+a} and one fresh distinction; same for b. Every allowed word of the finished structure gets equal weight, so an old word is weighted by its number of lawful fresh values: 1 if the two inputs are equal, 2 if not. Outcome: any parity of the observer's own four record bits (three old, one fresh). "Hidden": each observer's complete record has exactly the same distribution for either far setting. Known at the level of distributions: a four-type old distribution gives CHSH 20/9 with hidden settings; a random sample of 1.3 million small structures found at most exactly 2 when hidden. Proof offered: if the two sides are joined only through ONE shared binary distinction h, the table is a mixture over h with weights q_ab(h) and local record distributions; hiding forces q_ab independent of the settings wherever a record depends on h, and where it does not the correlations factorize, so a common local model exists and CHSH <= 2. Census: enumerate every side table T[own three values, cut values] made by at most 3 (if affordable 4) knots of arity 3 or 4 (even parity, odd parity, not-all-equal) with at most 3 internal distinctions and a cut of 1 or 2 shared distinctions, two holders per distinction; combine all ordered pairs; report the largest CHSH among hidden pairs and overall, under this weighting and under the control weighting in which each old word's weight is divided among its extensions. Predictions: cut 1 never exceeds 2 when hidden (theorem); cut 2 in this class never exceeds 2 when hidden; without hiding the class exceeds 2; the control weighting never exceeds 2.

Design D (sources that share one supply). Under two serial rules (finish each funded parent step before recomputing proposals; recompute proposals after every single birth), join two recorded sources (structures that each "tick": a recurring birth returns to the same marked original knot) through one shared donor knot, following the two attachments an earlier study used for an inert consumer. The construction details are frozen in an addendum before any growth is run. For all admitted birth orders (or 1,024 sampled), count each source's ticks through 400 births and whether a tick is lost for good. Controls: each source alone; the inert consumer. Predictions: under finish-each-step no source loses its tick; under recompute-after-every-birth some pair and order ends one source while the other continues; for every such pair there are orders ending either source (so order, not cost per tick, decides). The interesting failure: a pair in which every order ends the same source.

Design E (which signals can an ordinary process send). Earlier studies produced Bell-type tables and signals by an experimenter's edit: adding one loose distinction to one of two existing knots, keyed to a setting or to an old value, or withholding it. Claims to check by replay on recorded histories: (R1) no existing knot ever gains a distinction under ordinary growth, so no ordinary event performs such an edit; (R2) births, parents and numbers of fresh distinctions are identical across all valued versions of one start; (R3) the freedoms inside one ordinary history are the fresh values, the allocation of interchangeable loose distinctions to children, and, under the serial rules only, the order of births; of these only the serial order can change which births happen elsewhere. Conclusion to be drawn if all hold: every recorded table compares different starts rather than events inside one history, and a test with settings arising inside a history must take them from fresh values or, under a serial rule, from birth order.

Design F (one rule that is both parity and not-all-equal). Every knot's word must have even parity and not be all equal (arity 3 or 4: exactly two ones). The structure grows by ordinary growth (unchanged, value-blind). Given paid input values, a newborn's fresh values must complete a lawful word; some inputs admit no completion (arity 3 with inputs 0,0 and one fresh). Reading frozen: an old word with no lawful completion at some birth is removed (it does not extend); track, for three recorded sources, how many lawful valued starts survive each step, whether surviving continuations are unique up to renaming fresh distinctions, and the fraction of "signals" (a newborn's two incoming values differ) at a recurring receiver over six returns. Predictions: the all-quiet start continues quiet forever on the five-founder source (already recorded); some lawful starts die within the horizon; every surviving start has one signal fraction.
