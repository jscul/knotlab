# Independent referee report on the birth choices with the most failing pairs

**Confirmed with fixes. No Block.**

The maximum fixes the closing sign at **948 of 1,177** eligible grown closures, with one vote per census start. Every archived pair maximum, nonzero minimum and holder-type verdict reproduced, including the 173 finished census types. The strongest evidence for dependence on the score is a comparison **at the same closure**. Of 719 closures where both maxima force a sign, they force opposite signs at 199 and the same sign at 520. Feasibility alone leaves the sign open at every eligible closure. Once a score is fixed, the holding and its birth decisions determine which signs attain its maximum. The later-round tally of 19 odd and 67 even describes one choice among ties. Thirteen of those closures admit either sign under the round-by-round rule. ## What holds

- **Population and codes.** Regrowing the 7,075 archived starts through four rounds with the 40-knot cap gave 1,819 closures from open starts. Whole choices admit odd at 1,187 and birth choices at 1,177. The latter comprise 1,091, 85 and one in rounds one, two and three. At every included closure I recovered the decidable pairs from their holders at birth, one binary parity equation per pair of knots, and a closing sign independent of those equations. All 1,177 archived dimensions matched. The largest grown code has 97 pair positions and 55 free choices. The largest finished census code has 105 and 91. The all-commuting word closes even in every code. [Population](population.json) · [Pair lists, equations and sign coefficients](codes.jsonl)
- **Frozen P2 and the measured splits.** With one vote per eligible start, the pair maximum gives **360 odd, 588 even, 229 free**. The frozen threshold was more than half forced. Of the 948 forced cases, 307 have a unique maximum and **641 have multiple maxima of the same sign**. By closing round, the odd/even/free counts are 348/534/209, 12/53/20 and 0/1/0. Where every decidable pair can fail, the all-pairs word is the unique maximum at all 82 closures, 53 odd and 29 even. The other 1,095 give 307/559/229. [Grown closures](closures.jsonl)
- **Control and holder types.** On the same 1,177 starts the minimum positive pair count gives **1 odd, 1,152 even, 24 free**. It disagrees with the maximum at 345 of 927 closures where both force a sign. A minimum of two occurs at 1,135 closures, each with a doubled meeting and an even verdict. Each chosen pair then has the same parity and sign coefficients as the other. Counting one for a holder type when *any* pair of that type fails gives **619 odd, 241 even, 317 free**. The 199 opposite verdicts include 191 changes from pair-even to holder-odd and eight the other way. [Cross-tables](tables.json)
- **Finished census.** With one vote per finished type and every unheld pair decidable, all 173 admit odd. Both maxima give **49 odd, 60 even, 64 free**, and the control gives **1 odd, 64 even, 108 free**. No two distinctions have the same holders on this roster, so the two maxima agree word for word. These are signs of products on finished patterns. [Finished types](types.jsonl)
- **Frozen hand cases.** The four eligible closures on four knots have three groups of decidable pairs. Their group sizes identify the cases. At census index 17 (sizes 1,1,1), maximum and minimum both have weight 3 and force odd. At index 24 (1,1,2), weight 3 forces odd and weight 2 forces even. At index 43 (1,2,2), weight 4 and weight 2 both force even. At index 57 (2,2,2), the feasible all-pairs word of weight 6 forces even and the weight-2 minimum forces even. In each case all three group parities must agree, and the majority parity of the three group **sizes** gives the maximum's sign. The separate three-knot start gives exactly the predicted matching of pairs, `(0,4), (1,5), (2,3)`, forced odd. For four knots around one (the literature's wheel), the unique weight-8 maximum and all four weight-3 minima close even. For five around one, the 13 weight-15 maxima comprise **11 odd and two even**, and all ten weight-3 minima close even. [All four-knot words](contrasts.json) · [Hand counts](tables.json)

## What was not run

The original integer programs were not rerun. For the 86 sequential rows the archive retained only the selected signs. It omitted the chosen words and round weights, so the original solver's first path through a tie cannot be independently identified from those records. All recorded signs were checked for attainment. Growth beyond the cap on the 25 cut starts, rounds beyond four, general reader decompositions and selection laws on other populations were outside this recount.

## How it was checked

[rebuild.py](rebuild.py) reuses the [earlier referee's core](../../LOCAL-COMMUTE-20261127-001/referee-R1/core.py) for its verified start roster, pair decisions and a second check of the reconstructed equations. It calls the archived growth engine. The new reconstruction gets each parity column from the two pairs of holders and the sign from their alternation. [extrema.py](extrema.py) uses exact integer dynamic programming. It merges partial choices with the same parity sums and discards sums the remaining groups cannot complete. Within each holder type it retains the best attainable score for each parity, including multiplicities for the pair extrema. [recount.py](recount.py) compares by start or type ID and also enumerates small codes directly. [rounds.py](rounds.py) independently minimizes omitted pairs at each round and follows every tied optimum, preserving distinct future checks and signs. Its second method enumerates the feasible affine choices at each round and verifies the signs, weights and four explicit tie-breaks. [contrasts.py](contrasts.py) matches finished holdings by exact isomorphism with meeting multiplicities. Neither published optimization module is imported.

From the repository root, run sequentially. The two JSONL result files preserve one answer per start or type. The JSON tables give the counts above. The exact search and history commands are also in [record_audit.json](record_audit.json).
