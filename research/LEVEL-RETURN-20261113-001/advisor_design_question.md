## Setting
Three-place not-all-equal (NAE) constraints ("knots") on Boolean variables. Level n: binary words (addresses) of length n. A binary invertible automaton with s = 3 states q0,q1,q2 (each a swap bit sw[q] and successors d0[q], d1[q]; reading from the FIRST letter: output b XOR sw[q], go to d_b[q]) defines tree automorphisms. Words W: e=(), (q,), (q,r) meaning r first then q, indexed in the order [(), (0,), (1,), (2,), (0,0),(0,1),(0,2),(1,0),(1,1),(1,2),(2,0),(2,1),(2,2)]. A placement is a triple of distinct word indices; the level-n pattern is the union over placements of knots {g1 x, g2 x, g3 x} over all x of length n, kept when the three images are distinct. Dropping the last letter maps level n+1 knots onto level-n triples (a two-to-one lift).

A census (levels 1..12, CaDiCaL with a conflict budget; rechecked with Glucose) found 48 patterns that are dead at some level and alive at a later one; 7 alternate with the parity of the level from level 5 on. The 7 (profile = alive/dead at levels 1..12):

Exploratory findings: minimal-ish dead cores grow with the level (e.g. 10, 30, 89, 602 knots at levels 3,5,7,9 for #3), so death is not one fixed small core repeated. Linear states <a,x> (XOR of letters at a set of positions) are states at every alive level read only for #2 (a = all ones, the word's parity, at odd levels) and #3 (a = first XOR last letter, at even levels); none for the others. No prefix embedding x -> v.x (|v| = 1 or 2) sends level n's pattern into level n+|v|'s.

## The ask
1. How would you prove, for all n, "alive exactly at levels of one parity" for such patterns? My idea for the alive half: a state computed by a small deterministic automaton reading the address (an automatic state), checked at every level by one product construction (states of the three runs, their outputs' states, divergence flags, length parity); linear states are the simplest case. Is that sound, and is there a better route (e.g. an explicit lift from level n to n+2 that avoids degenerate projections)? For #2: parity(g(x)) = parity(x) XOR (number of swapping steps on the run) — does that give a one-line proof?
2. The dead half: what mechanism makes death come back every two levels without a fixed core or a prefix embedding? Candidates: an embedding by a non-prefix map (insert a letter in the middle? an automaton map between levels n and n+2), a parity obstruction (an odd count forced by the knots, like an odd cycle in a two-fold cover), or a linear-algebra one (the knots' images span something whose dimension parity depends on n). What computation would discriminate fastest?
