# Independent checks on the information bound

The larger construction tests how many different counts can occur while the two parent holdings stay fixed. The bounds use the total number H of loose distinctions left at those parents after allocating the marked birth. Both the current physical size and H can grow across the family. For each *one* fixed finite pair of holdings, the number of possible fresh counts is finite. [General construction and proof](demand_proof.md)

In the unrestricted construction divide the possible siblings into those sharing the first parent and those sharing the second. Designate which cross-pairs have another common parent. A maximal set using both sides in which all such cross-pairs exist contributes one successor through the marked knot. Each sibling without a partner on the other side contributes one pair instead. Independent enumeration agreed on every one of the 2, 16 and 512 cross-relations with one, two and three siblings on each side. With three siblings per side the number of required uses ranges from one to six.

For the information lower bound, a finite partial order supplies the cross-pairs. Let i and j share a further parent exactly when j does not precede or equal i. A maximal choice using both sides is specified by a nonempty proper downward-closed subset I, with I on one side and its complement on the other. An independent extra point gives every sibling an opposite-side partner. There are as many uses as downward-closed subsets minus two. Adding an independent point doubles the number of such subsets. Adding a greatest point increases it by one. These operations write different binary integers using the same number of points when the binary words have the same number of ones. For word lengths two, four, six and eight, the replay checked every such half-weight word. It found respectively 2, 6, 20 and 70 distinct counts on fixed holdings within each length. At length eight the counts range from 540 to 990. The proof covers every even word length.

The literal four-point comparison uses 241 old knots and 31,254 distinctions in both starts. The two marked knots each hold 29 distinctions, including five loose ones. The nonempty groups and each of their physical parent sets were generated afresh under each law. The marked child receives eight versus ten. Every physical star passes the parity test by connectedness after deletion of its holder. All shared bundles can be made mixed under not-all-equal, and the check verifies that no star contains another. Direct comparison verifies that the identities of the two whole parent holdings and of every physical not-all-equal bond touching them are the same in both starts. Exact parity minima on all subsets of the parent holdings agree by the two-router proof. This last assertion is analytic.

There is a smaller comparison already one update beyond its start. Use a five-knot path with every consecutive meeting doubled. Give its three alternating live knots h+2−k, k and h+2−k loose distinctions, where 2≤k≤h+1 and h≥2. The two initial pair births each retain h loose distinctions. When these two knots join, their common parent's roster has k members. Their complete physical holdings match across k up to renaming and the marked fresh count is 2h−k. The check exhausts all 44 choices at h=2 through 9 under both knot laws. Equality of every bond reading on those later parent holdings is a separate question.
