Cold design review before two small studies are frozen. Give (1) the strongest objection, (2) one concrete change, (3) anything the predictions get wrong by plain reasoning. At most 300 words in all. Do not praise.

Setting. Structures are sets of two-valued distinctions with knots on them: parity knots (even or odd) and not-all-equal knots, of 3 or 4 places. Under "ordinary growth" a distinction has one holder (loose) or two, and a birth takes loose distinctions. A Bell-type test: Alice holds x1,x2,x3 and Bob y1,y2,y3 of an old structure; setting a in {0,1} adds a new three-place not-all-equal knot on x1, x_{2+a} and one fresh distinction (same for Bob); every allowed word of the finished structure has equal weight, so an old word is weighted 2 if the two chosen inputs differ and 1 if equal; a record is the observer's three old values and its fresh one; "hidden" means each observer's complete record has exactly the same distribution for either far setting. Known: a four-type distribution gives CHSH 20/9, hidden; with weight ratio rho the same family gives 4 - 8 rho/(1+rho)^2 (68/25 at rho 4, 260/81 at rho 8, above 2 sqrt 2). An exhaustive census of two-holder structures (sides of up to 4 knots, 222 million pairs) and 284 million sampled pairs found at most exactly 2 when hidden. An exploratory 18-knot structure in which six internal distinctions have three or four holders (parity knots sharing a pair of distinctions act as copies: even(x,p,q), even(c,p,q) forces c = x, and a third knot on p,q gives a second copy) reproduces the 20/9 side tables exactly. Proved for parity alone: in a connected two-holder part the only rule on the loose distinctions is their total parity, so a loose value can be tied to at most one other loose distinction.

Study A (verification and consequences). (i) Enumerate all words of the four finished 18-knot structures by brute force, with no side-table shortcut: score, exact hiding, a linear program for a common local explanation, the control weighting (old words equal, divided among extensions), and parity births in place of not-all-equal births. (ii) Every policy in which one observer's setting is any function of its three old values (256 functions): the largest change of the other observer's complete record. (iii) k = 2 and 3 births per setting, each on its own copies of x1 and x_{2+a}: score and hiding. Predictions: (i) 20/9, hidden, no common explanation; control and parity births at most 2; (ii) some value-keyed policy changes the far record (1/9 expected); (iii) 68/25 and 260/81, hidden.

Study B (smallest). Class L: observer distinctions loose (one old holder). Class S: observer distinctions may already have two holders, and the setting birth gives them a third. Internal distinctions may have up to three holders. Exhaustive census of sides of at most 4 knots (5 if affordable) and all pairs; greedy reduction of the 18-knot structure; ten minutes of seeded search up to 9 knots a side. Size is counted in knots and in extra holders (holders beyond two, summed). Predictions: a hidden excess exists with no distinction held more than three times; class L has none with both sides of at most 4 knots; class S has one with at most 10 knots in all (pencil: 9).
