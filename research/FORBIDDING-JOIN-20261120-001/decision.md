# Does forbidding an old combination give the entanglement signature?

**The result depends on what the settings can change.** If an observer freely varies which reading is taken on one surviving presentation, both rival laws obey CHSH ≤ 2 and both single-observer marginal distributions stay fixed. A join can remove allowed states permanently. As long as the same survivors are counted at each setting, every survivor still supplies all four answers. This proves the requested obstruction. ## What each law changes

For each funded birth, let p count its parents and q its ordinary fresh count on the *actual* preceding presentation. With h=max(0,3−p), the one-fewer law grants min(q,max(h,q−1)). The arity-floor law grants min(q,h). Both retain old constraints, the current candidate and input map, the private ownership of fresh distinctions and the ordinary exception at an implied last birth. Both impose the given knot law at the birth. Either can impose a previously unmet requirement when the last fresh distinction is withdrawn. With at least one private fresh distinction every old state still extends across this particular birth. Withdrawing a spare can matter later by leaving too few distinctions to fund a subsequent round. These different effects were separated before measuring.

The map within each funded round fixes all inputs and grants before imposing the relations. Reordering these installations gives the same conjunction. Complete orders of a *specified* history respecting its prerequisites have the same constraints on their common completed births. An infinite order requires coverage of the intended births. No guarantee of free present-state rescheduling follows, and the ordinary future map can demand a birth that a rival can never fund. The proof of order independence on an already supplied map cannot establish that the map is still generated.

A homogeneous parity presentation always admits zero on every distinction. For unsigned not-all-equal, every distinction has at most two holders, has distinct occurrences in a support, and every holder has at least three places. Those conditions allow each holder to receive at least one zero and one one. They persist under the rival transitions. Every finite prefix therefore has a complete state. For an actually infinite history, finite choice on each named bit and consistency of the growing constraints give at least one assignment on all its distinctions. This existence result specifies no probability on the infinite assignments. A stalled or closed finite presentation has a different fate: its allowed states persist there. Projecting the whole allowed relation onto a particular displayed support asks a stronger question. An imposed relation can keep an allowed state while losing a word or even a forgetting test at a former knot. ## What the free-settings obstruction says

For any fixed surviving stage let w be one complete state. Reading I reports one of two distinction values at each observer. Reading II reports whether the local pair equals 00 or 11, according to the setting. Thus both prospective signs at both observers exist on the same w. The value of A₀(B₀+B₁)+A₁(B₀−B₁) on w is either +2 or −2. Averaging under one nonnegative common weighting gives the bound in all eight sign conventions. Summing over Bob's answer leaves the weight of states on which Alice's local answer has the specified value, independently of Bob's setting. The same argument holds with the roles reversed and for the entire local pair. The theorem applies to *any* setting-blind restriction and to any weighting for which these hypotheses hold, including each defined weighting in the experiment.

For any family of readings of one such stage, every state supplies a common assignment to their contexts. In fact every individually supported context event has a preimage which extends it to a common assignment. Pieces chosen from several *different* states may conflict even when every pair extends. A family of exact projected GHZ or Mermin–Peres requirements, each allowed and compatible on overlaps with no global solution, cannot arise on a nonempty stage. Imposing all contradictory parities on one system would give an empty presentation and no observable correlation. Surviving old states need not include every old state for this argument to apply.

## What one further change buys

Counting altered contexts, one is enough. Begin with two separated marked pairs, three independent fair preparation bits and free settings. In a common preparation the single-bit reading has correlations (1/2,1/2,1/2,−1/2) and CHSH 2. Change the rule for forming Bob's pair only when *both* settings are zero. All trials give an answer. The correlations become (1,1/2,1/2,−1/2), so CHSH is **5/2**. Both complete local pairs remain uniform in all four contexts. The four tables have an explicit quantum realization. Biasing one shared bit gives Bob probability 3/4 versus 5/8 of one at his setting zero. That version signals and fails the entanglement test.

The same fair tables can instead be produced by retaining only half of the preparations at 00, with all preparations retained at the other three settings. This is a setting-dependent weighting of retained states. In the all-trials construction the setting dependence is in the response. Both mechanisms require a supplied operation after the settings are known. The worked example fixes the preparation, both readings and the meaning of “least.” Derivation of such a response and weighting from a growth law remains open.
