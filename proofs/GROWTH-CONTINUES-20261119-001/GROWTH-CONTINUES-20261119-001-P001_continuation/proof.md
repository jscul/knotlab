# What a funded growing end carries forward

## Standard statement

Consider a finite graph with a specified family of cliques covering its edges and an integer supply at each vertex. Take the maximal cliques of size at least three that the family does not hold, the edges they leave uncovered, and the singleton copies that use the remaining supply. Children meet by intersecting parent sets. We give two sufficient conditions for indefinite iteration, a terminal criterion for a reached complete graph of order at least three, and finite and infinite examples with arbitrarily large cliques of very small replacement fraction.

## The two ways to certify continuation

A group of at least three junctions is legal when it is maximal among pair-compatible sets and no old block holds it. Every candidate pair outside all such groups is itself a group. A funded round supplies one loose distinction per group at each participating parent and uses each spare loose distinction for a one-parent child, also called an offshoot. The children at the next growing end are candidates exactly when their parent sets intersect. Each distinct child gets at least one fresh loose distinction if two or more children are born. Its supply for subsequent groups is $f=\max(d,3-p,1)$, where $d$ is its group demand and $p$ its number of parents. A single child taking every loose distinction may be implied and close the stage. These claims include all births and are invariant under renaming. [Hub form, Lemma R](../../REDUCE-BLOCKS-20261119-001A/REDUCE-BLOCKS-20261119-001A-P001_reduce/proof.md)

**Continuation theorem.** Suppose an initial round is funded, or start at a nonterminal reached stage. Either of the following conditions on that stage suffices for births at every later round.

1. A junction has two loose distinctions and occurs in at most one group.
2. The nonempty whole growing end has a permutation preserving its maximal blocks and open supplies and sending each junction to a distinct junction such that no maximal block contains them both.

**First condition.** The two loose distinctions go to two distinct children. At least one child has that junction as its only parent. A third child cannot have that parent. The one-parent child therefore has exactly one candidate, its sibling. This pair is a group, since a larger group containing the one-parent child would need another candidate. Its parent count is one and its demand is one, so it has exactly two fresh loose distinctions. It satisfies the same condition. The presence of its sibling excludes the single implied closing birth. The argument repeats at every age, even if all of the sibling's other parents belong to large overlapping groups. It proves at least two births per round.

For positive ages there is also a sharp exclusion. A unary child of a parent with $s$ loose distinctions has precisely $s-1$ candidates, its siblings. All pairs containing that child are residual since the single old parent holds every compatible set containing it. On a connected nonterminal reached stage with at least two junctions, every live junction has positive demand: every candidate pair is covered. A junction with two or more parents thus has supply exactly its demand. An offshoot at the following step can only come from a one-parent junction with demand one, which has supply two and fires condition 1. If no offshoot is made, all next children have at least two parents. Connectedness persists because every old pair is covered by a child and every parent has a child. Thus the absence of offshoots from such a step excludes any later reappearance before closure. This argument concerns complete rounds. The examples in the archive satisfy their initial funding test.

**Second condition.** Write the permutation as $s$. Blocks, groups, supply and residual multiplicities are invariant. Carry a group child with parents $P$ to the child with parents $sP$. Carry singleton children at each parent to singleton children at its image using a bijection of the equal multiplicities, consistently around each orbit, for example by numbering locally and keeping the index. If $P$ met $sP$, it would contain a point $x$ and its image $sx$. These are incompatible, whereas $P$ is a pair-compatible set or a singleton. Thus each child is disjoint in parents from its image. On the new roster the permutation carries a parent block to its image block and preserves fresh supply by the demand formula. No image can be the original child, so the whole nonempty funded step has at least two births. The same condition therefore holds at every later age. On the partnered start, exchange each junction with its partner. The same proof applies to a fully isolated, funded component with the indicated symmetry, since a child cannot have parents in two different candidate components. The highest growth rate and even unbounded per-round counts need their own proof. A two-junction stage with supplies two and one, for example, can make two births forever.

## Ending criterion on the whole growing end

A nonterminal reached stage in which *every pair* of its $m\ge3$ live junctions is compatible always closes. If no block holds the whole stage, the whole stage is the unique maximal group and uses each junction once. No junction there can have just one parent, since compatibility with it would force all the others to share that parent. All parent counts are at least two and all supplies equal one. The unique child takes every loose distinction, is implied and closes.

If a block holds the whole stage, its candidate pairs are uncovered and each is a group. The demand $m-1\ge2$ forces that same fresh supply at a reached stage. The following rounds are the single-block law: starting with the $m$ singleton labels, layer $j$ consists of all $j$-subsets; each $(j+1)$-subset is born once from its $j$-subsets. Here is the reason. Two distinct $j$-subsets meet as candidates when they overlap in $j-1$ labels. For $j\ge2$, take two of these with intersection $I$ and union $U$. A third that meets both either contains $I$ or lies inside $U$. A third strictly of the first kind and one strictly of the second kind cannot meet each other in $j-1$ labels. It follows that a mutually compatible family is either in a held $(j-1)$-label parent block or in the family of all $j$-subsets of one $(j+1)$-set. Each latter family is unheld and covers the pairs that generate it. At layer one the pair rule starts the induction. There are $m-j$ next groups per $j$-subset. At $j\ge2$ a junction has $j$ parents and exactly $m-j$ loose distinctions until the last all-parent child. There are $m-1$ further birth rounds followed by zero. An initial supplied roster with the same single block and exact supply $m-1$ also has this future. Extra initial supply, or a condition on a strict part of the end, is outside the statement. At $m=2$ one loose distinction at each closes; two at one end and one at the other can continue indefinitely. **Exact remaining question after offshoots disappear.** On a connected, nonclosing trajectory with no offshoot in a given round, all newly live junctions have at least two parents. While there are at least two live junctions and the trajectory stays open this remains true, by the exclusion above. In this regime the growth closes if and only if some whole reached candidate graph is complete. The forward direction examines the last nonempty birth: it must be one implied child taking all loose distinctions, so it joins all live parents together and every pair among them is compatible. For the reverse direction use the preceding proof for three or more live junctions. With two, their single pair requires one loose distinction each, and both have at least two parents, which forces exact supply one each and closes. A single birth already has the terminal implication. Equivalently, after the last witnessed group-only round, the open test on the twelve undecided archive starts is whether some later complete list of child parent sets is pairwise intersecting. One common parent for the whole list is unnecessary. This equivalence says nothing about how many rounds it takes to find such a list.

## Large groups under the two conditions

For a legal group $G$, write $c(G)$ for the least number of current maximal blocks covering every parent. Write $r(G)$ for the number of parents $u$ for which a live junction outside $G$ misses $u$ and meets every other member of $G$ as a candidate. This counts possible replacements individually. A condition at any stage is $|G|\ge s$, $c(G)\ge3$, and $r(G)\le\varepsilon |G|$. The recorded threshold is $\varepsilon=3/2000$, with individual sizes supplied. A replacement need only pass the stated compatibility test. Requiring the changed set to be an entire legal group would be a different reading.

**The condition can recur, strictly larger each time, for arbitrarily many rounds of a finite growth.** In the single-block tail at layer $j\ge4$, the $j+1$ parents of a group are all the $j$-subsets of a fixed $(j+1)$-set $U$. An old block meets this group in at most two parents, and every pair is held by some block. Covering it takes exactly $\lceil(j+1)/2\rceil$ blocks. A junction outside this group that is compatible with any parent has exactly $j-1$ labels of $U$, so meets precisely two of its parents. Replacing one would require meeting $j$ of them. Thus $r(G)=0$. The group size rises by one in the next layer until the unique final birth. Taking $m$ arbitrarily large gives any finite number of consecutive strict increases. This applies to the actual 781-junction single-block tail of start-04.

**The second continuation condition also applies to arbitrarily large groups with zero replacements.** Begin with $n\ge3$ partner pairs and use $b\ge2$ junctions of each kind on each side. Two distinct junctions are candidates unless they are on opposite sides of the same pair. Give each candidate pair its own block. The legal groups choose an entire side from each pair. There are $2^n$ such choices. Every member occurs in $2^{n-1}$ groups. With this exact supply there is no offshoot. Each group has $nb$ members, takes $\lceil nb/2\rceil$ blocks, and admits no individual replacement: an outside junction misses the entire opposite side of size $b$. Swapping the two sides of every pair preserves blocks and moves every junction to an incompatible one. At the next age each child's only noncandidate is its complementary choice. The old parents give the same $2n$ distinct half-blocks as in the partnered induction (the $b$ copies give duplicate blocks). Every child has $nb\ge3$ parents and exact new demand, so the reduced future agrees with that of the partnered start from its first born stage. At $n=3,b=2$, each group has six parents, needs three blocks and has zero individual replacements. These three values are identical for a group at layer five of a sufficiently large single-block finite tail. The argument uses the whole pattern of meetings.

For completeness the pair-block inputs in this construction are realizable at a positive age. Make one old live junction for each requested pair block, and for each intended child take all its incident pair labels plus three private live labels. Declare two old labels compatible when they are in one such intended set. Its private labels make every intended set maximal. Give one dead hub to each other maximal compatible set and retain a block for every candidate pair outside those held blocks. This gives the same pair compatibility and holds exactly the undesired maximal sets of size at least three. Every candidate pair occurs in an intended group. Connect the dead hubs in a cycle, give each old live label as many loose distinctions as there are intended groups using it. All groups are funded and no offshoot is born. Each group has at least three old parents, their mutual parent incidences realize exactly the desired pair blocks, and demand fixes its fresh supply. Padding private labels are old parents of one child each and supply no candidate pair in the next growing end. Every live label meets a dead hub, every dead hub is linked to its neighbors in the cycle, and every live label has a loose distinction. Deleting any one holder from the augmented holder graph leaves the others connected: deleting a live holder preserves the dead cycle, deleting a hub leaves the dead path and every live label can still reach the outside holder, and deleting the outside holder leaves the hubs linking the live labels. Each dead hub holds at least two live labels, so the remaining dead path still touches the live labels on deleting one hub. This verifies the parity support condition. Direct control at $n=3, b=2$ reconstructs the literal start and resulting twelve-junction growing end.
