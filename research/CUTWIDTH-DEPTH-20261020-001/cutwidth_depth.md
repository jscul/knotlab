# Depth is cutwidth plus one

Epic `CUTWIDTH-DEPTH-20261020-001`, 2026-10-20. This is the decision note.
`DEEP-HOLES-20261020-001` proved that an impossible charge network is refuted
exactly at the graph's toggle width by every field that cannot compute in the
charge group. It also observed that, on cubic graphs up to 10 vertices, this
equals cutwidth + 1. This epic asked whether a sweep that also removes
vertices could ever do better, and whether "cutwidth + 1" holds in general.

## What was found

### The theorem (B, prose with executable constructions)

`CUTWIDTH-DEPTH-20261020-001B-P001`:
- **Peak elimination.** Take any sweep whose steps cost at most k. It can be
  rearranged so that every cut it visits has at most k − 1 edges.
  - A step into a set whose cut already has k edges must toggle a vertex all
    of whose edges are cut, and so must the step out.
  - Swapping the two toggles lowers that cut by at least one and keeps both
    steps within k.
- **Monotonicity of the cut game.** Fomin and Thilikos (2003, Lemma 1, after
  Bienstock and Seymour) show that undoing moves never helps keep a
  submodular function low. The proof restates it.
- **Lower bound.** Together, these show the toggle width is at least
  cutwidth + 1 on every connected graph with at least 3 vertices.
- **Upper bound.** A sweep that only adds vertices costs at most
  cutwidth + ⌊degree/2⌋.
- **At maximum degree 3 the bounds meet.** The toggle width, its monotone
  version and cutwidth + 1 coincide.

**Consequence.** On a connected cubic graph, an impossible charge network is
refuted by linear local reasoning:
- at level 3 by rings that compute in the charge group;
- **at exactly cutwidth + 1** by every other field, whatever the charges.

A side identity follows: on connected cubic graphs, the modified cutwidth is
cutwidth − 2.

### The census (A, exploratory; all five frozen predictions hold)

`CUTWIDTH-DEPTH-20261020-001A-E001` covers 1,453 graphs:
- **cubic graphs:** all 112 connected cubic graphs on 4–12 vertices. The 85 on
  12 vertices were reached by sampling at the published count (OEIS A002851),
  and the sampler recovered exactly the 19 on 10 vertices as a control;
- **small graphs:** all 141 connected graphs on 3–6 vertices;
- **sampled graphs:** 1,200 random graphs of degree up to 8.

Its findings:
- **At degree ≤ 3:** toggle width = its monotone version = cutwidth + 1, always.
- **Above degree 3:** gaps of 2 and 3 above cutwidth occur. Every 4-regular
  sample has a gap of exactly 2.
- **Removals never help** on any of the 1,453 graphs, in the cut game or the
  toggle game.
- **Depth certificates.** On all 85 twelve-vertex cubic graphs the depth was
  confirmed at certificate level: a refutation at cutwidth + 1, and a
  consistent solution at cutwidth.

The independent check recomputed every width with separate algorithms and
confirmed the 85 classes pairwise non-isomorphic by canonical codes. The
proof's constructions ran on 4,359 sweeps, 2,823 of which removed vertices.

## What it means

- **The depth of a charge hole is a classical layout parameter.** In a network
  of three-distinction knots, linear reasoning over coefficients that cannot
  compute in the charge's group must hold the network's cutwidth plus one
  distinctions at once. Fewer will not do, and more is never needed. The
  extra one is the star of the vertex being swept across the boundary.
- **Knots on more distinctions can need more.** At degree 4 and above the
  depth can exceed cutwidth + 1 by up to ⌊degree/2⌋ − 1. The census sees gaps
  of 2 and 3. "Cutwidth + 1" is the signature of knots on three distinctions.
- **It closes the arc that started with "which knots remember a loop".**
  - Group-like contact remembers loops (`KNOT-LOOPS-20261019-001`).
  - Loop holes are shallow (`HOLES-COHOMOLOGY-20261019-001`).
  - Charge holes are as deep as the layout's cut width
    (`DEEP-HOLES-20261020-001`).
  - That depth is exactly cutwidth + 1 for three-distinction knots (this
    epic).
- **It does not construct space.** The layouts were laid out by hand, and
  cutwidth is a width, not a distance.

## Nearest known results

- **Cutwidth** is a classical layout parameter, NP-hard in general.
- **Fomin and Thilikos**, *On the monotonicity of games generated by symmetric
  submodular functions*, Discrete Applied Mathematics 131 (2003). They give a
  monotone conquest game for cutwidth; Lemma 2 of the proof is their Lemma 1.
- **Bienstock and Seymour (1991)** prove monotonicity in graph searching.
- **Tseitin formulas.** They are hard outside characteristic 2 (Grigoriev
  2001; Buss, Grigoriev, Impagliazzo and Pitassi 2001). Galesi, Talebanfard
  and Torán characterize resolution width, depth and space by a
  cops-and-robber game.

**Novelty: not found stated, after a brief search.** We did not find:
- the exact level of the affine relaxation for Tseitin-type charge networks
  over a field of the other characteristic (the toggle width), nor its value
  cutwidth + 1 on cubic graphs;
- peak elimination;
- the sandwich cutwidth + 1 ≤ toggle width ≤ cutwidth + ⌊degree/2⌋.

A targeted search is needed before any claim of novelty.
