# Paying for a join across two spent witnesses

Supply one new eligibility rule on finite lawful connected holdings. An individual name e must have two distinct spent holders w,z. Removing only their meeting on e must disconnect w from z, and each must also meet at least one current live holder. Take the union of *all* these live neighbors as a proposed parent group. Keep every ordinary multi-parent proposal, include each distinct group once, charge every member one of its presently loose names, and give unary births to the remaining loose names. If any payment fails the whole cut is vetoed. The proposed witnesses and qualifying name are not true parents or donated inputs. This rival and its marked readings are choices. The ordinary law is the model under comparison. [Frozen definition](../../../research/LOOSE-NAMES-AND-SPENT-JOINS-20261120-001B/preregistration.md)

## Funding, continuity and the first two cuts

The donor sets on the two sides are disjoint, since a common donor would reconnect the two witnesses even without e. There is no pre-existing ordinary group spanning these two sides: a witness adjacent to a live donor on each side would reconnect them, and neither endpoint of e is live. The new group is a genuine paid birth with distinct live parents when it is funded. A policy that instead requires a spent witness to contribute one of its own old names, while preserving old holdings and the two-holder limit, cannot create such a child. Every name of that witness already has two physical holders.

Forecast the *actual* next multi-parent groups with one provisional private bit per newborn. Changing that positive private count leaves all physical meetings and eligibility predicates in the forecast the same. Each child with p parents is granted its forecast demand, subject to the floor max(1,3−p). With a sole implied parity closing there cannot be a new qualifying group, since that would need two distinct living donors. A funded nonclosing step pays for the whole next plan.

For each old word, distinct funded children may choose their private bits independently to complete their rules. A terminal zero-fresh parity child is implied by the sum of the old rows. A veto leaves the old words as they were. Arbitrary allowed words on a newborn extend to the full system as well: under not-all-equal pinning its inputs fixes at most one loose distinction per old holder and permits an old completion. Under connected parity the only relation on the set of *all* old loose bits is their joint even parity; every proper subset is free. If one child takes all of them, the closing case supplies precisely that already-implied relation. Full projections on old holdings persist by old-word extension. See the study for erasure and the possible redundant two-place closing. [Projection and essentiality](../../../research/LOOSE-NAMES-AND-SPENT-JOINS-20261120-001B/rule_and_states.md)

The new trigger may operate on either the initial cut or the next one. On any cut of age at least two, the only spent holders that meet current live newborns are members of the immediately preceding cohort. Distinct members of that cohort have disjoint physical holdings; each old loose bit was consumed only once. Hence those spent holders cannot share the candidate authorizing name. Beyond these two cuts ordinary co-input and parent rules apply on the holdings the rival actually produced. Each of the first two cuts, including the first forecast, still has to be checked.

## A direct inverse check on the acyclic controls

On a holder tree the ordinary proposals are exactly one pair for each pair of live neighbors of any one holder. The added groups are the unions specified above for the spent endpoints of single tree meetings. At the following cut an original holder can meet current live children only if it contributed a loose name. Between two formerly live originals a single initial meeting e remains separating precisely when no first child has parents on both sides of the original tree cut at e. This follows because each of the two old sides remains connected, and a child gives an alternative way across exactly when it meets both sides. Thus both exceptional cuts can be reconstructed from the tree and the actual paid parent sets, without recomputing bridge eligibility on fully grown holdings. From the cut after the first births, the distance between the entire two tagged cohorts in their actual co-input relation gives the additional number of steps to a common descendant on funded continuing cuts. No single function of either marked state distance or their combination predicts all funded acyclic arrivals.

## What it costs to transmit and to decide

Bound the ordinary holder separation in the starting presentation between ancestors R and S of a common child at parent step t≥1. Any two participants of a first birth are separated by at most three starting meetings. Two age-one children proposed together either share a predecessor, or they each have a predecessor that is one of two adjacent spent witnesses. After that special cut ordinary child adjacency follows true parents. A common descendant at step t therefore gives a route using at most t first children, with at most three starting meetings within each and one between consecutive children. The separation is at most 4t−1. The study gives a paid chain with separation exactly 4t−1 for every t and replays t=1–4 under both laws. This physical ancestry bound does not bound the dependence of the eligibility test. A single far addition can reconnect the two parts around e, canceling that local proposed birth in one cut at arbitrarily large original holder separation. The seven-knot recurring source has a single initially spent holder; none of its old shared names is individually separating. Adding births preserves each pre-existing alternate route, so the second-cut trigger is absent as well. In the 21-knot recurring source initially spent holders have no directly shared name with each other and live originals have no direct meetings. In both sources the first prices and all later eligibility and parent groups are ordinary. Both proved recurring releases persist under each knot law. The additional expenditure is nonetheless decisive on other starts, including the paired one-loose-name shortages that veto a previously funded plan.
