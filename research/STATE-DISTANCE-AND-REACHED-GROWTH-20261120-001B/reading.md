# From pair readings to the current parents

Give a zero the sign +1 and a one the sign −1. For every pair, the sign mean is $C_{ij}=1-2r_{ij}$. The observation gives all these numbers and their common carrier. Every word and its complement have equal weight under either reading. Consequently the probability that three specified signs are equal is $(1+C_{ij}+C_{ik}+C_{jk})/4$. In fact the probability of a specified signed triple $(a,b,d)$ is $(1+abC_{ij}+adC_{ik}+bdC_{jk})/8$. Each positive probability refers to a possible complete stage word. Formation ages and the source word count are hidden. [Frozen reading](preregistration.md)

## What ordinary formation guarantees

Every funded cut consumes every loose distinction at its previous end once. A child takes one input from each distinct parent in its group. Two children in the same cut share no distinction. They may have common parents, which is the relevant witness for the following cut. Under not-all-equal every child receives at least one private fresh distinction. Its inputs can all agree on some previous word, so no child can use the already-implied zero-fresh exception. Every old word extends. All current loose distinctions consequently belong to the most recent births. Each current live holder meets each other holder in at most one name. At observation age at least two its immediate predecessors also had this single-meeting property. Arbitrary repeated meetings can still occur among the supplied older holders. The observed carrier may contain an originally closed component. For a current private name $u$ in live holder $H$, summing over its allowed values gives, for $y\ne u$,
\[
 C_{uy}=-\mathbb E[s_y s_j\,\mathbf1_{H\setminus\{u\}\ {\rm constant}}],\qquad j\in H\setminus\{u\}.
\]
When the other names in $H$ are mixed, both choices of $u$ have equal weight conditional on the rest. When they are constant, $u$ is forced opposite. This holds for uniform words and also for recursive division of each old state's mass at this cut. The denominator of that division does not involve the value taken by the newest $u$. At the source the uniform identity holds for every loose name. All other names in $H$ attain the lowest entry in $u$'s row, which is strictly negative by full projection.

For a newest $u$, these are exactly the minimizers. Given an exterior $y$, pin $H\setminus\{u\}=0, u=y=1$. Any other holder sees at most one pinned zero. If it also contains the exterior one it is mixed. Otherwise a still-unsatisfied pinned rule has at least two free places and every untouched one at least three. The finite completion lemma extends the assignment, with both positive weights. In this word the exterior $y$ differs from the common value. Hence it gives a strictly larger entry. This establishes an exact test for the **support around a loose index**. It does not assert which indices are loose. A second reading identifies a *previous* holder. Let $v$ be an old input in $H$ with preceding holder K and $f$ a current loose name in H. Under uniform weighting the minimum of $C_{vz}-C_{fz}$ is attained on $K\setminus\{v\}$. When $K\setminus\{v\}$ is mixed, exchanging $v,f$ cancels the two contributions. When constant at sign s, $s_v=-s$ and $s_v-s_f$ is either zero or $-2s$. The minimum is strictly negative by local projection and extension. It is strict off K if K met other preceding holders at most once. For an older exterior, this follows by fixing $K\setminus\{v\}=0, v=z=1$ and extending; set $f=0$. The same proof works directly on the full current stage for a fresh exterior $z\ne f$. K and H are already mixed. Every other holder meets K in at most one place and never contains f. Every unresolved pinned rule retains two places. Thus, under uniform weighting, a mark of one current private name in a known holder is enough to identify its other private names as those with identical exterior pair rows: a spent input would have a strictly negative difference on its other holder. Under recursive division use only older names. The old marginal is exactly its preceding distribution. Every input $v$ was loose at the preceding cut, so its ordinary row minimum there recovers K as above. Both statements yield all witnessed subsets and thereby all next parents and prices when the current role assignment is recovered. This holds for any number of holders after at least two funded cuts, and after one cut with the stated single-meeting condition on the relevant source holders. ## The 28-name result under recursive division

Start with a four-place knot. Its four unary children each have three fresh names. Every old word has $7^4$ extensions and their uniform first-stage count is 33,614. At the second cut six binary children each take a distinct loose name from each of a pair of those four siblings and receive two fresh names. For each child the conditional extension count is three on equal inputs and four on different inputs. Every choice of complete fresh assignments to different children is conditionally independent. The total number of extensions of an old word ranges from 729 to 4,096. This gives 61,954,240 uniform words. Under recursive division each old word retains mass $1/33,614$, with its mass divided by its own extension count. In the uniform reading those same old words receive mass proportional to that count. ## A class at arbitrarily large sizes under the inherited weighting

Suppose every holder of a connected original component has a loose distinction. The connected components of the relation $C_{xy}\ne0$ coincide with the physical connected components at the source and at every later funded cut under recursive division. In each original holder a loose name has strictly negative $C$ to every other name of that holder, even when there are multiple meetings. Thus the measured relation connects every physical component internally. At each funded cut the entire old pair table is preserved under recursive division, and every new holder connects its loose names to each other and its old inputs by negative $C$. Different physical components have independent word measures by uniform source weighting and factorization of the extension counts. Their cross means vanish by complement symmetry. Induction gives the claim. It continues to hold if every original component's nonzero correlation relation is already connected and the component has at least one live holder.

Consequently a table consisting of any number of these 28-name components and zero signed means between them forces exactly four births per component, including the named parent/fresh profile **against every reached rival whose original components satisfy the preceding conditions**. Its detected components cannot be interchanged piecewise because all measured names in each must belong together. A funded not-all-equal component with a live source makes at least one birth at each cut, so the 28-name theorem applies within each recovered component. Whole components and their symmetries carry their named profiles with them. More generally the same argument composes any collection of connected rate tables each having its own reached-role reconstruction theorem under this weighting; their nonzero relations must be connected. At first cuts from a source in which each holder is live the same component visibility holds under uniform weighting: the loose and spent difference formulas put every pair of names in a preceding holder in the same measured component through a child that consumed one of its openings. The uniform law need not preserve the old marginal at later steps.

Conjoining the recovered ternaries gives all allowed words, and the previously proved finite classification gives subsequent births. This is an all-size claim within the ternary promise. ## The 28-name table without a preceding cut

For any arbitrary legal start with the **uniform** observed table, every triple permits a constant, every physical holder has at least four places, and its word count is a positive multiple of 61,954,240. The last number is exactly the least common multiple of the denominators of the individual named three-bit probabilities reconstructed from the pair data. The 28-name carrier allows at most four such multiples. These restrictions also apply to closed preparations. The pure count gives four possible presentations even under the full displayed symmetry, so we compared the actual named rates of every remaining cover.

There is an exhaustive result without any reachability or source-arity promise. Require only that the **twelve even permutations of the four indices** preserve the competing collection of physical holders, using their displayed action on names. These permutations act on the four original labels, on the twelve ordered labels of sibling inputs and on each of the two six-member classes of present paired names. No exchange between the two members of a pair is required. To enumerate a possible holder take its full orbit under these twelve permutations. If the holder uses $m$ members of a name class of size $n$ and its orbit has $s$ holders, then its contribution to the degree of each member is $sm/n$, an integer at most two. Thus all possible orbits are obtained by iterating the 16 original/first-stage names modulo this action, every mask on the other 12 names and the class-degree check. Every invariant collection is an unordered selection of such orbits. Require degree one or two on all names and remove nested supports, which contradict full local projection and essentiality. This retains a superset of all lawful competitors of the specified symmetry.

Exactly **846** possible holder orbits give **28,220** complete degree-bounded covers. Among them **19,884** have incomparable holders. Exact inclusion–exclusion over each selected set of constant holders leaves **48** covers whose word count has the required divisor. Recomputing all 378 indexed pair entries leaves exactly the displayed 11-holder presentation. Requiring all 24 index permutations leaves 3,806 incomparable covers and eight divisibility survivors. Adding six independent exchanges of paired names leaves 399 and four. Each restriction gives the identical unique final presentation. A lawful, possibly unreached start with a different outcome would therefore have to break at least one of the twelve even permutations while preserving all of their measured pair symmetries. The unqualified arbitrary-start comparison remains open. Recursive division requires a formation history to specify the observation.
