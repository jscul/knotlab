## What the quantifiers decide

Assume a single finite nonnegative old weighting. Conditional on the two visible old records i,j, the local completions form a product. Write their sizes as m_x(i), n_y(j). Sample each finished configuration once, allowing old multiplicities in the common preparation. The local binary reading may use its own setting, old record and fresh distinctions. Every old value affecting the local count or reading is included in that reader's record, available when it chooses its setting. Ignore values with zero completions at both settings. There is at least one surviving pair. A policy chooses the setting before completion, and the resulting table uses a single normalization across its chosen completions.

Here is a direct common-source construction. At fixed (0,0), denote the old-record joint probabilities by p_ij and their marginals by u_i,v_j. Changing only Alice's choice at i multiplies row i by r_i=m_1(i)/m_0(i). Equality of Bob's complete-record distribution in particular keeps its old marginal v_j. Cross-multiplication after normalization gives (r_i−1)(p_ij−u_i v_j)=0. In the reverse direction the corresponding identity holds with s_j=n_1(j)/n_0(j). It suffices to test one fixed address on the receiving side. No division at zero is needed on the surviving old values.

Let I contain precisely those Alice values on which the counts agree at her settings, and J the corresponding Bob values. Outside I × J, p_ij=u_i v_j. Under fixed x,y, the difference from the weighted product is supported on I × J. More explicitly, write r_i^x and s_j^y for the ratios to the respective baseline counts (i.e. 1 at setting zero). The normalizer factors as R_x S_y, the product of their marginal means, since the ratios are both 1 on I × J and the difference p−uv sums to zero there. Membership in I and J is therefore independent with probabilities u(I)/R_x and v(J)/S_y. Given both memberships the conditional law on I × J is the original one. Its two marginals are the original marginals restricted and normalized on I and J because every entry outside that block factors.

Pre-sample that conditional pair as shared randomness. At each side use a local random choice with its own membership probability. On membership use its pre-sampled value. Otherwise sample from its setting-weighted marginal outside the set, independently. This reproduces every cell, including cases with just one membership. A zero-probability set never needs a sample. Then sample fresh values on each side from the local completion law. This gives local potential answers for both settings from one common source. Each deterministic list of answers has signed four-correlation sum plus or minus 2, so all fixed-setting CHSH inequalities hold. This proof concerns immunity to *every* sender policy in *both* directions. A single unchanged record under one policy is insufficient.

## Tests against the wording

In the seven-knot example the four fixed-setting marginals hide both ways and CHSH is 19/9. Switching Alice on just 001 changes Bob's event 0001 from 1/12 to 2/21 at either address. Switching her on 010 and 101 together leaves Bob's entire law unchanged at either address. The latter is a nonconstant function on values that occur. There are also harmless Bob policies. Thus the literal assertion that no individual adaptive choice can be harmless is false even for this very example.

Even immunity to every policy of one designated sender alone is insufficient. Share two independent fair old bits U,V, with a further old distinction carrying the complement of V. Alice has the same completion count at both settings and reads U or V respectively. Bob reads U. His one not-all-equal birth takes U and the complement of V at setting 0, or U and V at setting 1, with one fresh distinction. The respective counts are 2 versus 1 for agreement versus disagreement of U,V at setting 0, and the reverse at setting 1. Alice can use identical private independent births at her settings. No choice of her address changes Bob's complete record at a fixed y. Yet E00=E01=1, E10=1/3, E11=−1/3 and CHSH=8/3. Bob's choices can change Alice's complete record.
