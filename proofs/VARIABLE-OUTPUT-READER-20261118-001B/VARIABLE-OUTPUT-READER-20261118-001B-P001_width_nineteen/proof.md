# Reading legal triples at nineteen distinctions

**Standard statement.** Consider the reachable binary parity-check systems of one serial extension law on nineteen coordinates. Give each pair of coordinates the least cardinality minus one of an inclusion-minimal nonzero dual support through the pair, with infinity when no such support exists. Does every weight-preserving coordinate bijection between two such systems preserve the admissible three-element input sets for the next extension?

**Nearest known result.** Allowing two or three new coordinates, the eighteen-coordinate comparison found that all admissible triples are preserved. It also gave a counterexample for the complete next-input family already at that width. A separate all-size reconstruction theorem assumes exactly three new coordinates on every step. The result here decides all admissible triples at nineteen and gives a smaller observation that suffices on this population. External novelty is unassessed. ## Result

Every full scalar bond-distance isometry between reachable width-nineteen arrangements carries the legal next triples exactly. The 8,191 incidence types yield 8,191 different unmarked distance tables up to renaming. Every permutation preserving one of these tables also preserves its actual legal three-parent choices. Of the 8,191 types, 582 admit at least one triple. Across one representative of each type there are 2,381 legal unordered triples. Each supports either allowed fresh count.

There is also a lookup using **the three candidate rows and one common bit for the entire arrangement**. The bit says whether the number of unordered pairs at bond distance four is odd. The three rows alone are insufficient. The published catalogue tests these four inputs for every one of the 7,937,079 candidate triples and returns the incidence-defined answer. The supplementary bit was found after inspecting failures of the three-row comparison. This is an exact finite classification at width nineteen.

## Why this covers every anonymous comparison

For each incidence, sum its parity equations in every possible subset. These sums are precisely the binary masks whose parity is always even on the allowed rows. A projected set of coordinates is constrained exactly when it contains a nonzero mask of that kind. Retaining the inclusion-minimal masks and taking the smallest size minus one through each coordinate pair gives the full scalar matrix, including its 171 off-diagonal unordered entries. Triple legality is read independently from present incidence.

The first isometry check groups distinctions whose direct transpositions leave all matrix entries fixed. Each such group has constant internal distance and constant distance to each other group. Recording each group size and internal distance gives a smaller weighted matrix. Every isometry induces a colored isometry there. Every such isometry lifts by arbitrary bijections inside corresponding groups. Thus all smaller-matrix lifts together with the transpositions generating permutations inside each group suffice to grade every isometry on the actual legal triples. Necessary invariant signatures may reject pairs of presentations. A match is accepted only via an exact bijection. This includes comparisons across different junction counts. The calculation returned 8,191 unmarked classes, and 36,707 generating permutations and lifts covered 527,278 table automorphisms. In 512 incidence types a symmetry takes a legal triple to a *different* legal triple. No symmetry takes it outside the legal set. The independent check computed canonical encodings of the complete matrix, first unmarked and then with the three proposed coordinates distinguished as a set. At a terminal labeling the encoding contains every upper-triangular entry. Invariant refinement and recursive individualization handle ties, with direct transpositions of indistinguishable coordinates counted by their factorials. For each legal marked class its orbit size is the order of the unmarked group divided by the size of the marked stabilizer. All 619 legal orbits are full. An isometry from a legal triple to a forbidden one would leave a partial orbit, or would give different marked sets in two presentations with the same unmarked table. Neither occurs.

## The observation needed by a triple

The three internal distances alone can give an ambiguous answer even inside one table. Give the seed three children A, B and C with fresh counts 3, 2 and 2. Join their pairs at D on AB, E on AC and F on BC, with fresh counts 3, 2 and 2. Add J at the last loose output of A with two fresh distinctions. Pick one loose distinction from each of D, E, F and J. The triples from D,E,F and D,E,J both have sorted distances 6, 7 and 7, with the same values as seen from D and E. Every constituent two-parent choice in either triple is legal. D,E,F have separate common neighbors for their pairs. D,E,J share A as a neighbor of all three, so the second triple is forbidden. [Supports and distances](reader.json)

Even the complete three rows can agree on opposite verdicts. Here the observation has 51 entries, the three internal pair distances and 3 × 16 distances to the other distinctions, with the outside columns kept together under one renaming. Two eight-junction histories agree in all 51 entries under an explicit permutation. The marked parents have indices 2, 4 and 6 on both sides. In one presentation they satisfy the three-parent law. In the other they all neighbor junction 5. Fourteen unordered pairs wholly outside the marked triple differ under the given permutation. On the two sides the total numbers of unordered pairs at distance four are **32 and 33**. Both histories were replayed and their full matrices reconstructed from the literal allowed rows. [Independent witness](check_near_witness.py) · [Histories, bijection and entries](near_witness.json)

To use the width-nineteen reader, count the unordered pairs at distance four once and take this count modulo two. For each proposed triple, retain its three mutual distances and the sixteen columns giving distances from each remaining distinction to the three. Forget the order of those columns and consistently forget the order of the three marked distinctions. The implementation orders endpoints by the opposite pair distance and their distance histograms, then minimizes over any remaining endpoint ties. Each histogram follows from the three rows themselves. This yields a canonical description of precisely those 51 entries. Those 51 entries already give their own contribution to the count, so the outside-pair parity would also suffice. Append the one bit from the whole table and look up the resulting description in the set of legal descriptions compiled for this width.

There are 616 accepted descriptions. With the three rows alone, eight forbidden candidate triples match a legal description. Adding the parity bit leaves zero false matches. The serialized reader recovered every physical input set on the forward roster and on the independently named reverse roster. It also passed a renaming check and rejected a deliberately changed equal-cardinality menu. This proves that one supplementary bit suffices given the three rows. The literal matched-rows example proves zero supplementary bits cannot suffice for that observation. Both assertions concern the declared finite family. The initial comparison and the search over three-row descriptions were specified in advance. The distance-four count and its parity were tested as follow-ups on this complete census.
