# What the births at a clock's two parents spend

**Standard statement.** In a graded incidence construction, serialize each finite level by consuming a distinct available position at each parent of a permitted event. An attached family can use arbitrarily many positions at each parent of a specified periodic event. We determine the number it uses, whether the event remains possible, and the event count at which a full level guarantees the first occurrence.

**Nearest known result.** The earlier marked-cycle study identified the same child and its period in three preparations. The serial-repair theorem gave the order-independence of a separately specified complete level and an implementation from current holdings when the originals are recognizable or supplied. The new assertions are the exact competing expenditures at the two marked parents, their funded residual, and the separation at one common count of individual births. The unguarded results below use literal finite histories. ## Two true parents at each turn

Use the three recorded sources and the ordinary parity or not-all-equal law. At original 0 of the eight-knot cycle there is one spare after paying for the two ordinary pairs. It produces the unique unary birth of this holder. A turn occurs when both end copies of that spare are selected in a run on the continuation 0, 2, 4, 6, with no other selection. The added holder, when present, has one shared distinction at its host and two loose distinctions of its own. Each neighbour of the host receives one additional loose distinction, paying for its new two-parent child with the added holder. Thus the treatment changes supply as well as demand. The matched alternatives are the bare source and attachment at host 3 or host 2. The prepared originals are supplied to the serial guard and the reader follows true parent histories. [Physical source construction and ordinary mark](../../../research/FREEDOM-CLOCKS-AND-BIRTHS-20261120-001B/rings.md)

At the $j$th turn, $j≥1$, the marked child has a run of $r=4j+1$ original occurrences. Both ends are original 0. Its two true parents, $P$ and $Q$, each retain this run and one selected end spare. The turn has parent age $4j+2$. Under the identification theorem, births at the following age correspond exactly to connected one-occurrence extensions of a parent, and the removable occurrences give precisely its distinct physical parents. Each of our sources passes the identification hypotheses as checked by the earlier marked-cycle study. Extension at a bare end gives its neighbouring original. Each admissible spare or attached occurrence adds a further choice, and a selected spare has already used that choice. [Identification and distinct-parent theorem](../../../proofs/RING-IDENT-20261119-001A/RING-IDENT-20261119-001A-P001_identification/proof.md)

On the bare clock, each of $P$ and $Q$ admits two original extensions and $j+1$ possible end or internal copies of the original 0 spare, one of which was already selected. It therefore has $j+2$ children, and holds exactly that many loose distinctions at birth. Attaching at host 3 puts an additional source occurrence between phases 2 and 4 of the marked continuation. In the run beginning at 0 it can be selected at offsets $2,6,\ldots,4j-2$. Neither outside end admits such an extension. There are exactly $j$ additional children at each of $P,Q$, hence $2j+2$ loose distinctions at each before any spending. Adding the holder at host 2 instead makes the additional occurrence available only on the continuation 1, 3, 5, 7. No child involving that holder takes a distinction from $P$ or $Q$. The old continuation retains its $j+2$ slots at each.

The extra choices at the two marked parents are actual consumer children. Each has the new holder in its ancestry because successive removals can retain the added occurrence down to that source holder. In the absence of an added occurrence every deletion stays in the old family. Adding one occurrence to the pure parent enumerates all possibilities for a mixed child at that parent. The two families of $j$ are disjoint since one retains only the left end spare and the other retains only the right. A different lift or a second name cannot identify them: the earlier physical identification is injective on runs with the indicated relative positions and distinguishes the actual deletion parents. This yields $2j$ distinct consumer children from the two donors, and each uses one of its donor's own loose distinctions. The consumer's direct spending at a single donor increases without bound with the turn count.

## Funding and fair orders

Give the depth guard the original cohort. Source-to-source incidences have depth difference zero. Every later sharing joins parent and child at successive depths, and following actual parents attains the minimum number of ordinary sharings back to the supplied set. At the least depth with any loose distinction, every holder of that depth has already been made. Recover its opening count at birth by retaining arrivals from the previous depth as inputs and treating every other slot as originally open, even if now shared with a child. For the originals use their initial source-sharing incidences. Compute the ordinary level roster and immutable fresh counts from these data, deduct the already written children by their actual parent sets and multiplicities, and offer the remaining ones. An offered child may spend any one currently loose distinction at each of its parents. Each such parent initially holds exactly as many openings as it has remaining birth obligations. Spending one child reduces both quantities by one at every donating parent. This is an invariant of all prefixes.

After all mixed children of $P,Q$ have been taken before the other siblings, $j+2$ slots remain at each on the host-3 preparation, exactly the bare opening count. If all siblings of the tick come first, exactly one slot remains at each. The tick still fires. Conversely, when it fires first it may spend a distinction that a consumer could otherwise take. Reversing the two orders exchanges those slot names and gives the same physical incidences up to that exchange and naming the two newborns. Funding is per parent, and a fixed name for each future child would be an additional convention.

Each complete roster is finite and contains both growing continuations. Every maximal order empties the current roster after its exact remaining number of births, even when multipart births take priority over unaries in that roster. Consequently every prescribed event of finite parent depth is eventually taken, so these orders satisfy both fairness requirements. This proof uses the depth guard. Weak fairness after global priority on an unguarded roster has another meaning.

Parity and not-all-equal are both physical here. In a connected parity presentation the only possible equation involving loose distinctions alone involves all of them. At the start of a nontrivial layer at least one other input is loose. After the first positive sibling is made its fresh outputs remain loose through that layer; the guard prevents spending them early. Every selected input tuple hence extends on old holdings. For not-all-equal the chosen input set removes at most one place from each old knot. Each still has at least two other places. The inherited two-colouring proof extends arbitrary assigned values on the selected inputs: in an odd-cycle component left with exactly two places per knot, choose one doubled colour opposite the chosen knot's prescribed input. Elsewhere colour so every knot has both values. Positive fresh supply extends every old state, and the old input freedom gives every locally allowed newborn assignment. Induction preserves each prior projection. Neither law encounters a sole closing birth on these continuations. ## Cost in individual births

Here is an independent count of the whole level rather than a count of clock labels. For one continuation and start phase $i$, take a run of $r≥1$ originals. Let $m$ count the available spares at offsets $0$ through $r-1$, and $h$ the copies of the added holder available in the $r+1$ intervening or end positions. At age $a>0$ a child consists of $a+1$ occurrences, so this run gives exactly $\binom{m+h}{a+1-r}$ choices. Sum over the four phases, both continuations, and $1≤r≤a+1$, treating an out-of-range choice as zero. The empty original run only describes the original added holder and contributes to no positive age. Terms omitting every added occurrence give the original bare counts within both preparations.

## The same marked event when each birth recalculates the next options

Under the archived unguarded rule, a candidate group consists of present live holders. A group already contained among one earlier child's parents is excluded. A unary is allowed when its parent has a surplus over the remaining groups. Each move spends one actual slot at each parent. The new child's fresh budget is its immediately recomputed demand, with the ordinary floors. This has a different supply invariant from the guard. To identify the mark, start with the named originals, match each ordinary deletion role to a physical child with exactly those old input holders, and retain every injective consistent realization of the complete ancestry. The read is from successive physical writes. Recoverability from an unordered final raw presentation was not established. [Previous unguarded rule and exclusion test](../../../research/FRESH-DEMAND-20261120-001/serial_orders.md)

**From the originals.** Among currently eligible events needed by the first turn, choose the highest still-needed parent depth, breaking ties by increasing actual parent list. Use unrestricted raw permission. This is an externally chosen fair-extendible prefix strategy. Its target knowledge supplies no permission to the growth. In all three sources the first 14 writes have identical parent lists when newly born knots are named by their birth number. These writes never use the added holder. On bare and opposite-continuation starts they give one fresh distinction each to births 10 and 12. Each spends that single slot in a different subsequent child. The needed age-three child would require both of these knots as parents. Both are exhausted.

That absence is permanent along every continuation of the recorded prefix. The three initial slots at original 0 were spent on its unique unary and on one child each with originals 2 and 6. A second unary there is impossible. Every remaining positive-age role in the ordinary ancestry has two or more parents. Once its actual parents are fixed there can be at most one exact child, because an earlier child containing them forbids another for all later time. Induction from the original holders thus fixes the two exhausted roles uniquely. The missing age-three child is required by the first marked turn. A different realization of either needed parent cannot rescue it. In the canonical replay the exhausted holders are 17 and 19 on the bare clock and 18 and 20 on the opposite-continuation clock. These identifiers locate knots born at events 10 and 12 on each history.

With the attachment on the marked continuation, events 10 and 12 instead receive two and four fresh distinctions. The same first 14 parent choices leave one and three slots at those two corresponding parents. Their exact joint child is admitted at event 15. The uniquely reconstructed first mark fires at event 20, consuming one loose distinction each from the children born at events 18 and 19 and receiving three fresh distinctions. All 20 births descend only from the old marked continuation. This exhibits a from-original, order-specific dependence on the paid preparation. The chosen run contains zero actual consumer births. It does not identify an advantage due to consumption. Under the other registered tie order and the registered joins-first cases, the ancestry-only strategy stopped after ten events; no general nonoccurrence follows from those stops. Across the twelve preparation, priority and tie-order cells per law there is one proved occurrence, two proved permanent losses and nine unresolved from-original choices.

In the narrower first-turn search allowing *only* consumer moves directly spending from a tick donor, none of the tested prefixes issues an exhaustion or prior-common-child certificate. The third, intervening birth in the displayed prefix is needed by this witness.

**Fair continuation.** Give every fixed-parent action a distinct natural-number code and, after the displayed prefix, take the available action of smallest code. A fixed code can fire only finitely many times because every firing permanently spends from its finite parents. If a continually available action were never served, a finite set of lower codes would have to fire infinitely often. Thus unrestricted prefixes extend to a raw weakly fair run. The same construction restricted to the joins-first pool proves weak fairness after priority for those histories. Every reported four-event loss uses only multipart births, so its prefix also admits the unrestricted fair extension. This argument allows a maximal finite run when its pool empties and makes no fairness claim for raw unaries masked by joins-first. It supplies no weighting on extensions.
