# When to send several computed answers together

The frozen sender reduced **six bits to four on all eight new coordination cases**, and **nine to four on the earlier compound case**. It kept these results on every one of the 512 possible inputs. The sender that could only commit a strictly shorter, one-question switch stayed at six on all eight. The eight are fresh experiments. The saved predecessor had six distinct six-bit cases, all of which the new joint sender also reduced to four. The original experiment was used as a recorded baseline. ## Which questions needed to switch together

In the eight new cases, the four questions collectively use six raw sources. Every one of those six is essential to more than one question. Sending the answer to a single question costs one extra bit while the other questions still need all six raw sources. On **each** wiring a singleton switch costs seven, every proper subset costs at least six, and switching all four costs four. The eight wirings have 9 to 16 distinct four-answer patterns, so three transmitted bits cannot suffice. They span two patterns of source sharing absent from training under every source or port renaming. The 24 reorderings test the same logical case. The earlier nine-bit case permits successive strictly improving one-question switches with widths 9, 8, 7, 6 and 4. The fitted singleton sender stopped after its one learned round. The eight new cases isolate an uphill first switch on the way from six bits to four. [Panel definitions and counts](panels.py) · [Independent search controls](../../artifacts/GROUP-SENDER-20261115-001/GROUP-SENDER-20261115-001-E001_atomic_switch/audit.json)

Sending all four answers would also pass those eight cases. The distinguishing evidence comes from **16 fresh wirings** on which a mixed packet was strictly shorter than either all raw or all computed. On one such wiring the questions are whether exactly one of a, b, c is true, whether any is true, the value d, and its negation. All raw costs four. All answers costs four. Any single switch raises the raw cost to five. Switching the two questions about a, b, c gives the packet **[exactly-one, any, d]**. The receiver can answer the fourth question by negating d. There are six possible answer vectors, so three bits are necessary and sufficient. Both the joint search and the direct proposal found that group in all four layouts. No question name or group label was supplied.

The held set includes changes in actual dependencies. Replacing a repeated majority question with majority of one source from each of three triples changed a delayed answer on 192 of 512 inputs per layout and changed the minimum from three bits to four. The two joint policies made that width change in all four layouts. In a separate pair, moving one operand of XOR to a fresh source changed 256 of 512 answers and raised the width from three to four. ## What the fitted rules did

The sender saw the public signed wiring and all nine raw bits. It sent the packet before learning the requested port. The receiver had the wiring, a copy of the frozen program, the literal packet and the requested port. Slot order and packet length were fixed by the wiring alone. Data operations for computing query answers used the preceding learned NAND parser. Public loops traversed the question, row and literal addresses. The experiment supplied two possible ways to test source relevance. One checked whether a source name occurred. The other evaluated the same frozen parser on hypothetical inputs differing in one source and observed whether its answer changed. Neither used grader answers at deployment.

Both search fits scored 4,096 program settings on 28 training wirings and 256 even-parity inputs. Feedback was terminal answer error followed by physical bits sent. Ties favored fewer public operations, then smaller programs. The joint fit selected occurrence tracing, accumulated the observations with a learned OR, and accepted a trial when its packet was shorter than the current best. It inspected 15 unnamed alternatives to the initial all-raw plan. **Enumerating these alternatives was supplied.** The trained one-switch policy selected the more expensive flip test and retained whether any flip changed the answer. It committed at most one strictly improving switch per wiring. [Sender actions and fitting](../../experiments/GROUP-SENDER-20261115-001/GROUP-SENDER-20261115-001-E001_atomic_switch/sender.py) · [Fitted programs and training scores](../../artifacts/GROUP-SENDER-20261115-001/GROUP-SENDER-20261115-001-E001_atomic_switch/results.jsonl)

The direct sender used the joint fit's trace and learned a single rule from 3,971 candidate expressions. It chose to send a question's computed answer whenever at least one of its traced sources lay outside the sources of all single-source questions. The same rule ran separately at all four ports, and all four choices were committed together. This head materialized **one** packet plan per wiring after freeze. Its widths matched the 15-trial sender throughout the experiment. They chose identical four-switch flags on 83 of 96 wirings. The runtime counts in the result record cover tracing and packet routing. They exclude the direct head's count-feature arithmetic. The experiment measures communication and does not establish a saving in total computation. ## Why twelve earlier three-bit cases still used four

Training included four independent questions written with irrelevant source names. Sending their four computed answers matched the four-bit optimum at lower public planning cost than testing which sources could change an answer. In fact, the unrestricted minimum widths summed to 92 bits over the 28 training wirings. The joint fit attained all 92 with the cheaper occurrence trace. On the twelve earlier padded three-bit cases, the new joint sender repeated that strategy. It transmitted four answers. The one-switch policy learned to distinguish a source that was mentioned from a source that could change an answer. It dropped both irrelevant raw bits on all 18 padded cases. Its single learned round left the two earlier compound cases at seven and eight bits.

The two fits selected different traces. After inspecting training settings and before inspecting held scores, we fixed a **separate diagnostic** that exchanged their frozen tracing routines. It used the previously trained switching and direct rules, with zero parameter updates. Joint switching with the flip test removed both irrelevant sources and cut two bits on **18/18** padded wirings. It sent three on the twelve dependent cases, four on the six independent-width cases, and four on the earlier nine-bit case. It used the unrestricted minimum on **92/96** wirings and gave **196,608/196,608** correct answers. The direct rule with that same trace achieved those counts too. The inverse exchange, occurrence tracing with one-switch moves, reached minimum width on zero of the 20 earlier wirings. This exchange identifies what each trained component contributed. The originally trained joint system reached **80/96** minima. [Frozen diagnostic specification](../../artifacts/GROUP-SENDER-20261115-001/GROUP-SENDER-20261115-001-E001_atomic_switch/crossover_freeze.json) · [Component exchange results](../../artifacts/GROUP-SENDER-20261115-001/GROUP-SENDER-20261115-001-E001_atomic_switch/crossover.json)

On a cold wiring with four distinct questions, flip tracing computed 512 hypothetical inputs per question, then compared 256 pairs of inputs per possible source. That is 2,048 parser evaluations and 9,216 comparisons before sending real data. A wiring-dependent plan can be reused across all 512 real inputs. The four-bit success certifies the number of transmitted bits. The extra tracing work matters in a practical implementation.

## Where three bits still failed

The four remaining wirings are reorderings of the questions a XOR b, b XOR c, c XOR d, and d XOR a. Their four answers have even parity. There are eight possible answer vectors, so any three of the computed answers are enough in principle. The frozen receiver can copy a transmitted answer or run the learned parser on transmitted **raw** sources. Its instructions for a missing answer cannot read and combine the other computed answers. Exhausting all 16 allowed raw/computed plans gives a minimum of four for this receiver. Rewiring the last question to d XOR e removes the parity relation and makes four bits necessary even for an unrestricted receiver.
