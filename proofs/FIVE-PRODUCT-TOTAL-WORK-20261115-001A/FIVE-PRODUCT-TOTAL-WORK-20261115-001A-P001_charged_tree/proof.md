# Exact charged tree work for the five-product pair

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

On six Boolean inputs \(x_0,\ldots,x_5\), let \(p_i=x_ix_{i+1}\) and \(Q=\sum_{i=0}^4p_i\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). The nine visible bits are \(x,s,u,v\). The rich evaluator requires \(s=Q,\ u=p_0,\ v=p_1+p_2\), the masks 1 and 6. The plain evaluator requires \(s=1+Q\) and accepts all four values of \(u,v\). There are 64 rich and 256 plain rows.

From a known affine case \(W\), one unit buys either a binary question on any visible affine bit or a call to one evaluator. A call replaces \(W\) by the affine hull of that evaluator's honest rows in \(W\), including the empty hull. The call supplies all its local affine consequences together. Known affine conditions and their Gaussian consequences can be used freely. A geometrically empty case closes. Testing that a nonempty case has zero honest rows costs the corresponding call. A raw tree has ordinary child continuations and uses no externally proved facts.

**The minimum total work is 23. Every optimal raw tree pays for seven questions, eight rich checks and eight plain checks.** The displayed tree asks \(x_1,x_3,x_5\). In each of the eight cases \(Q\) becomes affine. The rich hull enforces \(s=Q\); the plain check then returns the empty hull. The tree has 14 structural child continuations, zero named citations, and five paid operations on every path. Charging an additional fixed unit to install the already supplied opposition \(t=1+s\) would make its total 24. [Inspectable tree](certificate.json) · [Verified counts](checks.json)

## A paid split must divide both sides in an optimal tree

The following argument works for any two finite sets of rows \(L,R\) in an affine Boolean space. Write \(N\) for the least number of leaves under the predecessor's free exactly saturated separate hulls. Assume a refutation exists and that both sides are initially nonempty. Then the least total paid work \(W_{\rm tree}\) satisfies
\[
                     W_{\rm tree}\ \ge\ 3N-1 .
\]

First, a proof for an affine case remains usable at no greater cost in an affine subcase. Restrict every question. If it is constant, keep its one continuation. At a source call take the actual hull of the source rows in the smaller case. This lies in the old hull by monotonicity. It may close immediately. Induct through the proof. More generally an affine map under which each smaller honest relation maps into the corresponding larger one pulls any larger tree back at no greater cost: affine maps take spans to spans, so the same induction applies to the images of the current cases.

Consider a paid question that is constant on the rich rows in its current case, with both evaluators still represented. Pay for one rich call instead. All rich rows, hence their affine hull, belong to the constant-answer child. Restrict that child's old proof to the new hull and discard the other child. The substitution costs at most the old split plus its retained child. A proper split of the geometric case had a second nonempty affine child, requiring at least one paid operation to close, even if it had no honest rows. Thus a proper split constant on either evaluator's traces was strictly more expensive than this substitution. A question constant on the whole case can simply be omitted. If either evaluator already has no rows in a nonempty case, its one empty call closes the branch.

Consequently every split in an optimal raw tree divides **both** evaluators' surviving rows. Remove the unary calls to view its binary branching structure and denote its leaf count by \(k\). It has \(k-1\) splits. Each final segment immediately after its last split, or from the root when there is no split, starts with both sides nonempty. Its first call therefore has nonempty hull. There must be at least a second call to close. These \(k\) segments are disjoint, independently of any paid checks earlier on their paths.

The free-saturation model simulates this tree with at most \(k\) leaves. At a free fixed case, a chargeable single-side update can be omitted because that case lies in the relevant hull of the larger paid case. A question pulls back to the smaller case, and constant branches can be pruned. A free collapse ends a branch early. Hence \(k\ge N\), proving the displayed inequality. Here \(N=8\), and the exhibited 23 operations attain it. Equality forces \(k=8\), two calls in each terminal segment and zero calls above any remaining split. Since the first check preserves all its own honest points, the second, empty call must use the other evaluator. This yields the stated itemization.

One can also prune a nonempty side call when its geometric hull changes no honest trace. In any two nonempty current cases with the same two trace sets the remaining minimum cost is equal. Simulate a proof by tracking those sets: a useful call takes the affine span of its tested traces and intersects the opposing traces with it; a split partitions both sets by the identical parity function. Questions constant on their union can be omitted by the preceding substitution or by directly keeping the populated continuation. Once either side is empty, a single paid terminal check suffices in a nonempty case. This argument concerns future proof cost; a computed affine subspace remains a legitimate result of a source call. The small reference optimizer allows even strictly geometry-shrinking calls that remove no trace.

## Freshly proved scalar facts

Suppose a named fact has one affine consequent \(p=c\) under a guard \(G\), established by a separately paid refutation of \(G\land(p\ne c)\), followed by one charged use. At the actual use, the known case entails the guard, including any paid guard checks needed for that entailment. Ask \(p\). In the \(p\ne c\) child run the paid refutation restricted to the current stronger case. On \(p=c\) continue with the original proof. The new split is paid by the one citation; the restricted refutation costs at most the original derivation. If \(p\) is already fixed, the corresponding continuation or contradiction suffices. A once-used guarded contradiction can itself be inlined under its established guard. Starting with innermost freshly paid scalar uses gives a raw tree costing no more. Thus the minimum remains 23 under this one-use convention.

For instance, after \(x_1=x_3=x_5=0\), the rich evaluator establishes \(s=0\) by ruling out \(s=1\) in one call. Paying one citation and checking the plain side on \(s=0\) costs three units in total. The inlined question on \(s\) with one empty check on each answer also costs three. The direct two-call leaf remains available. The checker verifies the original rich relation has \(s=1\) elsewhere, so the use of this fact is genuinely contextual. A named block of several separately discharged affine conclusions and a lemma with several consumers have different accounting. The scalar replacement above uses one split for one consequent. A primitive source check continues to return its entire affine hull for its stated one-unit price. [Scalar expansion and raw controls](check.py)

## A larger path and the grouped family

Let \(r\ge1\) and use independent original pairs \(a_i,b_i\) with one reported bit \(w_i\) per pair. Put \(e_i=w_i+a_ib_i\). The rich evaluator requires all \(e_i=0\); the plain evaluator requires \(\sum_i e_i=1\). The exact minimum work on these \(3r\) visible bits is \(3\cdot2^r-1\).

Both sources span the entire root. For one block, both the graph of a product and its opposite span the three visible bits. For the rich side use their Cartesian product of honest graphs. For the plain side a product with one opposite graph and all other honest graphs is already spanning. A first side call therefore does nothing. In rank one any nonconstant first question divides each spanning source, leaving both sides present in both children. The two checks per child lower the cost to at least five, attained by fixing \(a_1\) and checking both evaluators.

For \(r\ge2\), take any first affine question \(q\). Select a block on which it has a nonzero coefficient. Find two honest triples in this block with opposite values of the local part of \(q\), possible since its four honest points span. In either target answer, let the remaining blocks vary freely. Choose between the two honest triples using the affine contribution of the remaining blocks, adjusted by the target answer. Each inserted coordinate is affine in the remaining visible bits. The new block always has zero error, so both smaller relations embed into that answer, including smaller plain points with arbitrary error patterns of odd parity. The restriction lemma gives \(W_r\ge1+2W_{r-1}\). Fixing all \(a_i\) makes every product affine; one rich call followed by a plain empty call at every leaf attains \(3\cdot2^r-1\).

For a path of \(2r-1\) products set \(a_i=x_{2i}\), \(b_i=x_{2i-1}+x_{2i+1}\) with zero-based \(i\) and \(x_{-1}=0\). Recover the odd \(x\)'s by prefix sums of the \(b\)'s. The total is \(\sum_i a_i b_i\). Map grouped words to \(s=\sum_iw_i,\ u=w_0,\ v=w_1\). For \(r=3\) this is an invertible recoding since \(w_2=s+u+v\). For \(r\ge3\) it sends each honest grouped relation into the original evaluator with masks 1 and 6, hence a cheaper original tree would contradict the grouped lower bound. The alternating original inputs still give the upper bound. **In particular the seven-product path with these same two masks costs exactly 47.** The checker checks every child embedding at ranks two through four, both original-relation images on the five- and seven-product paths, and both charged upper trees. ## Controls on strategic check placement

The lower bound can be strict. In three visible bits take rich rows \(\{0,2,3\}\) and plain rows \(\{1,4,5\}\). The free process closes one leaf. Each side's first hull contains one honest row from the other side, so two paid calls cannot suffice. Three do. Independently, the reference dynamic program enumerates every affine question and every genuinely smaller single-side hull in every affine subcase. For all 6,050 ordered pairs of disjoint nonempty three-bit relations, the unrestricted paid optimum agrees with the optimizer allowing only splits that divide both sources. The observed \((N,W_{\rm tree})\) counts are 5,098 for \((1,2)\), 896 for \((1,3)\), and 56 for \((2,5)\). [Reference optimizer](charged.py) · [Numerical record](checks.json)

An example on six bits \(t,y,a,b,c,d\) requires an early check for optimality. On \(t=0\), let the plain relation be \(P\), consisting of \(c=d=0\) at \(y=0\) and \(a=b=0\) at \(y=1\). Let rich be the other 24 rows with \(t=0\), and give all 32 rows with \(t=1\) to plain. The rich hull initially fixes \(t=0\). Ask \(y\), then check plain and rich on each branch. Each plain restriction there is an affine set disjoint from rich. The total is **six**.

Every query-first proof costs at least seven. A first question on \(t\) gives a pure child costing one and a \(t=0\) child costing five: both sources span that base and the displayed \(y\)-tree attains five. For any other first question, \(P\) meets both of its \(t=0\) halves since \(P\) spans that base. Each such half has at least nine rich rows, enough to span its 16-point base slice. The plain points fill the \(t=1\) slice and include a \(t=0\) point, so span the entire child. Neither possible first source call in either child immediately removes the other evaluator. Any split there incurs one operation and two nonempty geometric children. Thus each child costs at least three. Splitting on \(t\) attains seven under the rule allowing side checks only in final segments. With no root question, a plain call has no effect, and a rich call leaves the five-cost base, proving six is optimal. The unrestricted dynamic program on all six-bit affine cases verifies both minima. This control shows why a restriction to late source checks needs proof for the fixed five-product instance.
