# An earlier payment that determines a later partner

**Theorem.** Under even parity and under not-all-equal, there are four connected, matched founder preparations with two intrinsically identifiable newborns $R,S$. Each retains two loose distinctions for the next funding. The number of older distinctions shared by $R$ is one or two according to its actual earlier birth. The extra distinction is a payment from a particular older holder $B$. A joint child of $R,S$ is born exactly when *both* received a distinction from $B$. Divert either earlier payment to the corresponding alternative holder, and both enrolled parties still spend two marks on two actual children. All four preparations have the same numbers and arities of births through the later funding. A second construction preserves a joint alternative for every enrolled party and distinguishes an anonymous indication of the past from the contact the growth rule uses.

On **one identical founder**, ordinary complete-cut growth determines its successive parent rosters and fresh grants, up to exchanging equivalent copies of a unary birth. Changing the values or binding different named slots to those children cannot change an intrinsically enrolled pair of structural roles into different parents. The constructive comparisons change earlier *source incidences*. No knot chooses among the four preparations or supplies a value-controlled structural update.

The nearest ordinary precedents are a [prepared witness that changes a later response](../../../research/INFLUENCE-CONE-20261120-001/physical_edits.md) and a [newborn whose larger bank pays an already forecast extra group](../../../research/SPARE-AND-NEXT-PAYMENT-20261122-001/payment_trace.md). The new comparison gives each party equal remaining supply while independently interrupting its link to a candidate common payer. Both alternatives continue. The proof uses the [frozen preparations and readings](../../../research/EARLIER-EVENT-AND-LATER-BIRTH-20261122-001A/preregistration.md) and the [ordinary group and fresh-grant rules](../../../research/ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/end_operator.md).

## The physical witness used by the ordinary rule

After a complete cut every earlier loose distinction was spent exactly once. It stays at its older payer and gains one new holder with the original value. At the new live end, two different newborns are witnessed together precisely when they have a common older payer. A same-cut sibling does not pay another sibling. No still older holder pays into this cohort. For each funded parent group the child receives one loose distinction from each of its actual parents. Each live payer pays once to each required group and once to a separate unary child for each unused loose distinction. For a nonclosing positive birth with $p$ parents and $d$ prospective group obligations the grant is $\max(d,3-p,1)$. Parity has a separate zero-fresh exception for a sole birth; every round used here has several births.

An ordinary group consists of a maximal pairwise-witnessed set of at least three live knots with no single common witness, or a witnessed pair contained in none of those larger groups. This last condition matters in both constructions. A physical record of a prior payment has two ends. The ordinary test uses the sharing between holders. One endpoint's ability to count its marked distinctions is a separate fact.

## A matched interruption of the very mark the holder can detect

Use the direct founder. The sole original loose marks of $A,A',C,C',K$ always identify their respective first children $R,R',S,S',J$. Write $X$ for the one of $R,R'$ whose anchor has a spent pair witness with $B$ in this preparation. Define $Y$ similarly from $S,S'$. All founders, spent witnesses and markings are prescribed before enrollment. Each spent pair witness meets exactly its two specified live holders. Every marker meets just one live holder and the only other contacts link spent holders. The live witnessed pairs are exactly $B$ with the selected A-family anchor, $B$ with the selected C-family anchor, and $B$ with $K$. No two anchors belong to a common witness. Hence these three pairs form. $B$ spends all three marks, the selected three anchors each spend one, and the two unselected anchors supply unary births. There are exactly five first births.

Write $b_A,b_C,b_K$ for three *different* actual loose marks of $B$, bound respectively to those three roles. Subsequent comparisons use a common binding to roles across arms. These physical names suffice for an explicit receipt independently of how an implementation assigns integers. Write $a,c,k$ for the unique marks of the unprimed anchors and of $K$. In the primary arm the first three relevant receipts are $(a,b_A)$ at $R$, $(c,b_C)$ at $S$, and $(k,b_K)$ at $J$. In the first interruption, $R$ receives only $a$, $R'$ receives its own anchor mark and $b_A$, and $S$ and $J$ retain their exact incoming marks. The second interruption does the same to the C family while keeping $R$'s incoming marks fixed. The two diversions can also be made together.

In the original founder, toggling $e$ moves exactly two existing incidence endpoints from $A$ to $A'$ (or back), those of their designated spent witness. They are zero-valued, already shared, and never pay $R$ or $R'$. The sets of names and values at $B$, $C$, $K$, and every original holder other than $A,A'$ are identical in this intervention. In particular $A$'s sole loose distinction $a$ is unchanged and pays $R$ in both histories. The new sharing $R$ has with $B$ in one history did not exist in the founder. Conversely, the $f$ interruption moves only the analogous two original C-family incidence endpoints. This locates the changed preparation and its mediated use.

At the first live cut, $X,Y,J$ are all jointly witnessed by $B$. Consequently their prospective groups are the **three pairs**, not an additional three-parent group. Each of the three has two obligations and receives two fresh distinctions. The remaining two first-born knots are isolated unary children, each also receiving two fresh distinctions by the three-place floor. Thus $R$ can distinguish its two earlier events merely by counting its own *shared* distinctions, one or two. It even has a different total size, three versus four, while its live budget stays two. No bit, payer label or global age is needed for this read on the enrolled $R$ at its first birth or just before payment. The extra shared distinction $b_A$ is precisely its contact with the only possible common older payer of $R,S$.

More strongly, register fresh names by the anchor whose unique child receives them and give all five first newborns fresh values 11 in every arm. Every input is zero and each new word has exactly two ones and at least one zero, so this same choice is lawful under *both* laws. Then the two complete holdings of $R$ have the same $a$, the same two loose names, and the same three values on those names. The only extra distinction is $b_A$, of value zero and coheld with $B$. At $R'$ it is the complementary difference. The holding of $S$ stays exactly fixed in the $e$ interruption, including both its fresh names and values. Following a registered holder after the next payment also preserves the local size three or four: its own names and values never disappear when their second holders are added. This persistence concerns a record of the earlier event. The proof of the later birth must still test which incidence the ordinary rule reads.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Yes | Yes | Joins with $S,J$ separately | Joins with $R,J$ separately | Yes |

Here “joins with $S,J$ separately” denotes two distinct binary births. Trace the target in its positive arm by assigning $r_1,r_2$ to $R$'s two actual fresh names and $s_1,s_2$ to $S$'s. Bind $r_1,s_1$ to their joint birth, and $r_2,s_2$ to their respective other joint births with $J$. The first two incoming names $b_A,b_C$ are still shared with $B$ when their younger holders make these new payments. They cannot be paid a second time. Any bijection of each fresh pair to the same two obligations is lawful as a binding. After the $b_A$ diversion, $r_1$ and $r_2$ each pay a distinct unary child of $R$, while $s_1,s_2$ pay real binary children. $J$ and the unselected and replacement roles are accounted for in all arms.

To exclude a bypass, $R$'s complete older parent set is $\{A\}$ or $\{A,B\}$, and $S$'s is $\{C\}$ or $\{C,B\}$. Since $A\ne C$, their intersection is nonempty exactly when both contain $B$. The only possible larger live group through either of these two in the positive arm would use the third sibling $J$; its three members are already witnessed together by $B$ and it therefore cannot absorb the pair. The two unselected unary children have no witnessed adjacency to any other first newborn. These exhaust all live and older roles. The later eligibility test depends on the two *new* coholdings, but all were forecast by the chosen founder. The same earlier receipt at $R$ can give either later answer when $S$'s preparation changes. The other endpoint and its exact original payment are held fixed when testing each arm of that dependence. No changed Boolean value is required.

In every arm the second cut makes three binary births among $X,Y,J$ plus two unary births at each unselected holder, seven in total. Its binary births each get one fresh distinction. Viewed as future parents, they form a three-member group with pairwise distinct common payers and no single payer of all three. Its four unary births get two fresh distinctions each. The third cut makes one ternary birth from those three binary siblings, together with one binary and two unary births for each pair of unary siblings. It is funded. The unpointed birth counts are five, seven and seven through three cuts. The target changes which continuing roles meet. The first three total fresh counts are respectively 10, 11 and 13. At the third cut the ternary has no outside sibling, each binary has two obligations and each unary has its usual floor.

## The same interruption with joint alternatives on both sides

Use the seven-live-founder construction. Denote the two donors by $P_0,P_1$. The anchor parents of $R,R'$ are $A,A'$ respectively. They are paid also by $P_e,P_{1-e}$ respectively. The corresponding donors of $S,S'$ are $P_f,P_{1-f}$. Finally $U$ pays exactly the A-family holder assigned to $P_w$. In fact these are the ordinary first parent sets: the pair witnesses give each of the four anchors one adjacent donor, with no inter-anchor or inter-donor adjacencies. The one tagged A-family anchor, its donor $P_w$ and $U$ are pairwise witnessed by three different dedicated spent holders, with no joint witness, so they form one ternary replacing its three constituent pairs. All other eligible groups are the three remaining anchor-donor pairs. Every founder spends its entire available supply.

Each donor thus has two actual children, one per family. No other earlier holder has more than one. Their two offspring form a pair with that unique donor as older witness. Different donor groups have no adjacency. All first newborns have a single prospective group and each receives exactly one fresh distinction. They pay two disjoint binary children next, one for each donor. Each of those binary newborns has one loose distinction and pays a unary child on the third cut. The three cohorts have respectively four, two and two births, with total fresh counts four, two and four, under both laws. Both $R$ and $S$ grow a joint child for every setting.

Consequently the exact target condition is $e=f$. If they differ, $R$ joins $S'$ and $R'$ joins $S$. If they agree, $R$ joins $S$ and $R'$ joins $S'$. This is a double diversion, with the alternative partner already present and funded. $U$, the anchors and the spent source witnesses all fail the common-parent test because none of them paid both $R$ and $S$. A donor's earlier two physical payments give the entire positive dependency. Fresh marks from the two matched children then fund the new joint birth. In particular, both contacts with the same donor are needed; a common value across distinct donors cannot substitute for one of them.

The anonymous indication at $R$ can be masked. It holds $2+\mathbf1_{e=w}$ shared marks and one private mark. Fixing $w$ makes the extra payment from $U$ a sign of the earlier donor choice. Changing $w$ at fixed $e,f$ changes that sign while leaving the joint target unchanged. Conversely, change $e$ and $w$ together, holding $f$ fixed. The number, types and *values* of $R$'s marks can remain identical although the partner changes. In the registered word all original loose bits are zero. Both matched $R$ holdings see the same number of incoming zeros. Even parity requires their one fresh value to be zero, and not-all-equal requires it to be one. These are identical anonymous valued local holdings within each law. They still share a distinction with different actual older donors. All eight settings are covered by the two formulas. The additional $U$ payment is a genuine held sign, yet is dispensable to the later joint birth. The direct construction exhibits a held distinction whose sharing is genuinely used.

## Physical validity, identity and values

All incidences in the frozen founders have one or two holders. The source is connected through the circuit of spent holders and the separate spent marker of each live founder. Every spent pair witness is incident to four zero-valued live-contact distinctions and four ones on its two spent meetings in the common founding word. Each live founder has exactly its two marker ones and at least one zero. Each marker has four spent-meeting ones, two founder-contact ones and at least one zero. The word is therefore independently lawful under parity and not-all-equal in all arms, including the variant with three sharings at every marker. It is the same assignment to correspondingly named slots. Every initial loose mark is zero. The older holdings differ *where the declared witness incidence moved*. They are not an identical physical founder. Other legal valuations, including the common complement under not-all-equal, yield the same structural calculation; complementing an odd-arity even-parity relation would change that law. One can instead toggle the two values on any spent-to-spent meeting as a lawful parity control.

The canonical statements apply to the **unconditioned** relations on the holdings. Both laws constrain the whole local word, permit at least one state, and allow each proper restriction to extend on a holder of at least three places. The following also checks that the full connected presentation does not covertly narrow these local supports.

* At the source, freeze any lawful local word of any holder. For parity, remove that holder and keep its assignments as fixed requirements on its neighbors. Each remaining connected component still contains a loose distinction. The spent circuit with any one member deleted is connected; an anchor stranded by deletion of its sole marker has its own loose supply. If a live founder itself is deleted, the spent circuit and other live supplies remain. On each component take a spanning tree, satisfy each nonroot equation toward the root, and use a remaining loose distinction there to set the final parity. This extends the fixed word. For not-all-equal, give two opposite values to every bundle of repeated sharings whose endpoints avoid the frozen holder. Every remaining spent knot has such a bundle. A remaining live knot without such a bundle has a loose mark it can give the opposite of its fixed common value if needed. This yields an extension as well.
* In a connected parity presentation, any assignment to a *proper* subset of all loose marks can be completed. Choose the remaining loose bits so their total parity is even, then solve the shared marks along a spanning tree. All the inputs to any one of our newborns are a proper subset of the payer cohort's loose marks; there is another fully funded birth. Thus every admissible local word at that newborn can be attained. Other newborns use their own distinct nonempty banks to satisfy their parity equations. Conversely any old complete valuation extends across the whole cut because every newborn here has at least one fresh mark.
* At the source under not-all-equal, all loose values may be set jointly in advance. Two opposite values on each founder-marker bundle guarantee a mixed word at every live knot independently of those values. The spent holders can each be kept mixed, for example by using opposite values on each double spent-to-spent meeting. Joint freedom of the loose frontier persists on these cuts. To prescribe every new fresh value, choose the incoming values of each newborn to include an opposite bit if its fresh bank is constant. Each newborn has at least one actual input, and these input sets partition the previously loose names. The old jointly free frontier admits their simultaneous prescription. Equally, one can first prescribe the whole lawful local word at a single newborn, realize its old inputs from that free frontier, and lawfully fill every other bank. As before, any old valuation also extends on the funded cut. This proves the full local-projection claim at all three cuts.

Every original holder has a repeated sharing. A positive newborn pays or receives at most one mark per other holder at a time, and an older holder spends its loose marks only once. It can never manufacture a later repeated meeting between the same two holders. Thus the set of holders with a repeated sharing recovers exactly the entire founding set, even from the later holdings. Restricting coholders to the founders recovers their original loose counts. Of the live anchors with one loose opening, each marked one has its own unique multiplicity of founder-to-marker sharing. Among the donors the larger loose supply identifies their class, and their separate multiplicities identify the donor within it. Following the sole original loose payment then recovers each tracked newborn. Coherent relabeling transports all these facts. Distinct registered partners in different histories can be told apart without an external diary or a display number. Uniform marker multiplicity three preserves every growing-end witness and grant and allows the same transit of pre-enrolled roles; the simple multiplicity decoder then loses its individual anchor labels.

## Identical founders, binding shams and interpretation

At any complete boundary the ordinary operator takes the live holders with their loose counts and the subsets of live holders having a common witness. It computes the entire list of multiple-parent groups. A shortage stalls the whole round. If funded, the remaining loose count at each parent fixes the multiplicity of unary children. The new witness sets are exactly the lists of children of each old payer. From these the same operator computes the next prospective demand and the fresh count, with the same sole-birth exception under a fixed law. No Boolean value or chronology of payments within a completed round enters these calculations. An induction on funded complete cuts therefore gives the same abstract roster, fresh counts, funding and halt status from identical structural founders. Its holder matching fixes structurally unique enrolled roles and allows exchange of duplicate same-parent unary tokens. An actual swap of two already numbered private slots before they are paid can change their future named coholders with the old names held fixed. A successor selected by following such a slot or a value can be a different holder. That is additional identity information, and can change a named route even when the structural roster is identical.

In the direct family *every* one of the six bindings of $B$'s three names gives the same three children of $B$. In the balanced family both independent exchanges at $P_0,P_1$ preserve their two child sets, giving four bindings per structural case. Downstream, permuting the new loose names at any fixed payer similarly changes which *particular name* a child receives, leaving its actual parent group unchanged. In all of these shams the tracked anchor has a single loose original mark, so its identity of first newborn is unaffected. Every local canonicity proof above allows ordinary future completions under either law. Writing the births of an already determined complete plan in a different order leaves these conclusions intact. A law that recalculates eligibility during a partial cut has a different input and lies outside the result.

The direct mechanism is a retained relation between holders. It gives an ordinary causal role to material history prepared in the source. A decision by $R$ based on its private values, or an autonomous branching of group structure from identical complete founders, would require further premises. Scientific referee pending.
