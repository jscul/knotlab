# The owner's request, as given

Epic `PARTS-MEET-AGAIN-20261124-001` (sub-goal B317, assumption A223; goals G15 with G17 and G18). Two messages of
24 November 2026, kept here in the owner's words so that the design can be checked against them. Nothing on this page is a
result.

## The request

> Please take up the fold of emergence (lead B317, assumption A223): a made pattern that reads another pattern and grows its
> own.
>
> What I want to find out: is there a growth law under which parts that have come apart can meet again, so that what one part
> builds depends on whether another part is there? Under every law we have studied so far, parts that come apart act alone for
> ever, which is why the copies are a population and not a machine.
>
> First step: take the laws from the other thread in which a birth may take a distinction that is already shared (rule R,
> A230; rule Q, A235; the move recorded in A225) and run them on the ring that returns and on the two-knot part that copies
> itself. Check the record first for what those studies already found, including where rule R re-reads in the wrong places.
>
> The test: run each start twice, once with the second part present and once without it. A part reads another when its course
> differs between the two runs. Stop at one part whose course depends on another, or at a reason this cannot happen under these
> laws.
>
> Hold it to the usual standard:
> - a frozen pre-registration, reviewed cold by the advisor before the freeze;
> - an engine written from the frozen text, checked against the existing one;
> - the knot test on every knot made, under parity and not-all-equal;
> - every added rule named as a scaffold and swapped before anything is claimed about the model;
> - no claims about living things or minds.
>
> If a part does read another, the next question is whether the reader was supplied by us or made by the growth. Say plainly
> which.

## The addition, before the design was frozen

The owner's idea, in his words:

> There's no way it wouldn't work. It might just cost complexity. Programming is a thing in our reality, there should be a way
> to reference information like joining 2 different clusters of child logic from the parent via something like an 'axis
> point'. So we can essentially reference chunks of logic.

What he asked to be done with it:

1. **Fold it into the design.** Test directly whether a knot that both clusters descend from can serve as the reference: two
   clusters of descendants becoming joinable through their common parent or ancestor. Treat that as a named arm of the study,
   not only as something rules R and Q might happen to do.
2. **Change what a negative means.** If rules R, Q and the A225 move do not let parts meet again, that is a result about
   those three laws, not the end of the line. The next step is then the least law that does allow it, with its cost stated:
   what has to be supplied by hand (the rule, the choice of which shared distinction, any record of who came from whom).
3. **Keep his conviction apart from the evidence.** "It must be possible" is recorded as the owner's intuition; the usual
   standard holds for anything claimed.
4. **Check the word.** "Axis" is already used in the guide for another idea of his, so "axis point" goes to the advisor and
   gets its own entry, or a better word is proposed for a knot that others are joined through.

Any change this makes to the pre-registration is to be reported to him before the freeze.

## How the study reads the addition

- The intuition is recorded as an assumption in the register, with the owner's words, and is not evidence.
- That some law lets parts meet again is cheap to arrange: a law that lets any two members join, whatever is built, does it
  at once. What the study measures is the least law that does it, what that law has to be handed, and whether what it builds
  are knots under the canonical test.
- The words were put to the advisor and then to the owner. His answer, the same day: "Maybe access point is what I meant, or
  Take seems fine too. But I like ancestral common knot for the definition." So the definitions say **ancestral common
  knot** for a common knot that is also an ancestor of the members it joins, the law of the named arm is **ancestor-taking**,
  the move is to **take** a shared distinction, and "axis point" and "access point" stay as the owner's quoted phrases.
- Later the same day the owner added: "Root might be a good alternative word too." It is his candidate for the knot that a
  set of knots descends from. It goes to the advisor with the design: it names the descent and not the meeting, and the
  growth studies already use "root" for the root of an equation that gives a rate.

## A further idea, the same day

> This is why we may need "function-like" logical operations where we can reference/sample logic. These are basically meeting
> patterns that are complex in nature. Maybe even so complex as to the indexing operation being logic based iterators rather
> than incremental. So like maybe intelligence as a fold has access to something like "the golden ratio", but it needs to
> index what part of the golden ratio it's looking at?

How the study reads it: a reference needs an index, which says which part of the other pattern is taken, and the index would
itself be made by the growth. In the laws where a birth takes a shared distinction the index is the choice of which shared
distinction is taken. The study registers that choice as the index and reports, for each law, what supplies it: a fixed
convention, every choice enumerated, or something read from the structure. A made pattern that steps the index is beyond this
study and is filed as a lead. No claim is made about minds.
