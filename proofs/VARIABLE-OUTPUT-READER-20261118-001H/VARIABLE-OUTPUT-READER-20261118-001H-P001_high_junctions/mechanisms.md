# Two competing last junctions on the same prefix

**Theorem.** Start with the same finite reachable ordinary presentation on the same named bits. Add one junction on a permitted input group \(P\) on one side and one on a permitted group \(Q\) on the other. Each introduces the same number \(h\in\{2,3\}\) of fresh bits. Fix every prefix bit and match the fresh bits by any bijection. Fix one fresh bit \(f\) and its image. If its row of pair minima agrees under this matching through bond cardinality \(h+3\), the complete three-parent menus of the two successors agree. In particular the conclusion includes triples using a fresh output. The entry from \(f\) to a second fresh bit is part of its row. Values above the cutoff can all be pooled.

An incidence isomorphism of the two named prefixes permits the same argument after transport. The theorem requires the marked new junctions to be last in the stated constructions, and the correspondence to match the two old incidences and the new output groups. An arbitrary isometry of current scalar tables need not have either property.

## What a short entry to a fresh output requires

Write \(K\) for minimum containing bond cardinality, \(p=|P|\), and \(F\) for the \(h\) fresh bits. A dual support through \(f\) has the form

\[
                    F\ \cup\ (P\mathbin{\triangle} C),
                    \qquad C=\partial U,
\]

where \(U\) is a set of old ordinary vertices and \(C\) is its cut in the wired prefix. If \(j=|C\cap P|\), its size is \(h+p+|C|-2j\). Every nonzero old cut has size at least three. Two inputs of a legal pair are loose at distinct same-color holders, and no three-bit bond can contain them, since all three-bit bonds are trivalent stars. Any two inputs of a legal triple have \(K\ge5\). The latter bound applies also to cuts of size at most four: a nonminimal cut decomposes into disjoint bonds, each with at least three bits. [Short bonds](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/separator.md) · [Bound through two members of a legal triple](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001E/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001E-P001_marked_symmetry/scalar_inverse.md)

For every actual input \(e\in P\), \(K(f,e)=h+p\). A cut through both must have \(e\notin C\), so \(j\le p-1\). For \(j\le1\), \(|C|\ge2j\). For \(j=2\), the two distinct-input holders give the same bound. The new star attains equality in the pair minimum. For the pair of two fresh bits, the minimum equals \(h+p\) when \(p=1,2\), and is at least \(h+2\) for \(p=3\). In the last case \(j=3\) needs \(|C|\ge5\). If \(q\) is an old loose bit outside \(P\), an entry \(K(f,q)\le h+p\) requires \(q\in C\) and \(|C|\le2j\).

We will use two frontier facts. Any ordinary set of at least three sites sends at least four outputs outside: the latest three sites make at least six, with at most two internal uses among them. If exactly four leave, these three sites each made two fresh bits and have exactly two internal meetings. In particular, a size-four shore containing a legal input pair has three or more sites, contains the seed, and is closed under parents. Its frontier comes from a birth-ordered path \(A\to M\to Z\), with one bit at \(A\), one at \(M\), and two at \(Z\). Such size-four shores are nested. A size-five cut containing an entire legal input triple is a bond and its ordinary shore is likewise closed under parents. Any parent-closed ordinary set with the inherited order is itself a lawful prefix, retaining witnesses for every pair among its sites. ## The parent count is determined

The fresh-to-fresh entry separates one input from two or three. Suppose two and three inputs could agree, and write \(P\) for the triple, \(Q\) for the pair. A common input is impossible by the fresh-to-input value \(h+p\). For each \(q\in Q\), the two-input side has \(K(f,q)=h+2\). The triple side would need a cut through \(q\) satisfying \(|C|\le2j-1\). This forces \(j=3\) and \(|C|=5\). Thus every \(q\) would be a spare bit of a five-bit frontier containing \(P\).

There is at most one such frontier for fixed loose \(P\). If \(U,V\) are two of its parent-closed shores, no bit connects their two differences, since its earlier holder would be a parent of the later one. Consequently

\[
         |\partial(U\cap V)|+|\partial(U\cup V)|=10.
\]

Each term is at least five, since each frontier contains \(P\). In moving from the intersection to either shore, a nonempty difference must consume as many frontier bits as it exports. It exports at least two, from its latest site. The two fixed spare positions of the intersection cannot feed two disjoint differences, since the three globally loose bits of \(P\) are unusable. Thus the shores are nested. A strict difference in a nested pair must consume both spares and export exactly two bits. It consists of a single site, because the latest two sites of a larger difference export at least three bits between them and none can feed back into the parent-closed smaller shore. Hence the two spares form a legal pair in the smaller prefix. This is impossible. Append the triple \(P\), then append the complementary pair. The pair retains its common neighbor and the two additions consume the entire old frontier. They would give the forbidden two final sinks. [Two-sink exclusion](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/inverse.md#two-elementary-source-restrictions)

Both bits of \(Q\) would therefore be the two spares of the same frontier. Their common neighbor is already available in its parent-closed prefix. The same complementary triple/pair contradiction excludes these competing parent counts.

## Equal parent counts

For any \(p\), triples made exclusively of old marks have the same verdict by the preceding common-prefix theorem. Its proof uses only the threshold \(K(f,e)\le h+p\) for an input \(e\) and the strict inequality for an old bit in a surviving legal old triple. Triples with two or more fresh marks have a repeated holder in both successors. A unary last junction has only one ordinary neighbor, whereas a holder in a legal triple needs two distinct pair witnesses. [Old-mark theorem and threshold](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F-P001_finite_interval/structural.md#separate-derivation-for-a-terminal-junction)

**Two parents.** For \(q\in Q\setminus P\), the necessary cut satisfies \(q\in C\), \(j=2\), \(|C|=4\). The distinct same-color holders of \(P\) must be \(A,Z\) on its frontier path, since \(M\) has the opposite color. The shore is unique for this fixed pair of loose bits. Indeed any two such shores are nested, and \(Z\), the later holder of the pair, must be latest in both. A strict extension cannot use either globally loose bit of \(P\) or an output of \(Z\), which would require a vertex later than \(Z\). Only the one position at \(M\) could feed the additional sites. Those sites export at least two bits, contradicting equality of the two frontier sizes. If \(Q\ne P\), all its bits thus lie on this unique frontier, including any shared members of \(P\). Its only possible two distinct same-color holders are again \(A,Z\).

With identical holder sets, a three-parent choice involving a fresh mark has the same witnesses on both sides. Its other two holders must have the same ordinary color as the new site. The chosen parent holders have the opposite color, so exchanging which loose bit they contribute cannot consume either old mark.

**Three parents.** The same reasoning applies if the holder sets agree. If the holder sets are disjoint, \(Q\) is still a legal old triple after the addition on \(P\). Its bits remain loose and the new site misses its holders. Each would then have \(K(f,q)>h+3\), in conflict with its fresh-to-input entry on the \(Q\) side.

Suppose the two holder sets are different and intersect. They have the same ordinary bipartition color. Choose \(q\in Q\) at a fourth holder outside the three holders of \(P\). Its necessary cut satisfies \(q\in C\) and \(|C|\le2j\). Since two members of \(P\) cannot lie in a cut of size at most four, \(j=3\). We have a cut of size at most six containing four globally loose bits at four distinct same-color sites.

Such a cut has exactly six bits and a connected parent-closed ordinary shore \(U\). To see this, count how many of its four specified holders are among its latest three sites. At most one would leave three earlier loose bits in addition to the latest three's four outgoing crossings. Three would give six outgoing crossings from the three same-color sites and at least one earlier loose bit. Thus exactly two are among the latest three. Four outputs from this trio and the two earlier loose bits exhaust the cut, so none enters. The trio has exactly two internal meetings, both at its sole opposite-color site. Thus at most one opposite-color holder in \(U\) can have a loose bit. The four specified loose bits leave at most two places for all other crossings.

Now suppose a fresh output at the new site forms a legal triple with two older loose bits at sites \(b,c\). Those holders have the color opposite to \(P\), so they cannot both have loose bits in \(U\). Each must meet a different holder in \(P\) as its witness with the new terminal site. They also need an old common neighbor distinct from these two witnesses.

If just \(b\) lies in \(U\), its loose bit leaves at most one ordinary exit. That exit must connect \(c\) to its own witnessing member of \(P\). The two-step path from \(b\) to \(c\) through their old common neighbor also uses the exit. An inside common neighbor would have to be that same member of \(P\), making it common to all three parents. An outside common neighbor would require \(b\) to be the inside endpoint of the exit, which has the wrong color. Both cases are impossible.

If both lie outside, their two distinct witnessing connections spend both exits, at distinct same-color inside endpoints and distinct outside endpoints. Their old common neighbor must also be outside, because no inside site has both exits. The inside endpoints are joined inside the connected \(U\) and \(b,c\) are joined outside, so an ordinary cycle crosses the cut. Ordinary cycles are sums modulo two of four-cycles, by resolving the latest site of each cycle with its parents' earlier common witness. A four-cycle using one exit must also use the other. Since all four endpoints are distinct, the two inside endpoints would have to be adjacent. They have the same color. This contradiction proves that no fresh output on the \(P\) side can occur in a legal triple. Exchanging the two presentations proves the same on the \(Q\) side.

These cases exhaust all marked triples. No comparison used a pair outside the one named fresh row or an entry greater than \(h+3\).

## The sharp horizon, including two genuinely high presentations

Here is a matched example at the next smaller horizon for each \(h\). From the seed start two separate unary branches, each with three fresh outputs at its first junction. In each branch spend these three bits on three unary children, each giving two fresh outputs. Make one two-parent, two-fresh addition on each pair of the three children. The branch root is their lawful common witness. The resulting three pair-join holders have two loose distinctions apiece. Taking one loose bit per holder gives a legal triple because the three children give distinct witnesses, and no earlier site meets all three pair joins. Call the triple on the first branch \(P\) and on the second \(Q\).

Cap \(P\) on one side and \(Q\) on the other, with the same \(h\) new names. For either group and any prefix cut containing \(j\) chosen inputs, all the corresponding \(j\) holders are in the cut shore and contribute **both** of their globally loose bits. Thus \(|C|\ge2j\). Every new dual word that uses the cap has cardinality at least \(h+3\) by the displayed formula. The exact sets of all dual words of weight through \(h+2\) in both successors are therefore identical to those of the prefix on the extended names. This also preserves the bonds up to that size, since minimality there only compares smaller words. The identity bijection preserves every pair entry clipped at \(h+2\). Yet the very same marks \(P\) have been consumed on one side and still form a legal triple on the other. At \(h+3\), a bit of \(P\) has that precise value to \(f\) on the first side and a strictly greater value on the second, by the old-mark theorem.

The prefix has fifteen junctions and 33 distinctions. At width 35 with \(h=2\), both successors have just one high junction, of arity five. At width 36 with \(h=3\), both have just one high junction, of arity six. Arbitrary unary additions along the seed's unused third output before the competing caps preserve the construction, so the examples hold at each stated width and every increment \(2a+3b\), \(a,b\ge0\). For the stated common-prefix comparison \(K=h+3\), or \(D=h+2\), is therefore the sharp horizon. A universal horizon for unrestricted high/high comparisons would have to reach \(K=6\). Whether one exists remains open.

At \(K=h+3\) even an unrestricted renaming cannot carry the marked sets to one another in these examples. The single ordinary bit joining a branch root to the seed is a bridge. Every other ordinary bit in either marked branch lies on an ordinary four-cycle, including every attachment of the three-parent cap. Thus the retained part containing the three marks contains the incoming bridge as boundary and precisely all bits born in that branch. It has 16 distinctions on the unconsumed side and \(16+h\) on the consumed side. The pair readings through the maximum ordinary arity already recover the bridge partitions and hence these different part sizes. ## Exchanging one marked terminal output with an older loose distinction

**Theorem.** Let a last junction \(v\) of arity \(d=p+h\) have output \(f\) in a legal triple with two older loose distinctions \(l_a,l_b\) at \(a,b\). Then \(K(f,g)=d\) for every other output \(g\) of \(v\). For every physically loose old distinction \(y\) outside this triple, the transposition exchanging \(f,y\) and fixing all other names changes the pair readings through \(K=d\). The theorem also applies to a site with all outputs still loose, since such a site can be deferred to the end of the construction. [Deferral](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001E/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001E-P001_marked_symmetry/terminal_cap.md)

Identify an input in \(P\) with its holder when speaking of ordinary meetings. The witnesses for \(v,a\) and \(v,b\) are different members \(x,z\) of \(P\), respectively. The witness \(r\) for \(a,b\) belongs to the prefix and lies outside \(P\), since no member of \(P\) meets both \(a,b\). In particular \(p\ge2\). In the wired prefix consider the paths from \(x\) through \(a\) and \(l_a\) to the exterior and from \(z\) through \(b\) and \(l_b\) to the exterior. They use disjoint distinctions and avoid the input bits of \(P\).

In any connected ordinary shore containing \(x,z\), both paths cross its cut. If exactly one of \(a,b\) is inside and every ordinary exit belongs to these paths, there is only one ordinary exit. For instance, with \(a\) inside, the outside path crosses on \(zb\). The old path \(a,r,b\) must use that same exit. This would require \(r=z\) or \(a=z\), both impossible. If both are outside and the only ordinary exits are \(xa,zb\), then \(r\) is also outside. Join \(x,z\) inside the shore and \(a,b\) outside through \(r\). The resulting ordinary cycle uses both exits. An ordinary square must use the first exit, since every ordinary cycle is a sum of squares. It must then use the second. All four ends are distinct and the two inside ends have the same color, excluding such a square.

For \(p=2\), the fresh-pair equality was proved above. For \(p=3\), a bond through \(f,g\) of size less than \(d\) would require an old cut of size five containing all three inputs. It is a bond, so its ordinary shore is connected. The two paths account for its two remaining bits. The preceding cases force both \(a,b\) inside. Its five bits would then all be loose, namely \(P,l_a,l_b\). Ordinary connectivity gives the whole prefix for its shore. The five bits have different holders, whereas the latest site of the prefix has at least two loose outputs. This proves the fresh-pair equality in both cases.

Suppose the transposition preserved the readings through \(d\). It fixes \(g\), so \(K(g,y)=d\). A witnessing old cut \(C\) contains \(y\) and has \(|C|\le2|C\cap P|\). The short-input bounds above force \(P\subset C\), for either parent count. The two paths use neither \(y\) nor any input bit, giving \(|C|\ge p+3\). Thus \(p=3\), \(|C|=6\). This cut is a bond because a decomposition into two disjoint ternary bonds could cover at most two inputs of a legal triple. Its two remaining path crossings, after \(P,y\), again force both \(a,b\) inside. The whole prefix therefore has precisely the six loose bits \(P,y,l_a,l_b\). Its latest site \(w\) must hold \(y\) and one of the five bits at the five prescribed distinct holders. Its two fresh outputs are its loose bits and its arity is at most five.

If \(w\) holds an input \(e\in P\), the transposition gives \(K(y,e)=K(f,e)=d\). The star at \(w\) forces \(d=5\) and has arity five. Of its three ordinary meetings choose a distinction \(k\) off the two specified paths. The star gives \(K(y,k)\le5\), so \(K(f,k)\le5\). The corresponding old cut contains \(k\) and all of \(P\), and has size at most six. Since it contains the loose input at \(w\), its shore contains \(w\) and its cut contains \(y\). The two paths give two further bits, requiring at least seven.

It remains to take \(w=a\), with \(w=b\) equivalent by exchange. The output pair \(f,g\) at \(v\) and the output pair \(y,l_a\) at \(a\) can each be exchanged physically. Together with the supposed scalar exchange \(f,y\), these exchanges give \(K(y,l_a)=K(f,g)=d\). Again \(d=5\) and \(a\) has arity five. Its three ordinary neighbors are \(x,r\) and one further site \(t\). The site \(t\) differs from \(z\), since \(z\) meets \(b\) and would otherwise be common to \(v,a,b\).

Take \(k=at\). As above, \(K(f,k)\le5\) forces an old cut containing \(P,k\) of size at most six. The two paths force equality, and the three inputs force a bond. The site \(a\) is outside because inside it would contribute both \(l_a\) and the extra bit \(y\). Thus \(t\) is inside. If \(b\) is inside, the only ordinary exits are \(ax,at\), neither available on the path \(a,r,b\). If \(b\) is outside, the ordinary exits are precisely \(ax,at,bz\), and \(r\) must be outside. The exit \(bz\) lies on a cycle via \(ax\), the connected inside shore and the outside path \(a,r,b\). A square using \(bz\) must use exactly one of \(ax,at\). In either case its two inside endpoints have the same color, and its four ends are distinct. This is impossible. All possible older loose bits have been excluded.

## Increasing the period of a fixed template

There is a separate obstruction to hiding a difference by growing a periodic ordinary cover. Fill every ordinary four-cycle by a disk in the classical square complex of the ordinary graph. Every closed walk of a reachable presentation contracts there. At a latest-born vertex either delete a backtrack or replace the visit by the old two-step path through the earlier common neighbor of its two parents. This reduces the occurrences of the latest vertex until the walk is constant.

Fix a finite connected ordinary base graph and one connected finite regular graph cover whose upstairs graph is reachable. Write \(F\) for the base graph's fundamental group before filling squares and \(N\) for the covering normal subgroup. For a base square the property that its lift closes is independent of the initial sheet by regularity. Let \(S\) be the subset of base squares with this property. Then \(N\) is exactly the normal closure in \(F\) of their square loops. Each selected loop and every conjugate lift closed. Conversely every upstairs closed walk contracts by upstairs squares, each of which projects to a base square in \(S\), because the covering is locally bijective and the ordinary base is simple. Thus the cover has index determined by its subset \(S\). The fixed finite base has only finitely many such subsets and consequently only finitely many possible reachable regular-cover degrees. Changing the template, omitting the ordinary graph-cover requirement, or interleaving the growth before a template exists lies outside this result.
