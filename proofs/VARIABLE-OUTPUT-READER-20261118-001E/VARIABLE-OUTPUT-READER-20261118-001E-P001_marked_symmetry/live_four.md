# Every equation on at most four distinctions through a legal input survives a scalar isometry

**Theorem.** Let \(l\) occur in a legal three-parent choice of one reachable presentation. Under any isometry of its full scalar table with that of any other reachable presentation, the parity equations on at most four distinctions containing \(l\) correspond exactly in both directions. The competing presentation may have an arbitrary history, and its matched distinction is allowed to be ineligible there.

Identify the distinction names by the alleged isometry. A nonempty equation of size at most four is a bond. Indeed any non-minimal equation splits over the binary field into disjoint bonds, and each has size at least three. A three-bit bond is an ordinary trivalent star, and the complete family of such stars is read from the scalar table. A four-bit bond with only two ordinary sites on its shore is the sum of two adjacent trivalent stars since their arities sum to six. A four-bit bond with at least three ordinary sites on its shore contains no distinction eligible in any triple. The original mark \(l\) is loose at exactly one junction \(u\). Write \(N_i(l)=\{x:x\ne l,\ D(x,l)\le i\}\). Every member of \(N_3(l)\) occurs with \(l\) in an actual bond of size three or four. By the classifications just given this bond can only be the star of \(u\) or a sum of two trivalent stars with one at \(u\). This gives three exhaustive row types.

- If \(u\) has arity five or six, \(N_3(l)\) is empty. The pair minima then exclude every bond of size at most four through \(l\) in either presentation.
- If \(u\) has arity four, \(N_2(l)\) is empty and \(N_3(l)\) consists of exactly its other three distinctions. A pair from \(l\) to any of these three attains its minimum on a four-bit bond. All the other bits of that bond must lie in \(N_3(l)\). This forces exactly this support in every competing presentation.
- If \(u\) is trivalent, its star \(S=\{l,a,b\}\) is the unique ternary star through \(l\) in either presentation. Every original four-bit bond through \(l\) is the sum of \(S\) and a neighboring ternary star. Such sums belong to every competing code. To exclude an extra rival equation \(W\) of size four through \(l\), first suppose \(W\) contains another member of \(S\). Intersection in three bits is impossible because their sum would have size one. Intersection in two bits gives a ternary equation \(W\mathbin{\triangle}S\), so \(W\) is a sum of two ternary equations that were already present in the original code.

  Otherwise the three other elements of \(W\) all lie in \(N_3(l)\setminus\{a,b\}\). In the original presentation this set comes entirely from a trivalent neighbor through \(a\) or a trivalent neighbor through \(b\), with at most one such neighbor for each. Each neighbor contributes only the two other bits of its star. Among the three elements, at least two must belong to one neighboring ternary star \(T\). The third bit of \(T\) is \(a\) or \(b\), so the intersection has exactly two elements. Again \(W\mathbin{\triangle}T\) is a ternary equation in the rival, hence in the original, and \(W\) belongs to the original as well.

Thus any four-bit parity equation that differs between two scalar-equivalent reachable presentations avoids every distinction usable in a legal triple in either presentation. Differences on other distinctions can still affect whether the three parents share one ordinary neighbor.

## An opposing transposition needs higher arity

For a symmetry of a *single* scalar table coming from a transposition of a distinction \(l\) usable in a legal triple and any other distinction \(e\), every parity equation of size at most four is fixed individually. If one contained exactly one of \(l,e\), the theorem in either direction would put both that support and its exchanged support in the same parity code. Their sum would be the forbidden two-bit equation \(\{l,e\}\). In particular, if \(e\) is shared at the holder of \(l\), its other junction has arity five or six. This rules out all the four-place neighbors left by the earlier same-holder transposition bound.

## Reconstruction when every junction has at most four distinctions

Assume that both reachable presentations being compared satisfy the stated arity bound. At every size the scalar table truncated after distance three then recovers each junction and therefore every triple verdict. This permits both fresh counts. A two-parent junction must produce two fresh outputs and a three-parent junction could never have been added. A unary junction may produce two or three outputs.

A nonlocal four-bit bond would give a parent-closed prefix of at least three junctions with only four loose distinctions. Its first two additions each use a single parent, since the seed and its first child are adjacent and have no common ordinary neighbor. These additions increase the loose count from three to at least five. Every later addition consumes at most two distinctions and makes at least two, so the count never falls. This excludes such a bond anywhere in the presentation.

Join each pair of distinctions at distance at most three. The possible sources are a ternary star, a four-place star, or the boundary of two adjacent trivalent sites. The ternary stars are read from distance two. For two of them sharing a bit, their sum is a bond of size four. The cut around both has this size and cannot split into two nonempty bonds since each has size at least three. Delete from the pair links all cross-pairs having one nonshared bit from each of these two stars. A cross-pair cannot also lie in a junction. Its common holder would be a third ordinary site adjacent to both sites of the adjacent pair, contradicting ordinary bipartiteness. Every removed pair therefore lacks a common site and every surviving pair has one.

Give each loose bit a temporary private leaf in the classical graph, making all ordinary meetings and private leaves simple and triangle-free. Three pairwise incident distinctions in such a graph share a single endpoint, since the only alternative would be a triangle. As every actual junction has at least three distinctions, the maximal cliques of the surviving pair graph are exactly the complete stars. The reconstructed incidence directly tests the holder and witness conditions. The full-law comparison allows a competitor containing a junction with five or six distinctions, so it does not inherit this extra arity promise.

## Why the same proof stops at five

Immediately before the final junction in the 14-junction construction, there are five loose distinctions at width 30. Together they form a bond. All ten pair distances among them are four. Two of their ten triples are legal and the other eight are forbidden. In the certificate they are \(\{17,25,28\}\) and \(\{17,25,29\}\). The full table forces the complete rank-13 parity relation. On adding the first triple with two fresh outputs one obtains the four-bit bond of the ineligibility theorem. This draws an exact line for the mechanism used here. A five-bit bond around a large shore can itself contain a legal triple. It also shows why a nearly identical local pattern on three inputs calls for inspecting the rest of the table.
