# Two moves suffice to expose the tracked loose distinction when its other witnesses are exhausted

Let \(x\) be the sole loose distinction at junction \(h\). The tracked shared distinction joins \(h\) to \(c\). Assume the other two junctions meeting \(h\) have exhausted their loose distinctions. If a continuation ever makes \(x\) eligible for a three-parent join, then a continuation of at most two moves already does so.

If \(x\) is already eligible, the empty continuation suffices. Retaining \(x\) fixes the junctions meeting \(h\), and an exhausted junction stays exhausted. Two junctions that are already present can only gain a common witness if they already have one. A new common witness must consume a distinction from each, which requires an earlier common witness. Existing witnesses persist, and a holder's loose distinctions can only be consumed. Thus the first legal triple using \(x\) must involve the newly added junction \(q\). Every newly added junction that shares a witness with \(h\) does so through \(c\) alone, so the third parent \(p\) must come from the starting arrangement and have no meeting with \(c\).

At its birth \(q\) consumes from \(c\) and from some junction \(z\) meeting \(p\). The pair \(h,p\) already has a common witness in the starting arrangement. The inputs from \(c,z\) give the other two pair witnesses, and leave no common witness across all three parents. If \(z\) was in the starting arrangement, its common witness with \(c\) was already present there. Both still had a loose distinction when used by \(q\), so the same two-parent join is legal as the first move.

If \(z\) was added later, its birth consumed from \(p\) and from a junction \(r\) already meeting \(c\). Indeed, \(c\) was already present at the birth of \(z\), so their first common witness must have been a parent of \(z\). If \(r\) was also added later, its only common witness with \(h\) was \(c\). The loose distinctions at \(p,r\) used by \(z\) would then already have formed a legal triple with \(x\) just before \(z\) was added. Their pair witness is required by the join at \(z\), and \(p\) has no meeting with \(c\). This contradicts firstness. Hence \(r\) was in the starting arrangement, with a common witness with \(p\) already present. Junction \(p\) initially had at least two loose distinctions because it supplied one to \(z\) and still supplied one to the candidate triple. Add a junction on \(p,r\) and then a junction on \(c\) and the first addition. Use two fresh distinctions at each move. The second addition is legal through \(r\), and one of its fresh distinctions forms a legal triple with \(x\) and a remaining loose distinction at \(p\).

The checked census found zero triples using the tracked loose distinction in 440 one-move and 6,556 two-move continuations, enumerated up to permutations of interchangeable loose distinctions at each holder that fix the tracked pair. The hypothesis applies to 21 tracked pairs in 14 sources, so none of those tracked loose distinctions can ever become eligible for a three-parent join. [Checked continuations](live_twins.json)

From this directory, the following command lists all 21 identities as ordered triples of source, loose distinction, and shared distinction.

```bash
python3 -B -c 'import json; print([(r["source"], r["tracked_loose"], r["tracked_shared"]) for r in json.load(open("live_twins.json"))["per_source_transposition"] if all(d["loose_bits_on_witness"] == 0 for d in r["initial_common_witness_control"]["other_directions"].values())])'
```

Together with the separately checked source in the [component test](active_part.json), this accounts for 22 of the 32 state-changing scalar transpositions at width 20. For each of the remaining ten pairs, the holder has another neighbor with a loose distinction. The three-move search leaves those ten open at larger depths. More general permutations and isometries between different retained parts remain part of the [full inverse question](proof.md).
