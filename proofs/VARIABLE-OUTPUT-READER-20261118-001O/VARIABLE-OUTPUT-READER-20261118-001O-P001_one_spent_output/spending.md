# What the one spent output determines

Write \(A\) for the fourth three-input, three-output site, \(a,b\) for its two still loose outputs, \(s\) for its spent output and \(B\) for the child that consumes \(s\). Write \(k\in\{2,3\}\) for the number of parents of \(B\), \(Y=\{d,e\}\) for its outputs and \(Q\) for the \(k-1\) inputs other than \(s\). The support of the child star is \(W=\{s,d,e\}\cup Q\), of size \(k+2\). A bond is an inclusion-minimal nonempty parity equation. \(K(x,y)=D(x,y)+1\) is the least cardinality of a bond containing two distinct named bits. Ordinary sites carry the equations; loose bits are wired to a single exterior only for the cut representation.

## The spent row through every subsequent use of the child

**Theorem.** At all finite widths in the stated one-spent branch the closed row of \(s\) through \(K=k+2\) is exactly \(W\). The \(k+1\) other names in this closed row each read \(k+2\). Every outside name reads strictly greater. Thus every reachable rival matching through \(D=4\) carries this entire named child equation. The observation is available even when later sites have consumed both outputs of \(B\).

Immediately before \(B\) is born, \(A\) is terminal and its three outputs are loose siblings. It can be deferred until immediately before that birth. Every old word containing \(s\) contains \(a,b\), since all three have the same sole ordinary holder. The smallest such word is a bond through all three. It costs at least five for a terminal three-input, three-output site. When \(k=3\), \(s\) is in the legal old triple \(\{s\}\cup Q\), and the fresh-pair minimum at \(A\) is then six. Thus before the birth of \(B\) every word through \(s\) costs strictly more than \(k+2\). [Terminal fresh-pair bounds and live-mark equality](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001H/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001H-P001_high_junctions/mechanisms.md#exchanging-one-marked-terminal-output-with-an-older-loose-distinction)

A new word containing \(s\) is \(W\triangle C\) for an old cut \(C\) omitting \(s\). Its size is \(k+2+|C|-2j\) where \(j=|C\cap Q|\). If \(j\le1\), a nonzero old cut costs at least three, exceeding \(2j\). For \(j=2\), the old cut contains two members of the legal old input triple and therefore costs at least five, also exceeding \(2j\). Thus at birth \(W\) is the only equation of size at most \(k+2\) containing \(s\).

Consider any later ordinary shore bounded by such a word. If it includes \(B\) and excludes \(A\), there are \(k\) bit-disjoint ordinary paths from \(A\) to \(B\) already at the child's birth. One is their direct meeting, and each other uses the earlier common witness of \(A\) and the corresponding other parent, then that parent's input to \(B\). For a three-parent child these two witnesses are different. If any later site is selected, the latest two selected sites among \(B\) and those born after it export at least three bits distinct from all the birth-path crossings. This yields at least \(k+3\). If the shore includes \(A\) and excludes \(B\), all \(a,b,s\) cross. A later selected site exports at least two additional bits from the latest such site. At cutoff five equality would leave no crossing within the old connected ordinary graph immediately before \(B\). All those old sites would then be inside with \(A\), and the other inputs of the excluded \(B\) give additional crossings. Thus in either orientation the shore selects no site later than \(B\); its labeled cut was already present at birth. The surviving star is a bond, proving the row assertion.

In a rival a bond attaining a pair on this row must use \(k+2\) bits all on its closed row. It has exactly the stated support. The reader can list this forced equation for each row with this cardinality and value. Different rows may pass the same test, and a forced equation alone need not be a physical rival site.

For each \(x\in Q\) and either retained cap sibling \(a\), one also has \(K(a,x)>k+2=K(s,x)\) through arbitrary subsequent growth. A short shore through \(a,x\) includes \(A\) since \(a\) is loose. If it excludes \(B\) its word contains \(s\) and was just shown to be \(W\), which omits \(a\). If it includes \(B\), the bit \(x\) enters that shore and \(a,b\) leave. In the presence of a later selected site, the latest two selected sites among \(B\) and later sites export at least three extra bits, exceeding the cutoff. At birth, an old word through \(a\) already costs more than the cutoff. A new word \(W\triangle C\) through \(a,x\) would use an old cut \(C\) containing \(a,b,s\) and omitting \(x\). To stay within \(k+2\) it must have \(|C|\le2|C\cap(\{s\}\cup Q)|\). An overlap of at most one is too small, and overlap two when \(k=3\) needs an old cut of size at least five through two members of a legal triple. This excludes the new word as well.

**Supplied predecessor consequence.** Suppose the two presentations attach a terminal two-output child on the same named physical prefix and transport its two output names. Write \(P_0=\{s\}\cup Q\) for the source child inputs and \(P_1\) for the alternative. The forced source equation \(Y\cup P_0\) and the rival star \(Y\cup P_1\) give the old equation \(P_0\triangle P_1\). It uses only physically loose old bits. By ordinary connectivity, it is empty or the whole old frontier. The latter would require \(P_1\) to consume both loose siblings \(a,b\) of the old cap, an illegal repeated holder. Thus \(P_1=P_0\). In particular all physical sites and all next marked permissions agree in this comparison, even when its rival child was not promised to consume from the cap. More generally, if the child outputs and the old equation spaces agree in two presentations, the forced word gives the same one-dimensional extension, and so every successor verdict agrees. ## If the spending child's outputs are both still loose

**Theorem.** In this case every reachable rival matching the full named table through \(K=6\) has the following ordinary incidence. The names \(a,b\) are loose together at one site, \(d,e\) are loose together at a different site, and \(s\) is their shared meeting. The source can have any of the three preceding three-output families. Both pairs and the meeting are determined under the supplied table isometry. The rival receives no output or site correspondence and no chronological promises.

If both outputs of \(B\) are loose, defer it to the end. Site \(A\) is terminal in the remaining presentation, so defer \(A\) to immediately before \(B\). This leaves a reachable old prefix \(X\), with \(n\) sites, on whose three legal loose inputs \(P\) site \(A\) is born. The other inputs \(Q\) of \(B\) are loose in \(X\), at different holders of the opposite color from the holders of \(P\). Every holder of \(Q\) shares a witness with \(A\) at one of the three \(P\)-holders. In particular \(P,Q\) are disjoint. [Deferring a site with no ordinary child](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001E/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001E-P001_marked_symmetry/terminal_cap.md)

Write \(L\) for the equation space of \(X\). Besides \(L\), the possible restrictions of a successor equation to the five new bits, and the corresponding restrictions to the old names, are

| New names | Old restrictions |
| --- | --- |
| \(a,b,s\) | \(P\triangle C\), \(C\in L\) |
| \(s,d,e\) | \(Q\triangle C\), \(C\in L\) |
| \(a,b,d,e\) | \((P\cup Q)\triangle C\), \(C\in L\) |

This follows by adding neither, one, or both of the two new physical stars. In the old prefix all words up to size four are the seed and genuine four-place stars. Indeed the old menu is positive because \(P\) is legal. The ternary-third branch then has at most one shrink, the binary-third at most three, and the unary-third branch admits no large four-shore. A large shore means one with at least three ordinary sites. The ordinary graph is simple and bipartite.

Through size five the first line can contribute an old bit only on a large old five-cut containing \(P\). To see this, an old residual \(J\) has size at most two. An overlap with \(P\) would give a forbidden cut of size at most four on two members of a legal triple, or a word through two. Otherwise \(P\cup J\) is an old cut of size at most five containing distinct loose bits at all three holders. The second line gives its true residual \(Q\) or, for \(Q=\{t\}\) with \(t\) on the old seed, the other two seed bits. Indeed any disjoint residual for a two-member \(Q\) would require an old four-cut through the two loose bits at distinct holders. A residual partly overlapping either choice gives a word of size at most two.

The third line through five has at most one old name \(x\). It cannot have none, since a cut consisting of the old loose bits \(P\cup Q\) would have to be the whole old frontier. After the two additions deleting \(A,B\) would then disconnect every old site from the exterior, contrary to two-site deletion. Nor can \(x\in P\cup Q\), since a proper nonempty subset of the loose frontier cannot be an equation. Thus \((P\cup Q)\cup\{x\}\) is an old cut. If \(x\) is loose it is the only old loose bit outside \(P\cup Q\). Otherwise it is an ordinary bridge, as it is the cut's only ordinary crossing. In particular the whole through-four word family consists of the old through-four words and the genuine child star when \(k=2\). A source pair of old names can shorten through five only when it is the two-element \(Q\). Members of any other old residual of size two already occur together in an old word of size at most five.

We now exhibit an input \(i\) to \(H\) farther than six in bond size from **every** one of the five new names. In the first line of the table a word of size at most six meeting such a shared input must come from an old six-cut containing \(P\) and \(i\). In the second line through six an old residual \(J\) has size at most three. For a one-element \(Q=\{t\}\), a residual meeting a shared input \(i\) of \(H\) requires an old cut through \(i,t\) of size at most four. A residual containing \(t\) in addition to \(i\) would give a nonzero old word through two. A through-four old cut is a physical star. Since \(t\) is loose and \(H\) has six places, this can happen only at its other holder, the parent of \(H\) on \(i\). For fixed \(t\) at most one of the three inputs qualifies. For a two-element \(Q\), a disjoint residual would give an old cut through five containing both loose \(Q\) at distinct same-color holders. Every such old cut has at most two sites and cannot contain both. Overlap of one can only give the old ternary seed, containing the other loose member of \(Q\) and \(i\). Before \(H\) is born the initial four-cycle has used two different seed bits: the first two unary children are siblings or a chain, and the first subsequent binary birth closes that cycle. If \(H\) uses the seed, no seed bit can still be loose. If both members of \(Q\) appear in \(J\), a nonzero old word has size at most one. Thus this second line excludes at most one further choice of \(i\).

In the third line through six the old cut differs from \(P\cup Q\) on at most two bits. It therefore has at most two ordinary crossings. Between the holders of any input of \(H\) there are three bit-disjoint ordinary paths at \(H\)'s birth, through its other two input parents and their distinct earlier pair witnesses. Such an old input can never cross a cut with only two ordinary crossings. We have now found \(i\) that occurs in none of the three lines through six.

An old output of \(H\) and this \(i\) have reading six. The old high-star theorem shows that no word of size at most six in the forced \(n-1\) dimensional low span contains that pair. Their reading remains six in the current source because \(i\) has no new short partner. In every matching rival a six-bit witness for the pair avoids all five new names and is independent of the forced low span. This supplies the \(n\)-th old-supported dimension. We can use the same \(i\) with each of the three old outputs of \(H\). Accordingly every old name occurs in some forced or required old-supported word, including any loose output of \(H\).

### Distinguishing the meeting from each loose pair

The unrestricted rival rank is at most \(n+2\), the actual successor rank. This is the four-three-output rank bound, using the unique ternary star, the width formula and the minimum-width theorem in reverse. [Rank bound](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001N/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001N-P001_fourth_at_the_parents/rank.md#the-rank-bound-from-distance-four)

Some true member \(i\) of **the new cap's input set \(P\)** satisfies \(K(i,d),K(i,e)>6\). Both \(K(i,s)\) and \(K(i,a)\) are at most six by that cap's star. To see the strict inequalities, inspect the second and third lines of the table. When \(Q\) is a singleton, a second-line residual \(J\ni i\) of size at most three would require an old through-four cut containing \(Q\) and \(i\), impossible for two loose bits at distinct old holders. With \(|Q|=2\), overlap of \(J\) with exactly one member likewise requires an old ternary cut containing \(i\) and a different loose holder. Overlap of both is impossible by the old minimum weight. Thus a second-line witness requires an old five-cut containing \(Q\cup\{i\}\). All ordinary shores pinning the two \(Q\) holders cost at least five. Indeed a smaller cut would be a local star and cannot contain both loose marks. If all three \(i\in P\) had such witnesses, their pinned minimum shores would have a union of cost five containing all five loose bits \(P\cup Q\). This would be the complete old frontier and would violate two-site deletion after attaching \(A,B\).

In the third line an old residual of size at most two meeting \(i\in P\) would leave an old cut consisting of some of the loose bits in \(P\cup Q\), with at most one further bit. It cannot be entirely loose, since it omits the globally loose \(i\) and is nonempty. If the extra bit is shared, it is an ordinary bridge, the only ordinary crossing. The shore contains the two other \(P\)-holders but excludes the holder of \(i\), because \(i\) is loose and does not cross. These three holders lie on the six-step ordinary cycle given by their three distinct pair witnesses in the legal old triple. No single bridge separates vertices on that cycle. This deals with both child arities and proves the strict inequalities.

Restrict the rival equation space to the five names \(a,b,s,d,e\). The old-supported rank bound makes its image at most two-dimensional. The forced child word has restriction \(\{s,d,e\}\). A witness through \(i,a\) avoids \(d,e\); it is independent of the child word in the image. The same is true of witnesses through \(i,b\) and \(i,s\), by the six-place cap and the strict inequalities. In a two-dimensional binary space containing \(\{s,d,e\}\), the nonzero elements avoiding \(d,e\) form a space of dimension at most one. Thus all three witnesses have the same nonzero restriction, precisely \(\{a,b,s\}\). The image is exactly the span of the first two rows of the table, and the unrestricted rival has full rank \(n+2\).

Every actual rival star containing \(a\) contains \(b\), and conversely. Two distinct ordinary sites cannot share two bits, so \(a,b\) are loose at a single site. Likewise \(d,e\) are loose at a single site. These two sites differ because no site has four loose outputs. The two patterns now force their one shared bit \(s\). In particular an isometry cannot send the two surviving outputs to separate holders or spent names in the terminal-child case. This argument makes no assumption on the direction of \(s\) in a rival history.

### The child word is a physical site in every terminal-child comparison

Call the two recovered rival holders \(A'\) on \(a,b\) and \(B'\) on \(d,e\). If \(s\) enters \(B'\), its actual input-output reading \(K(s,d)\) gives \(\deg B'=k+2\). Every bit of its star is then on the closed row of \(s\) through \(k+2\) and its star equals \(W\). This also settles either orientation as soon as \(\deg B'\le k+2\), since the actual value \(K(s,d)=k+2\) gives the reverse bound. It remains to exclude a larger \(B'\) when \(s\) leaves it. Its three outputs are then \(s,d,e\). The ordinary shore of the bond \(W\) contains \(B'\) because \(d,e\) are loose there, and excludes \(A'\) because \(a,b\) are loose there. All three outputs leave. A shore on at most two sites would cost at least the degree of \(B'\), since a second site has at least three bits and can cancel at most one meeting. If \(B'\) is earlier, they export at least four in addition to its three. The sole possibility is \(k=3\), \(\deg B'=6\), with precisely five outgoing bits and no entering bit. In the source \(K(a,s)=6\) when \(k=3\). Only a word in the first line contains both names, and the legal old triple \(\{s\}\cup Q\) forces fresh-pair minimum six before spending. ## All permissions for every terminal three-parent spending child

**Theorem.** Suppose \(k=3\) and the two spending-child outputs are both loose. Through \(D=5\) every reachable mixed rival under every naming correspondence gives the same current marked verdicts. ### Recovering a unary- or binary-third old prefix

First recover the **old physical prefix on the old names** when the third three-output addition was unary or binary. The previous rank transfer for these two families forces its entire \(n\)-dimensional equation space \(L\) in the rival. Excise the two physical sites \(A',B'\) and the five bits, wiring any old remaining free end to the exterior. Every old name occurs in \(L\), so each has at least one remaining ordinary holder. The \(n\) old rival site stars form an independent basis of exactly \(L\). This is a graph representation of the old space even without a proof that its inherited birth order is lawful.

The positive source prefix \(X\) has a two-connected ordinary block carrying the legal triple \(P\). In the unary-third family the ordinary graph consists of that single block and at most one leaf attached by a bridge \(t\). The two \(Q\)-holders each have at least two distinct old neighbors, one witnessing their pair with \(A\) and another witnessing their pair with each other. ### Recovering an old prefix with a six-place site

In the remaining old family the unique six-place site \(H\) takes three inputs and makes three outputs. Since \(P\) is a legal old triple, the old prefix has either zero or one shrinking site. We already have \(n\) independent rival words vanishing on the five new names, including the span of all \(n-1\) old source low stars, and every old name has an old rival holder. Among old names the source pair readings through five remain the old ones except possibly for the pair \(Q\). For a candidate old rival site with at most five places, comparison to these readings shows it must be a physical old source low site. Here are the details of the one potentially changed entry. Through four every old source word is the seed or a physical four-star and the whole through-four word family is common, as the ternary spending child contributes no new four-word. A physical rival five-star meets the common seed in at most one bit. Its other four or more nonseed bits must be pairwise incident at low source sites. The old low-site supports agree physically.

If no old site shrank, any true input to \(H\) is beyond six from the five new bits. Fix \(i'\in P'\). Its reading to \(s\) is at most six. This witness must have the first of the three new-name patterns in the true source. Indeed, only the first and second contain \(s\). In the second, an old residual \(J\ni i'\) of size at most three with precisely one common member of \(Q\) would require a cut through three containing two loose bits at distinct holders, \(i'\) and the other member of \(Q\). Such a cut remains a five-bond in the presentation immediately after \(A\) with its selected shore omitting \(A\). The two \(Q\) are loose marks in the legal triple \(\{s\}\cup Q\), whose third holder \(A\) is outside. ## A terminal binary spending child after an earlier six-place site

**Theorem.** If the third three-output birth had three parents, and the binary spending child still has both outputs loose, every matching rival through \(D=5\) has precisely the same physical sites and all the same marked permissions.

Through five, no pair of old names changes. In the first line the old residual is empty at this cutoff in the sense that no such word exists, in the second any two old names in the residual already belonged to a seed word, and in the third line at most one old name occurs.
