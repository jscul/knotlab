## Counting and sampling without path weights

Let A and B be the two blocks. Put their common sites aside since each will occur in every group. Write L and R for the remaining left and right sites. For each left site x, let D(x) consist of the right sites unable to join it. Choosing left sites omits the union of their D(x) from the right. If U is any such union, take **all** left sites with D(x) contained in U and **all** right sites outside U. This is a maximal group. Every omitted right site has an incompatible chosen left site, and every omitted left site has an incompatible chosen right site. Conversely, maximality forces both of these choices. Hence distinct unions correspond one-to-one to maximal groups. The usual graph-theoretic description is a maximal clique in the union of two cliques.

Removing a duplicate row or a row equal to a union of other rows leaves this family unchanged. The corresponding reduction also works on columns. The omitted flag of a right site is the logical OR of the left inclusion flags of its nonneighbors. If its column of nonneighbors is the union of selected columns, its omitted flag is the OR of their omitted flags. The codes record the physical names and restore all such rows, columns and common sites. Restricting to a subset of right columns gives an exact count for a smaller induced stage. The full physical counts in the table use every column.
