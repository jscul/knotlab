# Does a consumer spend distinctions the clock could use?

Prospective design for B262, 21 November 2026. Read the November 20 postulate lead, the complete-holdings serial repair, and the marked-clock report before choosing this test. The earlier report gives the three preparations, a marked birth and its parent-age period. The serial study gives a one-birth rule. It leaves open the cost in individual births and the competition at the actual parents of that mark. No new outcomes have been obtained.

**Preparations and observer.** Start with the recorded eight-knot cycle, one shared distinction per consecutive pair, two loose distinctions per original, and one spare at original 0. The previously identified marked continuation visits 0, 2, 4 and 6. At age $4j+2$, $j≥1$, the marked child holds a run of $4j+1$ original occurrences with the spare at each end selected and nothing else selected. Its two actual parents each omit one selected spare. Identify the first occurrence from physical ancestry and the mark, using the earlier proved identification of clusters with physical births. Check its parent ancestry before reading the count. The spare denotes the marked holder’s unique unary offspring in the baseline and in both recorded attachments. Interchanging its initially loose labels has no effect on that structural identification. In the supply-only control its unique unary remains identifiable at 0, although the other funded holders acquire extra unaries. Record whether it occurred in the strict prefix before the chosen event and in the inclusive prefix after it. Count an actual birth once, whatever its arity. A clock tick is itself one physical event.

Compare the original with the recorded attachments of one new holder at original 3 (on the marked continuation) or 2 (on the other continuation). Each adds a new shared distinction at its old host, two loose distinctions at the new holder, and one loose distinction at each of the old host's two neighbours. Preserve every original support place and mark under these additions. A consumer birth means a child having the new holder as an actual ancestor, including as a direct parent. Test membership by tracing spent input distinctions to old holders. An ancestor need not be the current parent. Check whether such children actually spend interchangeable slots at the same current parent as a pending tick. Report the number of consumer births and the total inputs consumed. In particular, call this load heavy only if the number of these withdrawals at a single given tick parent is shown to grow without bound along successive ticks. Otherwise give the counts and limit the conclusion.

Give the serial guard the original cohort as a marked preparation. This is the prior paper's explicitly source-supplied variant. From the current holdings orient each added sharing by increasing minimum parent depth from the originals, recover the least depth still holding a loose distinction, and reconstruct the ordinary complete birth roster and immutable fresh budgets of that full depth. Subtract siblings already written. An occurrence offered by this guard uses any one actually loose distinction per named parent and appends a child with new distinct fresh distinctions. Check the proper-input test for parity and the keep and projection conditions for not-all-equal. The rates taken at completed cuts therefore apply to this serial law. Uncoordinated recalculation is tested separately below.

Search breadth-first through four births, with a ceiling of 200 expanded states per source and search variant, non-tick moves prioritized by presence of consumer ancestry, then more target parents, then sorted current parent IDs. Stop on the first tick, a blocked ancestry-only strategy, or after all ancestors of the first tick have been considered. The outcome of this targeting strategy bounds only those schedules. Warm-start and from-original results get different claims. Neither is needed to close the funded-clock stop if it is satisfied independently.

**Predictions to test.** The cost per turn in whole physical births will increase already from source to first completed turn on both attachments. A disjoint or paid load can delay an event in that accounting. The target's actual pairs of parents, parent ages and turn counts should agree on completed cuts for the two recorded attachments. On the same continuation predict exactly $j$ distinct consumer siblings at each of the two parents of tick $j$, with $j+2$ available slots per such parent on the bare clock and $2j+2$ on the attached clock before any sibling there fires. Predict zero consumer siblings at either tick parent on the other continuation. Try to prove or falsify these predictions by the complete child-deletion rule, including extension at either end, indistinguishability of slots and the possibility of duplicate parents. Report separately whether the mark is still enabled after its competitors spend from its parents and whether a funded consumer can erase it. Numerical inequality, common ancestry without a common parent, and a late chosen priority cannot by themselves answer that last question.

**Stop and validation.** Stop the scientific search at a matched pair with different ticks per actual birth or an all-size funded-clock result on this named class. Test the next full turn as an independent boundary and retained controls. If neither survives, report the first failed gate and the actual outcomes on the specified panel. Parse current physical supports and recover parents and inputs for the decisive events, check the accounting before and after every write and independent complete-cut and symbolic counts, and challenge slot choice by reversing names or by swapping the first two named events. Record both laws even if their incidence ledgers agree. The finite runs illustrate an all-size statement only when supported by the separate argument. No statistical rate over fair orders, rate of physical time, or inference of a new contact is under test. The panel should take well under ten minutes; register anything larger with the epic lead before starting it.
