# Two three-fresh joins are readable, and the family extends to every count

**Yes.** Start from the three-place parity junction and allow each later join to make two or three fresh distinctions. If one reachable arrangement uses at most four distinctions at each junction, its bond distances determine every junction and every permissible next choice at every finite size. The count of joins making three fresh distinctions can be arbitrarily large. A rival may use any junction the law permits and any growth history. The claim holds under every renaming of the current distinctions. **How much distance is enough.** Pick one horizon. Read the exact bond distance up to that number and record just “larger” for every more distant pair. The smallest horizon that always settles an unordered triple is as follows, counting additions after the initial seed.

| Joins making three fresh distinctions in the small-junction source | Sharp bond-distance horizon |

| Zero or one | 3 |
| Exactly two | 4 |
| Each count of three or more | 5 |

**What broke at two.** The earlier counting proof allowed an extra independent parity equation for the first time. That can actually happen. In the width-27 example the source allows the three marked distinctions to join. In the rival they have already been used together. All 351 pair entries agree when distances through three are visible. On the two fresh outputs of the rival's extra junction, the source bond distance is six and the rival's is four. This pair settles the ambiguity at horizon four. Those two unrestricted full tables have different histograms. **Why the result holds at arbitrary width.** A rival trying to replace a four-place junction by a larger group would change which pieces separate when one distinction is removed. The table detects that. The only remaining way to change the arrangement is to insert a five- or six-place junction between distinctions that appeared loose in the original. Its incident distinctions must lie together in a bond of that size. Counting the inputs and outputs of their original holders exhibits two that require a larger bond there. A rival with the same pair entries therefore has exactly the same junctions. **Remaining question.** A full-table counterexample for three-parent joins now requires a five- or six-place junction in **each** proposed arrangement. It also needs at least 27 distinctions by the earlier complete comparison. This result sharpens the evidence for G1 and A97. The unrestricted question remains open. The width-18 obstruction for smaller groups falls outside the small-junction family. Its competing histories have identical full allowed states, so increasing the number of distinctions in a state-derived bond reading cannot recover their different one- and two-parent permissions.
