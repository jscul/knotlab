# When a pair reading carries all later orders

**Standard statement.** In a connected finite wired parity presentation, a reading of a nonempty set of distinctions is the least cardinality of a bond containing that set, with infinity allowed. At a reached stage fix every distinction on every live ordinary junction pointwise. Study the smallest \(r\) for which every permutation of the stage preserving its readings through order \(r\) has a coherent extension preserving every later reading. A stage without a strict successor has order zero. The results below give all-size structural tests, an unbounded class of sampled starts of exact order two, and a structural obstruction to sufficiency at two.

**Nearest known result.** The predecessor identifies this strict-future problem with preservation of the current binary equation space when continuation is possible. It also establishes unbounded worst-case order. The new conclusions locate sufficient and failing cases at order two without having to grow the entire future. ## 1. A certificate on every finite open stage

Let \(C\) be the span over \(\mathbf F_2\) of the ordinary junction rows. A bond is a support-minimal nonzero row. Its support \(B\) is recognized by \(K(B)=|B|\). Each open future round introduces its new equations with private fresh distinctions. The only fresh-free equation in a closing round is already the sum of the previous junction rows. Consequently the equations supported wholly on the old distinctions remain exactly \(C\). Different old spans give a failed bond equality in one direction at every later stage. Equal spans and the fixed end extend through one round by the transport theorem, and then through all rounds by induction. This proves the required equivalence for a self-renaming, including the need to check that a successor exists. [Old-support argument](../../../research/BOND-READING-SCALE-20261119-001/limiting-reading.md)

A transposition of names \(i,j\) sends a row \(w\) to \(w+(w_i+w_j)({\bf e}_i+{\bf e}_j)\). It preserves \(C\) exactly when the columns of \(i,j\) agree on all generating rows or \({\bf e}_i+{\bf e}_j\in C\). At order zero all permutations of movable names are allowed. At order one all permutations within each class of equal singleton minima are allowed. Transpositions with one representative generate the symmetric group of their class. This decides these first two cutoffs exactly.

Write \(c_x=K(x)\) and \(R(x,y)=[K(x,y)=c_x]\), with its diagonal included. The set \(U_x=\{y:R(x,y)\}\) is the union of all minimum bonds through \(x\). If \(|U_x|=c_x\) the entire set is one bond determined by the observations. All such rows and the live rows generate an invariant subspace of \(C\). Full rank certifies that pairs suffice. Another forced row can arise when the pair minimum is \(m\) and exactly \(m\) names have readings at most \(m\) to both specified names, including the two marks themselves. Every minimizing bond lies in that intersection and must fill it. These tests can be supplemented by refinement of invariant named classes, testing the generating permutations of the resulting upper bound on the symmetry group, and in codimension one intersecting candidate residue classes. Any verified generating superset of the allowed group suffices if every generator preserves \(C\). Failure of an upper-bound test alone does not exhibit an allowed harmful permutation. [Rank, color and codimension-one proofs](../../../research/BOND-READING-WEIGHTINGS-20261119-001/structural.md#certificates-for-arbitrary-reached-stages)

The directed predicate \(R\) is efficiently computable. For a name \(x\) with endpoints \(s,t\), all minimum bonds containing it are the minimum \(s,t\) cuts. Both shores of any such cut are connected by positive capacities. After one maximum flow its \(s\)-shore is exactly a successor-closed set in the residual graph containing \(s\) and avoiding \(t\). To require an oriented second distinction \(a\to b\) to cross, take the residual closure of \(s,a\) and test whether it avoids \(t,b\). Either orientation can work. Thus one flow per distinct holder pair gives the full directed reading, with duplicate names restored before testing literal permutations. Full pair symmetries preserve it, which makes a successful directed certificate sufficient for the full reading. [Algorithm and controls](../../../research/BOND-READING-WEIGHTINGS-20261119-001/pair_certificate.md)

## 2. Exact criterion for a reached ordinary tree

Suppose the ordinary meeting graph is a tree after merging all repeated meetings between each pair of holders into a bundle. Each ordinary leaf has a loose distinction by the genuine-star condition. Represent each loose name by its own tip in the ordinary tree. A connected ordinary subtree is a bond shore since every complementary branch reaches the exterior. Conversely the shore omitting the exterior in any bond is connected. The cost of an off-path branch toward \(v\) from \(u\) is the lesser of the \(uv\)-bundle weight and the sum of the loose cost and all other branch costs at \(v\). The positive values are computable by two passes through the tree.

For two distinct positions \(e,f\), their reading is their two bundle weights plus the sum of all off-path branch costs along the intervening ordinary path. A connected shore must include that path, and the off-path branches can be optimized independently. Comparing readings to fixed loose tips beyond opposite ends of the path therefore distinguishes different weights and any two positions separated by a positive off-path cost. Positions still confusable must have the same weight in a single chain of bundles whose intermediate junctions have two ordinary neighbors and no loose names.

Let \(c\) be the minimum of the bundle weights on a maximal such chain and the two costs of all outside branches at its endpoints. If weight \(h\) occurs at two or more positions, a pair of names from one of those bundles reads \(h+c\). A pair from two different such bundles reads \(2h\). Thus when \(c<h\) a complete bundle can move only as a whole. Any permutation of whole equal-size bundles in the chain preserves \(C\), since the sum of the intervening junction rows is the union of two chosen bundles. When \(c=h\), all names at these equal-weight positions have the same entries to outside names and to other names within this group. The only obstruction occurs if at least two of these bundles have size \(h\ge2\) and neither has a live holder. Swapping one literal copy between them then preserves every singleton and pair entry while splitting an old bundle in the image of a junction equation. No cut can split that bundle. This is an exact criterion, including the cases where the growing end pins some of the bundles. ## 3. An unbounded class with exact order two at the first reached stage

Start with a finite simple tree of at least three junctions, assigning \(\max(0,3-\deg v)\) loose names at \(v\). Suppose the first round is available and every multi-parent group uses only leaves. These conditions can be tested from the original tree in linear time. In a tree every clique of at least three live vertices pairwise two steps apart has a common middle vertex, so first-round multi-parent groups are the individual pairs of live neighbors of each site. Availability limits the number of joined leaf neighbors to two or three. A degree-two site in this class has only a unary birth.

For two leaves at a holder, the first round produces a pair junction and a unary junction on either side. The original spokes are individually distinguished by their pair readings to fixed names on those unary junctions. Each spoke gives three on its own side and more than three on the other side. For three leaves, the three pair junctions and the old leaves alternate on a six-cycle. Each spoke reads three precisely at its two incident cycle names. Old names outside the group treat the corresponding probes equally by local symmetry. Thus each old spoke at a joined group is fixed. The isolated three-leaf star also has this property. Its eight-vertex wired graph at age one is cubic and bipartite, and its only three-name bonds are individual stars.

Compress each identified group to unit exterior outlets at its old holder. Two or three distinction-disjoint routes through the group attain its separating cost, with every identified spoke on a minimum cut. A singly attached old leaf retains its old ordinary link and has two probed outlets via its unary children. A degree-two unary parent has one probed outlet. For a core name and a marked outlet, a bond crossing both pieces separates their two terminals, and the connected shores in the two pieces glue independently. These marked costs are therefore precisely the costs on the compressed tree. Its ordinary degrees \(d(v)\) are all at least three. For distinct mark positions \(e,f\), subtracting two from their reading gives \(\sum_{v\text{ between }e,f}(d(v)-2)\). Two probes at opposite ends distinguish every remaining core position. All names are fixed by a pair isometry.

There is always a multiple-leaf group in this class. Without any live candidates a degree-at-least-three vertex would have at most one live neighbor, forcing at least two neighbors of degree at least three. This cannot hold on a nonempty induced forest. A path also has live candidates. Inside a multiple-leaf group two old spokes have equal singleton minima. Their exchange fails on a spoke-to-probe pair of size three. For the wrong pairing a permanent old four-cycle excluding the probe forces at least one additional old distinction. Any putative future bond of size at most three on the wrong pair would therefore be wholly old, where its support was already excluded. The successful old three-star persists. This proves strict least order two, even with only a subsequent pair as target. The local newborn demands give an available second round, including the implied closing round of the three-leaf star. ## 4. A structural failure at order two

Let the exhausted holders \(A,B,C,D\) have five unit meetings \(AC,AD,BC,BD,CD\). Assume \(C,D\) have no others and the connected remaining graph has \(A,B\) separating cost one. Exchange \(AC\) and \(BD\), fixing every other name. The five internal singleton minima are three. Each internal pair minimum is three except for the two opposite spoke pairs, which read four. The bound of three at a pole follows by putting both middle holders at the other pole and using a unit cut in the remainder. Separating an opposite spoke pair costs at least four and putting \(C,D\) alone together attains it. The middle stars attain three for their incident pairs. These values respect the exchange.

For a mixed pair including one marked name in the remainder, any containing bond also marked internally separates \(A,B\), since the pieces share only these two holders. On a separating cut orient the pole values as zero and one. Replace the levels of \(C,D\) by \(1-D,1-C\). This interchanges the roles of \(AC,BD\), preserves the number of internal crossings and leaves all remainder crossings fixed. Each middle holder joins the pole on its new shore, and the remainder of each shore stays connected to its own pole. The transformation is reversible, including when the requisite bond is absent. Thus the full pair reading matches. The image of the old row \(AC,BC,CD\) contains all three links of the \(B,C,D\) cycle. No cut has odd intersection with a cycle. The span changes, establishing a strict lower bound of three at any such stage with a successor. [Full marked-cut proof](../../../research/BOND-READING-WEIGHTINGS-20261119-001/short_bonds.md#why-the-remaining-stages-need-order-three)

On the 12 identified sampled shapes with this obstruction, preserving the weight-three bonds alongside the marked lower-order data sufficed. In six shapes recovered rows spanned \(C\). In the other six every lifted generator compatible with the weight-three relation preserved \(C\). These finite upper bounds, together with the all-size lower bound on this motif, settle those 27 observations at order three. The theorem supplies no uniform upper bound of three on arbitrary presentations containing the motif. A different archived stage preserves triples and changes an equation.
