# Carrying a sum of pair products through a degree-two proof

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

Let \(p_i=x_i y_i\) on \(m\) disjoint pairs of original Boolean input variables. Suppose a later part of a refutation in the named, multiset \(\mathrm{Res}(\mathrm{PC}_2/\mathbb F_2)\) calculus receives the joint quantities
\[
 A=\prod_{i=1}^m p_i,\qquad U=\sum_{i=1}^m p_i .
\]
It can carry \(U\) directly as the quadratic polynomial \(Q=\sum_i x_i y_i\), alongside a correctly declared name for \(A\). For \(m\ge2\), folding all \(x\)'s and then all \(y\)'s into the running product declares no individual \(x_i y_i\). This gives both quantities on the same original assignment. The possible pairs of values \((A,U)\) are \( (0,0),(0,1),(1,m\bmod 2)\). To realize the first two take respectively zero or one true pair product. To realize the third take every pair product true.

In a supplied proof suffix, this replacement gives a polynomial-size degree-two replay whenever every required old premise has a derived image, each displayed polynomial image respects the formal degree cap, and each retained product declaration has two affine image factors. These conditions can be checked on the actual rows, including the selected occurrences and explicit multipliers. The Boolean disjunction for \(Q\) has a derivation from the original input Boolean axioms in \(O(m+1)\) nodes. Unrestricted additive use of the aggregate is allowed by the degree cap. A disjunction may carry \([Q=0]\) alongside a deduction on an entirely different atom.

## Nearest known result

Pang's proof of Fact 7.4 derives the Boolean alternative for a monomial in a bounded-degree calculus. The product-name predecessor explicitly constructs the Boolean alternative for \(f+g\) and \(fg\) from those of \(f,g\). It also supplies the multiset rule replay under an extra common disjunction. Here those operations are applied to a sum of independent pair products with a simultaneous retained AND. The additional issues are the exact degree test for an XOR port, actual transferred premises at the cut, and a strict refutation with both aggregate pivots. ## The Boolean alternative for the expression

Write \([f]\) for \(f=0\) and \(E_f=[f]\lor[f+1]\). From \(E_f\) and \(E_g\), for \(i,j\in\{0,1\}\) select \([f+i]\) and \([g+j]\). To get the sum choice, add the selected atoms. To get the product choice for two original variables, multiply the first selected atom by \(g\) and add \(i\) times the second selected atom. The resulting selected polynomial is \(f+g+i+j\) or \(fg+ij\). The two opposite premises remain as side disjuncts. Weaken to both alternatives for the result, together with those two side disjuncts. Cut opposite values of \(j\), then opposite values of \(i\), contracting the duplicated common side occurrences. This gives \(E_{f+g}\) or \(E_{fg}\). The sum construction performs only additions, weakening, cuts and contractions, even when the inputs are already quadratic.

Make \(E_{x_i y_i}\) once per original pair, then make the Boolean disjunction for their successive sums. Every multiplication in this derivation has degree at most two. On a fresh DAG and \(m\ge1\), the implementation charges 28 nodes per disjoint pair, including its two variable axioms, and 22 per addition. Its total is \(50m-22\). For the empty sum the true axiom and a weakening suffice. Repeated written sums of up to \(m\) terms give polynomial encoding length. The same construction works for every explicitly listed formal quadratic \(f\), including squares. A constant term exchanges its two alternatives.

## Exact formal degree test

Assume \(m\ge1\). Consider a reduced degree-two polynomial whose variables are the port \(U\) and symbols \(Z\) carried identically as formal variables in the replacement, including any retained AND name. Uniquely write it as
\[
 P=P_0(Z)+U\bigl(b+L(Z)\bigr)+cU^2,
\]
where \(L\) is homogeneous linear and \(b,c\) are scalars. Under \(U\mapsto Q\), the only possible homogeneous degree-four part is \(cQ^2\). The formal polynomial ring is an integral domain, and \(Q\ne0\), so the cap first requires \(c=0\). The degree-three part is then \(QL\), forcing \(L=0\). Conversely \(P_0+bQ\) has degree at most two. Thus the aggregate can occur only as an additive occurrence in a reduced atom at this particular interface. For \(m=0\) its image is zero.

This test concerns the symbols that actually remain. Where aliases are also substituted, expand and collect the *whole* image before testing it. For instance a defining bridge \(U=(\sum_i p_i)\cdot1\) supplies the equation \([U+\sum_i p_i]\). Multiplying it by \(z\) is a legal old rule whose selected image is zero after both substitutions. It replays as multiplication of zero by \(z\). A new retained declaration with factors \(U+\sum_i p_i,z\) likewise replays with affine factors \(0,z\).

Every explicit multiplier is a guarded-calculus inscription subject to the cap, even if the selected atom is zero. For two nonzero formal polynomial images their product degree is the sum of their degrees. An image \(Q\) can therefore multiply a scalar, and multiplying a positive-degree image by \(Q\) exceeds the cap. The old product \(Uz\) with fresh \(z\) becomes genuinely cubic. The old square \(U^2\) becomes \(\sum_i x_i^2y_i^2\), of degree four. Boolean agreement on all inputs does not identify either formal inscription with a lower-degree one. Even \(AU\), which as a Boolean function equals \((m\bmod2)A\), has the cubic formal image \(A Q\) when \(A\) is a retained variable. The retained-declaration rule separately requires affine factors, so writing a new declaration with factors \(Q,0\) fails that rule although its product vanishes. A proof that replaces such an inference by other derived rows poses a different test.

## Proof replay with certified boundary premises

The original proof and the replacement prefix use the same original variables and CNF. Check the entire old proof and each deduction in the new prefix. Match the designated input-pair declarations and, when used, the declaration \(U=(\sum p_i)\cdot1\) up to factor order. The more general suffix interface accepts the supplied current images for old names. A claimed interpretation for a retained name, such as the crossed product for \(A\), comes from its actual prefix definitions. The generic suffix procedure on its own proves a syntactic substitution theorem for the supplied map.

For every old parent before the cut referenced later, require a new derived line with exactly the image multiset. An original axiom, the true equation, or a Boolean alternative can be obtained from its own genuine source. A singleton with zero polynomial can be obtained from the true axiom. For example the mapped XOR definition becomes \([0]\). Other old defining equations or ordinary facts require real replacement derivations if cited. If neither the expression for a used name nor the required line is available, the check fails. Correct values of the two aggregate ports on every input alone do not grant every old line. For example the old two-pair equation \([A+p_1p_2]\) has a degree-four image if its pair factors are substituted directly. A new derivation of a particular boundary consequence can still suffice.

A common multiset \(H\) of extra degree-two side disjuncts may accompany every output line. A premise already carrying \(H\) can be used directly, and a bare derived premise can be weakened by it. Proceed in old order. A genuine CNF or true axiom is reused. A Boolean axiom on an imaged variable uses the construction above. New product declarations remain acyclic and take the next fresh output variable after their two mapped factors pass the affine test. For ordinary rows, use the same selected occurrences, coefficients and imaged multipliers. Polynomial substitution commutes with addition and multiplication. Weakening, contraction and false-constant deletion preserve multiplicities under the substitution, including collisions of previously different polynomials. A binary step puts two copies of \(H\) into the result, so contract exactly one extra copy of each occurrence. The replayer compares each line with the exact image plus \(H\), and the independent kernel validates every output inference. The last line is \(H\), and an empty \(H\) gives a refutation of the original CNF.

The direct suffix test and construction take polynomial time in the explicit old proof, new prefix, image list, supplied imports and \(H\). Each old monomial has at most two factors. If each image has at most \(M\) monomials, each such expansion has at most \(M^2\) terms before collection. The Boolean constructions are linear in the term counts of their requested forms, with their results reusable. Each ordinary row uses one primitive step and at most \(|H|\) extra contractions. This estimate charges the supplied replacement prefix. The construction supplies no general algorithm to find arbitrary missing prefix derivations. ## A strict two-pair rewrite

The saved refutation has input pairs \((x_1,y_1),(x_2,y_2)\) with coordinate IDs \((1,3),(2,4)\). The old declarations compute both products, their AND, and their XOR. The new declarations compute the AND by \(((x_1x_2)y_1)y_2\) and the XOR is written \(x_1y_1+x_2y_2\). Kept proof names have local IDs in their own certificate. The new chain has three product names and none computes either original pair product.

The CNF forces \(x_2,y_1,y_2\) true by four strict ternary clauses per unit, using two private padding variables. With original switch \(s\), for each \(H\in\{s,\neg s\}\) it supplies \(H\lor x_1\) and \(H\lor\neg x_1\), each expanded into two strict clauses with opposite private padding literals. Ordinary resolution derives the stated units and binary clauses. With side literal \(s\), the old and crossed chains each derive \(s\lor[A]\) and \(s\lor[A+1]\). With side literal \(\neg s\), the old pair-name derivation yields both conditional values of \(U\), and the new derivation yields the corresponding conditional values of \(Q\). For each product, \([f+i]\) multiplied by \(g\), followed by adding \(i\) times \([g+j]\), derives \([fg+ij]\) with the originating side disjuncts. Adding the definition moves this atom to a named product where one exists. Addition alone combines the pair values to obtain \(Q\). This supplies all four aggregate facts directly from the original clauses.

The 78-node derivation of \(E_Q\) then replaces a one-node Boolean default, for a net increase of 70 nodes. ## Checks and scope

The checker reconstructs both saved proofs and validates their actual rules. It independently evaluates every old and new deduction on all 65,536 assignments to the 16 original variables, including the private padding and unused original coordinate. Additional fixtures with three, four and five pairs check both port values on their full pair-input cubes. Complementary quadratic guards merge to an independently checked 316-node refutation, and a duplicate quadratic guard survives twice with its required multiplicity. Controls reject a wrong or missing boundary derivation, a forged source axiom, a leaked old AND equation, a cubic used on a path to the final contradiction, a concealed excessive multiplier of a zero atom, a formal square, and an inadmissible retained factor. One control with multiple substituted aliases verifies an actual high-term cancellation. The symbolic result is uniform.
