## What the training observations identify

For each of the 16 bins let *h* be its count on one image. The fixed padded sensor gives a total of 81. A proposed group reads the sum *z* of the counts in its bins. Its complement reads 81 − *z*, and a fitted class mean changes from *m* to 81 − *m*. Both absolute discrepancies stay exactly the same, including ties. There are 32,767 unordered partitions into two nonempty groups once this exact duplication is removed. This rule can distinguish a learned group only up to complementation.

Actual ambiguity can be larger. Suppose bin *a* is included and *b* excluded, and their columns are identical on every training row. Exchanging them preserves every fit, within-class variance, subset size and stability under resampling the training rows. The exchange can change the decision on a new image that separates those two bins. When both columns are always zero the training evidence gives no basis for their membership. A size preference or tie policy can settle a choice, and that choice must be credited to its source.
