# Independent referee report on parity's shortage
**Confirmed with fixes. Two Blocks, one on the explanation for zero shortages and one on the parity law used after a far addition. The recorded shortage and first-hearing counts stand.**

A joined start can make an implied birth that owes joins and receives nothing. One such birth owed eight. When that birth leaves the roster, eight surviving members still owe their original demand and three owe one less. All can pay. In the earlier referee's example under the ordinary count and the oldest eligible local take, four survivors each owe three while holding one.

**What “short” measures.** Before each completed round of the unchanged growth, the reader considers knots holding at least one usable loose distinction. For each member made after the start, it counts the groups that member owes before cancellation and compares that count with its usable loose distinctions. The inequality is strict. A zero-funded implied birth can occur in the provisional demand roster and be missing from the subsequent live roster. The shortage column counts a short surviving member. It cannot by itself count implied births, diagnose their demand, or describe a changed growth. The reader also stops before testing the state left by its last completed round. Only two unchanged histories ended with an owed implied birth. Directly checking both final rosters found zero short made members. In the others, each funded birth can pay its demand and removing a zero-demand birth cannot change a group. On the earlier eight-knot positive control, cutting the read after round 1 gives zero, and including round 2 gives four. Switching only to not-all-equal, or switching off the take under parity, gives zero in that control. ## Fixes

1. **Block — a joined start makes an implied birth that owes joins.** Section C says “The referee's implied-birth shortage needs a start where a birth is implied while owed a join; none of these joined starts makes one in the rounds read.” The full shortage was absent. Its stated prerequisite was present. The four-knot receiver had five shared pairs and loose counts of 0, 1, 2 and 2. The stub sender was attached at the fourth knot. Under the cross-part take, the round-3 birth with 15 inputs was implied, owed eight joins and received zero. The sum modulo two of all ten starting equations and all six first-round equations gives exactly those 15 places. The rules on them allow exactly the 16,384 even words. Forgetting any one distinction allows all 16,384 remaining words. Every knot in that growth passed its own projection check through round 3. Of the groups containing the implied birth, five groups of four became groups of three and three pairs disappeared. Nobody's owing rose. In the earlier eight-knot witness, removing the implied member turned one group of five into six pairs among four survivors. Each then owed three while holding one. To reproduce the shortage, losing the implied member must leave some survivor owing more than it holds. [Exact sentence in the README](../README.md#what-was-found-c) · [Both growths and projection checks](condition.json) · [Earlier referee's Block](../referee-R1/report.md)

   There is a separate round-1 example with a three-knot receiver and a one-knot sender. An implied birth with 12 places owed ten joins. All ten incident pairs disappeared. Those 12 places satisfy four independent parity equations, including the parent's equation on three of them, so this particular birth fails the knot test. The 15-place example above passes that test. With **no take**, an owed implied birth is impossible in these unchanged joined starts. All 500 starting preparations were checked for connectivity through sharings and a maximum of two holders per distinction. With no take, every loose distinction used for a birth acquires exactly one further holder and each fresh one begins with one holder. For their shared distinctions to cancel in a sum of old parity equations, the two holders must be taken together. Because the start is connected by sharings and every birth shares with its parents, a nonempty sum giving only loose distinctions must use every old knot. A birth taking all loose distinctions is the only birth, and no older member retains a loose distinction. Its demand is zero. A take can give a shared distinction more holders, invalidating this argument. [Reconstruction](condition.py) · [Closing rule, §2 step 8](../../PARTS-MEET-AGAIN-20261124-001/definitions.md)

2. **Block — the changed growths retain the equation from before the addition.** Section C says “`parity.py` runs B's census (the same 100 receivers and five senders) under the ordinary count with the parity knot law, with no take and with each recorded take.” That describes the unchanged runs. In a changed run, the addition updates the member's places and holdings, while the next parity test still uses its old equation. The member already held a loose distinction. Only its edited equation contains either that distinction or the new one, so every actual sum of equations contains both or neither. The cached old equation contains exactly one. In the earlier eight-knot control, adding to one parent changes the correct funding of the join on distinctions 10, 11 and 2 from zero to one. The cached equations still give zero.
