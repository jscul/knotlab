# Independent calculations on the whole newborn table and the higher-donor clock

## A single law for all eight newborn settings

Write $m$ for the ordinary probability that the three loose values are mixed and $u$ for the probability of three plus answers when everybody asks the change question. Draw a complete triple of change answers with exactly its measured distribution. If all three answers are plus, choose three mixed ordinary values with probability $m/u$ and assign equal mass to their six possible words. In every other case, including the unused share of the plus triple, give all readers the same fair ordinary bit. These six values are assigned before anyone chooses a setting. Here $u>0$.

For a not-all-equal newborn, this is a genuine probability assignment. In a fixed exterior sector the six mixed ordinary triples have amplitude $C$, while the homogeneous triples have amplitudes $aC,bC$. Every old completion can forbid at most one homogeneous triple, so $a+b\geq1$. The unnormalized mass of the mixed ordinary answers is $6C^2$. The all-plus change mass is $(6+a+b)^2 C^2/8\geq 49 C^2/8$. Adding these masses with their true normalizers gives $u\geq m$ both with spent words retained and with their amplitudes added. A selected exterior need not have ordinary complement symmetry. In the full preparation, flipping every ordinary bit pairs it with its complement at the same weight and preserves each change answer. This is where leaving the exterior unread enters the proof.

With only change questions the construction reproduces the sampled triple. With one ordinary question, the fair ordinary complement makes either answer equally frequent for each pair of changes, as in the measured table. With two ordinary questions, unequal answers force the third change answer to be plus in the actual preparation: the two amplitudes along that remaining bit are equal in every sector. Among the mixed ordinary words two thirds disagree on the given pair. Their total probability is therefore $2m/3$ and all of it belongs to a plus change, as in the construction. Complement fixes the division between 01 and 10 and between 00 and 11. The complete change marginal fixes the remaining equal-pair masses. With three ordinary questions, complement and permutation symmetry give equal weights to each homogeneous word and equal weights to the six mixed words. This covers all 64 joint cells, including the forced zeros.

For parity, let $L$ be the subspace of allowed full binary words, or its projection onto all loose names on the erased preparation. The projection has constant fibers. Sample the ordinary word uniformly in $L$ and independently sample the change word uniformly in its binary orthogonal complement. For disjoint sets of queried ordinary and change bits, the signed moment is one precisely when the ordinary mask belongs to the complement and the change mask belongs to $L$. Otherwise it vanishes. The same moment follows from the constant amplitude on $L$. These moments determine every complete outcome. In particular the two parity readings can use different distributions of change answers. The first funded round from one isolated knot gives one unary child per original place. Every child has one paid name and, at widths four and five, respectively three or four fresh names. Direct word counting on the four-place source and a separate contraction over all the other children's completions give the same reduced matrices. At width four the retained not-all-equal comparison has $m=6/7,u=7/8$. The erased comparison gives $m=161538/175063,u=188461/200072$ and succeeds on $25009/38416$ full launches. At width five the clean launch probability is $2136691/2700000$. Under parity the corresponding two launch probabilities are $1/16$ and $1/32$. Refusing a clean outcome does not assign any reader an invented sign. Representing a common explanation as a probability distribution on predetermined answers is established mathematics. Pitowsky and Svozil used it to characterize admissible correlation tables. The result here is the explicit distribution forced to be available by ordinary newborn holdings together with these supplied preparations and questions. It does not construct a native eraser or signed reader. [Pitowsky and Svozil, 2001](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0011060)

## What changes if the paid bit is announced

On each first newborn of the four-place not-all-equal source, fix the paid parent bit at a named value before any setting is chosen. This ordinary announcement has probability 1/2 for either value. With the spent values retained, one homogeneous triple of fresh bits is then forbidden. The seven other triples have equal positive amplitudes. Holding the paid bit unread instead gives the equal mixture of these two preparations.

Let v be the forbidden homogeneous value. For any six predetermined answers, if at least two change answers are minus, then either all three ordinary answers equal v, or some reader with a minus change has at least one of the other two ordinary answers different from v. The indicator of the left event is bounded by the sum of the four indicators on the right. Checking all 64 lists gives maximum difference zero. The two conditioned measured tables exceed it by exactly **1/14**, although their earlier signed score remains 12/7. This independently confirms a real effect of disclosing a physical paid name. It leaves the proved unread-exterior result intact. [Exact indicator and full tables](newborn_check.py)

An accessible alternative uses three sibling children of that same source. If all nine of their loose bits are zero, the three corresponding paid bits must be one. The original not-all-equal law then forces the focal child's paid bit to zero. Reversing every bit gives the opposite orientation. The seven surviving fresh words have one past each. For each of these two named announcements the ordinary probability is 1/4802, the probability conditional on the clean herald is 1/50018, and the joint probability with the clean herald is 1/76832 per initial launch. Either named orientation gives the same 1/14 violation. Pooling the two without identifying which occurred restores a common explanation.
