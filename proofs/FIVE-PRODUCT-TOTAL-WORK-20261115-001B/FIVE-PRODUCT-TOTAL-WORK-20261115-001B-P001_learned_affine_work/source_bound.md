## A lower bound with any number of learned affine literals

Only paid citations may share an already derived proof. The main proof, together with one derivation for each distinct stated lemma, is a forest of ordinary proof trees when citation references are removed. In a component, a question has two continuations; a use or nonclosing local check has one. Claims are checked before use. A cited positive literal restricts the current affine guard. A discharged affine literal under its assumptions is proved by entering the counter-assumption and deriving a conflict.

Every paid check of a source \(D\) in an affine guard \(C\) can be read as the axiom
\(C\Rightarrow\operatorname{aff}(C\cap D)\). The paid split, linear implications, discharge, and guarded citation rules preserve truth on any assignment satisfying all the source axioms the certificate used. An inconsistent proof therefore has to cover every assignment by at least one violated source axiom. An assignment of the rich evaluator can violate only a plain-source axiom, and conversely.

For the plain source project \(C\) to \(W\) on \((x,s)\); the two private bits are free. Each fiber of this affine projection in \(C\) has the same direction space. When \(s\) is free in \(W\), the plain graph spans \(W\) unless the restricted \(Q\) is affine. In the latter case the plain graph is affine and disjoint from the rich graph on an input domain of dimension at most three. When \(s=\ell(x)\) is pinned in \(W\), the opposite level \(Q+\ell=1\) either spans its input projection or lies in a proper affine hyperplane. In the latter case its indicator, a Boolean quadratic, is zero or a product of two affine factors. This follows by setting the constant hyperplane coordinate to zero: every surviving monomial contains that coordinate. Its one-set is affine and disjoint from the rich rows, and is empty or has density at least a quarter. A zero level of the defect identically gives an isotropic source domain of dimension at most three; in the rank-at-most-two case the input projection has dimension at most four by the rank restriction inequality. Each effective plain check therefore eliminates ALL rich points in its current guard, at most twelve. There are 64 rich rows, hence at least six distinct plain checks.

Recode the rich graph as three independent triples \((y_i,w_i,t_i)\) with \(t_i=y_iw_i\). A plain row has an odd number of bad triples, meaning \(t_i\ne y_iw_i\). Test the 192 plain rows with exactly one bad triple, 64 of each position. For every affine guard of codimension one or two in the nine visible dimensions, enumerate the rich points, compute their affine span, and count these plain rows in the guard and outside that span. The program checks 174,762 guards. The maximum is 48. The 372 maximizing guards have counts \((48,0,0)\) or \((24,24,0)\), up to permutation of the positions. A codimension-zero guard supplies no rich fact. Codimension at least four gives at most 32 visible rows outright.

For codimension three the guard has 64 visible rows. If a vertical direction flips the parity of the three outputs, at most 32 rows can be plain. Otherwise the parity of the outputs is fixed as an affine function of the \(k\) input coordinates in the projection, where \(4\le k\le6\). A given input has at most \(2^{6-k}\) rows in the guard and at most three with a single error. On all affine domains of dimensions \(4,5,6\), the maximum numbers of inputs with either value of \(Q+\ell\) are respectively 12, 20, and 36; the replay independently enumerates those domains and affine functions. Thus there are at most \(\max(3\cdot12,2\cdot20,36)=40\) single-error rows in this case. No rich check excludes more than 48. Four could cover 192 only by four disjoint maximizers. Each maximizing contribution to one error position is divisible by 24 and the required count 64 is not. A successful proof uses at least five distinct rich checks. Inside each component, guards can only shrink along an ordinary continuation. After a plain check has excluded all raw rich rows within its affine guard, no descendant in that component can exclude any further raw rich row. Globally effective plain checks thus form an antichain within each component. The six required ones need six different leaves among all components. With \(q\) paid questions and \(m\) distinct reachable learned lemmas the forest has \(q+m+1\) leaves. If one of the named results is genuinely reused, the number of citation charges \(c\) is at least \(m+1\). This gives \(q+c\ge6\). Every such proof with genuine reuse costs at least \(5+6+6=17\). If useless or unreachable stored lemmas occur, discard them first. This lower allows multiple named intermediate affine literals and both evaluators' contributions; it does not assume original rich rows survive to a terminal proof. The 23-unit tree supplies a larger explicit upper bound when reuse is optional.
