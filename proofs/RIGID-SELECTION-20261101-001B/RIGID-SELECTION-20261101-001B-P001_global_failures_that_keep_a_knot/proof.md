# Global failures that keep a knot

Status: Proved

Ticket `RIGID-SELECTION-20261101-001B`, 2026-11-01.

## Question

- **What ticket A proved.** Selection is rigid over fields, under blurring, for S3 and D_p, and for simple three-value algebras.
  Over Z_(p^k) the criterion leaves room: after a line is removed, a non-cyclic solution group might carry a knot.
- **The question.** Do the coset laws of Z_(p^k), k ≥ 2, have global failures that keep a knot? These laws compose like cycles, so a
  yes means composing like cycles does not force rigid selection.

## Dependencies

- **`RIGID-SELECTION-20261101-001A`.** The criterion, the order of K_L, cyclic groups give ties, and `zlocal.py`.
- **`MUTUAL-SELECTION-20261031-001Z`.** Ticket C's `census.json`: all 60 connected cubic bipartite graphs with at most 16 vertices.

## Setting

- **Systems and terms.** Systems, global failure and rigidity are as in ticket A.
- **Signed Z4 at k places.** On every line the equation is x_a ± x_b ± … = c_L (mod 4), with one sign per incidence. These relations
  are cosets of subgroups of Z4^k, so they belong to the coset law of Z4. That law composes like cycles by KNOT-CIRCUITS Theorem 1:
  every subgroup of Z4 has a unique maximal subgroup.
- **Substrates.** A substrate is an incidence graph with one side read as lines. A graph and its dual (the other side read as lines)
  give the same substrate up to isomorphism exactly when some automorphism of the graph swaps its sides.

## Theorem

1. **Four places.** The 6-point substrate whose lines are the complements of the edges of a hexagon carries a signed Z4 system that
   fails globally. After removing any one of its 6 lines, the 8 remaining solutions have knots of three variables.
2. **Three places.**
   - **An example.** A signed Z4 system on a 9-point cubic substrate fails globally. After removing one of its lines (line 0), the 8
     remaining solutions have knots.
   - **The census.** On all 73 connected cubic substrates with at most 8 points, under every signing class, the global failures of
     signed Z4 are rigid. So 9 points is the minimum for a cubic signed Z4 space that keeps a knot.
3. **Unit weights.** Unit-weighted Z8, Z9, Z25 and Z27 laws fail globally and keep a knot on many census substrates (sampled).
4. **The group behind a kept knot.** Every removal found that keeps a knot leaves a non-cyclic solution group, as ticket A requires.

**Consequence.** Composing like cycles does not force rigid selection. The coset laws of Z_(p^k), k ≥ 2, compose like cycles and
have globally failing spaces that keep emergence inside.

## Proof

**Item 1.**
- **The substrate.** It has points 0–5 and the hexagon 0–4–2–3–5–1–0. Each line omits the two ends of one hexagon edge, so the
  substrate is 4-regular and 4-uniform.
- **The system.** Lines and signs as in `check.py` (−1 is written 3), with right-hand side c = 2e₀.
- **Why it fails globally.**
  - The cokernel is M ≅ Z2 ⊕ Z4.
  - Every class g_L has order 4, and all share the socle point (0, 2), which is [c].
  - By ticket A's criterion, [c] is a nonzero element of every ⟨g_L⟩, so the failure is global.
- **The solutions after a removal.**
  - Since every g_L has full order, ann(g_L) = 0 and K_L = ker A for every L: one group of order 8 = |M|.
  - It is generated by u = (0, 1, 2, 1, 1, 0), of order 4, and v = (2, 0, 2, 0, 0, 2), of order 2.
- **A knot.** On x = au + bv the variables 0, 1, 2 are (2b, a, 2a + 2b).
  - **Constrained as a whole.** They satisfy x₂/2 ≡ x₀/2 + x₁ (mod 2): the bit of x₀, the bit of x₂ and the last bit of x₁ have even
    parity. That leaves 8 of the 2 · 4 · 2 = 16 combinations of ranges.
  - **Every pair is free.** Each pair takes all combinations of its ranges.
  - **So a knot.** {0, 1, 2} is a circuit of three variables.
  - **The others.** The same holds for {0,2,3}, {0,2,4}, {1,2,5}, {2,3,5} and {2,4,5}: each has two two-valued variables and one
    four-valued one.
- **Verification.** Everything above is also checked by brute force over Z4^6.

**Item 2.**
- **The example.** It is checked by brute force over Z4^9.
  - The cokernel is Z4. The classes g_L are 2 on lines 0, 6, 8 and units elsewhere, and [c] = 2.
  - After removing line 0 the group K_0 has order 4 · 2 = 8 and is not cyclic. The 8 solutions carry ten knots: eight with one
    four-valued and two two-valued variables, and two parity knots among three two-valued variables.
  - Removing line 6 or 8 also gives a non-cyclic group of order 8, but no knot. The other six removals give cyclic groups of order 4,
    hence only ties.
- **The census, read both ways.**
  - The census lists incidence graphs. 13 of the 60 are not isomorphic to their duals, so the substrates number 73. The check confirms
    by canonical forms that these 73 are pairwise non-isomorphic.
- **Every signing class is covered.**
  - Switching a point (x ↦ −x) or a line (negating its equation) maps solutions bijectively. So it suffices to fix the signs on a
    spanning tree of the incidence graph to +1.
  - Whether a failure keeps a knot depends only on A and L. The solutions after a removal are a coset x₀ + K_L, and translation
    preserves circuits. So one global class per signing settles it.

**Items 3 and 4.**
- **Sampling.** These are sampled: 30 random unit weightings per simple substrate and ring.
- **Item 4.** Ticket A's item 2 predicts it (a cyclic group leaves only ties), and every kept knot found confirms it.

**Consequence.** Signed Z4 equations belong to a law that composes like cycles, and items 1 and 2 give global failures of that law
that keep a knot. ∎

## What this does not establish

- **Which knots can be kept.** That is ticket D. The kept knots here have at most one variable taking all values and hide one digit;
  D proves this is forced.
- **Sampled counts.** The unit-weighted counts are samples. Only the signed Z4 census is exhaustive.
- **The two-valued knot.** By KNOT-CIRCUITS Theorem 2 it does not compose like cycles, so these examples say nothing about it
  directly.

## Novelty

- **Classical.** Minimally inconsistent linear systems over fields.
- **New here.** The examples over Z4 that keep a circuit, and the minimum of 9 points at three places. No source was checked for
  them; the literature comparison is in ticket D.
