# An attachment at a different small holder

Consider any correspondence of the pair table through bond distance five on a live one-spent source. Write \(K=D+1\). The previous inverse theorem fixes all small physical sites and, in the sole-six-place case, the pair \(F=\{a,b\}\) loose at the six-place site \(H\). These names designate objects in the proof. The reader receives no marked site or formation order. The ordinary graph \(X\) of recovered small sites is connected and bipartite. Four distinct small holders meet \(H\). An added attachment \(y\) is presently loose at its small holder \(v\). Retaining even one old attachment forces \(v\) to have the color opposite \(H\) in the source. The predecessor theorem rules out disjoint four-sets. ## A different small holder forces a parent-closed six-bit boundary

The rival star gives \(K(a,y)\le6\). The minimum through \(a\) is six, so some source bond through \(a,y\) has size six. Its ordinary shore contains \(H,v\). In the one-for-one exchange, if the holder of \(y\) differs from that of the removed attachment, it must also differ from all three retained holders. Otherwise two distinctions would be shared by the same pair of ordinary sites. Thus \(v\) is not adjacent to \(H\) in the source.

**Claim.** Any six-bit shore containing \(H,v\) under these conditions contains every parent of each of its sites. This uses only the formation law, its two surviving loose bits at \(H\), the loose \(y\) and the opposite colors of the two nonadjacent holders.

A shore on only those two sites has boundary at least nine. In a larger shore the latest three sites produce at least six outputs and can use at most two of them internally. If \(H\) precedes the latest three, its two loose bits add to the four latest exports and no incoming bit fits. If \(H\) is among the latest three, its third output raises their export bound to five. An entering bit would force exactly five exports, all born at the latest three. Therefore \(v\) is one of these sites. The latest three produce at least seven outputs, so exactly two ordinary meetings among them are necessary. Their ordinary graph is a three-site path. Any two of opposite colors on this path must meet. This contradicts the stipulated nonadjacency of \(H,v\). This proves the claim.

This deduction also holds for any number of changed attachments, as soon as some newly proposed holder has the opposite color and did not meet \(H\). It is a source-side implication of the clipped readings. A short boundary alone does not imply that the entire pair table is preserved.

## Why the surviving formation records allow no new holder

The closed search over a selected parent-closed shore includes both color choices, the current loose counts and the pairs and triples of holders that have a witness. Requiring a live menu and a six-boundary through a loose bit opposite \(H\) leaves only a ternary spending child \(C\), in the binary-third, two-shrink family. The terminal-child case already has its physical supports recovered against unrestricted rivals. In the other surviving case the final three sites are \(H,C,D\): \(C\) takes one output from \(H\), and \(D\) takes one output from \(C\). Both take three parents and make two outputs. The old prefix before \(H\) has eight loose bits. These formation restrictions are the exhaustively checked input to the following argument. Exactly seven old distinctions have been spent, three at \(H\), two at \(C\) and two at \(D\). Denote the remaining old loose bit by \(t\). The loose distinctions are the two at \(H\), the spare output of \(C\), the two outputs of \(D\), and \(t\). Their four holders are distinct. The ordinary path \(H,C,D\) puts \(H,D\) on one color and \(C\) on the other. Three legal holders must share a color because each pair has a common neighbor. Hence the existence of a legal triple itself proves that the holder of \(t\) has the color of \(H,D\). These three holders are the only possible legal group. Either loose bit at \(H\) and either at \(D\) can represent its holder, so all four named choices with \(t\) are legal.

The only loose distinction of the opposite color is the spare at \(C\). A rival attaching it to \(H\) must release the current \(H,C\) meeting or duplicate this same pair of sites. All three of the old inputs at \(H\) consequently stay attached. The exchange uses two names held by \(C\), hence preserves all ordinary incidences and leaves \(C\) as the sole loose holder on its color. It therefore preserves all four named permissions. The count of available names excludes exchanges involving additional holders as well. This conclusion compares the live source with every reachable rival supplied by the preceding recovery. In fact the two names at \(C\) have different readings through six to each old high input. The putative last exchange is itself observable, so the physical sites agree throughout. ## What changes if the given source has an empty menu

The live-source result applies in reverse whenever the rival itself admits a one-spent source history and has a live menu. A rival from a different formation branch need not admit such an ordering.

There is a uniform bound even on the branch outside the live-source hypothesis. Every one-spent O source has at least five loose bits, since \(H\) has two surviving outputs. It has no four-bit boundary enclosing three or more ordinary sites. Such a shore exports at least four from its latest three sites, so it is parent-closed. It must omit \(H\), since every cut through \(H\) costs at least five. It contains the mandatory two unary, three-output births of its restricted history. If they are its only three-output births, the minimum-width family excludes a four-loose frontier. If it contains another such birth, all three nonseed three-output births other than \(H\) are inside. No remaining move increases the loose count because \(H\) takes and makes three. The completed source would then have at most four loose bits. This proves the exclusion. Consequently its seed and all its four-place sites transfer as independent equations to every rival. [Four-word transfer](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F-P001_finite_interval/structural.md#recovering-every-equation-through-size-four) · [Minimum-width lower bound](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J-P001_seven_to_six/family.md)

The number of sites of arity at least five in an O source is at most five. If the third three-output birth was unary, binary or ternary, denote its parent count by \(p\), and the number of shrinking joins by \(j\). There are \(10-p-j\ge5\) loose bits, and the number of sites of arity at least five is \(1+j+\mathbf 1_{p\ge2}\). At least \(n-5\) of the \(n\) source site equations are therefore forced, and none uses either loose bit of \(H\). Every rival must supply one more independent equation through such a bit. Its rank is thus at least \(n-4\), and at most \(n\) by the four-birth rank bound. The common width \(2n+5\) allows at most twelve nonseed three-output births in any unrestricted rival. When the spending child takes three parents, its five-place equation is separately forced by the row of the spent bit. It is independent of the seed and four-place sites and also avoids the two loose bits at \(H\). In this case the same bound improves to ten. For an empty source with a sole six-place site and at most two shrinking joins, all the physical low sites and the two loose bits at that six-place site still transfer by the low-span argument. In that derivation the live hypothesis supplies at most two shrinks and a six-bit minimum through the loose pair. The former holds here by hypothesis, and the latter follows independently from the resulting loose count of at least six. The ruling out of a large replacement five-site uses the legality of \(H\)'s birth, which holds in the original empty-menu source. This recovers the rival rank \(n\) and four three-output births without asserting that the rival has a current triple. If a live rival in this last subclass cannot itself be realized as a one-spent O source, then in every lawful orientation its six-place junction must be the third three-output birth. The remaining unary or binary three-output birth must be its strict descendant through its sole ordinary child. Otherwise one can expose the remaining three-output site and all its ancestors, along with the other ancestors of the six-place junction, before that junction. The two mandatory unary births still precede it. This linear extension is lawful. For each pair of parents an earliest common neighbor precedes the younger parent and has an edge to it, so it comes earlier in every linear extension. A common neighbor meeting all three parents of a legal triple cannot arise later, since it would have to join on those same three in the presence of the original junction. Thus the six-place site could then be fourth with exactly one output spent. This would place the rival in the live-source theorem. The strict-descendant alternative is a necessary condition for an exception in this subclass. The general empty-source comparison remains open beyond the existing nonshrinking and terminal-child results.
