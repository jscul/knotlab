# What one growing knot can predict

**Stage 1 result, referee pending.** Three knots at a reached growing end each hold one loose distinction. Growth joins all three. In one lawful start that birth brings no fresh distinction. Adding a single loose distinction to the center of the same four-knot start makes the birth bring one. The three knots, their loose counts, and every common-holder witness in their entire growing-end component agree before the birth. Both starts meet the proved identification conditions and both rounds are funded. Thus no finite radius gives the full parity update in that class. This meets the owner's stop condition even when stalls are set aside. [Frozen observation and stop](definitions.md)

There is an exact local result underneath the exception. A funded, nonclosing update uses radius **one** in the identified class and **two** on general lawful starts. Both bounds are attained. On a funded step without a closing birth the minimum separation between the children of two live knots at separation \(d\) is exactly \(\max(0,d-1)\). The reading used for that equality and the information needed to calculate fresh counts are different.

## The operator on the present end

Throughout, the original physical presentation is finite and connected, and each distinction has one or two holders. Its growing end may have several components, even on a four-knot source.

Write \(L\) for the live knots, \(h(u)>0\) for the number of loose distinctions at \(u\), and \(\mathcal B\) for the sets of live knots a single knot meets. Keep the downward closure of this family. A ball restricts these sets to its displayed knots. Two different knots are one end step apart if they occur in a common member. At positive age the witness is a parent. At a source the witness can be any source knot. A ball centered on one knot never visits another component.

1. Form the inclusion-maximal sets of at least three pairwise adjacent live knots that are held by no one witness. Also take each adjacent pair contained in none of those sets. These are the groups. Every adjacency belongs to some group.
2. Each \(u\) owes one distinct loose distinction to each group containing it. If any owes more than \(h(u)\), the entire round stalls. Otherwise each group gives one birth with those parents. Make one unary birth for every remaining loose distinction. Denote the resulting collection of parent sets by \(\mathcal P\), keeping separate copies of repeated singletons. Exactly \(h(u)\) births have \(u\) as a parent.
3. To compute demand, regard all births as provisionally live. Their witnessed sets are the child rosters at the old parents. Two births are adjacent exactly when their parent sets intersect. Apply the group rule to these sets and let \(D(P)\) count the next groups that contain \(P\).
4. For \(p=|P|\), a nonimplied birth receives \(\max(D(P),3-p,1)\) fresh distinctions. In a connected parity presentation the old inputs are implied precisely when they comprise **all** loose distinctions. The old equations are sums of knot stars. A sum involving only loose distinctions cannot cross a shared distinction, so its summands are all knots or none. In a funded round this implication occurs just when \(P\) is the sole birth. Then \(D(P)=0\), the birth receives zero, and the new live end is empty. Under not-all-equal the keep clause always needs a fresh distinction on these nonempty funded inputs. The inherited proof supplies an old allowed state in which the distinction taken at each parent has the same value. The two laws have identical groups and lookahead demands up to this exception. The source law used here has the archived exception to the three-place floor at an already implied last birth. Imposing that floor even at a unary or binary last birth is a different convention. The distinguishing last birth proved below has three parents and three places, so it gives the same obstruction under either convention. This proves L1 up to renaming, including the next witnessed family. Loose distinctions of one parent are interchangeable. A funded nonclosing step pays all of its successor's demand, so the first shortage of the ordinary fewest-count rule can only be at an initially supplied end. A closed stage supplies no next live knot. No round number enters these tests.

A control explains why the family of witnesses is part of the end. Three live knots with two loose distinctions each can be jointly met by one spent knot. This gives three pair births. If each pair instead has its own spent witness and there is no joint witness, the same three end distances and opening counts give one triple birth and three unary births. The latter source is realized by a six-knot alternating cycle, doubling each physical meeting so the spent witnesses have at least three places. Each original star is a parity bond because deleting any one holder leaves the rest connected to the wired exterior through another live holder. The second source fails the no-first-triple identification condition. Its first end consists of the triple birth with three loose distinctions and three unary births with two each. Each unary shares just its own earlier parent with the triple. Restarting the identified-class calculation there gives a three-ended tree of blocks, with one continuing chain from the leftover at each unary. Its growth has dimension three by formation size.

## The four-knot counterexample

Let the center \(S\) meet each of \(U_1,U_2,U_3\) once, on respective distinctions \(s_i\). Each \(U_i\) holds two further loose distinctions \(a_{ij}\), one for each \(j\ne i\). Give \(S\) no loose distinction in the first source and one, \(z\), in the second. The sources have four knots and respectively nine and ten distinctions. Every declared parity star passes K1–K3 in the *whole source*. Wire each loose distinction to the auxiliary exterior. After deleting \(S\), all leaves reach the exterior. After deleting a leaf, \(S\) reaches the exterior through either remaining leaf. Every star is therefore a minimal nonempty cut, and its projection is precisely its one even-parity relation. A nonconstant assignment at any chosen star also extends under not-all-equal. With the center specified, each leaf can choose its free ports to satisfy its relation. With a leaf specified, give another shared distinction the opposite value and complete the remaining leaves.

In both sources each pair of leaves has exactly one witness, \(S\), which also holds the three together. When \(S\) becomes live it has no common neighbor with any leaf. There is no valid first triple, no repeated pair witness, at least two loose distinctions, and sufficient openings for every group. These are the four conditions of the identification theorem. In the second source \(Q\) is alone in another component, with demand zero and a two-fresh unary floor. When all three \(y\)'s join, lookahead demand is zero in both histories. In the first, all loose distinctions have been taken by this one birth. In the second, the two loose distinctions at \(Q\) have their own births. The keep clause requires one fresh at the triple. For fate, the leaf piece is a single finite three-point block. The matching positive-age component has three knots, each with one loose distinction, and three two-member witness sets, one at each \(U_i\). The identity matches it unrooted and at all three possible roots. No finite radius, including one that exhausts the component, reveals \(Q\). The next birth through any matching root therefore refutes the full finite-radius assertion. The sole zero-fresh birth has three places, so it is an actual ternary closing birth under the registered convention.

In the second layer the one common birth changes its opening count, and two more births occur only in the larger arm. The first has no live end thereafter. A comparison of fractions over two later live ends is consequently undefined. These denominators belong to this example.

### What the two other rulers can see

The allowed relation on the \(y\)'s alone already reads the difference if the **full stage's allowed states** are projected onto them. Without the spare it is even parity, four assignments. With the spare all eight extend because the unique parity condition solely on loose distinctions also involves both fresh distinctions at \(Q\). Thus supplying that projection imports a piece of information absent from the witnessed end component.

At the first end the smaller wired presentation is the ordinary cube of graph theory, with levels \(S\), the three \(U_i\), the three \(P_{ij}\), and the exterior \(O\). The other presentation adds one \(S\)–\(Q\) distinction and two \(Q\)–\(O\) distinctions. Any two of the three \(y\)'s, and also all three, have least common bond size **three versus four**. For the upper bounds, take all seven ordinary cube knots on one side and \(O\), together with \(Q\) when present, on the other. Each side is connected. For the lower bounds a cut crossing two marked \(O\)–\(P\) distinctions restricts to a nonempty cube cut. A smaller shore of size two, three or four has at most one, two or four internal meetings and hence at least four, five or four crossings. Thus every three-cut is a vertex star. No singleton has fewer than three. Only \(O\)'s star contains both marks. It already separates \(S\) from \(O\), so the added route through \(Q\) forces at least one more crossing. Pair bond distance, which subtracts one, is therefore two versus three. The bond test includes global completions of cuts.

Ordinary separation in the entire accumulated stage can be made arbitrarily large. Join \(S\) to \(Z\) by three internally disjoint chains of \(L\ge4\) ordinary steps. The first knots \(U_i\) after \(S\) each have two loose distinctions. Give all other old knots zero, except for zero versus one at \(Z\). Double the consecutive interior meetings on every chain so every old knot has at least three places. The graph after deleting any one holder is connected and still contains a loose \(U_i\), so every star is a bond. For a not-all-equal control doubling *every* meeting lets one set the two values at every unaffected meeting opposite, extending any one allowed star assignment. The same check applies with only the consecutive interior meetings doubled. If an interior star is specified, it can constrain end meetings only along its one chain. In particular on \(L=4\) each of the two middle links is already doubled.

The eligible pairs and the first two layers through the \(U_i\) are exactly those just proved, since \(Z\) shares no neighbor with them. Both starts are identified, with the same respective fates. In the whole reached structure the distance from \(Z\) to any \(P_{ij}\) is exactly \(L\) ordinary steps. Assign grade \(k\) to an old holder \(k\) steps along a chain from \(S\), grade zero to each \(P_{ij}\), and grade \(L+1\) to the possible unary at \(Z\). Neighboring grades differ by one. A route of length \(L\) goes from \(Z\) down one chain to the appropriate \(U_i\) and then to \(P_{ij}\). The two positive-age live components remain at infinite end separation. If the roots are \(P_{ij}\) and the unary at \(Z\), their whole-stage distance is \(L+1\). The same minimum bond sizes of three and four hold at every length. Keep one copy of each initial meeting from the four-knot calculation to obtain its cube on the old center, the three near holders, the three pair births and the exterior. In the larger source the additional route from a near holder through the far holder and its unary to the exterior requires one further crossing. ## Where the finite-radius rule holds

**Every lawful source, on a funded nonclosing step.** Radius one at \(u\) lists all its groups because every member of a group through \(u\), and every rival that could enlarge it, is adjacent to \(u\). The local witness sets decide which groups are held. Thus radius one supplies every child through \(u\), with its parent set and all unary multiplicities. For any such child \(P\), every one of its prospective neighbors is a child of some \(v\in P\). The complete child roster of \(v\), including the *entire* parent set of each child, lies in the input ball of radius two at \(u\). Intersect those parent sets to find every candidate among \(P\)'s neighbors and every set of them held at a common parent. Checking maximality through \(P\) never requires another birth. This gives \(D(P)\) and the exact fresh count. A radius-\(r\) target around these children uses at most \(r+2\) input steps. Moving one end step between births transfers through a common parent, and all parents of a birth are pairwise adjacent. The argument is local for all funded not-all-equal steps.

If parity closure is admitted but the **whole live end is promised connected**, radius two still suffices. A proposed closing child must take the only loose distinction at each of its parents. An outside vertex in their component then has to be reachable across a first adjacency leaving the parent set, within radius two of \(u\). If there is none and no parent has another loose distinction, the one child exhausts this component. More generally the single supplied bit “there is another live component” settles exactly the remaining global ambiguity. Each funded live component yields one connected child component until a sole implied birth closes. To see this, take a route in the old component, cover each adjacent pair by a child, and join consecutive covering children through the intermediate parent. No child has parents in two different components.

**Sharp general control.** Make four possible live holders \(u,a,b,x\), and for each of \(ua,ub,ab,ax,bx\) give the named pair its own spent witness. Double the meetings of each witness with its two holders. Give \(u,a,b\) loose counts \(1,2,2\) and vary \(x\)'s between zero and one. The ordinary graph is a subdivision of two three-cycles sharing a meeting. Removing any one holder leaves it connected and still wired to a loose holder. All original parity stars are bonds. Under not-all-equal, prescribe any allowed assignment at one star and set the two values opposite at every doubled meeting away from it. Each other star has some other neighbor and is satisfied. The rooted radius-one views at \(u\) agree, including all witness incidences restricted to that ball. The group \(G=\{u,a,b\}\) exists in both. In the first it has two unary siblings, one through \(a\) and one through \(b\). They have no common parent, so both prospective pairs through \(G\) remain and \(G\) receives two fresh. In the second there is a second group \(\{a,b,x\}\), which is \(G\)'s sole sibling, so \(G\) receives one. Neither compared step stalls or closes. Both sources fall outside the initial identification conditions. The smaller arm subsequently has an identified two-chain end, while the larger arm closes at the next step under parity and relays under not-all-equal. If desired, give the witness of \(ua\) two loose distinctions in **both** sources. It then supplies a separate continuing live component in both, since its neighbors are in the opposite part of the original bipartite presentation. The same two-versus-one calculation now holds with two nonclosing futures.

**Sharp identified-class control.** Within the proved class the same conditional update takes just **one** input step. For a unary birth through \(u\), all its \(h(u)-1\) siblings are held together at \(u\), so it has exactly that many prospective groups, all pairs. For a birth \(P\) with at least two parents take \(u,v\in P\), and let \(B\) be their unique common witness block in the current end. Then
\[
 D(P)=h(u)+h(v)-|B|.
\]
The block is wholly visible at radius one of \(u\). All the near parent sets are already known from this radius, and applying the floor gives the fresh count. Thus an identified radius-\(r\) successor neighborhood uses at most \(r+1\) input steps under the same promises.

At source age the no-first-triple condition makes \(P=\{u,v\}\). Its siblings at its two parents number \(h(u)-1\) and \(h(v)-1\). A prospective unheld group through \(P\) can only be the three two-parent sets \(\{u,v\},\{u,w\},\{v,w\}\). This follows because every sibling is unary or binary, so a pairwise-intersecting family through \(\{u,v\}\) either shares one parent or has exactly that form. A \(w\) adjacent to \(u,v\) must belong to \(B\) because the common witness of \(u,v\) is unique and a third holder outside it would give an unheld triple. Each of the \(|B|-2\) possible \(w\)'s therefore replaces two prospective pairs through \(P\) by one triple. This proves the formula at source age.

At positive age the identification theorem gives unique compatible lifts of the two candidate parents and their common predecessor. Write these ordinary clusters \(K+x\), \(K+y\), with common predecessor \(K\) and child \(K+x+y\), for distinct \(x,y\). The common-parent block is the roster of all children of \(K\), of size \(h(K)\). The uniqueness just invoked concerns the whole orbit, so two translated copies cannot add another parent or another child to these counts. The newly exposed sets for two distinct old boundary points \(x,y\) are disjoint because a common point there would give a second route between the branches in the incidence tree. \[
 |\partial(K+x+y)|=|\partial(K+x)|+|\partial(K+y)|-|\partial K|.

The predecessor and either parent of an ordinary multi-parent child are proper clusters. Their open counts equal these boundaries. For a proper multi-parent child its boundary counts its demand, since it has no unary extensions. For the whole finite piece the ordinary boundary and demand are both zero. ## The separate distance theorem

For any funded step with a surviving end, let \(C(u)\) be the nonempty set of children of \(u\), with distinct copies of unary children retained. Let \(d\) be end-step distance and \(d'\) the same reading in the new end. If \(d(u,v)\) is finite, then
\[
 d'(C(u),C(v))=\max(0,d(u,v)-1).
\]
For arbitrary particular children \(X\in C(u)\), \(Y\in C(v)\),
\[
 \max(0,d(u,v)-1)\ \le d'(X,Y)\le d(u,v)+1 .
\]
Infinite old distance stays infinite. A closure has no surviving end, so no numerical distance is assigned there.

For the first upper bound, cover each adjacency of an old shortest route by a child containing both parents. Consecutive covering children share the intervening old parent and hence are adjacent or identical. This costs at most \(d-1\) for \(d\ge1\), and \(u=v\) has a common child. To obtain the particular-child upper bound one may go one extra sibling step at each end, or at most one step between siblings at \(u=v\). Conversely a route of length \(k\) from \(X\) to \(Y\) gives an old route of length at most \(k+1\) from \(u\) to \(v\) by using a common parent at each new step. Different parents of the same child are adjacent. At \(k=0\) both old knots belong to the same parent set. This reverse argument also excludes routes between old components. It uses the *coverage* of old adjacencies and the *clique* condition on births in opposite directions. Either property could fail under another growth rule.

Both bounds are attained. On a five-ring with three loose distinctions at every original holder each gets a unary child, and adjacent parents also have a common pair birth. At the common birth distance is zero from distance one. Their two unary children have distance exactly two, since each has a different single parent and both meet the common pair birth through their parents. More generally between two unary children of distinct parents at distance \(d\), any route must first use its source parent and last use its target parent, costing at least \(d+1\), as attained by the upper-bound route. The fully spared five-ring grows exponentially. On all funded nonclosing layers, iterating the minimum formula for *inclusive* descendants gives \(\max(0,d-k)\) after \(k\) steps, with infinity fixed. Pure lineages need not include the joint descendants that realize this minimum.

The stop concerns the parity law's one global fresh-count decision on a connected physical start whose end need not be connected. The end still carries a well-defined nearness, exact descendant-distance bounds, and a finite-radius rule for the funded nonclosing update. This paper establishes none of the later sampled frequencies or interaction tests.
