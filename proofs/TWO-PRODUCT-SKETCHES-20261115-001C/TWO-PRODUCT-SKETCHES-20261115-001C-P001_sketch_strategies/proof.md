# Optimal shared sketches and exclusions for unequal sketches

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

On 32 common input bits let \(Q(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{30}x_i x_{i+1}\). The first evaluator exposes its total \(s=Q(x)\) and one selected edge sum \(a=A(x)\). The second exposes its total \(t=Q(x)\) and one selected edge sum \(b=B(x)\). Impose \(s+t=1\). A solver can query any affine Boolean form on the inputs and these four outputs. Within a case it repeatedly intersects the affine hulls of the two evaluators' separate exact relations, restricted to the current affine space, until no more changes occur. A leaf closes when this space is empty. All underlying products outside the two selected sums are hidden. Masks and the public input order are chosen before branching.

* The common first-16-edges mask requires **exactly 512 leaves and depth nine**, even with adaptive queries mixing both blocks' inputs and outputs. The common noncontiguous mask \(\mathtt{0x33333333}\) attains the same bounds. **512 is the minimum across all common masks**, including arbitrary noncontiguous choices. Replacing either sketch by its complementary mask is an affine change of the visible coordinates.
* For any complementary decomposition of a nonsingular \(Q\) into independent blocks of positive polar half-ranks \(r,s'\), the exact fixed-mask optimum is \(2^r+2^{s'}\) leaves and depth \(1+\max(r,s')\). There are 557,056 masks with additive half-ranks on the 31-edge path, including the two constant mask choices, which reduce to the one-total procedure.
* For **unequal** masks, if each of \(A,Q+A,B,Q+B\) has polar half-rank at least eight, every query tree needs at least 512 leaves and depth nine. Another sufficient condition for this lower bound is that independently complementing the masks can make their union have a vertex cover of size at most seven. These disjoint tests cover **4,041,073,256,924,708,864** of the \(2^{62}\) ordered pairs, including common pairs. A cheaper strategy, if one exists, requires an unequal, noncomplementary pair among the 570,612,761,502,679,040 pairs outside these two tests. The exact minimum for the remaining asymmetric choices is open in this proof.

Here the half-rank \(\rho(f)\) is half the rank over \(\mathbb F_2\) of the polar form \(f(x+y)+f(x)+f(y)+f(0)\). The unconditioned 31-edge path has half-rank 16. All size and depth claims use this one unsatisfiable instance and the declared hull rule.

## The two-block lower bound

If \(Q=U+V\) has nonsingular polar form on \(2(r+s')\) coordinates and \(\rho(U)=r,\rho(V)=s'\), the two radicals have trivial intersection, as an element of both lies in the radical of \(Q\). Their dimensions sum to the full dimension. On the radical of \(V\), the restriction of the polar form of \(U\) is nondegenerate: a vector annihilating that summand also annihilates the radical of \(U\) and hence annihilates the entire space. Interchanging \(U,V\) gives the other summand. Split off pairs of vectors of polar product one in each summand and continue on their perpendicular complements. After an invertible linear input change, each quadratic is a sum of products of disjoint coordinate pairs in its own block, plus an affine function of the inputs. Subtract the respective affine terms from the displays. This proves independence from rank additivity and also checks that public mixed queries remain affine under the change.

Use \(p,q\) for the displayed left block sums, and \(p+d,q+1+d\) for their right counterparts. These are affine coordinates after using the opposite-total constraint. Let \(K_{r,s'}\) be the full affine parameter space of the two blocks' input pairs and \(p,q,d\). Both evaluators separately span it. On the left the two block sums contain distinct quadratic monomials, precluding a new affine identity with either sum, and \(d\) stays free. On the right replace \(p,q\) by \(p+d,q+1+d\). A live affine copy means each side's honest points in the copy span it. Such a copy survives every hull intersection within any larger case containing it.

For reference a single block \(K_r\) has its inputs and one free displayed value \(p\), with predicates \(p=U\) and \(p=U+1\). Its minimum is \(2^r\) leaves and depth \(r\). The upper bound fixes one input per pair. For the lower bound the full space is live for \(r\ge1\). For \(r>1\), every nonconstant query contains a block input coefficient or the coefficient of \(p\). The following pair-removal construction, with no second block, leaves a live \(K_{r-1}\) in both answers. A nonconstant split of a live \(K_1\) costs at least two leaves.

For the two-block induction, a query constant on the live copy preserves it in one child. A nonconstant query depending only on \(d\) selects which group's discrepancy is odd. In each child fix all inputs and the honest sums of the other block to zero. The two children respectively retain a \(K_r\) and a \(K_{s'}\).

Every other query touches some input pair \((x,y)\) or its block's sum \(p\) (rename blocks if needed). Write \(p=p'+z\), where \(p'\) is the residual block output and the erased pair will have \(z=xy\) on *both* sides. After this substitution the query is
\[
  \ell(x,y,z)+g(w),\qquad
  \ell(x,y,z)=\lambda_xx+\lambda_yy+\lambda_pz\ne0 .
\]
Here \(w\) contains **all** surviving parameters, including \(p'\), the other block's output, all its inputs and the discrepancy. The four legitimate triples \(000,100,010,111\), in order \(x,y,z\), affinely span all three coordinates. Choose two legitimate triples \(u_0,u_1\) with \(\ell(u_i)=i\). To force query answer \(c\), use
\[
 (x,y,z)=u_0+(u_0+u_1)(c+g(w)),\qquad p=p'+z .
\]
This is an affine map on the residual parameter space even when \(g\) uses \(p'\) and the other sum. Its chosen triple is always legitimate. It is injective: all unchanged inputs remain available, and the chosen triple is recovered from its two input bits, which differ between the two triples. This determines the added \(z\) and hence \(p'\). Left honesty lifts because \(p'=U'\) gives \(p=U'+xy\); right honesty lifts with the same discrepancy. Therefore each child contains a live affine copy with that block shortened by one pair.

If this was the last pair of the block, set its discrepancy to zero, put \(p'=0\), and leave the other block's discrepancy equal to one. The same formula works even if \(g\) uses that remaining block's displayed sum. Each child now retains its scalar live copy. This detail prevents crediting an empty block with a free value or a paid split.

Induct on the live ranks (and the remaining tree height for constant queries). The pure-discrepancy split costs \(2^r+2^{s'}\). A pair split with \(r>1\) costs at least \(2(2^{r-1}+2^{s'})\). For \(r=1\) it costs at least \(2\cdot2^{s'}\ge2+2^{s'}\). The depth recurrence gives at least \(1+\max(r,s')\) in the same three cases. Conversely query \(d\) first and, in the selected odd block, fix one factor of each pair. Its sum becomes affine in both evaluators and they demand opposite values, closing the leaf. This attains both bounds.

## The actual 31-edge displays

Put \(y_j=x_{2j+1}\) and \(z_j=x_{2j}+x_{2j+2}\) for \(0\le j<16\), with \(x_{32}=0\). The input change is invertible by recovering the even inputs backwards. The product \(y_jz_j\) equals the sum on edges \(2j,2j+1\), except the last bundle, containing only edge 30. Any union of bundles therefore realizes two independent blocks, without adding a new input observable. Selecting \(j=0,\ldots,7\) gives the first 16 edges, while selecting even \(j\) gives \(\mathtt{0x33333333}\). All \(\binom{16}{8}=12{,}870\) balanced bundle selections have the same exact cost. Selecting seven bundles gives the unequal ranks \(7,9\), 640 leaves and depth ten. The checker expands the linear factors into the literal path monomials and checks the surviving affine form in each of all 512, 512 and 640 leaves of these three trees. [Leaf replay](checks.json)

For growing paths with \(4h-1\) edges (\(h\ge2\)), the same bundles give half-ranks \(h,h\) and \(2^{h+1}\) leaves. The next argument with thresholds \(h\) and \(2h\) proves this optimum across all common masks. The zero-mask or full-mask observations are affine functions of the already public totals and reduce to the scalar instance.

## A lower bound for large ranks on both sides

We give the argument with a parameter \(h\ge2\). Suppose \(\rho(Q)\ge2h\) and each of the four partial forms \(A,Q+A,B,Q+B\) has half-rank at least \(h\). Then all cases of codimension below \(h\) remain unchanged, and every case of codimension \(h\) contains a nonempty fixed subspace. Consequently every closed tree costs at least \(2^{h+1}\) leaves and depth \(h+1\). The assertion at codimension \(h\) allows the case itself to shrink.

Three elementary facts control the argument.

1. Restriction to a codimension-\(u\) input space decreases half-rank by at most \(u\), since it deletes \(u\) rows and \(u\) columns in an adapted basis. Translations and affine summands preserve the polar form. Rank is subadditive.
2. The absolute bias \(\left|\mathbb E(-1)^f\right|\) of a Boolean quadratic is at most \(2^{-\rho(f)}\). In polar-pair coordinates it is a sum of \(\rho(f)\) disjoint products and affine terms. Each signed average for a pair has magnitude \(1/2\) and a nonconstant linear term on the radical gives zero. For two equations \(f=u,g=v\), expand their two indicator functions. Every value pair occurs if the sum of the absolute biases of \(f,g,f+g\) is below one. This applies after adding any affine functions. Three half-ranks at least two suffice. A second sufficient pattern has a designated form of half-rank \(w\ge3\) and the other two of half-ranks at least one. Indeed the latter two ranks \(a',b'\) satisfy \(a'+b'\ge w\), so the sum of all three biases is at most \(2^{-w}+1/2+2^{-(w-1)}<1\).
3. Both levels of a quadratic of half-rank at least two affinely span its input space, since if one level lay in a proper hyperplane the opposite hyperplane would be constant even after losing at most one unit of half-rank. For half-rank one, write \(xy\) plus an affine form. If a radical linear term is present, each level is a graph of a nonaffine function over the other coordinates and spans. Otherwise absorb the two linear pair terms by translating the pair. One level consists of three pair states, spanning the space, and the rarer level fixes two independent input bits and is exactly an affine subspace of codimension two.

Eliminate \(t=1+s\), giving three output coordinates \((s,a,b)\). For a nonempty affine space \(W\) of codimension \(d\) in the unrestricted inputs and these three outputs, let \(j\) be the rank on the output columns of independent equations for \(W\). Its input projection \(L\) has codimension \(d-j\), and its output fibre has \(3-j\) free directions. In an affine coordinate system over \(L\) the fibre is the same direction space at every point. Project its directions onto the two outputs controlled by one side: \((s,a)\) on the left or \((s,b)\) on the right (the right total differs by a constant). Call the dimension of that image \(e\). The hidden coordinate on that side has dimension one, so
\[
 \max(0,2-j)\le e\le\min(2,3-j).
\]
A point of \(L\) extends on that side exactly when it satisfies \(2-e\) independent combinations of its two quadratic output equations, including the affine terms supplied by \(W\). The \(e\) remaining output bits then equal independent complementary combinations on those inputs. Directions purely in the side's hidden coordinate remain free. This description follows by Gaussian elimination on the two output coordinates.

First take any nonempty affine \(W\) of codimension \(d\le h\). All three nonzero formal combinations of either side have half-rank at least \(h-(d-j)\ge j\) on \(L\). If there are no equations on \(L\), an actual side point exists. One equation requires \(j\ge1\), hence is nonconstant. Two equations require \(j\ge2\) and Fact 2 guarantees a common solution. Thus **each side meets every nonempty affine space of codimension at most \(h\)**. Every hyperplane half of a case of codimension less than \(h\) contains a point of each side. Both hulls there equal the entire case.

Now take \(d=h\), so the restricted half-ranks are at least \(j\), and the total's half-rank on \(L\) is at least \(h+j\). We inspect all allowable output-column ranks.

* **\(j=0\).** All outputs are free above \(L\), and the total remains nonaffine. On each side at most one of its sketch and its sketch plus total can be affine on \(L\). If present, that identity links its own sketch to \(x\) and possibly \(s\). The two sides' identities can be imposed together since they define separate coordinates \(a,b\) without restricting \(x,s\). The resulting space is live. Project away a passive sketch when testing a side. Its remaining graph spans the space because every remaining nonzero combination of its outputs is nonaffine. This also proves the conclusion if neither or just one side contributes an identity.
* **\(j=1\).** All pencil forms are nonaffine and the total has half-rank at least \(h+1\ge3\). A side with \(e=2\) spans immediately. With \(e=1\), its input truth set is \(H=\{f=0\}\) for one pencil form with an affine term, and its remaining output is another independent form \(g\) on \(H\). No affine function equals \(g\) throughout \(H\): for every proposed affine function \(l\), both values of \(g+l\) occur on \(H\), by the second bias pattern in Fact 2 applied to \(f,g+l,f+g+l\). If \(H\) spans \(L\), this proves that the side spans \(W\). Otherwise Fact 3 makes \(H=L'\) exactly a codimension-two affine subspace of \(L\). Both sides can have \(e=1\) only when the lone output row uses neither \(a\) nor \(b\), hence pins \(s\) to an affine form. Its input equation then comes from the high-rank total and spans on either side. In the exceptional case only one side has \(e=1\); take it as the left. Its row has the form \(a+\epsilon s=l(x)\), because \(b\) must have coefficient zero, and a row with only \(s\) was just treated. On \(L'\) this row is satisfied identically for its honest outputs. The left reduces to \(s=Q(x)\), with \(b\) free. The total still has half-rank at least \(h+1-2\ge1\). The right graph of \(s=1+Q,\ b=B\) can force at most one additional affine definition of \(b\) in terms of \(s,x\), since \(Q\) remains nonaffine. Impose it if present. Each side then spans the same nonempty space over \(L'\) just as for \(j=0\). The right-exceptional case exchanges \(a,b\).
* **\(j=2\).** For \(e=1\), its single truth set spans by Fact 3, and the remaining output admits no affine expression on it by the three-ranks-at-least-two bias bound. For \(e=0\), the side's actual points over \(L\) satisfy two quadratic equations and the unused output direction stays free. On any hyperplane half of \(L\), the three half-ranks remain at least one and the designated total still has half-rank at least \(h+1\ge3\). Fact 2 gives a common solution on each half, so these input points span \(L\). Both sides thus span \(W\).
* **\(j=3\).** Here \(e=0\), and all outputs are pinned affinely to \(x\). Upon restricting to any hyperplane half of \(L\) the three half-ranks are at least two, so the two equations have a common solution. Again each side spans \(W\).

This proves the claim about successive restrictions. Up to level \(h-1\) a nonconstant query has two nonempty answers on the unchanged case. At level \(h\) every case still requires a split, even if saturation first shrinks it. The binary tree therefore has at least \(2^{h+1}\) leaves. For \(h=8\) the checker builds affine bases of actual left and right solutions on seven 31-edge fixed subspaces with output rank \(0,1,2,3\), including a rank-one level imposing two new input equations. An uneven branch can close earlier for a poorly chosen mask. On a three-edge path a left sketch of just the middle product, pinned to one, forces both its inputs to one and makes the total affine. This branch closes after one question. [Whole-evaluator checks](checks.json)

## Small supports, complete common-mask comparison and asymmetric census

Independently one can choose either \(A\) or \(Q+A\) on the left and either \(B\) or \(Q+B\) on the right, since each side sees its own total. Suppose the chosen two edge sets together have a vertex cover of size \(c\). Fix those \(c\) input bits, making both chosen quadratic forms affine on the remaining input subspace. Define the ambient sketch bits using those affine forms and their own side's total (use \(s\) on the left and \(s+1\) on the right if a complement was chosen). This embeds a scalar opposed-total problem whose remaining half-rank is at least \(\rho(Q)-c\). Each scalar evaluator's solutions map into the corresponding full evaluator. Monotonicity of hulls then carries the scalar lower bound to the full model. In detail, any scalar live copy used by the single-block adversary survives in the full problem, and every full query pulls back to an affine scalar query. With \(c\le7\) this gives at least \(2^{16-7}=512\) leaves and depth at least nine.

For each path mask, a consecutive run of \(k\) selected edges contributes \(\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor\) to its half-rank and the same number to its minimum vertex cover. Alternating vertices give the cover and disjoint alternating edges require this many vertices. The path polar matrix has the same rank by successively removing the endpoint and its unique neighbor as a nondegenerate pair. Runs have disjoint vertex sets. For a common mask, if either complementary sum has half-rank at most seven, choose it on both sides and apply the small-cover argument. In every other common case apply the large-rank theorem. The exhibited midpoint attains 512. On \(4h-1\) edges replace seven by \(h-1\), eight by \(h\), and 16 by \(2h\).

For all ordered masks, count binary edge strings by the half-ranks of the zero and one runs. Appending a digit increases its rank count exactly when the previous length of the same run is even, including a new run of length zero. A dynamic program carrying the last digit and the parity of its run counts all \(2^{31}\) masks. Exactly 2,010,227,968 have both ranks at least eight. Squaring gives 4,041,016,483,329,409,024 ordered pairs covered by the large-rank theorem.

For the small-cover test each edge has one of four ordered colors \((A_i,B_i)\). Each choice of complements selects a union containing every color except one. No two of these four unions can have cover at most seven, because together they contain the full path, whose cover size is 16. For each fixed omitted color the dynamic program gives one way to append an edge outside the union and three ways to append an edge inside, charging the run rank as above. Multiplying by four gives another 56,773,595,299,840 ordered pairs. For every such pair each side has a mask or complement of cover at most seven, so the two tests are disjoint. The checker compares the rank recurrence against Gaussian elimination over every mask through 14 edges and the paired count against direct mask-pair enumeration on smaller paths. [Counting and embeddings](solver.py) · [Numerical certificate](checks.json)

An honest input distinguishes the two observation models. On the three-edge path with \(x=(1,1,0,0)\), the first-edge mask has sketch one and the last-edge mask has sketch zero, while both evaluators have the same honest total. A discrepancy between genuinely different sketches cannot identify a failed evaluation by itself. The unclassified asymmetric portion therefore requires an additional argument or a checked cheaper tree.
