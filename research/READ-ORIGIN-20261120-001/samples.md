# The same present holdings, midway and complete

Every one of the **164** sampled proper middles has a different effective
parent roster under a restart on identical lawful holdings. Among the
**115** common event occurrences, 16 get different fresh counts. The
original parent set and the same marked loose distinctions are available
under both readings in four of the randomly marked comparisons with
different counts. After completing the original cohort, all **164**
carried histories and restarts agree on the entire next ordinary step.
Parity and not-all-equal give the same results on this sample.

## Weighting and gates

The first weighting is a uniform labelled tree, drawn by a uniform
Prüfer word. The second independently draws such a tree and adds one
uniformly selected absent meeting. This induces a weighting by the ways
of drawing an augmented tree. Each meeting has one shared distinction.
At each holder of ordinary degree \(g\), the source has
\(\max(0,3-g)\) loose distinctions plus one with independent
probability \(1/4\). There are 64 draws per size and weighting.
All 640 sources pass both literal knot-law projection gates.

For each funded source the ordinary evaluator supplies the first full
roster and its counts. The sample shuffles that roster, then picks a
length uniformly from one through one less than its size. The retained
births have the ordinary parent incidences and immutable fresh counts.
Every funded draw has at least two births and contributes one proper

Repeated unary offspring have separate slots in the weighting and can
exchange their loose inputs at their common parent. The two laws share
sample choices. Each weighting supplies actual histories; a competing
source can be any lawful source on the same physical holdings. Sources
and coupled histories provide the sampling units, with multiple event
occurrences from a history dependent.

gate under each law in every sampled middle. The genuine interpretation
keeps the remaining original cohort. The restart applies the complete
ordinary operator to exactly the same current support rows. A restart
with a shortage supplies an empty available roster and preserves the
proposed groups and shortages in the receipt.

## Completion and the reverse census

The stored ordinary presentation carries its actual roster of live
children into the next step. Separately, the checker initializes a
presentation from exactly those completed physical holdings and applies
the same ordinary operator. After matching holder names and allowing
exchanges of each parent's loose distinctions, all 164 next rosters,
fresh counts and statuses agree per law. Both evaluations form the next
step on all 164 cases. The completed cohort spent all earlier loose
distinctions. Its live roster is therefore the set recoverable directly
from the current holdings.

The reverse census exhausts every nonempty holder subset in each
sampled present of at most nine knots, once per law. Its histories span
zero or one original cohort, including a full cohort. Each putative
source retains the entire support of each included holder. It must
connect physically. Every omitted holder must have exactly one shared
input per original parent and share with no other omitted holder. Its
remaining distinctions must agree with the immutable fresh count in a
funded ordinary plan for those originals. The tally checks exact
parent-set multiplicity. Lawfulness of the retained constraints follows
from the literal projection checks on the whole current presentation.
Deleting constraints preserves all permitted patterns on each surviving
support, where the surviving law still imposes precisely its own
constraint. The census also retains the lawful fresh-source interpretation
when its next step stalls.

All 125 enumerated middles have multiple origins and next menus, even
up to automorphisms of the current physical incidence. The census
checked 21,379 subsets there. The other 39 middles exceed its holder
bound. The full response includes the exact parent sets, their
multiplicities, their fresh counts and the step status. A marked
response records availability and fresh options on one fixed set of
loose inputs. In 114 of the 125 frames that response differs across
compatible origins. Symmetries use the exact holder relation with
numbers of shared distinctions and loose distinctions retained. When
the mark is held fixed the automorphism must preserve the marked
parent set. Such a map lifts to a permutation of distinctions
preserving the corresponding marked inputs. At most six next-menu
classes remain after either symmetry quotient. On these fixed inputs,
the highest conditional code widths are three bits for the whole
response and two for the marked response.

Disclosing that the presentation is a proper middle leaves differing
next menus in 92 of 125 frames. Disclosing the true source size too
leaves different menus in 61 and different marked responses in 50.
These counts are exact for the bounded inverse domains of the sampled
frames.

Of the 164 actual completions, 72 admit the same bounded inverse

interpretation and the fresh-source interpretation agree as above.
In 14 of the 72 frames, an additional unfinished interpretation yields
a different next menu. Declaring a true complete boundary and having
holdings which could arise at such a boundary are different inputs.
Restricting the census to completed histories restores agreement on all
these frames. The other 92 completions exceed the census bound.
