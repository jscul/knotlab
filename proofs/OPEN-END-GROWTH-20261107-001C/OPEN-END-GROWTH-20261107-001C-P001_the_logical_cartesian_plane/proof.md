# The logical Cartesian plane: the product of two number lines

Status: Proved (items 1–2); item 3 is a conjecture with finite evidence

Ticket `OPEN-END-GROWTH-20261107-001C`, 2026-11-07.

## Standard statement

Let C1 ⊆ F_2^{V1} and C2 ⊆ F_2^{V2} be binary linear codes, and P = C1 ⊗ C2 their tensor product: the
arrays whose columns lie in C1 and whose rows lie in C2. Its dual is
P^⊥ = C1^⊥ ⊗ F_2^{V2} + F_2^{V1} ⊗ C2^⊥.

1. Suppose K1 is the support of a minimal nonzero codeword of C1^⊥ containing p and p′, and K2 the same
   for C2^⊥ containing q and q′. Then L = (K1 × {q′}) Δ ({p} × K2) is the support of a minimal nonzero
   codeword of P^⊥. It contains (p, q) and (p′, q′) and has |K1| + |K2| − 2 elements.
2. Let T be the "thread" code on Z, with two coordinates x_t, L_t per site and checks
   x_{t−1} + L_t + x_t = 0. In T ⊗ T, a code on Z^2 with four coordinates per site:
   - the finitely supported codewords are dense;
   - they are generated by translates of one codeword of diameter 1;
   - hence every minimal support of P^⊥ through two entries has at least (sup-distance of their sites)
     + 1 elements;
   - with 1, that least size is within constants of the ℓ¹ distance, and balls grow quadratically.
3. **Conjecture:** the least minimal support through (p, q) and (p′, q′) has exactly |K1| + |K2| − 2
   elements, minimized over K1 and K2. When p = p′ (or q = q′) it is the least such K2 (or K1).

## Nearest known result

- **Product codes and their duals.** Product codes are Elias's (1954). Their duals are the direct-sum
  codes C1^⊥ ⊗ F + F ⊗ C2^⊥, whose minimum distance is min(d(C1^⊥), d(C2^⊥)) (MacWilliams and Sloane,
  ch. 18).
- **Quantum codes.** Tensor and hypergraph products are the basis of hypergraph-product quantum codes
  (Tillich and Zémor, 2009).
- **Classification:** `known-with-delta`. No source was found for the minimal dual codewords through two
  given positions, the L-shapes and the conjectured formula, or for the reading.

## Setting and reading

- **Parity laws and their product.** A parity law's allowed states form a code C, and its knots (K1–K3)
  are the minimal supports of C^⊥ with at least 3 elements. Bond distance is the least size of such a
  support through two distinctions, minus one.
- **The product law of two laws** has P as its allowed states:
  - its distinctions are pairs (one distinction of each law);
  - an assignment is allowed when every column is an allowed state of the first law and every row of
    the second.
- **Why this is the Cartesian plane.** This is the Cartesian construction applied to logic: each axis is
  a logical law, and nothing else is drawn. With both axes the number line grown in
  `OPEN-END-GROWTH-20261107-001B` (the two-way thread), it is the owner's Cartesian plane whose axes are
  logical number lines.

## 1. L-shaped knots

**Proposition 1.** With K1, K2, p, p′, q, q′ as above, the support L is a knot of the product law, of size
|K1| + |K2| − 2, through (p, q) and (p′, q′). Hence

  δ_P((p, q), (p′, q′)) <= δ_1(p, p′) + δ_2(q, q′) − 1   (p ≠ p′, q ≠ q′),
  δ_P((p, q), (p, q′)) <= δ_2(q, q′).

*Proof.*
1. **W is a check.** Let w1 ∈ C1^⊥ and w2 ∈ C2^⊥ have supports K1 and K2. Then
   W = w1 ⊗ e_{q′} + e_p ⊗ w2 lies in P^⊥. Its support is L, since the corner (p, q′) occurs twice and
   cancels.
2. **Setup for minimality.** Let W′ ∈ P^⊥ have support inside L. Write a(r) = W′(r, q′) for
   r ∈ K1 ∖ {p}, and b(c) = W′(p, c) for c ∈ K2 ∖ {q′}. For all u ∈ C1 and v ∈ C2,
   u^T W′ v = v(q′)·Σ a(r)u(r) + u(p)·Σ b(c)v(c) = 0.
3. **What the projections are.** K1 is a circuit, so the projection of C1 to K1 is the even-weight code
   on K1, and likewise for C2 and K2.
4. **The arms are constant.**
   - Taking v with v(q′) = 1 and u with u(p) = 0 makes a orthogonal to every even-weight vector on
     K1 ∖ {p}, so a is constant.
   - Symmetrically, b is constant.
5. **Only the whole L remains.** Now take u(p) = 1 and v(q′) = 1. Then Σ a(r)u(r) = a·u(p) = a, and
   likewise for b, so a + b = 0. Thus W′ is 0 or W, and L is a minimal support.
6. **The row case.** It is the knot K2 placed in row p. Its projection is C2 projected to K2, because
   row p of the product ranges over all of C2 when p is not pinned in C1. ∎

## 2. The product of two number lines is a plane

**Proposition 2.** For the two-way thread T on Z, the product law T ⊗ T on Z^2 has no ends: its finite
moves are dense. They are generated by translates of m ⊗ m, where m = {L_t, x_t, L_{t+1}} is a finite move
of T of diameter 1. Consequently:
- every knot of T ⊗ T is 1-chained, and δ_P(u, v) >= |site(u) − site(v)|_∞;
- with Proposition 1 and the thread's bond distance δ_T(x_s, x_t) = |s − t| + 1 (a tree, LOGIC-AXIS D),
  δ_P lies between the sup and the ℓ¹ distance of the sites up to additive constants;
- bond balls of radius r hold Θ(r^2) distinctions: the plane's dimension, read from allowed states alone.

*Proof.*
1. **The thread's finite moves.** A finitely supported state of T is determined by its loops, which have
   finite support and even total. These are spanned by adjacent pairs {L_t, L_{t+1}}, so the finite moves
   F_T are generated by the translates of m. F_T is dense in T: truncate a state's loops outside a window,
   fixing the parity with one loop beyond it.
2. **The product's finite moves.** A finitely supported array of P has rows in F_T and columns in F_T, so
   it lies in F_T ⊗ F_T, which is generated by translates of m ⊗ m.
3. **Density.** For finite R × Q the projection of P is T_R ⊗ T_Q, since u ⊗ v ∈ P for u, v ∈ T. So
   a finitely supported W annihilates P iff W ∈ T_R^⊥ ⊗ F + F ⊗ T_Q^⊥. The same holds for F_T ⊗ F_T,
   because (F_T)_R = T_R by density. Hence F_T ⊗ F_T and P have the same annihilator, and the closure of
   F_T ⊗ F_T is P (Pontryagin duality; P is closed).
4. **The ruler.** By LOGIC-AXIS A, Proposition 2, P splits at range 1, and by its Theorem 1 every knot is
   1-chained.
5. **Taxicab.** The thread's bond distances are linear: its knot graph is a path, LOGIC-AXIS D,
   Proposition 2. So Proposition 1 gives δ_P <= |Δs| + |Δt| + O(1), and the ball counts follow. ∎

**Reading.**
- **Taxicab.** The Cartesian plane of two logical number lines measures distance as the taxicab sum of
  its axes: an x-count plus a y-count of loops, never the diagonal.
- **Not grown.** No local growth rule in the census grew this plane (ticket A). It is the product of two
  grown lines. The line grows; the plane is the product of lines.

## 3. Exact equality: conjecture and evidence

**Conjecture.** δ_P((p, q), (p′, q′)) = δ_1(p, p′) + δ_2(q, q′) − 1 when both axes move, and the moved
axis's distance when one is fixed.

**Evidence** (check 3, exhaustive search):
- every pair of thread(2) ⊗ thread(3);
- 260 random pairs of thread(3) ⊗ thread(3);
- the plain sum δ1 + δ2, without the −1, is refuted by the same data.

Proposition 1 gives "<=". A proof of ">=" would need to rule out shortcuts through several rows and
columns at once. Such a shortcut would be a minimal dual codeword of a product code that is not an L.
None appeared in the searches.
