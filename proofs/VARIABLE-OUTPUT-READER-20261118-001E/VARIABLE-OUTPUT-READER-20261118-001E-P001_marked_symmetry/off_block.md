# A loose input cannot exchange with a shared distinction in another ordinary block

Let \(l\) be a loose member of a legal triple at a junction \(u\), and let \(e=uv\) be a shared distinction there. Choose a six-junction cycle from the three holders and their three distinct pair witnesses. If \(e\) lies outside the ordinary 2-connected block of that cycle, the transposition exchanging only \(l,e\) changes the full bond-distance table. This holds for all mixed counts and sizes. The claim concerns an actual transposition, which fixes the entries used as witnesses.

If \(e\) is an ordinary bridge, the scalar separator test distinguishes it from \(l\). Otherwise the component \(A\) of the ordinary graph minus \(u\) containing \(v\) is disjoint from the component \(B\) containing the other five cycle sites. Indeed, an ordinary path from \(v\) to the cycle avoiding \(u\), together with \(uv\), would put \(uv\) in the cycle's block. There are at least two edges from \(u\) into \(A\), since \(uv\) is not a bridge. Write \(o\) for the wired exterior. Two edge-disjoint paths go from \(u\) through its two cycle witnesses to the other marked holders in \(B\) and then to \(o\) through their loose marks. [Exact separator test](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/proof.md#which-branches-to-remove)

If any site of \(A\) precedes \(u\), the connected prefix before \(u\) is entirely in \(A\). The two cycle edges from \(u\) into \(B\) are outputs. Together with \(l\) they use all three possible outputs of \(u\). Thus \(B\) is its only other component besides \(A\). The wired network through \(B\) between \(u,o\) has ordinary minimum cut two, by the two paths and the two incident edges at \(u\). Remove this network and restore its two edges at \(u\) as loose outputs. The resulting presentation is reachable by omitting the \(B\)-sites in the original history. All parents of retained sites are still present and no removed site could witness a pair of distinct retained parents, since it only attached at \(u\). If \(l,e\) were interchangeable, the two-terminal bond formula would preserve their interchangeability in this reduced presentation. But \(u\) now holds three loose outputs, a case in which the exposed-site theorem forbids this transposition. [Two-terminal formula and exposed-site theorem](scalar_inverse.md#replacing-a-piece-attached-at-two-vertices)

Otherwise every site of \(A\) follows \(u\). Exactly two outputs of \(u\) go into \(A\), since \(l\) must remain loose. On its own \(A\) with \(u\) is reachable by starting at a ternary seed on those two outputs and \(l\). The restricted history takes only local parents. A site outside \(A\cup\{u\}\) cannot witness a pair of different parents in that set. The site \(v\) joins at \(u\) and can have at most one other parent. Three parents would require two different earlier neighbors of \(u\) in \(A\) besides \(v\), but there is only one other edge from \(u\) into \(A\). Thus the star of \(v\) has size at most five. For any output \(z\) of \(v\), this star gives \(D(e,z)+1\le5\). By ordinary simplicity \(z\) is not incident to \(u\).

Suppose a full bond of size at most five contained \(l,z\). Its ordinary shore contains \(u\) because \(l\) is loose. Each of the displayed paths through \(B\) contributes a different crossing. Restrict the shore to the reachable local presentation on \(A\cup\{u\}\). Its cut contains \(l,z\) and has size at most three. Every nonempty cut has size at least three there and every size-three cut is a ternary star. The only such star containing \(l\) could be at \(u\), which does not contain \(z\). This contradiction gives \(D(l,z)+1>5\). [Size-three cut characterization](scalar_inverse.md#short-old-equations)

Consequently a transposition of this particular loose and shared type that reverses a triple with holder \(u\) would have to act inside its six-cycle's ordinary block. A general isometry can move several distinctions and need not have this form.
