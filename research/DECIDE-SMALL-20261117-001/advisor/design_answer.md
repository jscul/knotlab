**Main changes I'd make:** don't stop at the first undecided pattern, cross-check the tests against each other, and revise two predictions that your own numbers contradict. This is from your description only; I ran nothing.

**(a) Missing tests**
- **Consistency check.** Running in order and stopping at the first decision hides implementation bugs. Run all five tests on every pattern, or a 10% subsample, and require that no pattern gets both "grows" and "bounded".
- **Ground truth.** Compute L_N(3, s) directly for s = 1..4 on about 50 decided patterns and compare with the verdicts.
- **Second tier.** Freeze stems of depth 3 and the two-block bond now as a separate tier, run only on tier-1 leftovers. You then report "undecided by the toolkit" and "undecided by its obvious extensions" separately, and the object of study isn't something a one-line generalisation of T4 kills.
- **Decorated test.** I'd leave it out until MODE-BOND's controls are in.

**(b) Dedup at depth 9**
Depth 9 is not proved safe. The clause set is generated by a product of the automaton with the placements, so two descriptions can in principle first differ well past depth 9. A false merge is the costly error, since it can swallow the undecided pattern. Cheap fixes:
- Recheck only the merged classes at depth 12 (or by window-tree type); singletons need nothing.
- Calibrate first on KEEP-REACH's 2,380: if the depth-9 and depth-12 partitions are identical, keep depth 9.
- Require that all descriptions in a merged class get the same verdict.

**(c) Stop rule**
- **Don't stop.** Run all draws, collect every undecided pattern, then take the first in draw order. The cost is the same order, you get a rate, and predictions about fractions need the full sample anyway.
- **Caps.** Label each undecided pattern "exhaustive" or "cap-limited" (T4's 8 members, T2's tiers, T5's l0 range). Rerun cap-limited ones at double the cap before they qualify.
- **Timeouts.** List them as a third class and rerun with a longer limit. As written, the hardest pattern can be skipped silently.

**(d) Predictions**
- **Toolkit decides everything: 30–35%, not 55%.** T3, T4 and T5 were each built for leftovers of the 583, so that success is in-sample. The new sample will likely have several times as many patterns. Against that, each late tool decided 5–6 patterns and none a single one, so unseen kinds are probably rare per pattern.
- **Distinct fraction: 10–25%, not 25–60%.** Your data give 583/2,380 = 24% and 181/786 = 23%, and the fraction falls as the sample grows.
- **T1 share: 50–80%, not ≥ 90%.** T1 decided 244/583 = 42% and 139/181 = 77%. Unless fresh fair-coin draws differ sharply from mutated ones, 90% contradicts both.
- **T3 + T4 + T5 ≤ 2%: keep, at about 65%.** It was 16/583 = 2.7% with mutants and 0% on the census.
- **Add two:** no cross-test contradiction (95%), and the depth-9 and depth-12 partitions agree (85%).

**What I expect:** one to three undecided patterns, at least one of them cap-limited rather than genuinely new.
