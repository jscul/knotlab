Brief cold review (~300 words) before I freeze held-out predictions. Flaws, overclaims, and whether the residual can be named.

Setting: complement-closed relations R ⊆ {0,1}^3 × {0,1}^3 (start ports, end ports), composed along call words. Forgetting means every long product is rectangular. Proved: a self-tie in some product (end bit j always equal or always opposite to start bit j) prevents forgetting. On single bits, complement-closed means full, equality or inequality, so every pairwise dependence is a tie.

Training half (6,321 families): no tie cycle ⇒ forgetting in all 2,126 chains and in 780 of 835 non-chains. Self-parity cycles (α·a ⊕ α·b constant, a coupling) never occur without a tie. Of 3,382 non-forgetting families, 3,322 have an identity cycle (a port passed unchanged), 5 have a tie forced through knots (3 equal, 2 opposite), and 55 have neither tie nor parity. In those 55 the recurring product is a knot-like coupling: a complementary pair of start states forbids some end states (fibers of size 7 or 8 of 8, sometimes 1 and 8), e.g. "not (start = 011 and end = 010)" plus its complement.

Planned held-out predictions:
H1: a tie cycle ⇒ non-forgetting, with 0 violations (a proof check).
H2: no tie cycle ⇒ forgetting in ≥ 99.5% of chains, and in 90% to 97% of non-chains, so "no tie cycle ⟺ forgetting" is refuted in non-chains.
H3: no self-parity cycle without a tie.
H4 (descriptive): the residual's recurring products are knot-like couplings.

Questions. (1) Is there a clean name or classification for non-rectangular complement-closed relations on 3+3 bits beyond ties and parities? Can "knot-like coupling" be defined precisely, so that "no tie cycle and no knot cycle ⟺ forgetting" becomes a theorem or near-tautology? (2) Are H2's bands sensible? (3) Anything presented as a test that is settled by construction?
