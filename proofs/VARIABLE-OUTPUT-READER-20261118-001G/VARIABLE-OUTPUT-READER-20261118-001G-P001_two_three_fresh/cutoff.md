# How far into the pair table one must read with two three-output joins

**Claim.** Suppose one lawful realization has junction arities at most four and exactly two additions making three fresh distinctions. For every pair read its least containing bond size \(K\) if it is at most five, and record just “larger” otherwise. These readings determine every junction support, hence all next choices, against every reachable rival. Reading only through four can give opposite verdicts even on a marked three-parent choice. Here the bond distance is \(D=K-1\), so the sufficient horizon in bond-distance units is four.

## Only one additional independent equation can fit

The short-equation theorem gives equality of all three- and four-distinction equations between source and rival using only the entries through \(K=4\). Every source site has at most four distinctions. Its site equations span its equation space \(L_H\), so \(L_H\subseteq L_R\). In any realization with \(j\) junctions and \(s\) three-fresh additions the total number of distinctions is \(w=3+2(j-1)+s\) and the equation-space rank is \(j\). The ordinary stars are independent since every nonempty sum has an output of its latest included site. For equal width and \(s_H=2\), containment allows a rival of the same rank, or one with one extra junction and exclusively two-fresh additions. [Short-equation theorem](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F-P001_finite_interval/structural.md#recovering-every-equation-through-size-four)

Every rival three-star is a source three-star. Every rival four-star is a source four-star, since the only other four-bit source equation is the sum of two adjacent three-stars and the rival's stars are independent. Let \(m\) count rival sites of arity at least five. Use the rival's physical stars as a basis for its cut space. The short source stars that are actual rival stars occupy distinct basis positions. Each of the other source stars has arity four and represents the boundary of a rival ordinary set containing at least three sites. Indeed, two-site four-bonds are sums of adjacent trivalent stars on both sides. The larger sets are parent-closed and linearly ordered by inclusion. For clarity, these conclusions use only the through-four data and reachability. [Proof of the star comparison and nesting](extension.md)

## The equal-rank case

This case gives \(L_H=L_R\), hence all three-parent permissions, immediately by the allowed-state theorem. It also gives equality of *all* pair entries because they are functions of the equation space. The full-table theorem in this family then identifies the actual sites and the entire next menu. This step does not assume that the triple menu alone determines the sites. ## Excluding a larger rank from truncated entries

For a strict rival every high site has arity five and used three parents. If there is a source four-star that is a large rival boundary, there are exactly \(m-1\) such stars. There are \(j_R-m\) common physical sites and \(j_H=j_R-1\) source sites. Modulo the common physical stars, the missing source stars have independent images because any relation between them would give a linear dependence among the source stars together with some of the common ones. These \(m-1\) images lie in the \(m\)-dimensional coordinate space indexed by the five-place sites. The image of a shore is its vector of memberships at those sites, because its boundary is the sum of the stars on that shore.

Let the smallest large shore contain \(r\) five-place sites. Every missing-star vector is one at each of those \(r\) coordinates. Every linear combination has all those coordinates equal, so for \(r\ge1\) its dimension is at most \(m-r+1\). If \(r=0\) the desired bound holds immediately. We conclude \(r\le2\). The shore can therefore be grown from the seed, uses at most two three-parent joins, has at least three sites and ends with exactly four loose distinctions.

The following finite closure proves this is impossible at **any history length**. In an all-two-fresh history the number of loose distinctions is \(3+u-t\), where \(u\) counts unary and \(t\) three-parent joins. A history ending with four and \(t\le2\) therefore has \(u\le3\) and at most six loose distinctions at every step. Keep the sites that still have loose distinctions, their loose counts and adjacencies, and, for each retired site, the subset of current sites it meets. Retain a subset only when it has at least two members and keep equal subsets once. Two or three active sites have a common neighbor precisely when they are contained in a retained subset or in an active site's neighbor set. A retired site cannot acquire new neighbors. At an addition, record the new site's adjacency to its parents, retire any newly exhausted parent together with its new adjacency, and restrict every mask to the current sites. Thus the state determines every allowed transition exactly. Count unary and three-parent additions, and count the number of sites up to three, retaining “three or more” thereafter. Permuting the active sites changes no transition. [Closure algorithm](frontiers.py)

Starting with the single seed, exhaustive closure gives 220 distinct states and 822 allowed transitions, including transitions returning to an earlier state. Of the states with at least three sites, 52 have five loose distinctions and 166 have six. The only state with four loose distinctions consists of exactly two sites. The original literal growth predicate was independently applied to a representative history for every transition. Complete closure, together with the state update, covers histories of unbounded length. [Fixed point and controls](frontiers.json)

It follows that no source four-star is a large rival boundary in the strict case. Exactly one rival site remains in addition to the common physical sites. Its five-place star has two fresh outputs, and the directed-cut argument gives a pair of those distinctions with \(K_H\ge6\) and \(K_R\le5\). The truncated observation through five detects the disagreement and excludes this last rival. [Five-place obstruction](extension.md)

## Four is insufficient

In a reachable width-27 example the two spaces have ranks 12 and 13. Every pair has the same observation when the cutoff is four, yet the very same three marked distinctions can join in the small-arity source and have already been consumed in the rival. The rival's extra junction introduces two distinctions whose least containing bond sizes are respectively seven in the source and five in the rival. Both sides use the same distinction names here, making the identity an explicit isometry of the truncated tables. The full tables even have different histograms. A width-29 source with two three-fresh additions supplies eight legal triples in another arrangement. This establishes sharpness within the family of readings that return exact pair minima up to a single threshold and coalesce all larger values. No restriction on the competing realization, no loose-status flag and no history are supplied to the reader.

## A second independent check at three additions

With exactly three source additions making three fresh distinctions, a strictly higher rank can again increase by only one. The rival then has exactly one three-fresh addition. As above, its innermost missing four-star shore could contain at most two high sites, hence at most two three-parent joins. If the exceptional output count is outside the shore, the all-two-fresh calculation already excludes it. If it is inside, the loose-count formula for the restricted prefix is \(4=3+u-t+1\). Thus \(u=t\le2\) and no intermediate prefix has over six loose bits. Allow exactly one additional fresh output in the same finite-state update and check every transition against the literal growth law. The closure has 323 states and 1,109 transitions across both values of the used-output flag. Every state with the flag set and at least three junctions has either five or six loose distinctions. This independently excludes the missing shore at arbitrary depth. [Mixed-count closure](frontiers_one_three.py) · [Receipts](frontiers_one_three.json)

With the common sites identified, a disputed rival junction has five or six distinctions, so the directed-cut bound detects it at \(K\le6\). The [width-26 six-place example](six_check.json) gives two legal histories with opposite triple decisions and 325 identical truncated pair readings through \(K=5\). This proves exactness at three additions. The [bounded separator argument](separator_horizon.md) gives the same upper bound for every number of three-fresh additions.

## The horizon for each number of three-fresh joins

For zero three-fresh additions take the seed with three unary children making two fresh distinctions each. Add one binary child on each pair of those children, again with two fresh distinctions. The resulting width-15 source is the seven-site core in the [named controls](six_check.json). Its last three sites have loose pairs \(\{9,10\},\{11,12\},\{13,14\}\). All three sites have arity four, so none of these six loose bits belongs to any three-bit equation, since each such equation is an actual trivalent star. The permutation swapping 10 and 11 therefore preserves the entire table through \(K=3\). It sends the legal triple \(\{9,11,13\}\) to \(\{9,10,13\}\), which repeats a holder and is forbidden. The older upper bound through four is already sharp for this zero-exception source.

A unary addition with three fresh distinctions can extend a comparison by one while preserving a truncated match and the two opposed verdicts. Attach at a common loose distinction \(b\) fixed by the comparison and separate from all candidate marks, matching the fresh outputs directly. Distances between old distinctions persist. A bond using a fresh bit and an old bit \(e\ne b\) must be the sum of the new four-bit junction and an old bond containing \(b,e\), cancelling the two occurrences of \(b\). Its minimum size is \(K(e,b)+2\). The pairs involving \(b\) and a new bit, or involving two new bits, have minimum size four. These formulas preserve the truncated match for any common cutoff. The old permissions persist because a unary child has just one ordinary parent and cannot be a common witness for several old holders. Repeat on a fresh loose output. [One-attachment proof](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001C/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001C-P001_triple_inverse/proof.md#how-one-attachment-changes-a-bond-distance)

Apply this extension once at the fixed bit 14 of the width-15 example to get the lower bound for exactly one three-fresh join. For any exact count \(s\ge3\), apply it \(s-3\) times to the width-26 paired histories with the first new join on their common loose bit 25. The observation through five still agrees and the marked triple still has opposite verdicts. Thus the sharp bond-distance horizon \(D\) is **three for zero or one three-fresh addition, four for exactly two, and five for each larger count**. In all three comparisons the rival is free to use the full original law.
