**Why the predecessor's seventh prediction failed.** Even birth terms commute on different readers. Only the product of the two odd terms changes sign on exchange. It requires both parents occupied, where their even terms vanish. Thus $K_iK_j=K_jK_i(-1)^{n_i n_j}$. Changing the listing transports each basis ket and every operator by the occupied-permutation sign. Exchanging actual births at fixed preparation and readings changes relative coefficients. The earlier fresh-vacuum tables alone could not identify the birth operator on already-occupied fresh modes.

**Why the crossing criterion holds.** Each occupied parent contributes two odd factors, at creation and birth. Grouping these by reader costs an odd number of interchanges exactly when two pairs of events alternate. The within-reader signs multiply to +1 on even words. For the resulting sign $(-1)^{q(x)}$, put $B(x,t)=q(x+t)+q(x)+q(t)+q(0)$ modulo two. The terms in the real correlation have the same sign exactly when $B(x,t)=x\cdot t$ for all even $x$. With the complex flip the corresponding condition is $B(x,t)=0$. Testing on the even-word basis $e_1+e_j$ shows that a pair relation has zero polar form exactly when its pairs are those across a split. This gives the two criteria for every even flip set $t$. In a round, reversing precisely the comparisons across a split requires both groups to be contiguous in creation order, by transitivity. Their exchange is a cyclic shift.
