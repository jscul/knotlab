## What should differ between the two training arms

Give the unchanged reader 64 presentations in each arm. Pair training slots on true letter, turn, placement, and the ink, stroke dimensions or base design intended to be controlled. Record explicitly which features of the drawing are allowed to change. Ordering the slots once for both arms avoids confounding a change of drawing with a different sequence of feedback. With binary guesses, the issued action and its correctness together convey the target exactly. An identical target schedule and the same majority-based acquisition policy give identical issued training guesses and correctness strings. The bitmaps can still write different observed counts. Count repetitions and unique shape identities in each cohort. A comparison of two fixed cohorts can identify the effect of those replacements on the fixed reader and evaluation panel. A population effect of increasing drawing diversity would need independent samples of cohorts.

Choose the pen variants by a visible letter-drawing rule. For example, an upright L has a descending stroke that bends to the right toward its lower end, while an upright T has a crossbar whose interior joins a descending stem. ## Which evidence would identify the training effect

For each held class, turn and blind design, record the matched directions of change: narrow wrong and varied correct, narrow correct and varied wrong, both correct, and both wrong. Use the same frozen threshold for each arm and both holding laws. A claim that varied exposure succeeds where narrow exposure fails requires the varied arm to meet the complete gate and the narrow arm to miss it. An improvement that leaves a class or turn below the gate is partial. If the two arms are equally accurate, their paired predictions can still disagree. Give exact coverage by underlying drawing as well as by bitmap. Shared padded windows make all translations of a given drawing and turn identical to the reader.

An opposite-reward swap can reverse the class calibration while leaving selected groups identical. A same-convention swap between the narrow and varied fits tests the actual change of interest. Read all four combinations of narrow or varied membership and narrow or varied class calibration with the same pixels, holding the remaining registers fixed. Compare the results with restoration, sham, clearing, and the ordinary arithmetic twin. This shows when changed membership, changed calibration or their combination alters a decision. If only the full fit is exchanged, attribute the result to the full fitted state. Preserve the original threshold tie, midpoint abstention, full maximizer family and complement convention.

This is positive if the class contains both counts.
