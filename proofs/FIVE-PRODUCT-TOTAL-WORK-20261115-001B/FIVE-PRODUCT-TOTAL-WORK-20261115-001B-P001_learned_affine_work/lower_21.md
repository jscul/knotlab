## Twenty-one paid operations with arbitrarily many guarded scalar facts

Under the same one-unit charges, every certificate costs at least **21**. The argument allows any finite acyclic collection of scalar implications with conjunctions of visible affine equations as guards, including closing citations. A source check returns the entire local affine-hull block. Source checks whose results will be used in another component require a named derivation and its charged citations.

Use \(E\) for the number of plain calls whose guarded affine axiom excludes at least one of the 64 global rich rows. The previously checked source estimate gives \(E\ge6\). The union of their exclusions covers all rich rows. Among the 256 plain rows, consider the 192 that have exactly one wrong product in the three-pair coordinates. They must all be excluded by rich source axioms. Each rich call excludes at most 48 such rows; at least five rich calls are necessary by the source census. The proof below uses these two previously checked bounds. It does not use the census of triples of 48-point maximizers.

Normalize the certificate as follows. Keep the full current affine assumptions in each ordinary component. If a source is evaluated on an enclosing guard, evaluate it on the actual current guard instead and specialize its continuation. The smaller actual hull satisfies every old affine conclusion there. Its paid cost cannot increase. Guards of named bodies retain their declared starting assumptions. A once-used scalar derivation refuting the guard together with the negation of its conclusion can be inlined by a split at its actual guarded use, putting its restricted refutation on the negative branch. The one citation pays for the split. A once-used contradiction simply inlines; unreachable declarations can be omitted.

Whenever an effective plain check is nonempty, its resulting affine case consists entirely of actual plain rows, as shown below. The rich source is empty there. Replace the entire ordinary continuation by the immediate unit-cost rich-empty check. Even a closing citation would have cost a unit, and the nonempty affine case could not close for zero. This move preserves the enclosing lemma's obligation if the check occurred in its body. Repeat with single-use inlining and removal of dead declarations. Nontrivial steps decrease work or, at equal work, eliminate citations or declarations. Each nonempty effective plain call now has its own immediate rich terminal. Sources are checked at their actual affine guards throughout. This normalization is important: counting potential violations of a weaker enclosing guard in place of a terminal's actual pure-plain guard would overcount.

If no genuine reuse remains, the ordinary-tree lower is 23. Otherwise let \(m\ge1\) be the number of retained lemma bodies and \(c,q\) the numbers of citations and questions. Each body is used at least twice, giving \(c\ge2m\). After removing citation references the proof has \(m+1\) ordinary components and \(q+m+1\) leaves in total. Effective plain calls are on distinct final branches within this forest, since each either directly closes or ends in its normalized rich-empty call. Thus \(E\le q+m+1\) and \(q+c\ge E\). With \(R\) rich calls the total is at least \(2E+R\), even if there are other plain calls.

### Plain guards in six original coordinates

On an effective plain guard \(C\), project to the six input coordinates \(x\) and the total \(s\). The two private outputs are unrestricted on the plain side. The projected affine space is either a free \(s\) over an affine input domain \(T\), or the graph of an affine \(s=\ell(x)\) on \(T\). Every nonempty fibre has the same direction kernel; lifting the plain hull from this projection gives its exact hull in \(C\).

If \(s\) is free, effectiveness requires \(Q|_T\) to be affine, since the graph of a nonaffine Boolean function spans \(T\) times its value bit. If \(s\) is pinned, the nonspanning plain level of \(Q+\ell\) is affine or empty. Indeed its indicator vanishes on the complementary half of some affine hyperplane and hence is the product of its defining literal and an affine function. If the factors have dependent linear parts, \(Q\) is affine on \(T\). In all affine cases \(T\) has isotropic direction for the nonsingular polar form \(J\), and extends to one affine three-dimensional Lagrangian coset. There are no more than eight original rich input words in the guard. Its pure-plain result, if nonempty, lies over at most eight words and the immediate rich call excludes at most \(3\cdot8=24\) singleton-error rows.

In the remaining case the factors have independent linear parts. The restriction of \(J\) to the direction of \(T\) has rank two. Hence \(\dim T\le4\): in a six-dimensional nondegenerate space the radical has dimension at most \(6-\dim T\). The plain level is the single affine coset where both literals are one, of size \(|T|/4\le4\). Its full inverse image in \(C\) is already affine, proving the needed pure-plain property. Its rich terminal excludes at most twelve singleton-error rows. The rich input words originally hit by \(C\) lie among the other at most twelve words. In particular a guard hitting more than eight rich words has this second type. A guard reaching twelve hits precisely one maximal twelve-point set of the preceding case theorem.

Write such a maximal set as \(K=(v+U^\perp)\setminus D\), where \(U\) is a two-dimensional isotropic subspace and \(D\) is one four-point \(U\)-coset in that enclosure. Every rich exclusion of an effective plain call fits inside some such \(K\), by the predecessor's complete terminal-set characterization. To apply that characterization to a raw guard, take the rich hull there and project to \((x,s)\). It is inside the raw guard. Since the plain restriction there was already affine and excluded all those rich points, free separate saturation closes this rich set.

### Two finite covers

1. **One affine Lagrangian and five maximal twelve-point sets do not cover the 64 inputs.** Fix a Lagrangian. Of the 5,040 maximal sets, 756 are disjoint from it. In a possible five-set cover of the 56 inputs outside it, at least one set must be disjoint from it since \(5\cdot12-56=4\). There are only two first-set cases. The file `cap_obstructions.py` enumerates all 1,080 Lagrangian cosets, checks transitivity on them, and explicitly constructs first-set orbits of sizes 84 and 672. The stabilizer uses translations of the first three input bits, the seven transvections \(x\mapsto x+J(x,v)v\) for nonzero \(v\) in the distinguished Lagrangian, and the six elementary paired shears preserving the pairing between the two triples of input coordinates. It verifies that every map is invertible, affine, and preserves \(J\) on directions. The larger action also includes the remaining three translations and the three individual pair swaps. There is no assumption that the displayed generators exhaust the full abstract symmetry group.

   For each seed, choose the first uncovered point. A successful cover contains a remaining set through that point which leaves at most \(12(k-1)\) uncovered when \(k\) slots remain. The search tries every such set and tests literal containment at the final slot. The numbers of recursive calls at budgets \(1,2,3,4\) were respectively \((464,4220,871,1)\) and \((1438,8282,768,1)\). Both seeds fail. As a positive control the same recursion accepts the six directly terminal predecessor sets; their actual bit masks are retained in the output.

2. **Two maximal twelve-point sets and five Lagrangians cannot partition the inputs.** A Lagrangian coset disjoint from a maximal set \(K\) avoids its entire sixteen-point enclosure. For its direction \(L\), \(\dim(L\cap U^\perp)=1+\dim(L\cap U)\), using \(L^\perp=L\). If the coset met the enclosure but avoided \(K\), its intersection would fit inside the one missing \(U\)-coset, contradicting this dimension formula. Consequently a proposed partition must have each of its two maximal sets cover the other one's entire missing coset. Their directions \(U_1,U_2\) satisfy \(U_1\subset U_2^\perp\) and conversely. If they coincide, the intersecting enclosures coincide and the two twelve-point sets overlap. Otherwise \(U_1+U_2\) is isotropic of dimension three. The two enclosures then intersect in an affine three-space containing both missing cosets. The missing affine two-spaces have distinct directions, so intersect there, whereas each was to lie in the other set which excludes its own missing coset. The checker independently tests every possible second set after verifying transitivity on all first sets.

### Counting the only low-work cases

* If \(E=6\), none of the six rich exclusions can fit in a Lagrangian. Enlarging one such exclusion to the Lagrangian and the other five to maximal sets would contradict cover 1. Thus all six effective plain calls have the pinned rank-two type, are nonempty, and have mandatory rich terminals excluding at most twelve singleton-error rows each. With only eight rich calls total, at most \(6\cdot12+2\cdot48=168\) rows could be excluded. Hence \(R\ge9\) and \(2E+R\ge21\).

* If \(E=7\), at least two guards are wide (hit more than eight rich words) to cover 64. Three wide guards together with only three further rich calls could exclude at most \(3\cdot12+3\cdot48=180\) singleton-error rows. With at most six rich calls we must therefore have exactly two wide guards. Their hit bound \(2\cdot12+5\cdot8=64\) leaves no slack: these hits partition the original inputs into two full maximal twelve-point sets and five eight-point sets. If two or more of the five narrow guards are nonempty, two wide and two narrow terminals plus two other rich calls would cover at most \(2\cdot12+2\cdot24+2\cdot48=168\). If exactly one is nonempty it must attain 24, since \(2\cdot12+24+3\cdot48=192\). This forces eight projected plain inputs, whence \(Q\) is affine on its input domain and the domain is a Lagrangian of size eight. Its eight rich hits fill that same Lagrangian. Every empty plain guard with eight rich hits also fills a Lagrangian. Thus whether zero or one narrow guard is nonempty the partition contradicts cover 2. Hence \(R\ge7\) and \(2E+R\ge21\).

* If \(E\ge8\), the separate rich-source census gives \(R\ge5\), so \(2E+R\ge21\).

The 23-operation tree remains an upper bound when sharing is permitted. The argument leaves 21 and 22 undecided. The three-maximizer residual computation is optional independent evidence: a separately implemented direct affine-hull enumeration found 372 distinct 48-row exclusions, 2,944 disjoint triples, and zero triples whose three residual 16-input supports coincide. None of the routing cases above depends on this triple count.
