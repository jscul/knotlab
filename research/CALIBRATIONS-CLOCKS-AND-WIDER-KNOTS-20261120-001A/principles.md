# What a certain ordinary answer can imply

With the second binary question supplied, let $w$ be an ordinary word, $t\ne0$ a proposed translation and $R_z(w)=(-1)^{z\cdot w}$ an ordinary parity. Conditional support means the set of ordinary words still possible on the observed outcome. ## Full pair

With P1 interpreted as a fresh preparation on the resulting ordinary set after a compatible read, a full pair prepares exactly $U=\{w,w+t\}$. Since $U+t=U$, fresh P2 gives certain plus. No sign representation is needed. If P2 itself quantifies over all conditional preparations and refers to their displayed supports, it already states this conclusion. If P1 describes only the fresh source before conditioning and P2 only a fresh source test, the implication fails.

With just fresh support $S$, let $D=\{d:S+d=S\}$. Supply an independent fair sign to each class under $D$ and remove the mean correlations between distinct classes. On a fresh proposed change $t$, the mean is $1$ for $t\in D$ and zero otherwise. This prescription is computed entirely from $S$. On the isolated not-all-equal ternary, $D=\{000,111\}$. Revealing the other two marks while proposing the first bit conditions on two full pairs and two singletons, with ordinary masses $2/6,2/6,1/6,1/6$. Retaining the original classes reports a fair sign even on either full pair. Restarting the same prescription on the conditional set gives plus on each full pair and a fair sign on each singleton. Thus the label objection concerns conditional memory. A set-only fresh rule that restarts can still give an unexposed plus chance $1/2$ and a revealed-then-forgotten chance $5/6$.

With the ordinary parity readings available, the $d-1$ independent parities orthogonal to $t$ on $d$ marks determine exactly its unordered pair. To average calibrated pair answers into a preparation's unconditioned answer requires their ordinary masses **and** agreement between forgetting these compatible reports and leaving them unread. With these supplied and $n=|S|$, $m=|S\cap(S+t)|$, the change expectation is $m/n$ and the chance of plus is $(n+m)/(2n)$. A compatible ordinary parity stays constant in a pair and supplies its sign in the average. The full archived tables additionally use the earlier ordered signs, the identification of compatible products with products of recorded answers and finite inversion of the binary sign moments. P1 and P2 alone do not identify all these joint measurements. ## Singleton

With the first proposal read as an **actual change of the word**, choose a separating ordinary parity $Z$ with $z\cdot t=1$. Its initial answer $r$ is certain on the singleton. Let $A$ be the reported binary sign and $Z'$ the later ordinary answer in the same trials. P3 gives $Z'=-r$ in every trial. It says nothing about $A$. If P4 says that the *initial* certain sign factors even when this ordinary question is asked **after** the change, it supplies $\mathbb E[A Z']=r\mathbb E[A]$. Direct arithmetic on the recorded signs gives $\mathbb E[A Z']=-r\mathbb E[A]$. Hence $\mathbb E[A]=0$, so both chances equal $1/2$. This argument uses ordinary probabilities for signs. It needs a product of the two reported values on the same preparation and uses a certain initial answer to constrain a later **incompatible** read. A certain-before product alone gives $r\mathbb E[A]$ whatever the bias. If marginal expectations in the axiom come from separate experiments, equality of the distributions of the compared $A$ reports is also required. A compatible-only P4 leaves the bias arbitrary.

With $\Pr(A=+)=\Pr(A=-)=1/2$, a word actually flipped from a singleton has its later parity opposite in **both** outcome classes. Thus the signed average can factor despite maximal disturbance of that parity. If P4 also demanded $\mathbb E[1_{A=a}Z']=r\Pr(A=a)$ on each recorded sign, both signs would have zero probability. Even the requirement $\mathbb E[Z']=r$ after the nonselective flip conflicts with P3. If P4 includes affine outcome projectors or arbitrary unsigned event indicators, this version is inconsistent with the actual-flip interpretation. One cannot replace the signed-product axiom by an ordinary rule that known events remain true.

With a *formal* product one can instead supply two-sided factorization on an initially certain ordinary sign, linear sign reversal of its expectation, and the order relation $ZC=-CZ$ for this mask. The two factorizations give $\langle ZC\rangle=\langle CZ\rangle=r\langle C\rangle$ and the order relation gives opposite left sides. This also forces zero. The usual word transport and signed ordinary evaluation explain the order relation because an ordinary sign reverses upon the transport. An operator product and two chronological recorded readouts are different experiments. No Hilbert-space representation was assumed in the arithmetic derivation. As one implementation of the formal one, a sharp sign measurement on a single word leaves $(|w\rangle\pm|w+t\rangle)/\sqrt2$. A subsequent separating ordinary value is opposite with probability $1/2$. The unitary translation certainly produces the opposite word and has no reported binary sign without an extra response rule. A full pair provides an explicit ordering control. Its positive uniform weighting has formal $\langle ZXZ\rangle=-1$. In a chronological ordinary-read, change-sign, ordinary-read trial the first read prepares a singleton and the mean product of the three recorded signs is zero for each of three continuations. The chance that the final ordinary value differs from the first is 1 for an actual flip, $1/2$ for selective projection, and zero for a stationary singleton coin. The assumption that an initially certain sign factors cannot move an initially uncertain $Z$ through this three-question sequence.

## What removing each principle permits

With P2 imposed on fresh inputs and P4 on signed products with initially certain ordinary values, take a word uniformly in the prepared support $R$. An explicit common reference rule flips the actual word, says plus on each complete pair and generates a fair sign on a singleton. To see that longer signed products are consistent on a fixed preparation, give words in $R$ the phase +1 and each exterior word a fresh independent fair phase. On moving from $x$ to $y$, record the product of their phases. Along any fixed string of moves the phases telescope to the two endpoint phases. All intervening ordinary sign readings are deterministic at the travelled words. If a separating initial certainty is reversed at the end, the last word lies outside $R$ and its independent phase averages the whole signed product to zero. When it is preserved, it factors directly. Initial ordinary reads multiply the actual sign. On an informative compatible preparatory read, use $R$ as the newly exposed set. For continuations preserving the read value, the exterior phases outside its sector are immaterial. These auxiliary phases give one stochastic implementation whose distribution is fixed by the prepared set. This construction specifies products of recorded binary signs in a fixed experiment and the explicit conditional preparation. Adaptive selection of individual outcomes and unsigned identity insertions are stronger axioms.

- Without P1's conditional restart, retain the initial $D$-class on a newly exposed pair. More explicitly, on each preparatory $R$ take the group $D_{\rm initial}\cap\{d:R+d=R\}$. Give its classes in $R$ independent fair phases and give distinct exterior words independent fair phases. Use the same endpoint response. On fresh $R=S$, translations in $D$ answer plus. The telescoping argument still factors certain signed products, and every act flips. A previously exposed full pair need not be contained in one initial class. This fails line 1 while respecting fresh P2. If P2 is stipulated anew on every displayed conditional set, deleting P1 cannot defeat line 1.
- Without P2, assign every word an independent fair phase, including both words of a full pair. The same telescoping argument applies and gives fair signs on a complete pair. Preparing a narrower set uses the same prescription on that set. P1, actual P3 and signed P4 hold.
- Without P3, keep the word and report plus. Every initially certain ordinary sign is unchanged in either position in any signed product. P1, P2 and even the stronger nondisturbance reading hold. The singleton always reports plus.
- Without after-order P4, actually flip and always report plus. This obeys P1, P2 and P3. Any initially certain ordinary sign compatible with all proposed moves still factors. On a singleton with an incompatible read the later product is $-r$. Thus compatible-only P4 cannot replace precisely the after-order premise used in the proof.
