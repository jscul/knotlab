# Lasting sources and meetings in ordinary growth

John's words, verbatim: "there are underlying rules that can exist that don't destroy each other but can create things. This is kind of like a generator in conway's game of life." He calls two lasting things that affect each other and both persist "a huge part of the theory".

**Ordinary growth can do both.** A finite source keeps producing new, distinguishable families of knots. After release, every parent of every ongoing member comes from its own family. Two such families can make a shared child while both continue indefinitely. A released family can also become a source in its own right. The proofs hold under parity and not-all-equal. Parentage supplies the order. No values, motion rule, feedback rule or clock were supplied. Independent referee pending.

**Source.** The smallest construction in the class covered by the fixed-map theorem has seven starting knots and 25 distinctions, minimizing knots first. Its first periodic products leave the source at cluster sizes 16, 24, 32 and then every eight. The simple mechanism is a choice between two return paths. A product that makes a distinctive return once every p positions becomes recognizable in every phase when a window contains two complete periods. At that size both its parents still belong to the source. Thereafter every parent belongs to the product. The complete present holdings let the reader recover the paths and observe both periods after arbitrary renaming. The looser requirement of just one parent in the packet gives a different verdict even on a bare five-knot ring. [Source proof](source.md) · [Reader controls](controls.md)

**Meeting.** In a connected 21-knot preparation two packets first release at size 16 and produce a mixed knot at size 17. Each still supplies all of its own continuing parents. A descendant of the mixed knot can take another input from a surviving party at every later size. A connected comparison keeps both parties and all their pure continuations but places their returns apart. They never make that shared child there. At the first release a corresponding productive knot receives 18 fresh distinctions in the joined preparation and 15 in the separated preparation. Its following pure child receives 18 versus 16. These prices follow from the prepared holdings. The pure production rate stays the same. The earlier three-knot comparison gives the simpler effect of two versus one single-parent births on an untouched holder. The two-ring controls with existing rings show mixed ancestry without a birth directly between the two wholly independent ring continuations. [Encounter and matched budgets](meeting.md) · [Built comparisons](fixtures.md)

**Minimum and timing.** Seven knots and 25 distinctions also suffice for a meeting that changes which two products can produce a joint child, under a second observer that explicitly recognizes a family of periodic words. The first case in that test gives two straight mixed births at size 160 for packets of 76 and 88 phases released at size 159. Exchanging one party's arm gives zero mixed births between those two parties in the same start. All four phase cycles coexist there, so the seven-knot comparison does not change an individual's total production. The 21-knot comparison does. The joint seven-knot minimum is proved only within the class whose ordinary growth is identified with connected clusters of the unrolled map. A competing original start outside it would need at least three knots. Eight knots can use 24 distinctions, so minimizing distinctions alone is another question. A first direct mixed birth must follow release by one parent step: after an intervening all-own generation the pure parties no longer have a common parent. The witness opportunity is present at release, even when their mixed descendants continue forever. [Compact proof](compact_meeting.md) · [Bound and enumeration](minima.md) · [One-step obstruction](components.md)

**Products making products.** In the 21-knot preparation, retain a released family that can take either of two returns at a fixed spacing. Inside that family a second return can occur once every ℓ+1 cycles. Each such period launches its own independently continuing product. The intermediate source and all its products have disjoint ongoing members. The basic periodic packet with only its two pure transitions cannot itself keep releasing independent subpackets. On withholding other births the pure incidence persists. Restarting a packet alone with the same loose holdings changes its output and fresh counts. A source that emits indefinitely many identical products with one bound on their size remains open. [Nested proof](nested.md) · [Solo replay](meeting.md)

## Final report as sent

2026-11-20 12:48 PST

**Yes. Ordinary growth can keep a source active, repeatedly produce things that continue on their own, and let two of them make something together while both survive.** The constructions work under parity and not-all-equal. The result is referee pending.

**The source.** Seven knots and 25 distinctions suffice. The first products leave at cluster sizes 16, 24 and 32, then every eight sizes. At release, every parent belongs to the source. From then on, every parent of every continuing member belongs to that product. This is the smallest start, counting knots first, in the class where the full parent structure is proved from the start. The unrestricted minimum is open. A single catalogue ring can persist indefinitely, but its surviving descendants share ancestors that prevent it from repeatedly releasing separate lasting products. [Source proof](https://github.com/jscul/knotlab/blob/main/research/GENERATORS-20261120-001/source.md)

**The meeting.** In a 21-knot start, two products emerge at size 16 and make a shared child at size 17. Both keep growing at their previous rates. Descendants of their shared child can keep taking inputs from one surviving product forever. In a matched start that keeps both products apart, the shared birth disappears, and a corresponding knot receives **15 fresh distinctions instead of 18**. The opportunity for direct contact must already be present at release. After one intervening generation in which each product supplies all its own parents, a first direct meeting is impossible. [Matched preparations and timing proof](https://github.com/jscul/knotlab/blob/main/research/GENERATORS-20261120-001/meeting.md)

**A product can itself be a source.** One released family keeps taking either of two returns. Within it, a less frequent return gives rise to independently continuing products of its own. The limit for the generator analogy is that successive releases use larger patterns. Whether one source can emit the *same finite thing* indefinitely is open. [Nested construction](https://github.com/jscul/knotlab/blob/main/research/GENERATORS-20261120-001/nested.md)

**Evidence.** The size check exhausted 996 connected starting shapes and 117,142 choices of live knots through seven knots. In the separate sample, 75/240 uniform labelled trees funded a first step, compared with 32/240 fresh such trees given one uniformly chosen extra meeting. All 480 passed both knot laws. Fourteen of 16 archived explosive starts qualified under each law. These panels tested the growth controls. The frequency of generators remains unknown.
