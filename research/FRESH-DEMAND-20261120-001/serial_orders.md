# The marked birth under different orders

The marked event has exactly the two original parents (U,V). A birth that also takes a third parent is a different event. The literal pair of sources uses seven knots, 71 distinctions, nine shared distinctions per ordinary meeting, and four versus two loose distinctions at (Z). Moving two loose distinctions from (Z) to (W) leaves all other supports and names fixed. Both laws give the same outcomes below. ## What follows from supplying the whole first-layer plan

The complete round plans one (U,V) birth with two fresh distinctions when (W) is spent, and one with one fresh distinction when (W) is live. In the first case (U,V) each also give a unary birth, and (Z) gives four. In the second case (U,W) and (V,W) give the two other pair births, and (Z) gives two unary births. The complete-layer demands at the marked birth are respectively two and one.

All (7!=5{,}040) orders of the seven planned births and all (5!=120) orders of the five planned births give the same respective literal support tables. The plan assigns distinct old inputs and distinct fresh names to each birth. Each order can write its current constraint on those names and preserve every preceding state by choosing its own fresh values. Conjoining the same written constraints gives the same allowed relation in every order, for parity and for not-all-equal. The two-versus-one result follows for every order given that complete plan, including the precomputed counts. ## What follows when the current stage chooses each move

The present-state rule lists groups among knots with loose distinctions. It removes a group if some earlier birth took all its parents. A knot can give a unary birth only when it has more loose distinctions than remaining groups. After each chosen event the rule spends one distinction per parent. It then computes the newborn's demand among the newborn and all knots that still have loose distinctions, using the ordinary group function before the prior-common-child exclusion. With joins-first, multi-parent moves have priority over every unary move. The checker uses the archived implementation and checks its group lists against exhaustive sets at the visited states. Firing (U,V) as the first move gives no current demand and one fresh distinction in either arm. When (W) is spent, doing the unary births at (U) and (V) first leaves both original parents live. The marked birth then has two distinct current groups, one with each unary child. It gets two fresh distinctions. Reordering the two initial loose positions at each parent leaves the physical supports fixed and gives this history on the same literal inputs that the whole-layer plan reserves. With the default archived first-loose choice, those original input names can instead be used by earlier births. The structural event is marked by its parents throughout.

The unrestricted loss can occur at move three. In the spent arm take a unary child of (U), then a unary child of that child, then join the latter with (U). This uses (U)'s last loose distinction. In the live arm start with (U,W), then give that child a unary child, then join the latter with (U). Both orders are currently admitted, give a concrete permanent loss of the exact target and have a continuing separate part at (Z).

The live arm's joins-first loss uses a genuinely new move. First make (X) on (U,W) and (Y) on (V,W), in either order. Each has one fresh distinction. The move on (X,Y) is now legal through their shared parent (W). If it fires before (U,V), call its new knot (R). The new group ({U,V,R}) has separate witnesses (S,X,Y), and no one of them meets all three. It replaces the exact pair in the list and takes both original parents' last loose distinctions. If (U,V) fires instead at any of its earlier opportunities, it receives one fresh distinction. All joins-first prefixes resolve in at most four moves. The spent arm admits just the marked pair initially under joins-first.

### Bounds beyond the replay and the role of fairness

No two original live knots on opposite sides of the split ({U,V,W}) and ({Z}) are adjacent or have a common neighbour. New knots meet only their parents. Inductively a birth within one part adds neither a shared distinction nor a common witness between the live parts. Each keeps at least one loose distinction, since a move confined to one part takes a proper subset of all loose distinctions. It is nonimplied under parity, and the inherited not-all-equal implication test also requires a fresh distinction. This keeps the two parts open at every finite stage. [Not-all-equal implication lemma](../NAE-GROWTH-20261119-001/nae_growth.md#why-it-holds)

When the exact pair finally fires, each original parent can have at most one earlier child since it began with two loose distinctions. The newborn's only possible live co-candidates are those earlier children. (S) is spent and cannot contribute. This bounds its demand by two. With (W) live, the first core move before the exact target can only be (U,W) or (V,W). Its child has one fresh distinction. To give the exact pair two separate current groups, that child would have to stay live and the other original parent would need an earlier live child. Before spending that first child's loose distinction, the only other available core move apart from the exact target is the opposite pair using (W). The two children then share (W), so after the marked birth the three form one group through separate witnesses. A later spending of the first child's only loose distinction can only remove that child from the demand reading. Thus the live-arm exact pair always needs one fresh distinction, at any depth.

An unrestricted order can keep making moves in the (Z) part. It has an eligible next move whenever it has an opening, and it cannot close while the core still has openings. Such an infinite order keeps the exact (U,V) opportunity enabled and is unfair. Under weak fairness, every continuously eligible event must eventually occur. In any infinite history each written knot has only finitely many eventual ordinary neighbours, because its support is fixed at birth. The candidate neighbourhood of fixed (U,V), its common witnesses, and all its loose-status flags therefore stabilize. If the exact event has neither fired nor been permanently lost, (S) still makes the pair a candidate. The eventual list then contains either the exact pair or a larger group through both that remains continuously eligible. A prior common child excluding that larger group would already exclude the exact pair. Fairness forces the exact event or a larger event that permanently precludes it. The finite witnesses of firing and of loss can all be extended fairly by giving precedence to the longest waiting eligible event. Fairness settles indefinite postponement of the decision, while the finite loss examples show that it does not force the exact birth.

### Allowed-state checks

The parity projection on each of the seven original supports has exactly its one even equation. The not-all-equal source check uses incomparable supports and shared bundles of multiplicity at least two. Any unpinned position in a bundle can complete it with both values, satisfying both holders. If every shared position at another knot is pinned, incomparability leaves a free loose distinction there for satisfying its constraint. This extends every locally valid assignment from a chosen original support. On the smaller twofold-bundle control, all 434 locally allowed assignments in each arm were also checked through the complete not-all-equal formula. Both primary and smaller controls have the same explicit event witnesses.

For each checked current event the replay verifies that all assignments on the old consumed inputs have extensions, using the full old parity basis or literal not-all-equal SAT. A positive fresh count permits every previous state to extend. The full old-input projection and the independently checked local relation give K1–K3 for the newborn. A false parity source that forces a proper projection and an underfunded complete step are both rejected. The replay checks exhaustion and the absence of a prematurely implied event without using either as a substitute for the marked pair. The same projection gate persists along the unbounded histories used above. In parity the old inputs form a proper subset of the loose roster of a connected stage, so no nonzero parity constraint is supported on them. In not-all-equal, fixing one consumed distinction per parent leaves each old knot at least two unfixed places. Apply the inherited two-colouring argument to these unfixed distinctions. Every component other than an odd cycle supplies both values at each old knot. In an odd cycle each old knot has degree two and therefore has one fixed consumed distinction. Colour alternately except at one chosen knot, and choose its repeated colour opposite to its fixed value. Any prescribed assignment on the consumed distinctions then extends. This gives K1–K3 for each new support together with the positive fresh count, and each old support keeps its previous projected relation.
