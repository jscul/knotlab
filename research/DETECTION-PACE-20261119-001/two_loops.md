## Which two structural effects combine

Write the attachment positions as `s,t` with `t−s=2m`. The cold original knots at `s+2,s+4,…,t−2` have two loose distinctions each. In the first layer their only possible multi-parent groups are the pairs to their even-index neighbors two old steps away. When both rings are loaded, the endpoint groups using `s` and `t` are vetoed. The new roster on this interior part consists of the released left unary, the consecutive inner pair births and the released right unary. At separation four the two released unaries share the same midpoint parent and can exchange their names. The criterion there is that a later knot descends from both distinct unaries.

There are `m` first-layer knots in this roster. Their parent-set intersection graph is a path. Each has two fresh distinctions and only consecutive members have an ordinary common parent. That gives one binary birth per consecutive pair and one unary for each surplus end distinction. The next intersection graph is a path with one more knot, and all again receive two fresh distinctions. Hence at layer `a` the selected path has `m+a−1` knots. The first-layer ancestry of a new knot, in ordered path position `j` starting at zero, is the interval from `max(0,j−a+1)` to `min(m−1,j)`. This follows by taking the union of consecutive intervals for a binary and retaining the end interval for a unary. One common descendant first appears at layer `m`, and there are `a−m+1` at every later completed layer. Their original ancestors are all strictly between the attachments. With either load removed at least one of the released endpoint unaries is missing. Each stage of the recurrence agrees with the literal results through layer ten.

At separation four, the two first children touching the same old midpoint have parent counts `[2,2]`, `[1,2]`, `[1,2]` and `[1,1]` in four-load order. There are exactly two in every preparation. Only the fourth has two distinct unary siblings, with their common child at layer two. In the literal record these are first children 47 and 48 and second child 124. Its only original ancestor is midpoint `b6`.

## What can pass the other attachment

Fix the left donor's inward unary at `s+2`, and the fixed far old holder `b_(t+2)`. With the right ring quiet, the first binary groups on `(s+2,s+4), …, (t,t+2)` all survive. Their first-layer children, preceded by the released unary, form a sequence with each consecutive pair eligible. Once the old loose distinctions have been spent, the whole next growing end is quiet. For any eligible pair the complete roster includes a child containing both parents. Consecutive such children either coincide or share the intervening parent. After collapsing adjacent repeats, this gives another eligible sequence one shorter or better. It yields a descendant of both endpoints by layer `m+1`. The last endpoint has the same fixed original ancestor `b_(t+2)` in every comparison. The mirror construction uses `b_(s−2)` and the right inward unary. The recorded literal witnesses attain layers 3, 5 and 7.

When the other ring is loaded, deletion of its first groups with the even-index path separates the interior roster from every birth through the fixed far holder. Any two first children in different components of the parent-set intersection graph have no shared parent. New live knots in a completed layer meet the preceding live layer only through their own consumed inputs. Thus distinct components of the first-layer graph never supply a common parent of two later live knots. Induction keeps the interior roster separate at every later completed layer. The two inward unaries of the both-loaded preparation can combine, but neither has a descendant containing the fixed old holder beyond the opposite loaded attachment. This is a statement about birth ancestry. A large rooted observation on the old path can still include earlier alterations through the ordinary old meetings.
