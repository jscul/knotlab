# Identifying the newest knots in the 31-name inherited pair table

The 31-name table forces six newest knots. Exactly names 17 through 30 are loose, and the next cut pays seven births. Four receive two fresh distinctions each and three receive three. The proof compares every positive-age ordinary not-all-equal history under recursive division of each older word's mass among its extensions. The source, its size and its ages may vary across rivals. ## Known loose names identify their common holders

Write \(C_{xy}=1-2r_{xy}=\mathbb E[s_xs_y]\) for signs \(s_x=\pm1\). Two rows agree outside their names on a carrier \(O\) if \(C_{xz}=C_{yz}\) for every \(z\in O\setminus\{x,y\}\). The following statement also holds at an arbitrary uniformly weighted source, including sources with repeated meetings.

**Partition.** Given two different names known to be loose in the stage whose table is observed, their sole holders are the same exactly when their rows agree outside those names and \(C_{xy}<0\).

If the holder is the same, exchanging its two loose names preserves every bit weight. At a reached stage both are fresh in the same cut and the extension denominator uses only preceding names. Their mutual correlation is negative. Indeed, for a loose \(x\) with companion set \(A\), conditional on the other names \(x\) is fair on mixed \(A\) and forced opposite on constant \(A\). Thus for every \(z\ne x\),
\[
 C_{xz}=-\mathbb E[s_z\,s_A\,{\bf1}_{A\ {\rm constant}}]\ \ge\ -p_A,\qquad
 p_A=\Pr(A\ {\rm constant})>0 .
\]
Here \(s_A\) is the common sign on the indicated event, and full projection gives its positive probability. Every member of \(A\) attains the lower bound. Equality at \(z\) means \(s_z=s_A\) in every allowed word with \(A\) constant, because all words have positive weight.

For distinct holders of \(x,y\), write \(B\) for the companions of \(y\). Both \(A,B\) are nonempty and lie outside \(x,y\). When the rows agree on that exterior, each other's companion sets attain the common minimum. Hence \(A\) constant implies that all of \(B\) has the same sign, and conversely. On their common event \(x=y\). Off it the two loose names are independently fair conditional on all other names. At a source the four assignments have equal word weights. At a reached stage their distinct latest holders impose no constraint on them once the companions are mixed, and all four assignments have the same ancestor and denominator. It follows that \(C_{xy}=p_A>0\). This proves the partition criterion without assuming that exterior minima always identify the complete previous holder. **Equivalence at positive age.** For a fixed inherited table, any one of the following determines the other two throughout the admissible population: the entire loose set, the family of newest supports, and the complete next roster of named parent supports and individual fresh counts.

1. A current loose \(f\) gives its latest support \(H\) as itself together with the exact minimizers in its row. Every newest knot has at least one loose name. Conversely, the earlier all-size orientation theorem identifies exactly the loose set of an independently established latest support under recursive division. [Loose-row minimum](../STATE-DISTANCE-AND-REACHED-GROWTH-20261120-001B/reading.md#what-ordinary-formation-guarantees) · [Orientation theorem](../LOOSE-NAMES-AND-SPENT-JOINS-20261120-001A/role_proof.md)
2. Remove the current loose names from the carrier. This preserves the entire preceding marginal. The inputs of the newest knots constitute exactly the previous loose set because each funded complete cut spends every loose distinction once. Apply the partition criterion on this **whole old carrier**. A block of previously loose names gives one previous holder and witnesses exactly the current children containing an input from that block. Every possible witness between current children occurs this way. The current children are pairwise disjoint, and an input was held by only one older knot before this cut.
3. The ordinary group rule applied to these witnessed subsets gives every multi-parent proposal. Each knot contributes its remaining loose names to unary births, with multiplicity. Applying the same group rule once more to the provisional births, using intersection of their parent sets, gives the proposed demand \(d\) at each. Its fresh grant is \(\max(3-p,1,d)\) for \(p\) parents. At positive age the next cut always pays under ordinary not-all-equal growth. All children of the just-completed cut were provisionally counted as live, all actually are live, and the true witnesses just identified are precisely those used by its lookahead. Each child's grant therefore covers its demand. Closed parts of the source add no demand. Since each current live knot spends every one of its positive number of loose names at the next funded cut, the distinct parent supports in the next roster give exactly the newest supports. ## Exhausting the proposed latest supports

For each putative loose mark \(f\) form its support from its negative row minimum. Its loose siblings are exactly the names in that support whose exterior rows agree with \(f\), including itself. Two different inputs of an actual latest support cannot have equal exterior rows. Discard choices of arity below three or with zero inputs. For one or two inputs \(I\) and fresh set \(F\), require, at every \(z\notin F\),
\[
 |I|(2^{|F|}-1)C_{fz}=-\sum_{u\in I} C_{uz}.
\]
This follows by averaging each fresh sign conditional on all old values. If the inputs agree there is one forbidden assignment on the fresh names, otherwise all assignments are allowed. These necessary local tests suffice for the finite reduction below. [Marked-role, old-input and conditional-mean proofs](../LOOSE-NAMES-AND-SPENT-JOINS-20261120-001A/role_proof.md)

Each complete named support is the union of its two columns. The nine possibilities have 511 nonempty subfamilies, of which 96 are pairwise disjoint as required of simultaneous births. For each, remove its proposed loose names and apply the preceding partition theorem to all proposed inputs. Members of a previously loose block must occur in the same previous support and the different inputs of a single newborn must occur in distinct previous supports. A former companion of \(u\) must attain its old row minimum. Allow all old non-input names attaining the minimum as possible additional companions, including ties caused by multiple meetings. Require enough to give each previous support at least three names. These conditions leave 71 families.

The partition determines the next witnessed groups on the selected supports. For each selected support compare its number of proposed loose names with the price it must have received at formation, \(\max(3-p,1,d)\), where \(d\) is its actual number of multi-parent groups at the next cut. Exactly three families survive: \(Q\) alone, \(E\) alone, and all of \(A,B,C,D,E,F\). The enumeration covers the subsets of nine named supports inferred from the table alone. It includes every combination of omitted knots, with all unrelated old holdings still unrestricted. ## The two singleton families

**Only \(Q\).** The unrestricted previous marginal on the 30 names other than 3 must have 5 loose. Its row minimum is \(-20737/290689\), attained exactly on 23, 24, 25. Its actual companions would be a subset of those three, with probability \(20737/290689\) of all agreeing. Yet the probability that all three agree is \(114239/581378\), already larger. This uses \(\Pr(a=b=d)=(1+C_{ab}+C_{ad}+C_{bd})/4\), valid for any signed binary distribution. It excludes every possible preceding source or reached stage.

**Only \(E\).** The previous 29 names omit 29 and 30, and 8 must be their only loose name. Its row minimum is \(-t=-2769/41527\), attained exactly on \(T=\{3,5,6,7\}\). Every two-name subset of \(T\) agrees with probability at least \(19379/41527\). Every three-name subset agrees with probability at least \(8305/41527\). Its actual companion set must have size at least two and agree with probability exactly \(t\). Consequently that companion set is the whole of \(T\).

If that 29-name stage was reached at positive age, its only newest knot must therefore be \(T\cup\{8\}\), with sole fresh name 8. At the preceding stage each of \(3,5,6,7\) was loose in a distinct holder. The induced row of 3 after forgetting 8 has its minimum only on 5 and 6. Neither can be a former companion, since both are other inputs of the same birth. No older age can give this branch.

If the 29-name stage was an original source, it was uniform. The following erasure applies to any such source. Suppose \(x\) is the only private name in holder \(H\), and \(T=H\setminus\{x\}\) has constant probability \(t\). Erase \(H\) and forget \(x\), putting uniform weight on the solutions of the remaining rules. Every remaining solution has one lift on constant \(T\) and two on mixed \(T\). For every residual event \(G\),
\[
 \Pr_{\rm erased}(G)=
 \frac{\Pr_{\rm source}(G)+\Pr_{\rm source}(G\cap\{T\ {\rm constant}\})}{1+t}.
\]
Further, for \(y\in T\) and any remaining \(z\),
\[
 C^{\rm erased}_{yz}=\frac{C^{\rm source}_{yz}-C^{\rm source}_{xz}}{1+t}.
\]
Indeed, on constant \(T\) we have \(s_x=-s_y\), and on mixed \(T\) the bit \(x\) is fair conditional on everything else. In the present source 5 was shared, so erasure makes it loose. Its corrected row now has minimum \(-1/14\) exactly on 23, 24, 25. The source probability that all three agree is \(114239/581378\), so the erased probability is at least \(114239/620144>1/14\). Every possible companion set for 5 is contained in these three, making its inferred constant mass \(1/14\) impossible. This exhausts the source alternative at arbitrary arity and with any additional old knots.

## The realized seven births

The sole remaining family comprises \(A,B,C,D,E,F\). The old loose names partition into the five previous holder blocks \(\{0,4\},\{5,6,8\},\{9,16\},\{12,13\},\{14,15\}\). Applying the ordinary rules to their children gives the full named roster below. There are no remaining openings for unary births.

| \(A,D\) | 3 |
| \(B,F\) | 2 |
| \(C,D\) | 3 |
| \(C,E\) | 2 |
| \(C,F\) | 3 |
| \(D,E\) | 2 |

The seven listed pairs account for all present witnessed pairs. The only three present knots that are pairwise witnessed are \(C,D,E\), which have a common witness in block \(\{5,6,8\}\), so they give three separate pairs. To price each resulting birth \(i,j\), initially count the other births through \(i\) and through \(j\). The single triple of births \(CD,CE,DE\) is pairwise witnessed with no common parent. For each of its members it replaces two prospective pair groups by one prospective triple. Thus the demand is the number of paid pairs at \(i\) plus the number at \(j\), minus two, with a further subtraction of one at \(CD,CE,DE\). This gives the prices in the table.

A lawful witness can be constructed from the inferred blocks, using all their allowed additional companions. Its five original supports are
\[
 \{0,4,7,10\},\quad
 \{3,5,6,7,8\},\quad
 \{1,2,9,11,16\},\quad
 \{10,11,12,13\},\quad
 \{1,2,3,14,15\}.
\]
Each has at least two private places, so every nonconstant local word extends by setting a private place at every other holder. Making one holder constant and satisfying the others at their private places witnesses its necessity. All names occur once or twice. The ordinary first cut groups the old names precisely as in the six surviving children, and the computed fresh counts pay their formation prices. Every old word extends. On each child any nonconstant local word extends because its old names came from distinct parents that each have a spare private place, and other births have private places of their own. The same freedom allows an isolated violation of each new rule. Every isolated violation of an old rule can still be extended through the new rules.

Literal enumeration gives 83,054 source words. For each word, average the fresh signs over each child's allowed patterns independently. Two fresh names from one child have conditional product mean \(-1/(2^{|F|}-1)\) on equal inputs and zero otherwise. Across children the conditional means multiply. Averaging over the 83,054 old words reproduces **all 465 indexed inherited correlations** without consulting a stored source or roster. This establishes existence, while the two singleton exclusions establish uniqueness of the reported roles and roster across all reached rivals. The small independent checks verified the previous-holder partition on 2,813 same-holder and 5,379 different-holder loose pairs across 2,349 connected sources through ten names, including seven distinct-holder pairs with equal exterior rows and positive mutual correlation. They also verified 60,366 uniform-source erasure identities. General recovery of the whole newest family from an arbitrary table remains open.
