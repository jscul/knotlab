# The paid value remains open at two separate keepers

Consider a finite lawful presentation with ordinary simultaneous funded growth, under even parity or not-all-equal. A one-parent newborn F receives one previously loose name u from P and three new names x, y, z. Observe the complete current words of at most two other holders whose observed banks avoid u, x, y, z. The claim here concerns complete holdings of these separate keepers, so it excludes P. Fix any jointly attainable words there. For **either** value of u, **every** allowed F word at that value extends those same keeper words. This holds for as long as the three original fresh names remain singly held and only ordinary growth acts.

**Count the original loose names at P.** Let their number just before this birth be L. The funded cut pays each exactly once to a different child. A multi-parent child receives only one payment per parent, and every unused name funds its own one-parent child. In the demand calculation the other children of P are exactly the candidates that can meet F, because P is F’s only old holder of a paid name. Any set of those siblings together with F already has the common witness P. Hence no uncovered group of three or more contains F. Its L−1 sibling pairs all count. Its ordinary grant is the greater of two and that demand. The single old name does not already imply F’s rule: it is open at P under parity, and a one-place not-all-equal condition has no allowed word. A grant of three therefore gives L=4. Write P’s old loose names as u, a, b, c. The next funded cut pays all three new F names. A stalled attempt changes no holding. **Extend a fixed joint observation.** Begin with a lawful complete word realizing the observed keeper words. Before F’s birth, only P holds u, a, b, c. Old observed holders therefore hold none of these names. Among other children born on this cut, each can take at most one of a, b, c. Even two observed children leave one payment v among a, b, c unobserved. Its recipient V is distinct from F and from the observed children.

Choose the required u. Under parity, reverse u and v together if u must change, preserving P’s parity. Under not-all-equal, give v the opposite of the required u, which makes P lawful with all its other old values fixed. All other old holders keep their entire words. Give F the desired allowed x, y, z. Only V can still need a different completion. Its other incoming values, including those paid by any additional parents, stay fixed. A private fresh mark suffices to complete any parity or not-all-equal input at V. If V receives zero fresh marks, the ordinary rule already requires its relation to be implied by the old lawful state set. The modified old word belongs to that set. F’s and V’s fresh marks occur at no other newborn on this cut. These changes preserve both prescribed keeper words and satisfy every holding. The argument also covers a stalled successor. **What the proof decides.** A single separate holder, or a fixed pair of such holders read jointly, cannot have a current word exclusive to one paid value at this stage. It says nothing about the conditional probabilities of shared words. A registered history, an earlier preparation fixing an extra name, a reading on the payer itself, or an intervening motion has additional information or operations. In a pure growing history an earlier old address keeps its value on every surviving extension, so a fixed ready keeper word cannot become either distinct answer by an ordinary step. New words can nevertheless carry real values.
