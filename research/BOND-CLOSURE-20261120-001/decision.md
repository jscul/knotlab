**Yes. On a connected lawful parity stage, any one pair of inputs decides whether a funded three-input join closes.** Pick whichever two loose distinctions you like. Their bond distance is **2 exactly when the join takes every loose distinction**, and **at least 3** when it leaves another. For this decision, every distance above 2 can be recorded as “farther.” The rule works under arbitrary renaming and uses no growth history. Independent referee review is pending. [Proof](https://github.com/jscul/knotlab/blob/main/research/BOND-CLOSURE-20261120-001/criterion.md)

The reason is that a single shared distinction cannot separate the parents of a legal three-input join. If it did, one knot would already witness all three parents together, which rules out the proposal. So a bond through two inputs that leaves the third parent outside must contain at least two shared distinctions as well. A bond of size three can occur only when the three inputs are the whole loose set. Bond distance is that size minus one.

**How much has to be read together?** For a join with p ≥ 3 inputs, read the smallest bond through **any p−1** of them. The join closes exactly when that bond has size p. This is the smallest single reading guaranteed to work whichever inputs are chosen. For a binary join, the guide’s three-place bond convention gives no containing bond when the pair closes and a finite reading when it continues. A lawful unary join cannot exhaust the end. If disconnected starts are admitted, the test decides closure of the component containing the parents. A separate component can keep growing unseen.
