# The question to the advisor (26 November 2026), before the fold study is frozen

Short, skeptical answers please. Context: the owner wrote "It is possible that time itself being a fold is kind of like
a 'meta' abstraction of the simulation. It feels like we're missing something for 'quantum' angle to work." The
coordinator asked: in ordinary growth with no added rule, is there any quantity defined on the path of reading (the order
of births or commits between two ends that agree) that is well defined, takes two values and multiplies along paths? If
one exists, does it put a sign across a split so that two ways of reaching one configuration can cancel? Stop at such a
quantity with a frozen test, or at an argument that every path quantity of the recorded laws is trivial. Any ordering
supplied is a scaffold, tested under another.

Facts from the record: (i) births can be made one at a time from a record of parents, and every order respecting the
prerequisites builds the same births with the same parents, inputs, fresh counts and places (refereed); (ii) both knot
laws are symmetric relations, and a round orders none of its births; (iii) the end does not always show its history: a
finished 16-knot structure (one four-place knot grown four rounds) has a renaming that swaps the start knot with one of its
offshoots, and in half-built states a place to a parent and a place to a child can be exchanged by a renaming; (iv) under
other rations parts copy themselves into identical copies that never meet again; (v) the exchange sign (swapping two
independent occupied births costs −1) carries the three-reader contradiction only for schedules whose crossings are the
pairs across a split, and it is an added rule (the reordering line stopped there).

**Part A, my argument that every quantity on orders of one history is trivial.** States are the order ideals of the
history's births; a step adds one birth b to an ideal I; a path quantity is c(I, b) = ±1 multiplied along a path. By (i)
everything the law makes at the step is the same for every I where b can be added, so any c computed from it is c(b),
and the product from I to J is the product over J \ I: it depends only on the ends. More generally, closed paths in this
graph are generated by squares (two independent births in either order; the literature's median graphs, whose cube
complexes are contractible), so c is path-independent iff it is +1 around every square; −1 on a square needs an
antisymmetric relation on an independent pair, i.e. an orientation, which by (ii) the law does not make. The exchange sign
is that orientation, supplied.

**Part B, a candidate for "two lines of descent".** Different readings of the descent of one end (which knots are start
knots and who is whose parent) are related by renamings of the end (fact iii). Quantity: the parity of the renaming that
relates two readings, as a permutation of knots (renamings that fix every knot, such as permuting one knot's loose
distinctions, act trivially and are ignored). It multiplies along chains of readings. It is well defined between two
readings only if every renaming that keeps a reading is even. A renaming that exchanges two identical parts of m knots
each has sign (−1)^m, a pairwise sign between the two parts; if the parts lie on opposite sides of a split, it is a sign
across the split, and the two readings of which part came from where would cancel in a sum weighted by it (the
literature's exchange of identical particles, whose sign comes from the configuration space, Laidlaw and DeWitt 1971,
Leinaas and Myrheim 1977).

**The frozen test I propose.** Grow ends by ordinary growth from the census of starts (three to five knots) for one to four
rounds, and the lone four-place knot to four rounds; read each end as a labelled multigraph (knots with their loose counts,
shared distinctions as edges); enumerate its renamings; take the true reading (start set and parent record) and its
images. Report per end: (Q1) whether the reading's own renamings are all even (the sign is well defined); (Q2) whether some
renaming relating two readings is odd; (Q3) whether such an odd renaming exchanges two disjoint parts far apart (a sign
across a split). Predictions: Q1 fails in most ends with a knot that makes two or more offshoots at once (twin offshoots
of one parent are exchanged by an odd renaming that keeps the reading, so the signed sum over the end's readings is zero:
the sign is ill defined, or read as a weight it removes the end, as two identical fermions in one state); Q2 holds only in
highly symmetric ends such as the 16-knot one; Q3 is rare.

1. Is part A right? What would you add or strike?
2. Is part B a genuine native quantity, or does the reading (start set, parent record) or the choice of permuting knots
   rather than distinctions supply it? Is "permutation of knots" the right domain?
3. Is the test well posed, and what do you predict? Anything to change before freezing?
