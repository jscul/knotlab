# Which marks recover the original knots?

One known original knot does not determine the other originals, even with their number supplied. The same three-knot presentation has two lawful two-knot origins that share a marked original and a marked original distinction. A full attached membership flag does determine the unfinished ordinary cohort. On a separate unbounded unit-meeting family, current arity supplies that full flag because the source has five places at every knot and the ordinary law produces only four-place knots. ## A mark on one original

Take three physical supports
\[
 A=\{s,a_0,a_1\},\qquad B=\{s,b_0,b_1\},\qquad X=\{a_0,x_0,x_1\}.
\]
All have arity three. The two shared distinctions have different pairs of holders. Fix $A$ as a known original. One can also mark $a_1$ as a known original loose distinction. Both marks are correct in each history below, for parity and not-all-equal.

| Original set | Written part of the first ordinary cohort | Remaining births |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\{A,B\}$ | The unary $X$ uses $a_0$ from $A$, with two fresh | One unary from $A$, two from $B$ |
| $\{A,X\}$ | The unary $B$ uses $s$ from $A$, with two fresh | One unary from $A$, two from $X$ |
| $\{A,B,X\}$ | None. The presentation is a lawful restart | One unary each from $A,B,X$, and the pair $\{B,X\}$ |

In either two-knot start there are no candidate pairs. Each original has two loose distinctions. It therefore has two unary children, each with one prospective sibling, and the one-parent floor gives two fresh distinctions. Exactly one of those children has been written. In the all-original restart, $B,X$ share a witness at $A$. They have two loose distinctions each and fund their pair and one unary each. $A$ has one remaining loose distinction and makes its own unary. Each displayed birth has two fresh distinctions. The difference is in eligible parents and their multiplicities.

All three interpretations are funded. The two proper histories and the restart each have two continuing live pieces, so neither law depends on an imminent closing exception. The source supports and the displayed current supports have their full intended projections under both laws. This can be checked by coloring the free leaves or using the exact parity and not-all-equal checks in the receipt.

The two size-two origins are exchanged by swapping $B,X$, swapping $s,a_0$, and carrying the two loose distinctions at each end holder with it. This fixes $A$ and $a_1$. If the endpoint identities are labeled, the two remaining rosters differ. Under renaming that fixes only the supplied marks, these two interpretations are symmetric. The three-original restart has four remaining births instead of three, so it differs even under renaming. No assertion of global minimality is needed for this pointed control.

Physical equality holds before any names are exchanged. Current supports, loose status, and the complete allowed state relation under each fixed law are identical across the interpretations. So current pair and triple bond readings are identical too. Direct enumeration of the seven distinctions yielded 16 allowed assignments under parity and 54 under not-all-equal, with six inclusion-minimal non-free projections under either. Both endpoint knots have ordinary degree one, and one of them is newly born in each two-knot history. Neither degree one nor a three-place support supplies an original mark.

## Persistence of marks and the complete-boundary control

A new child uses exactly one loose distinction from each parent, then receives previously unused distinctions of its own. Later births add incidences only between the new holder and older ones. Thus any pair sharing at least two distinctions was already present with that multiplicity at the source. If every source knot is incident to such a repeated meeting, its current endpoints give the exact source set. A source with only unit meetings never acquires any repeated meeting. A two-knot repeated control retained only its original repeated pair through three ordinary steps in both laws. [Predecessor's repeated-meeting reader](../SERIAL-REPAIRS-20261120-001/present_proof.md#an-unrestricted-present-reading-that-works-with-either-fairness)

Current cycle membership is also ambiguous. The two-knot unit start above is a tree. After both first unary children from $A$ exist, the next ordinary pair that takes from both creates a physical cycle containing $A$, its two unary children and their pair child. Three of its four holders are born. This also puts new holders in the subgraph left after iterated deletion of ordinary degree-one holders. More generally every physical meeting other than an old-to-old meeting changes birth depth by one. An odd physical cycle therefore certifies that at least one meeting belongs to the source. That fact alone does not recover the original set.

After the *entire* first cohort in the two-knot history has been written, the physical presentation has six knots. The old two originals are now spent. Its next cohort has six births under either interpretation of the source set, two pairs and four unaries, all with two fresh distinctions. The reason is general for any complete boundary that is a lawful restart. The original growth and the restart have exactly the same complete support presentation and current loose distinctions. Earlier cohorts are spent. The ordinary operator sees the same live holders, same witnesses and same opening counts on both sides. By induction the later ordinary rosters, arities, and closure or funding outcomes agree up to exchange of equivalent loose slots. Provenance ambiguity at a true boundary need not affect the continuation. [The present ordinary operator](../ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/end_operator.md#the-operator-on-the-present-end)

## What full source membership determines

Give one attached original/not-original flag at every current holder. In an ordinary complete-cohort prefix, each birth has at least one parent at the preceding birth depth. It therefore has a physical route to the original set of length equal to its depth. Every shared distinction changes depth by one, except that original meetings stay at depth zero. So shortest physical holder-step distance to the flagged original set gives the depth. All non-source meetings can now be oriented from parent to child. This recovers the actual parent sets at every born knot. A flag that follows its holder under a renaming gives the same reconstruction after that renaming.

The least depth still supplying a loose distinction identifies the unfinished cohort, or all open distinctions are gone. If it exists, the entire cohort has already been written. It includes currently spent holders. Each member's birth supply is its current arity less its incoming shared distinctions. At depth zero subtract the distinctions shared with other originals instead. Read the witnessed groups from the source meetings or from the immediately preceding holders, and compute the ordinary complete plan on those restored supplies. Subtract the actually observed children at the next depth by parent set and multiplicity. This gives the remaining physical birth roster and the required fresh count at each birth, including the parity zero-fresh exception. There is no need to supply orientation, a clock or a precomputed future map once the full attached membership is given. ## A five-place source with a four-place birth signature

For every odd $m\ge7$, take $m$ live originals $u_i$, indexed modulo $m$, with meetings $u_i u_{i+1}$, and $m$ spent originals $w_i$ with meetings $w_i w_{i+1}$ and $w_i w_{i+2}$. Add exactly one meeting $u_i w_i$ at each index. Every meeting is a single distinct shared distinction. Give each $u_i$ two loose distinctions. Each $u_i$ holds three shared distinctions and two loose ones. Each $w_i$ holds five shared distinctions. Every original thus has arity five, with $2m$ originals and $6m$ distinctions in all.

After deleting any one holder the remaining ordinary graph is connected. Each cyclic set remains connected after one deletion, and the surviving matches connect them. A live holder with loose distinctions remains. This verifies the singleton bond test for each parity star. For not-all-equal, pin any allowed assignment on one original star and remove that holder. Every remaining ordinary holder has at least two unpinned places. The remaining ordinary graph with unpinned loose tips is connected and has a holder of degree above two. The archived two-colour lemma gives both values on unpinned distinctions at every remaining holder, irrespective of the pins. [Parity and not-all-equal growth tests](../NAE-GROWTH-20261119-001/nae_growth.md#why-it-holds)

Each $w_i$ witnesses at most one live member. Each $u_i$ witnesses the pair $u_{i-1},u_{i+1}$. The candidate relation is an odd cycle, in the order obtained by steps of two in the source index. It has no valid triples, and every holder owes two opens to its two pairs with no unary left. The pair births form a cycle again because two pairs of the previous cycle meet exactly when they are consecutive there. Each birth has two parents, demand two, and two fresh distinctions. The same relation with two opens per holder repeats at each depth under both laws. This proves unbounded growth with **four places at every birth**. Each stage has $m$ births, so parity never reaches a sole closing birth.

Arity is immutable. In this family the current five-place holders are exactly the originals, and the four-place holders are exactly the births. The five-place condition on the originals comes from the supplied start family. The four-place separation is produced by ordinary joining. Under a five-place source promise alone, observing a four-place holder certifies a post-source birth. Recovering the entire source by arity also uses the proved restriction on every later birth. The three-place pointed example shows why the same reasoning needs an arity separation premise.

The checker verified both laws through five layers for $m=7,9,13,17$. At $m=7$ it tested 127 before-completion snapshots per layer, including the empty subset and all 126 nonempty proper subsets. At the larger sizes it tested 20, 28 and 36 per layer, respectively, consisting of the empty subset, each one-child subset, each subset missing one child, and one fixed intermediate subset. It compared the marks against the full attached membership flags and the exact remaining ordinary roster. Joint holder and distinction recodings also passed. The all-depth and all-size statements follow from the cycle argument.
