# Repeated finite products and later meetings under ordinary growth

**Scope.** The start is finite. All products are disjoint from their release onward and continue by taking every immediate parent from themselves. Other, mixed births are permitted. Two ordinary knot laws are compared at complete funded cuts under the previously proved formation rules. Size counts one further parent step. Numerical and indexed conclusions have separate evidence. Independent program referee pending. [Definitions fixed before measurement](../../../research/COPIES-AND-MEETINGS-20261120-001/preregistration.md)

**Finite copies.** For a start where the complete parent structure is identified with connected clusters in the unrolled map, let W bound the number of knots in a product *only at release*. Its later sizes are arbitrary. Beyond size 2W, compulsory ancestors of a continuing packet include an anchored endless chain or every phase of one primitive return of period at most W. There are finitely many such possibilities. Two disjoint packets cannot retain the same compulsory ancestor. Earlier cuts contribute at most the sum of their finite numbers of knots. This bounds all lasting releases of one finite product before a recognition reading is imposed. The proof includes attached chains, finished parts, arbitrary turns, reversal and non-interval clusters. In the seven-knot construction holding widths 1–3, 4, and 5–7 throughout admits respectively 0, 2, and 3 simultaneous lasting diagrams. Bare periodic returns can repeat a single diagram indefinitely. Distinct releases of that diagram require their own continuing ancestry. [Proof and exact capacity](../../../research/COPIES-AND-MEETINGS-20261120-001/bounded.md)

The elementary implication from a bounded number of finite consecutive words to periodicity is classical. Its proof is included. What matters here is proving that *every* same-size interval along a supported infinite route is a required physical ancestor, bounding the number of its phases up to reversal, and counting disjoint lasting packets even when they widen after release. [Morse and Hedlund, *Symbolic Dynamics* (1938)](https://doi.org/10.2307/2371264)

**Outside the identified class.** Pure packets still have only finitely many everlasting single-knot launches. Moreover packets kept at width three or less at every subsequent step must retain one of finitely many continuing unary lines, including those arising after a singleton component. Four internal members and a two- or three-knot launch that subsequently widens remain open in general. These conclusions use the actual positive-age ordinary birth rule. [Uniform-width and launch-only arguments](../../../research/COPIES-AND-MEETINGS-20261120-001/bounded.md)

**General timing.** At each funded continuing cut the witnessed separation of two inclusive descendant families drops by exactly one until they first overlap. The distance from an all-parent packet to the inclusive family of another release can never decrease. Both conclusions follow by lifting a witnessed route to actual children and tracing shared parents on the return. They hold without identification and survive any relabeling. Across separate witnessed parts independence by parentage holds forever on continuing cuts. [Two transfer inequalities, funding and closure](../../../research/COPIES-AND-MEETINGS-20261120-001/propagation.md)

**Physical changes.** One added loose distinction at the opposite end of a path gives a necessary second ancestor and strictly increases the count of the receiver’s descendants from size d+3. One added shared distinction in a connected arrangement retains both existing products and makes their descendants meet at all later sizes. In a seven-knot recurring generator opposite-arm products released together at K=52(n+1)+3 first share output at 2K−10, even though their pure packets never meet. In a three-return start with 21 knots and 60 distinctions, a descendant of an older release can supply the other parent of an immediate child of a still-pure younger product. Changing a single physical holding preserves the receiver’s parent diagram, own holdings and child count, and changes the child's fresh count by one. A larger start comparison keeps both products and removes this route entirely.
