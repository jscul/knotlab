# Toggle width is cutwidth plus one on graphs of degree at most three

Status: Proved

Ticket `CUTWIDTH-DEPTH-20261020-001B`, 2026-10-20. `DEEP-HOLES-20261020-001B-P001`
proved that an impossible Z_p charge network is refuted exactly at the
graph's toggle width over every field in which p is invertible. This proof
identifies the toggle width with a classical parameter: cutwidth + 1, on
connected graphs of maximum degree at most 3.

`check.py` runs the proof's constructions as algorithms on all 1,453 graphs of
the census `CUTWIDTH-DEPTH-20261020-001A-E001`. It turns 4,359 toggle paths into
vertex orderings of cutwidth at most k − 1, where k is each path's cost: 1,453
optimal paths and 2,906 random ones, 2,823 of which remove vertices along the
way. It also checks the monotone bound on every graph. Three controls fail as
they should.

## Definitions

- **Toggle path.** A sequence of vertex sets from ∅ to V, each obtained from
  the previous one by adding or removing one vertex v.
- **Step cost.** The step from U costs |δ(U) ∪ star(v)|, which equals
  |δ(U) ∪ δ(U △ v)|.
- **Toggle width.** tgw(G) is the least k such that some toggle path has
  every step cost at most k. The monotone toggle width only allows
  additions.
- **Cutwidth.** cw(G) is the least, over vertex orderings, of the largest
  number of edges leaving a prefix.

## 1. Peak elimination

**Lemma 1.** Let G be connected with at least 3 vertices, and let P be a
toggle path whose steps cost at most k. Then some toggle path P′ has every
step cost at most k and every visited cut of size at most k − 1.

**Proof.** Call a visited set U a peak when |δ(U)| = k.
- **Peaks are interior and isolated.** The endpoints have cut 0. Every step
  cost is at least the cuts on both sides, so all cuts are at most k.
- **Where a peak's neighbouring toggles go.** Let a peak U be entered by
  toggling v and left by toggling w. The entering step costs
  |δ(U △ v) ∪ δ(U)| ≤ k = |δ(U)|, so δ(U △ v) ⊆ δ(U). Since
  δ(U △ v) = δ(U) △ star(v), this says star(v) ⊆ δ(U): every edge at v is cut.
  The same holds for w.
  - Two peaks cannot be adjacent: that would need a toggled vertex with no
    edges.
- **If v = w**, the path goes back where it came from. Delete the detour.
- **If v ≠ w**, toggle w before v: replace U by X = (U △ v) △ w.
  - The new cut is δ(X) = δ(U) ∖ (star v ∪ star w), plus the edge vw if v and
    w are adjacent. So |δ(X)| = k − deg v − deg w + 2[vw ∈ E].
  - That is at most k − 1. The only exception is v and w both of degree 1 and
    adjacent, which is the single-edge graph.
  - The two new steps cost k − deg v + [vw ∈ E] and k − deg w + [vw ∈ E],
    both at most k.
- **Termination.** Each operation removes one peak and creates none, so the
  process ends. ∎

## 2. Monotonicity of the cut game

**Lemma 2** (Fomin and Thilikos, *On the monotonicity of games generated by
symmetric submodular functions*, Discrete Applied Mathematics 131 (2003),
Lemma 1, following Bienstock and Seymour's proof for graph crusades).

**Statement.** Let ρ be submodular on the subsets of V, and let
∅ = A₁, …, A_r = V satisfy ρ(A_i) ≤ k and |A_{i+1} ∖ A_i| ≤ 1 for all i.
Removals are unrestricted. Then some sequence with the same properties is
monotone.

**Proof** (restated).
- **Choosing a sequence.** Choose one minimizing Σρ(A_i), and among those,
  Σ(|A_i| + 1). Both quantities are nonnegative integers, so a minimum exists.
- **Unions never help.** If ρ(A_{i−1} ∪ A_i) < ρ(A_i), replacing A_i by
  A_{i−1} ∪ A_i keeps both step conditions and lowers Σρ, which is
  impossible. So ρ(A_{i−1} ∪ A_i) ≥ ρ(A_i).
- **Intersections never cost more.** By submodularity,
  ρ(A_{i−1} ∩ A_i) ≤ ρ(A_{i−1}) + ρ(A_i) − ρ(A_{i−1} ∪ A_i) ≤ ρ(A_{i−1}).
- **So the sequence is monotone.** Replacing A_{i−1} by A_{i−1} ∩ A_i keeps
  the step conditions and does not raise Σρ. Minimality then forces
  |A_{i−1} ∩ A_i| = |A_{i−1}|, that is, A_{i−1} ⊆ A_i. ∎

`check.py` runs this as an algorithm: apply either replacement while it lowers
(Σρ, Σ|A|). On every input it ends monotone.

**Theorem A.** On every connected graph with at least 3 vertices,
**tgw(G) ≥ cw(G) + 1**.

**Proof.** Take a toggle path of cost k = tgw(G).
- Lemma 1 gives a one-vertex-at-a-time path whose cuts are all at most k − 1.
- Lemma 2, with ρ = |δ(·)|, gives a monotone path with the same bound: an
  ordering whose prefix cuts are all at most k − 1.
- So cw(G) ≤ k − 1. ∎

## 3. The monotone bound

**Lemma 3.** Follow an ordering whose prefix cuts are at most cw. Adding a
vertex v with d neighbours already placed costs
|δ(U)| + deg v − d = |δ(U + v)| + d ≤ cw + min(d, deg v − d). That is at most
cw + ⌊deg v/2⌋.

**Theorem B.**
cw(G) + 1 ≤ tgw(G) ≤ monotone tgw(G) ≤ cw(G) + ⌊Δ(G)/2⌋.
**At maximum degree at most 3 all four are equal: the toggle width is
cutwidth + 1, and vertex removals never help.**

**Consequence for knots** (with `DEEP-HOLES-20261020-001B-P001`). On a
connected cubic graph, an impossible Z_p charge network is refuted by linear
local reasoning:
- **at level 3** over any ring that computes in Z_p (characteristic p, or Z);
- **at exactly cutwidth + 1** over any field in which p is invertible,
  whatever the charges.

A monotone step costs deg v plus the number of edges passing over v. So on
cubic graphs the monotone toggle width is 3 + the modified cutwidth, and
Theorem B gives **modified cutwidth = cutwidth − 2** on connected cubic graphs.
We did not search for this identity in the literature.

## Observations above degree 3 (census, not proved)

- **Larger gaps.** The sandwich of Theorem B is attained with gaps of 2 and 3
  above cutwidth. For example, 4-regular graphs on 8–12 vertices all have
  tgw = cw + 2. So "cutwidth + 1" is specific to knots on at most three
  distinctions.
- **Removals still never help.** On all 1,453 census graphs, up to degree 8,
  the toggle width equals its monotone version. General monotonicity is
  conjectured, not proved.

## Nearest known results

- **Cutwidth.** A classical layout parameter (VLSI layouts); NP-hard to
  compute in general.
- **Monotonicity.** Fomin and Thilikos (2003) give a monotone conquest-game
  characterization of cutwidth for symmetric submodular connectivity
  functions; Lemma 2 is their Lemma 1. Bienstock and Seymour (1991) prove
  monotonicity for graph searching.

**Novelty.**
- Lemma 1, peak elimination, is elementary and we did not find it stated.
- Theorem B and the identification of the charge hole's depth with
  cutwidth + 1 rest on `DEEP-HOLES-20261020-001B-P001`. The search was brief,
  and we did not find either stated.
