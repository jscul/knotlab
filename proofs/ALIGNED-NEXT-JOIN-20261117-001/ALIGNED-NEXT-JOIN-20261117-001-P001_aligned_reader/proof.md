# Reading every complete round with the original eight attached

## Standard statement

Consider the finite initial segments of the three-dimensional integer orthant under the growth rule below, with its binary cycle-space relation restricted on eight internal distinctions to the original eight cut tuples. The minimum cardinality minus one of a minimal nonfree projection through each pair determines every legal next simultaneous group and its available inputs on the observed distinctions. Four thresholds suffice, at bond distances two, three, four and five. This is an all-stage theorem with an exhaustive finite calculation in its proof.

## The observation and the answer

Use classical coordinates to specify and check the family. At stage Iₜ the junctions are the nonnegative triples u of coordinate sum at most t, where t ≥ 4. Each has three outgoing distinctions, indexed by (u,i) for directions i=1,2,3. They are shared with the corresponding next junction when present. Each junction imposes even parity on its incoming and outgoing distinctions. For the eight sites u in {0,1}³, their direction-three distinctions are the original bits K. Their joint relation S allows the two constants, each coordinate function and its complement. All eight are shared at I₄. The reader receives the mixed distance table on *all* present distinctions in any naming. A table entry is the least size minus one of a finite support of size at least three through that pair on which the projected states pass K1–K3. Infinite values and all values above five can be represented by the same symbol.

A live junction has loose distinctions available for the next round. A parent pair qualifies if it has a common current ordinary neighbor. A triple qualifies if its pairs qualify and there is no one neighbor common to all three. The simultaneous rule takes every qualifying triple, every qualifying pair outside those triples, then singleton attachments for the remaining loose distinctions. Each parent must assign a different loose distinction to each use. Groupings and the full set of permitted assignments are the answer. The actual choice of one among symmetric assignments is free under the supplied law.

1. Find the classes of three distinctions whose distance rows agree away from the two positions being compared. Equivalently the transposition of any two in a class preserves the entire truncated table. Each such class is the three loose outputs of a live junction.
2. Take any member of a class and collect all distinctions attaining the least positive entry in its row. Include the member itself. This gives the full junction. Removing its three loose outputs leaves the incoming distinctions.
3. Two different live junctions qualify as a pair when some incoming distinction of one is within bond distance five of an incoming distinction of the other. Among three pairwise qualifying parents, test whether one incoming distinction from each can be chosen with all three cross-distances at most five. Include the triple exactly when this last test fails. Keep the pairs outside the included triples and fill the remaining uses with singletons. At each parent take all bijections between its three loose distinctions and its three uses.

The sets, scalar comparisons and bijections in these instructions commute with every renaming. In particular an arbitrary representative at step 2 gives the same junction. The rule receives no positions, original marks, state rows, stage number or history. The rest of the proof verifies the reader.

## 1. All changed short bonds fit in one finite calculation

Call the coordinate sum of the anchor u in (u,i) its grade. Call a bond short if it contains at most six distinctions. Write C for the pure parity relation and Y for C restricted by S on K. Projections on any fixed observed support in Iₜ agree with those of the corresponding infinite system. Every parity addition has three fresh distinctions and four lifts per old state. Continuing the aligned rounds therefore extends every finite assignment in C or Y. In particular whether a *particular support* is a bond never changes after all its distinctions have appeared. Its pairwise minimum could still drop if more supports become available.

Use the elementary four-step cycles of the classical orthant. Link two observed distinctions if both occur on one such cycle. The finite-projection lemma of the cubic construction shows that the pure marginal separates across the connected components of these links on any finite set. Indeed its linear checks are precisely the finite cuts, characterized by meeting every elementary cycle evenly. Splitting a check across the components gives a check in each component, since no elementary cycle meets two components of that check. The projected constraints thus split. A step in this link relation changes anchor grade by at most one.

For a support F in Y, apply this factorization on F ∪ K. Condition K on S, allowing all its members to interact. A component disjoint from K retains its pure marginal independently of everything else. Consequently every distinction of a *new* mixed minimal support F must lie in a component connected to K on F ∪ K. Otherwise F lies entirely in a pure component, or factors with a separate nonempty part and fails minimality. Leaving the last visit to K on such a path takes at most |F| steps. Each distinction of F has grade at most 3+|F|.

If a pure minimal support F disappears, its nonempty restricted projection is still constrained. It contains a proper minimally constrained subset W that was free in the pure relation. The argument just given puts W at grades at most 3+|W|. The pure minimal F was connected by elementary cycles. Starting from any member of F outside W one can reach W in at most |F|−|W| steps inside F. The same bound 3+|F| follows. This reasoning applies also to potential unary or binary constraints in the finite calculation.

Thus **every changed support of size at most six is wholly present in I₉**. The exact census there gives 247 short pure bonds, 726 short mixed bonds, 479 additions and zero removals. All 479 additions involve only 45 distinctions, every one of grade at most **three**. No constrained minimal projection of size one or two appears. By the marginal argument this is now the exact list of possible changes on every aligned round. A row of the truncated distance table belonging to any bit of grade four or more therefore agrees with its pure row on every entry. [Finite certificate](transfer.json)

## 2. How the finite census proves completeness

The census uses finite binary linear algebra and the eight actual old rows. It avoids enumerating the assignments of I₉. Wire the unused ends to one auxiliary exterior vertex for the calculation. There is no exterior junction in the growth rule. Write ∂U for the sum modulo two of the parity checks at an ordinary vertex set U. Every pure check has this form. For a fixed tuple on K, every determined parity of observed bits has a support h of the form ∂U symmetric-differenced with a subset of K. This follows by adjoining the coordinate equations fixing K to the pure matrix. Deleting K from the finite wired graph leaves it connected in the stages used. Equivalently the pure code admits every K tuple, which also follows by choosing the vertical and second-direction outputs and solving for the first-direction outputs. Hence each such word h has a well-defined parity as a function of K.

If |h| ≤ 6, the unmarked boundary of U has at most six members. For each of the three coordinate directions count its occupied coordinate lines in U. Each such line contributes a distinct positive exit. At most four lines in the third direction can lose that exit when K is removed. The three projection counts therefore sum to at most ten. If their values are a,b,c then |U|² ≤ abc. To see this, majorize the count of triples by the sum of the products of the three two-coordinate projection indicators. For each (x,y), Cauchy–Schwarz in z bounds the inner sum by the product of the two row norms. Cauchy–Schwarz over (x,y) then bounds the full sum by the product of the three indicator norms, namely √(abc). The largest integer product with sum ten is 36. Hence |U| ≤ 6. The [enumerator](check_aligned.py) grows every connected set up to size six after deleting K, and combines disjoint, mutually nonadjacent pieces with additive unmarked cost at most six and total size at most six. It enumerates every marked subset that still leaves a check support of size at most six. This short counting inequality is the classical discrete Loomis–Whitney bound. [Loomis and Whitney, 1949](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9904-1949-09320-5)

It remains to decide which joined checks detect the nonlinear constraint. Write each allowed old tuple as a free constant bit plus one of four cut choices, represented by 0 or one of the three coordinate unit vectors. Each determined parity h has a four-bit signature that gives its value on the constant function and the three coordinate functions. For an observed F, consider the vector space of such h supported in F. Possible assignments on F form a union of cosets of the fixed-K kernel. They are all possible precisely when the possible values on a basis of these determined parities cover every combination.

A nonzero h of signature zero is already a linear obstruction. Otherwise the signature map on this space is injective. Its subspace with constant coefficient zero controls all remaining obstructions, since the freely variable constant supplies the missing binary choice if there is an odd-signature direction. A zero- or one-dimensional subspace of the remaining three coefficients always gets its full image from the four cut choices. For a two-dimensional subspace their images cover four values exactly when they are all distinct. Their nonzero differences comprise the three unit vectors and the three weight-two vectors, so the sole successful plane is the one orthogonal to (1,1,1). A three-dimensional subspace contains a failing plane. Thus every constrained F contains either a nonzero zero-signature word or the union of two supported words whose even signatures span a failing plane. Conversely each of these candidates really constrains the projection. Keeping just the inclusion-minimal candidates gives exactly all short bonds. This treats the nonlinear old relation directly. On I₄, replacing it by its affine hull changes 139 threshold entries.

As a separate projection check, fix the observed bits and the old tuple, and delete their distinctions. An assignment extends if the prescribed boundary parity of every remaining ordinary component separated from the exterior vanishes. Necessity follows by summing its junctions. For sufficiency assign the residual parities along a spanning tree in each component, leaving the unconstrained exterior to absorb the final parity in its own component. Test all eight tuples. This procedure verifies all 479 added bonds at I₉ and every one-bit erasure of each. It also checks all reported minimal supports at I₄ and I₅. ## 3. Recognizing every live junction

A transposition within the three loose outputs of a live junction preserves the full mixed state set. They occur together at that one parity junction, and all eight originals are internal. These are always genuine distance twins.

In the pure truncated table two bits whose transposition preserves the table must have the same incident junctions. The pure inverse reconstructs the whole star family. A transposition that changes a star replaces a single bit and keeps at least two members fixed. Two different stars meet in at most one bit. Thus the transposition fixes each star, and the two bits have the same ordinary endpoints. Two distinct shared bits cannot have this property in the simple ordinary graph. Only loose siblings can.

If both bits have grade greater than three, their rows in Y are pure by §1. A transposition preserving their truncated mixed rows is therefore a transposition preserving the pure table and must exchange loose siblings. To cover a putative twin involving a core bit b of grade at most three, choose a different outgoing bit a at the same anchor. The two belong to a short star. All pure stars survive by §1, so their bond distance is at most five. A twin c of b is either a or is at distance at most five from a. Hence c has grade at most five. Indeed a new short bond is confined to grade three, while a pure short bond spans at most two grades. The latter bound follows because each short pure bond is a star or the symmetric difference of the stars of two adjacent junctions.

For b and c of these bounded grades, **every short support touching either one is already present by I₇**. In particular, their rows on those bits stabilize there and entries against all later bits exceed five. On the exact I₇ table none of the 60 bits with grade at most three has any twin. That exhausts the low-grade possibility forever. The finite checks at I₄, I₅ and I₆ give exactly the true loose triplets there as well. Thus for every stage the classes read in step 1 are precisely the true loose triplets. [Stability check](transfer.json)

## 4. Recovering the next joins

Write v for a live junction. In the pure table the smallest bond through a loose bit at v is exactly its own star. The star has size at most six. Any other bond small enough to tie or beat it would have to be a two-star cut involving v. Its size is deg(v)+deg(w)−2 for its neighboring junction w, which is strictly greater than deg(v). Thus all least-positive entries in a loose bit's row are precisely the other distinctions of the star. For t ≥ 4 each loose bit has grade at least four, so its mixed row is pure. This proves step 2.

For two different live junctions v,w, take incoming distinctions e=(p,v), f=(q,w). In the pure system their bond distance is at most five if p=q, since their shared parent star has at most six members. For the converse, suppose p≠q. No single star contains both. A two-star witness would need one ordinary endpoint of each bit, with those two endpoints adjacent. The two lower endpoints have the same grade, as do the two upper endpoints. An adjacent choice would therefore have to cross, for example v adjacent to q. The upper junction v then has two different predecessors p,q, so its arity is at least five. The lower q is beyond the origin and has arity at least four. Their cut has size at least 5+4−2=7. This rules out a short witness. Thus the test identifies a shared predecessor exactly in the pure table.

For t ≥ 5 these incoming bits have grade at least four. Their mixed rows are pure as well, so the pair test remains exact. For three live junctions a common old neighbor must be a predecessor. Such a predecessor exists exactly when there is one incoming bit at each junction for which all three cross-pairs have the same lower endpoint, as detected in step 3. The prescribed pairs, triples, residual singletons and all distinct-bit assignments now follow from the original policy. At I₄ incoming bits can have grade three. Here an exact check of step 3 against the original policy handles the exception, recovering all 30 candidate pairs and all 21 required groups. Four candidate pairs have an additional short witness there. The reader only asks which joins qualify.
