**Yes.** On the three-dimensional branch with the original eight betweenness distinctions attached, the anonymous scalar bond distances recover the full set of permitted next joins at every complete round from I₄ onward. The reader then finds which of these junctions must participate in the same next join. At this stage it recovers three one-parent joins, twelve two-parent joins and six three-parent joins. Each junction's three loose distinctions can exchange their roles freely. The answer specifies every permitted assignment on the current distinctions, including all of those exchanges.

## Why it works

Two distinctions are interchangeable in the short-distance table when swapping them leaves every table entry the same. The three loose distinctions at a growing junction have this property. Their closest distinctions reveal the others at their junction. Distances between the incoming distinctions then reveal which growing junctions must join together. The first stage was checked directly, including which triples qualify. [Reader](../../proofs/ALIGNED-NEXT-JOIN-20261117-001/ALIGNED-NEXT-JOIN-20261117-001-P001_aligned_reader/read_frontier.py)

The original eight keep affecting what states are allowed. For supports of size at most six, however, all 479 extra bonds they introduce involve just 45 distinctions in the first four levels. Beyond that region the growing junctions have the short distances of pure parity. A finite check also rules out an old distinction impersonating one of the loose triples at a later stage. Together these arguments establish recovery at every round. Exact tests through I₁₀, including renamed inputs, agreed with the original growth rule.
