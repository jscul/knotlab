# Solving the original guarded formula after regrouping

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

There are \(r\) ordered stages, two interchangeable branches per stage, and at most two occurrences of each middle name and each pad name in its own role. A branch has an incoming and an outgoing implication, each with a pad and two signed halves. A half is enabled when all three of its private guards are on. Endpoint padding and witnesses remain private. Let \(k\) be the minimum number of *names* to omit from the nesting test after choosing a legal whole-branch order. Omission from this test means the solver will assign a single common value to both physical occurrences.

1. SAT decision with a witness for the original guards takes \(O(r+1)\) indexed work when \(k=0\). In general it takes \(O((r+1)^2+2^k(r+1))\) indexed work and \(O((r+1)^2)\) words of space. In particular, \(k=O(\log(r+2))\) gives polynomial work.
2. At boundaries across which no name is shared, one may use different solvers on different consecutive blocks. There are layouts on which both \(k\) and the count \(q\) of mixed-polarity pads grow linearly with \(r\) on an active contradiction, yet a witness-producing algorithm takes \(O(r+1)\) work for **every** guard vector on that wiring. Removing any enabled guard from the stated contradiction restores satisfiability.
3. The two-use compiler on \(n\) source names has at least \(3n+2\) exceptions in every legal order, including when no optional clause is switched on.

## Choosing an order, conditioning names and restoring a witness

Swapping whole branches at one stage exchanges two conjuncts with the same two boundary variables. Exchange the middle and both pad positions, **and all twelve associated raw guard bits**. The private clause witnesses travel with their own clauses. This is a bijective renaming of private data. All equalities on identified names are preserved. Unswapping the output reorders its triples of occurrence values, while stage boundaries stay in their original order.

First run the linear test for \(k=0\). On failure compute an optimal order and an exceptional set \(D\) by the quadratic algorithm. For each of the \(2^{|D|}\) assignments, fix both occurrences of every name in \(D\) to the same bit, and run the fixed-order interval algorithm on the chosen reading. Every actual witness restricts to one of these assignments. Any returned witness satisfies the original constraints after inverse permutation. The interval derivation uses linear work and space per assignment. Only the current assignment, interval records and witness need be retained. Both optimization algorithms use word indices of \(O(\log(r+2))\) bits. Grouping depends only on the supplied identities, so it may be reused for a different guard vector.

## Combining solvers at an unshared boundary

A cut is admissible if no middle or pad occurs on both sides. The two sides then share only the intervening boundary bit. To find all such cuts in linear time, scan the six positions of each stage and remember the farthest partner stage seen. Cut after the current stage when that farthest stage is no later than the current one. With distinct role identifiers for the two kinds of name, this scan omits exactly the forbidden cuts. Local dense indexing costs linear total work.

Before external endpoint constraints, every nonempty block allows boundary pairs \(00,11,01\). Constant states witness the first two. For \(01\) one can set all intermediate boundaries and all middles to zero and the last boundary to one. Every implication with all its guards enabled then holds, for arbitrary pad values. Each block thus has just one open question: whether it allows \(10\). If the external pins force the first boundary true and the last false, there must be a block whose left boundary is true and whose right boundary is false. Conversely, any such block's witness extends across the entire layout by constants one to the left and zero to the right. Without those two external requirements a constant witness suffices. The empty layout has a single boundary on which the two forced endpoint requirements may conflict.

A block with private pads admits \(10\) precisely if its required implications contain no directed path from the first to the last boundary. An implication is required only when both of its signed halves are active. In every other case its private pad can release it independently. Assign one to all middle and boundary names reachable from the first boundary and zero to the rest. On each dropping implication choose a pad value that releases its inactive half. This supplies the internal witness in linear work, for arbitrary guards and middle-name identifications. On other blocks use the optimum-order solver above or any correct decision-with-witness procedure for a \(10\) input. The work is the sum across tested blocks, plus a linear scan and reconstruction. The implementation returns boundary bits and all middle/pad occurrence values in original order. All other guarded-CNF witnesses extend privately. [Implementation](solver.py)

## An active family with many unavoidable exceptions

Fix an odd positive \(d\) and use \(2d\) stages. Their upper middles in stage order are \(A_0,\ldots,A_{d-1},A_0,\ldots,A_{d-1}\). All lower middles and all pads are private. Every two different \(A_i\) have alternating occurrences in four different stages. All legal orders therefore require exactly \(d-1\) exceptions. For even \(i\) enable the complete incoming implication at stage \(i\) and the complete outgoing implication at stage \(i+d\), both using \(A_i\). For odd \(i\) exchange those stages. Leave all other implication halves off and both endpoint requirements on.

The active implications give a single directed sequence from the first to the last boundary, meeting \(A_0,A_1,\ldots,A_{d-1}\) in that order. Between two consecutive middles it visits boundary \(d+i+1\) when \(i\) is even and \(i+1\) when \(i\) is odd. These selected boundary indices are all distinct, and odd \(d\) makes the last boundary \(2d\). Splitting either occurrence of any repeated middle from its mate disconnects the sequence. Switching off any guard on it leaves a half disabled on an implication with a private pad and also permits a \(10\) witness. Removing an endpoint guard relaxes that endpoint: choosing its two private padding values can falsify only the disabled sign pattern. Every such single change restores feasibility, while deciding an arbitrary guard vector on this wiring takes linear reachability work.

Now append \(d\) fresh stages. In each stage share one new pad between the two incoming implications, enabling opposite single signed halves so each pad value forces one incoming branch. Make both outgoing implications unconditional. Identify the lower outgoing pad of suffix stage \(j\) with the lower outgoing pad of stage \(d-1-j\), using a fresh name for each such mirror pair. Keep all remaining names fresh. The mirror pairs nest in the displayed order. Each stage's incoming pair lies wholly between or outside the endpoints of every mirror pair. For \(d\ge3\), the first mirror pair spans the entire suffix, so it is one block in the cut scan. The original odd prefix is also one block. For \(d=1\), each part is a single block as well.

At the displayed contradictory guard setting each suffix stage forces a branch. Disabling one live guard permits a drop in its stage. For an affected incoming half, choose the shared pad to disable the other branch, and use the missing guard to release the affected half. For an affected outgoing half, choose the incoming pad so only the affected branch conducts, and set its outgoing pad to release the implication. The mirror occurrence of that outgoing pad does not obstruct the choice: on the far side of the dropping stage all boundary and middle values can be constant. Splitting the two incoming occurrences of any shared incoming pad similarly releases both branches. A dropped endpoint requirement permits a global constant witness.

The suffix contributes zero to \(k\), even with arbitrarily deep nesting. At this guard setting it contributes \(d\) to \(q\). The prefix contributes exactly \(d-1\) to \(k\) and zero to \(q\), so both counts grow linearly in the \(r=3d\) stages. Across **all** guard vectors the prefix uses the private-pad reader and the suffix uses the nested-pair reader. The cut argument gives linear total work. At the specified contradiction all \(24+12d+18d\) enabled guards are individually indispensable. This demonstrates why two parts of one formula can require different ways to group their choices.

## Forced exceptions on the compiler

For every signal \(v\) in the existing compiler there is a middle name \(G_v\) with one occurrence immediately before its block of ports and one after. For each signed variable \(v=\pm x\), choose the distinct wire entering \(v\) from the source in the first complement disjunction for \(x\). One occurrence lies inside the ports of \(v\), the other outside. Pair the source wrapper with the dedicated global entry wire and the sink wrapper with the dedicated global exit wire. This gives \(2n+2\) pairs of names with strictly alternating occurrences in four different stages. Each pair forces at least one exception under every legal swap.

For each \(x\) also use the two distinct wires of the second complement disjunction, from \(+x\) and \(-x\) to the sink. Their occurrences in the sink block are in positive, negative order. The sink block precedes both signed-variable blocks, where the remaining occurrences are again in positive, negative order. These four stages are distinct. All these extra wire names are disjoint from the previous ones. Thus \(3n+2\) pairs are disjoint as *names*, and \(k\ge3n+2\). The certificate concerns the physical wiring and applies to every guard choice. For a listed catalogue with \(m=8n\), \(r=44n+8\) and consequently \(k=\Theta(r)\). An all-off optional catalogue already has these alternations and admits a satisfying source assignment.

## Replay and evidence boundary

A direct SAT solver checked the decision and each returned original-position witness by pinning all its bits on the original guarded clauses. Inputs cover every effective one-stage activation, multiple endpoint choices, larger layouts, partially active raw guard triples and the empty layout. A negative control exchanges the occurrence names while leaving their raw guard blocks in place and obtains the opposite decision. The two active families were checked through \(d=11\) for both the private prefix and the combined instance. The proofs above apply to every positive odd \(d\). The raw replays establish finite comparisons. [Run and controls](check.py) · [Counts](checks.json)
