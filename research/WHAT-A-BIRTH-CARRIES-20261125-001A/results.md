**Under the recorded ordinary count with the recorded taking rule, the course of the first pattern does differ with
how many of the second pattern's members were left usable, in every numbering of the start, and what does it is one
count the law hands over. The first pattern's first join has one candidate to take, the distinction the start
shares, and it then receives one fresh distinction for each birth the second pattern's usable members make that
round: 2 + M fresh distinctions, on all 16,880 runs made, with no name used. The link is the second pattern's start
knot, which holds the start's shared distinction, so the meeting is supplied by the start. Where the count is equal
(all of the second pattern's members usable, or all but one) the two sets of courses share a course under both ways
of choosing and both knot laws: nothing separates those two. In no numbering is a difference composed over several
births. So no pattern read anything here: the law passed a count through a start knot.**

The words. X is the first pattern, the two round-1 offshoots of one start knot; Y is the second, the round-1
offshoots of the other start knot, which holds h loose distinctions (h = 3 and 4). A bar placed by hand after round 1
leaves a of Y's h members usable: "which second pattern" is that number a, and nothing else. The law is the ordinary
count with short knots retiring and release, with the taking rule of the study of parts that meet again (a birth may
take a distinction held by a knot its parent meets), choosing the oldest candidate, or the newest as its swap. The
course from X only is what the births descended from X alone build, with all names forgotten. M = a(h − 1) − a(a − 1)/2
is the number of births Y's usable members make in round 2.

## The pair with equal counts

When all of Y's members are usable, or all but one, M is the same and the first join receives the same number. The
sets of courses then share a course at every prefix of rounds, in every one of the eight combinations of h, way of
choosing and knot law that were run. Many single numberings do give different courses there (for h = 3 under
take-the-newest, 219 of 360 pairs of numberings), and where they do the first difference is a demand or a birth's
inputs at round 2, 3 or 4; but for every such course some other numbering of the other case gives the same one. So
under names a single run can differ, and whatever the numbering the two cases are not separated. Where a
difference was classed, it went through the meeting or was left unclassified by the registered rule (an X-only parent
giving a different one of its own fresh distinctions to a birth whose parents answer one another); in every numbering
the registered verdict is "handed in one value", never "composed over several births".

## What this shows and does not show

- **What the law hands over.** Under the ordinary count a join's fresh count follows its demand, and demand is taken
  after the takes. The first pattern's first join takes the one distinction it can, which a start knot of the other
  pattern holds, and so its demand counts that pattern's births of the round. This is a count the round already makes,
  passed to a newborn of the first pattern. It is the law's doing in every numbering: the join has a sole candidate,
  so no name is used at that step.
- **It is not a pattern reading another.** The owner's fold of emergence asks for a pattern the rules make, whose
  course depends on another pattern, and that makes a pattern of its own. Here the dependence is one value at one
  birth, the link is a start knot and so supplied, and the third property is not tested.
- **Supplied**: the two starts, built by hand; the bars that leave a members usable; the taking rule and the way of
  choosing, which uses names (the results are "under take-the-oldest, or take-the-newest, whatever the numbering");
  the bounds of four rounds and 800 knots. The rule that takes every candidate was left out of the search as a
  reversible choice of the thread and appears only in the planted case.
- **Bounded**: two starts (h = 3 and 4), every numbering of each, four rounds. The made designs with five and six
  members were run on one numbering only.
- **Not run**: 400 of the 17,280 registered runs of the main run (take-the-newest under not-all-equal at h = 4,
  numberings 185 to 209). The registered sizes understated the cost, the knot test under that combination taking up
  to 125 seconds a run, and the run was cut at the hour by a rule recorded before it started. Take-the-newest under
  parity was then run whole (4,320 runs in about two minutes). The cells not run are not negatives; the one cell they
  bear on is already not disjoint on the 185 numberings run, and a shared course stays shared.
- **What it leaves for the study's larger design.** Recorded growth already makes one pattern's course depend on
  the other, as a count passed through a start knot. What was not found, and is what the constructive question now
  asks for: a link through a knot made by births, and a difference composed over several births.
