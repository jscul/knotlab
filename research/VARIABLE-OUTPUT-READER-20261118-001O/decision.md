# Spending one output keeps the holders of the other two visible

**A triple can survive the spend.** At 25 distinctions, the fourth junction holds 18, 19 and 20. Six triples are available before 18 is used. The four that use 19 or 20 remain legal under the same names. At 23 distinctions a spend can instead create the first two legal triples. These are the minimum widths for the two different events. **At every size in this family, if any triple can join, the two surviving outputs must still be loose together in every matching table.** Bond distances through five also fix every other junction on the corresponding distinctions. If there are two six-place junctions, the entire arrangement is fixed. If there is one, the only possible ambiguity is which four distinctions connect it to junctions that are already known. Any two alternatives must share at least one of those connections. This rules out moving an input that stays loose from one holder to another. Using up an input, or changing a common neighbor of its holders, could still change a verdict.
