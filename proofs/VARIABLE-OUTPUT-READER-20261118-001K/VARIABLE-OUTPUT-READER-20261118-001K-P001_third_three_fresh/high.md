# The unique six-place equation can change under an exact table match

**Counterexample.** Two sources in the third-three-output family have 28 distinctions and the same 378 full pair readings on identical names. Their exceptional junctions impose different six-bit parity equations. Both sources have an empty three-parent menu at this stage. Thus the exact table can lose the junction equation and the allowed states, while the question about a transported legal triple survives this example.

Use the published width-25 prefix in the previous ticket. Its six loose bits are \(E=\{12,20,21,22,23,24\}\). The distinct holders of \(12,20,21\) form a legal triple. Bits 21 and 22 are loose at the same holder. Append one site with fresh bits \(F=\{25,26,27\}\), using \(P=\{12,20,21\}\) in the first history and \(P'=\{12,20,22\}\) in the second. The original two unary additions each made three outputs. Before the displayed join, all other additions made two and exactly one used three parents. Both extensions obey the original rule and give the required third three-output addition. [Literal predecessor](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J-P001_seven_to_six/frontier.json)

The assignment whose only one-bits are \(11,12,14,21\) satisfies all first-source junctions and violates the second exceptional equation. Replacing 21 by 22 reverses the verdict. In each source every fresh bit has \(K\le6\) precisely to the six old bits in \(E\) and the two other fresh bits. Here \(K=D+1\) is minimum bond size. Each of the nine input–output readings is exactly six. The replay independently obtained every matrix entry by enumerating connected wired shores and by minimizing parity supports. It checked all physical growth steps and every possible triple of loose bits. The receipt includes the full common upper triangle. ## All possible alternatives at the exceptional birth

Let \(H_0\) be the old prefix, \(L\) its equation space, \(P\) the three actual input bits and \(F\) the fresh bits. At most one shrinking join can have occurred before the special join. Before that birth we are still in the minimum-width family, whose exact closure shows that no further three-parent group is legal after the second shrinking join, even following additional binary joins. A shrinking join means three parents and two outputs. [Minimum-width closure](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J-P001_seven_to_six/family.md#all-histories-have-at-most-two-shrinking-joins)

Every old state extends. Every new equation outside \(L\) has the form \(F\cup Q\) for an old-bit set \(Q\) with \(P\mathbin\triangle Q\in L\). Old bonds remain bonds. All bonds still have size at least three, and the seed is the unique ternary bond. Thus every six-bit equation is a bond, since a decomposition into smaller disjoint equations would require two distinct ternary bonds.

No old equation of size at most four contains two members of \(P\). In fact every cut on at least three old sites has size at least six. The first three selected sites have boundary at least seven. On subsequently inserting a site with \(p\) inputs, \(h\) outputs and \(j\) earlier selected neighbors, the increase is \(p+h-2j\). Only a shrinking join with all three parents inside decreases the boundary, by one. A one- or two-site bond containing two of the chosen loose bits would need the two distinct same-color holders in one site or an adjacent pair, which is impossible. Every equation of size at most four is a bond. [Chronological boundary identity](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001I/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001I-P001_cycle_obstruction/nonshrinking.md)

If \(|Q|\le3\) and \(Q\cap P\ne\varnothing\), then \(Q=P\). An overlap of just one would give an old equation on at most four bits containing the other two inputs. An overlap of two would give a nonzero equation on at most two bits. If \(Q\cap P=\varnothing\), the cut \(P\cup Q\) has all three distinct ordinary input holders on its shore, because the three input bits are loose. Its size is therefore at least six. We have proved the following exact alternatives for all new equations through size six:

\[
 F\cup P,\qquad F\cup Q\quad\text{where } |Q|=3,\quad Q\cap P=\varnothing,\quad P\cup Q\in L.
\]

**Before any shrink**, a cut on at least three sites costs at least seven. Only the first alternative exists. The closed row of any fresh bit through \(K=6\) therefore consists exactly of the six bits in the new star. Any rival bond realizing the value six with an input bit must be that star. This applies at arbitrary birth width.

**After one earlier shrink**, the whole old loose frontier \(E\) has size six and is an old bond. All old-old readings through six keep their old values. Any pair of old bits in a newly available size-six bond already lay together in \(E\) if both are in \(P\), or in \(P\cup Q\) if both are in an alternative \(Q\). There are no smaller new equations. Fresh-fresh entries are all six. A fresh bit reads six to precisely the following old bits, and greater than six to every other old bit:

\[
 R(P)=P\ \cup\!\!\bigcup_{C\in L,\; |C|=6,\;P\subseteq C}(C\setminus P).
\]

Two legal input groups \(P,P'\) on this same old physical prefix thus give identical clipped tables on matching names exactly when \(R(P)=R(P')\). Their full equation spaces agree exactly when \(P\mathbin\triangle P'\in L\). Since this difference consists of old loose bits, such a cut must select all or none of the old sites, by ordinary connectivity. Apart from identical choices the only possible equality would be \(P'=E\setminus P\). The minimum-width closure excludes two legal complementary triples on six loose bits.

In particular, exchanging a chosen input with a spare bit at the same holder preserves the entire clipped table. The old physical symmetry exchanging the two preserves the old table and the old six-cuts. Both bits belong to \(R(P)\), since \(P\subset E\) and \(E\) is a six-cut. Hence the exchanged set of fresh-row neighbors remains \(R(P)\). Their two-bit difference cannot be an old equation. In the width-28 example, the replay also finds equality at every larger entry.

For this exchange the immediate triple menus always coincide. An eligible old-only triple after the join would have to be exactly \(E\setminus P\). If the new site witnesses any one of its pairs, those two old holders were parents of the new site and already had a witness. A common witness originally present could not disappear. Thus that old-only triple would already have been legal, excluded by the preceding complementary result. Every remaining legal triple must use exactly one fresh bit and two old holders on the color of the new site. Neither of the exchanged bits has a holder of that color. Interchanging them leaves all other physical holders and ordinary meetings unchanged.

## Why looking at alternatives on that prefix exhausts the unrestricted rivals

More generally suppose the entire source has **exactly one** shrinking join, possibly after the six-place junction. Then the source and any rival matching through \(K=6\) have the same actual ordinary sites of arity at most five under the supplied renaming. Indeed the source has a unique ternary site and every ordinary shore with at least three sites costs at least six. The reverse through-five theorem makes every actual rival junction of arity at most five an actual low-arity source junction. There is only one five-place source site, so the rival can have at most one five-place site and hence at most one shrinking join. It too has the same unique ternary seed and the same short-shore bound. Applying the theorem in the opposite direction gives equality of the actual low-arity supports. [Through-five theorem for unrestricted rivals](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J-P001_seven_to_six/inverse.md#five-bit-equations-at-the-first-shrink)

For \(n\) source sites the width is \(2n+4\). In any rival with this width and unique ternary, the first two additions must be unary and make three fresh bits. Its number \(r'\) of three-output additions is odd from the width identity \(2n'+1+r'\), so \(r'\ge3\) and \(n'\le n\). The common low-arity sites already number \(n-1\). Every rival has at most one other site.

In fact it must have one, even when every high output has been used. Write \(v\) for the high site and choose an input holder \(a\) distinct from and nonadjacent to the seed. When the seed is also a parent, use either other parent on the seed's color. Otherwise the seed cannot meet all three parents by their eligibility. Put \(e=av\), and choose an output \(f\) of \(v\). The pair has \(K(e,f)=6\), by the input–output theorem. No word of size at most six in the span of the low stars contains both. To see this, the ordinary shore of such a word omits \(v\). It must contain \(a\), and \(f\) must have a later low holder \(b\) in the shore. These two sites are distinct and have the same color. On those two sites alone the cut exceeds six. For a larger shore of size at most six, equality in the chronological estimate forces its first three sites to form a path of total arity eleven, including the seed. Every later site has all parents inside. Indeed one strictly negative increment is needed, leaving room only for that fully internal shrinking site and zero increments elsewhere. The crossing \(f\) must then enter one of the first three sites. With \(a\) older than \(b\), the three are the seed, \(a\) and \(b\). To connect the same-color \(a,b\) the seed would have to meet both, contrary to the choice of \(a\). The claimed absence in the low span follows also when \(f\) is loose, since the omitted \(v\) would be its only holder. A rival needs an additional site equation to achieve the observed pair minimum. [Input–output theorem](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J-P001_seven_to_six/structural.md#an-input-and-an-output-read-the-whole-arity)

Thus every rival has the same physical low sites and exactly one six-place site. It also has exactly three three-output additions. Apart from the first two unary additions and its unique six-place site, it makes two outputs at every join. Its common five-place site is therefore a shrinking join. Both sides have six loose bits. The ordinary graph of the low sites is connected. The first three ordinary sites form a path, and the fourth must close its square on the two endpoints. Each subsequent site has at least two distinct old neighbors, preserving connectivity after any one ordinary site is deleted.

Every bit with two low holders is fixed as an ordinary meeting. Every bit with no low holder belongs to the six-place site and is loose. Each remaining bit has exactly one low holder. These last bits are divided between the unique high site and the exterior. If the high site has \(q\) loose bits, each part has size \(6-q\). All low holders supplying the high site's part have the same color in the common connected low graph. If a rival attaches its high site to the other color, the two parts must have been completely exchanged. The high-star difference is then the sum of all low stars and the allowed states still agree. In general two choices of parts give the same states precisely when the parts are identical or completely exchanged. Indeed a low-span word supported on bits with exactly one low holder selects a constant set across every ordinary low-low meeting, hence all or none of the connected low sites. A different split within one color can change the state relation, as the example shows.

At the exceptional birth with one previous shrink, the three bits of \(F\) have no low holder. In every rival they must be the three loose outputs of its unique six-place site. Its remaining bits are a triple of the old frontier. The site may be deferred to last. Removing a site with no ordinary children cannot lose the first witness of a later parent pair, since any pair it witnesses among its own parents already had an earlier witness. Its three input holders cannot gain a common witness after its original birth, either: the first such site would have had to take all three while the deferred site was already a common witness. Thus the old low graph is exactly the same physical prefix and its input choice \(P'\) must have been legal there. Consequently the \(R(P')=R(P)\) criterion above exhausts **all** reachable rivals at birth. After subsequent joins, the classification by a single split still holds when there has been just one shrink in total. It does not by itself decide every legal triple for different splits.

**Every unrestricted matching rival has the same immediate labeled triple menu at the exceptional birth.** For the one-earlier-shrink case, the newest site of the common old prefix has at least two bits still loose. Its six loose distinctions therefore have at most five distinct holders. All permissible input groups use the same ordinary color, since three distinct holders of each color would require at least six holders. Call the common color \(A\). Every old-only triple after the birth was excluded above. A triple involving a fresh bit needs two distinct old holders of the opposite color. If fewer than two exist, the entire menu is empty on both sides. If at least two exist, there are at most three distinct loose holders of color \(A\). Every possible input choice then has exactly the same three physical parents. All old bits of the opposite color remain loose or shared exactly as before, and the fresh site's meetings with the old sites are the same. Every marked triple consequently has the same verdict under the given renaming. Before any earlier shrink, the stronger recovery of the physical star already applies. These two cases exhaust the possible birth stages.

This conclusion also applies after any number of later additions if all three high outputs are still loose. Defer the high site until last as above. The remaining prefix belongs to the minimum-width family and still has a legal triple for that last step. Its history can therefore have at most one shrinking join, by the minimum-width closure. This reduces the current physical snapshot to one of the two birth cases.

For comparison, if there are **no shrinking joins at all**, every actual junction is recovered through \(K=6\) at every finite stage, including arbitrary binary continuations after all high outputs have been used. There are no five-place source sites. The through-five theorem excludes every five-place rival site, so the rival cannot shrink either. Both have the unique ternary seed and every shore of at least three sites costs at least seven. The within-class reader through six recovers every actual junction. ## A bounded list of all rival histories' physical presentations after one shrink

At exactly one shrink the table determines a list of all possible physical presentations, with a constant bound on the number of candidates independent of the history length. The bound includes candidates subsequently rejected. Work on the supplied bit names. The low stars are directly readable through five. At three and four, pairs through the current threshold are precisely the meetings in single stars of at most that arity. At five, erase the cross-pairs from the sum of the seed and each incident four-place star. These are the only additional pairs from two-site bonds. Their two members cannot themselves share a holder, by simplicity and bipartiteness. The maximal remaining cliques of at least three are the stars of at most the current arity, since three or more mutually meeting distinctions share one holder in this triangle-free graph. The short-shore argument above justifies each step on the source and all the rivals under consideration.

There are \(12-2q\) distinctions with one low holder. Choose the \(6-q\) that also meet the high site, in at most \(\binom{12}{6}=924\) ways. Its \(q\) bits with no low holder are automatically loose outputs. The two initial unary sites have at most 15 ordered possibilities. The first must be among the at most three low neighbors of the seed. The second must be a different neighbor of the seed or a neighbor other than the seed of the four-place first unary site. This leaves at most five choices given the first. Reject roles of the wrong arity and any repeated holder for the high site.

The seed has indegree zero. Each chosen unary site has indegree one, the five- and six-place sites both have indegree three, and every other site has indegree two. These counts prescribe all indegrees of the entire ordinary graph, so there is no need to guess which high bits were inputs. There is at most one acyclic orientation with a given indegree sequence. If two orientations differed, the edges on which they differed would have equal in- and outdegree at every vertex in either orientation. Following such edges would produce a directed cycle. To construct the acyclic orientation, repeatedly remove a vertex of current required indegree zero, orient all remaining incident edges out of it, and subtract them at their other ends. A deficit or a stalled nonempty graph rejects the candidate.

Direct all loose bits out from their holders. Test fresh counts, the two initial unary roles, and the actual witnesses at every join in a topological ordering. It suffices to test one such order, choosing the prescribed first two additions first. In a lawful orientation, every necessary earlier pair witness can be taken as a parent of the younger member of the pair: a first common neighbor born later would have used the pair before it had a witness. Its presence precedes the join in every topological order. Three input parents have no other common neighbor anywhere in the finished ordinary graph. A putative later common neighbor would itself have taken these same three parents when the original child already witnessed all three. Thus any candidate possessing a lawful order passes this test. Finally remeasure the candidate table through six. One can enumerate all sets of at most six bits, keeping the sets whose deletion yields two connected wired shores with each deleted bit crossing. This test includes parallel loose ends. The resulting list includes every reachable rival and retains only reachable rivals with the prescribed readings.

This gives at most \(924\cdot15=13{,}860\) role assignments even after the three outputs have all been consumed. The procedure permits the actual legal menus of the survivors to be compared on any proposed triple. The width-28 example shows why the list can have genuinely different state spaces.

If two surviving presentations have the same high outputs and the same two unary sites, their low-low ordinary orientations agree by the indegree argument after subtracting the specified high-to-low inputs at the affected low sites. Delete the high site and its ordinary descendants. What remains is a common parent-closed physical prefix containing all chosen high parents on both sides. Its legality follows by restricting the original ordering and retaining an earlier witness for every pair as above. Put all these sites first, then the high site and its remaining descendants in a common topological order. Thus in this case a difference of high inputs amounts to one choice at a common prefix followed by identical joins, with the same old input names at every later site.
