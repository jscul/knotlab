# Singleton copies and common ancestry in the weighted sample

Of 480 lawful sources, 97 funded their first layer under both laws.
In **235 of 2,604** eligible pair episodes, initially unwitnessed marks
eventually had a common descendant within the observed window. The first
shared descendant always occurred at the initial witnessed separation.
In none of these episodes did a new birth take a pure parent from one
marked side and a parent carrying ancestry from the other. Sixty-four
episodes kept both pure lines alive at the first common descendant.

The scorer marks each unordered pair of distinct live knots at each
reached cut. Each mark is a proposed singleton packet, including knots
inside larger current components. A continuing pure member has all
parents in the immediately preceding pure family. An inclusive
descendant needs some parent in the preceding inclusive family. As a
separate event, a child can take a pure parent from one side and a parent
already carrying the other side's ancestry. A child of two pure parents
is counted as direct. A common descendant is recorded even if the
birth closes with zero fresh distinctions. Distances are compared only
between nonempty live families across a continuing step.

Pair episodes from the same source, and episodes anchored at different
sizes in one history, are dependent. The two laws are paired replays.
Both results and denominators below count actual observed episodes.

cut and at least two observed funded steps afterwards. The two laws

## Finite width-one copies

A same-parent unary sibling pair is a finite width-one release control.
Its two packets have one knot each and equal holding type, using arity and
loose count with each shared distinction retained as a unit meeting.
An ongoing width-one line may retain one unary continuation and ignore
other births. Every retained step still uses only its own preceding
knot as a parent. This test scores preservation of the single knot's
holdings. The relation between the two packets can change.

There are 125 such sibling pairs in parity and 127 in not-all-equal,
including ends with zero observed follow-ups. Among the 113 per law with
one observed follow-up, 51 have two same-type width-one continuations.
Among the 98 per law with two follow-ups, 36 retain both lines through
both steps. Fourteen starts per law, eight trees and six augmented trees,
form new same-type unary sibling groups at more than one observed size.
This counts separate birth occurrences. A continuing signature on one
earlier knot contributes no new release by itself. The four-layer window
leaves repeated release beyond it and indefinite disjoint continuation
unresolved for these draws.

## Reading the current witnessed holdings

In the same age-one physical frame of `tree/4/00`, the unordered pairs
`(5,7)` and `(6,8)` have equal loose counts of two apiece, zero shared
distinctions and zero common witnesses. This weaker present reading
gives the same answer data for both queries. The full witnessed end
distinguishes them. The first pair lies in separate current components,
while the second has witnessed separation two. A forecast using only
the full live witness relation and the two marked knots gets the two
different two-step ancestry outcomes. The unpointed reading is the same
whole frame. The pointed witness relations fail exact isomorphism.
Joint renaming of holders and distinctions preserves both forecasts.
No prior ancestor identities enter this reader.

The supplementary profile of each mark's loose count, candidate degree
and incident witness-set sizes, together with their pair separation,
had no conflicting two-step pure-survival answers among the 2,604
eligible episodes after matching the observation depth and anchor age.
This records agreement of the observed types within the sample.
