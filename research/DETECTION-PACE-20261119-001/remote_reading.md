# What a remote old mark can actually read

An old shared path mark at positive offset \(d\) detects the once-loaded ring at layer \(n=\lceil d/2\rceil\) by inspecting the **nearest current knots that still hold loose distinctions**. At that layer they hold exactly two fewer loose distinctions under load. No clock, list of ancestors or global bond query is needed for this reader. All readings of the specified outward descendant region agree before \(n\). One retained original anchor suffices to preserve the structural distinction at every later age. In contrast, a region restricted to knots whose *every* original ancestor is at offset at least three on that side agrees forever, even with each of its physical ports and its projected parity law supplied.

These claims concern the funded plain path, one attached five-ring, four end spares, quiet loose inputs and the ordinary cold-pass/hot-veto rule. Extra ring spares at \(c_2,c_3\) do not affect the positive-offset calculation. The attachment is \(b_s\) on the path \(b_0,\ldots,b_N\), with \(4\le s\le N-4\) as in the padded-source theorem. On the right the original mark \(e_{s+d}\) has holders \(b_{s+d},b_{s+d+1}\); on the left reflect these definitions. No further outer margin is required: the two end spares continue their respective parities beyond the last original holder as a chain of virtual points in the cluster proof. The supplied location is one old holder, one physical mark, or the specified old tail. No such coordinate is available to growth. ## Local data and allowed states

For a knot set \(V\), expose its knots, every physical distinction they hold, the incidences to those knots, the types, and optionally the actual stored values. Keep a distinction with only one displayed holder even when it has a second holder outside. Its displayed incidence alone does not say whether the second holder exists. A physical loose/shared flag is a separate option. The arity of a **displayed** knot is available by counting its ports. Outside knot names, outside arities, paths through missing holders, global bond values, and counts of discarded objects are absent. All comparisons allow physical renamings preserving the stated marks, types and colors. Port allocations may be coupled in the two histories; independently permuting equally valued ports changes no such reading.

The restriction of the realized allowed states to old shared distinctions alone is especially simple. Prescribe arbitrary values on **all** original shared distinctions. Each original holder can satisfy its parity by freely setting one of its original loose distinctions. Each later child equation takes a fresh distinction, so the resulting old assignment extends at every layer of either realized structure. Thus every projection on old shared path marks is the full Boolean cube. Their actual stored values also stay at the common reference. A minimizing bond containing old marks is free to use *new* distinctions, and the unrestricted stage can already distinguish a load by counting its extra births. These are separate inputs to a reader. [Conservative lifts and first-round selection](../STATE-FEEDS-GROWTH-20261119-001/selection.md)

There is a useful exact criterion for supplying the projected parity law on a ported region. If the complement of \(V\) in the wired holder presentation is connected to the wiring exterior, the full allowed-state relation projected onto all visible ports is **exactly the parity equations of the displayed knots**. Indeed a global equation is a cut. If supported only on the displayed ports it cannot separate any two adjacent outside holders, including a loose connection to the exterior. The entire connected complement is consequently on one side, and the equation is a sum of displayed stars. This linear argument concerns the allowed relation; it does not add outside data to the actual observation.

## Two remote regions and their current recognition

Let \(A\) be a nonempty set of original holders. Write \(P_A\) for knots whose original ancestors all belong to \(A\) and \(D_A\) for knots having at least one original ancestor in \(A\). Old holders count as their own ancestors. Each definition includes every qualifying birth up to the current stage.

Let \(F\) be the holders of actually loose physical distinctions, and use ordinary knot steps through shared physical distinctions. At stage \(T\) precisely the age-\(T\) knots are live. An age-\(t\) knot has distance \(T-t\) from \(F\): each step changes age by at most one, and every knot has a chain of children to the present end. Hence \(T=\max_v d(v,F)\), the old source is the set \(O\) attaining this maximum, and

\[
 v\in D_A \iff d(A,v)+d(v,F)=T,\qquad
 v\in P_A \iff d(O\setminus A,v)+d(v,F)>T .
\]

An earliest-to-latest route of length \(T\) consists solely of genuine parent-to-child steps. This proves both identities, with distance to an empty set taken as infinity. The full stage can therefore *select* these regions at present without a stored growth history. The same distances identify the original and first-born knots at every later stage, so the complete first-layer birth count remains readable in a saturated observation. A restricted region handed to the observer may hide the outside holders needed for the initial selection.

The complement of \(P_A\) stays outside along every child path, since any excluded original ancestor is retained. Its knots reach the exterior. For \(D_A\), every outside knot can descend to an outside original holder through parents avoiding \(A\). If \(A\) is one holder, a contiguous packet of holders, or the right tail \([s+d,N]\), each remaining component of the original path and ring reaches an excluded end spare whenever the packet avoids the attachment. Its pure unary path reaches the exterior. When the tail starts at the attachment itself, the remaining off-path ring holders form a separate original part. At age zero each still has its own loose port. At every positive age in the quiet arm the first child on \(c_1,b_{s-1}\) connects that part to the retained left path, with both parents outside \(A\). In the loaded arm each of these hot ring holders has its own outward branch. This establishes the projected-law equality for the regions used here. An arbitrary disconnected choice of roots can enclose an outside part and needs its own check.

**The all-ancestor restriction.** For the tail \(A=[s+d,N]\) with \(d\ge3\), every source holder in \(A\) has its complete original roster of children, with the same parent sets, in both arms. No changed original proposal reached that far. Inductively, if a knot's ancestors lie in \(A\), the whole child roster of each of its parents matches. Every possible partner is a sibling at some parent. Those siblings' known parent sets determine their eligibility, larger groups, residual pairs and next demand. Thus the pure knot's own complete child roster and fresh count match. The argument works in both directions, including mixed children through a pure parent. It gives an isomorphism of **all** the data displayed on \(P_A\) at every age, including individual ports, actual sharing/looseness flags if supplied, actual values, and the projected parity relation above. Consequently every invariant function of that observation agrees. [Full-roster coupling](../STATE-FEEDS-GROWTH-20261119-001/locality.md)

This bound on the pure region is sharp. For \(d=1\), quiet mixed pairs through \(b_{s+1}\) with \(c_1,c_4\), and through \(b_{s+2}\) with \(b_s\), each give a new pure unary under load. There are three extra pure first births. For \(d=2\) only the last contribution remains, giving one. At \(d=0\) the first pure count rises by six: four refused pairs through \(s\), one wholly in the tail and three mixed, plus the two through \(b_{s+1}\) with \(c_1,c_4\). A refused mixed pair adds one pure child for its sole inside parent; a refused pure pair adds one net pure child. These counts describe a region selected by its ancestry. With \(A\) marked, they remain readable in the original-to-first distance shell inside \(P_A\), because every pure knot has all its parents inside.

## An anonymous positive reader and its first affected layer

Supply one original holder \(a\), or supply only the shared mark between two original holders. Explore present incidences outwards until the first knots holding actually loose distinctions are reached. Return the multiset of loose counts on all knots at this first distance. For an old holder this distance in knot steps is \(T\); for an old mark it is \(2T+1\) incidence hops. These knots are precisely the current descendants of the supplied holder or the union of the descendants of the two holders. Testing true looseness requires only the incident distinctions of a reached knot and their possible second holders. Thus actual inspection stops by \(2T+2\) incidence hops from a holder or \(2T+3\) from a mark. No outside degree annotation, original-coordinate index, prior reading or layer counter is required.

In the next table \(v^m\) means \(m\) distinct nearest live knots with \(v\) loose distinctions, omitting terms of multiplicity zero.

| One original holder at offset \(d\) | First differing layer | Quiet multiset | Loaded multiset |
|---|---:|---|---|
| \(2k,\ k\ge1\) | \(k\) | \(2^k,4^1\) | \(2^{k+1}\) |
| \(2k+1,\ k\ge0\) | \(k+1\) | \(2^k,4^4\) | \(2^k,3^2,4^1,6^1\) |

At an **old shared mark**, add the results for its two holders on the same layer. At first difference the two descendant sets are disjoint. For even \(d=2k\) their total loose counts are \(4k+8\) quiet and \(4k+6\) loaded. For odd \(d=2k+1\) the totals are \(4k+22\) and \(4k+20\). At odd offset the single nearer holder's *total* agrees at arrival even though its per-knot multiset already differs.

**Derivation.** In the established cluster presentation the old path through a root has two lists spaced by two old places. A birth at age \(t\) is a connected cluster of \(t+1\) points containing an original point. Its parents remove one point while retaining connectedness and an original point. Its loose count is the number of allowed one-point extensions. On the straight tail, a cluster containing the marked holder is an interval with one choice at either end for extension. Beyond a physical path end the virtual spare continues this list. The loaded map uses the actual surviving source pairs and replaces every freed slot by its prescribed pendant. This describes the actual parent incidences and counts in both histories. [Cluster and loaded-roster proof](../SAME-SIDE-READING-20261119-001/side_bonds_proof.md) · [First leading intervals](../SAME-SIDE-READING-20261119-001/local_proof.md)

On the even list put \(x_j=b_{s+2j}\), with the marked root \(x_k\). Quiet \(x_1\) has an inward neighbor \(x_0=b_s\); under load that link is replaced by an inward pendant with one endless chain. At age \(k\) the unique cluster reaching this first different point is the interval extending wholly inward from \(x_k\). It has four extensions when quiet, two when loaded. Each of the other \(k\) intervals has two. At all earlier positive ages every interval through the root has the same real endpoints, parent choices and two extensions in both arms.

On the odd list let \(x_j=b_{s+1+2j}\) and take root \(x_k\). The quiet \(x_0\), in addition to its outward neighbor, lies in the four-point block with \(y=b_{s-1},c_1,c_4\). Under load it instead joins \(y\) and gains two distinct finite pendants. At age \(k\) the inward interval first reaches \(x_0\) and has four extensions in both arms. Earlier intervals have two. At age \(k+1\) there are \(k\) ordinary two-extension intervals and one interval through \(x_0\) extended once on its outward side, with four choices next. The other three clusters have reached \(x_0\) and taken one of its three inward choices. In the quiet arm each has four extensions: one outward, two remaining members of the four-point block, and the chosen member's one continuation on its other side. Under load choosing \(y\) gives six: one outward, two pendants at \(x_0\), two pendants at \(y\), and the further path neighbor of \(y\). Choosing one of the two pendants at \(x_0\) gives three: outward, \(y\), or the other pendant. This also works at \(k=0\), when the last two births are unary.

At the shared mark, up to and including the stated first layer the clusters through its two holders are disjoint. They lie on their respective parity lists; the exceptional clusters just enumerated use up their full size reaching the seam-side changes and cannot include the other marked holder. At the first differing layer each exceptional cluster has its outermost original path holder among these two roots. Adding the other, more distant roots cannot duplicate any exceptional contribution. Before this layer no root farther out has reached its first change. In particular the entire ported \(D_A\) of the outward tail agrees through every \(T<n\). Indeed every cluster through a root in that tail is still one of the same ordinary intervals, with identical parent deletions and arity. An interval that just reaches odd \(x_0\) has the same four extensions in number. At \(T<n\) these further choices are outside the stage whenever they could first differ. Distinguishing only inside holders, the boundary ports can therefore be matched individually. True loose flags, actual values and the projected parity law also match. These interval correspondences give an isomorphism of the two objects. For the whole outward tail at the first affected layer, common intervals from farther roots contribute equally, leaving the same strict difference in total loose counts among current knots. With one retained location, persistence is proved below.

**Zero offset.** The seam's own star, visible from \(e_s\) if the incident values are exposed, holds occupied old ring distinctions at age zero. The mark's own value remains quiet. Structural incidence is identical at the source and first differs at layer one. For the one-spare ring the four quiet children through \(s\) have loose counts \(4,4,4,5\); the four loaded unaries have three each. The \(s+1\) holder contributes the odd row with \(k=0\). The two groups of children are disjoint, giving \(33\) versus \(28\) loose distinctions at the mark. With zero or two spares at \(c_2,c_3\) these counts are \(32\) or \(34\) versus \(28\). For an adjacent pair used to specify its common holder, use the holder's own offset; the nearest of those marked edges can be one old step nearer the seam.

## Persistent observation with hidden outside holders

Inside a single-root \(D_{\{a\}}\), a knot's distance from \(a\) equals its birth age, since it has an all-inside ascending path from that root. With a rooted shared mark the same holds for the minimum step distance from its two holders. At every stage later than \(n\), the fresh counts of those first affected knots are also the numbers of their children, all inside the descendant region. Thus the original histogram can be recovered from consecutive shells even if the open ports carry no true looseness flag.

There is a read requiring no later growing-end information and no label on every original in the larger tail \(D_A\). Keep only the one supplied old holder or shared mark and the complete physical stars of the displayed knots, with outside holders hidden. Take all knots within \(n\) ordinary steps of that root (of either holder for a mark) **inside this region** and return the multiset of their star sizes. Every path of length at most \(n\) starting at an original holder stays in ages at most \(n\). A path ending at an older knot cannot use an age-\(n\) knot at an intermediate step. The older contribution therefore agrees by the \(T<n\) isomorphism. Its age-\(n\) contribution consists exactly of the enumerated nearest descendants of the root. This smaller rooted observation is unchanged as a multiset at every later stage, and still distinguishes the two arms.

For the even holder, the exceptional cluster has arity six quiet, versus four loaded, or three loaded at \(k=1\) when it is unary. For the odd holder a single loaded cluster has arity eight, while its corresponding quiet clusters have at most six. For a shared mark the sum of these new star sizes is larger in the quiet arm by two when \(d\ge3\), three at \(d=2\), and five at \(d=1\), since any lost parent at the first layer is also absent from its child's arity. At \(d=0\) the analogous layer-one star-size drop is eleven on the one-spare ring. Thus persistence requires neither shared/loose annotations nor a recorded first observation. The single supplied location matters. Dropping **all** its root markings from the tail observation gives a different isomorphism question. Dropping ports entirely also changes the input: in the single-root descendant graph the first affected knots' child counts are visible from layer \(n+1\) onward.

## A fixed physical neighborhood, selected without a cone or growing end

There is also a persistent reader which uses only present incidence around an old shared mark \(M=e_{s+d}\), independently of how the remote region is supplied. Take all ordinary knots within \(w\) knot steps of its two holders, record all their ports, and sum their arities. A terminal port's other holder and its true loose status are unnecessary. This uses at most \(2w+2\) incidence hops from the marked distinction. From stage \(T=w\) onward this entire open-port view is fixed: no later-born knot or new path through such a knot can enter this radius, and every displayed star is retained.

For \(d\ge2\) the smallest \(w\) at which any invariant of this full open-port view can distinguish the preparations is \(w=\lceil d/2\rceil\). At this \(w\) the quiet-minus-loaded arity sum behaves as follows.

| Mark offset | First differing layer and difference | Permanent difference |
|---|---|---:|
| \(d=2k,\ k\ge1\) | \(k\), difference two (three at \(k=1\)) | Same |
| \(d=2k+1,\ k\ge1\) | \(k\), difference two (three at \(k=1\)) | Four from \(k+1\) (five at \(k=1\)) |

To verify this, an age-\(t\) knot in the radius-\(w\) view lies in the descendant set of an original holder \(z\) within \(w-t\) old knot steps of the root holders. Conversely all such descendants enter. This follows by replacing latest excursions in old-to-old ordinary paths through the parents' earlier common witness, then tracing an arriving shortest path to an original ancestor. [Exact distance-to-ancestor proof](../SAME-SIDE-READING-20261119-001/local_proof.md)

The nearest such \(z\) has donor offset at least \(d-w+t\). When \(t<d-w\), every contributing cluster through those roots is before its first different interval just described. This matches all knots, parents, overlaps, star sizes and physical port colors there, using the common interval names. If \(w<\lceil d/2\rceil\) then every contributing \(t\le w\) qualifies, proving the all-reading null on the smaller open-port view.

At \(d=2k,w=k\) the first cohort outside that null is exactly the age-\(k\) descendants of the two root holders. Their arity difference is the shared-mark calculation above. At \(d=2k+1,w=k+1\), the first possible cohort is \(t=k\), from old roots within one ordinary step of the two holders. Only the single nearer old neighbor at offset \(2k\) is then at its first different interval. Its exceptional inward cluster ends at that neighbor and does not reach another of the contributing roots, so its difference is counted once. Exactly the rooted age-\(k+1\) cohort then adds two more. Outer ends can remove only unchanged, more distant original roots in this argument. At \(d=1,w=1\), structural arity first differs by five at age one; at \(d=0,w=1\) it first differs by eleven for the one-spare ring. The \(w=0\) arity of the two original holders is fixed. Actual ring colors are separately visible as soon as a displayed star holds an occupied ring distinction.

At all \(d\ge2\), the positive structural read just proved is strictly short of the donor knot at \(2d+1\) incidence hops; as \(d\) increases, even the least distance from an inspected holder to that donor grows without bound. The measuring radius itself grows with \(d\). A common bond can consult information outside these open stars unless its own locality is proved. The previously established uniform bound for a fixed bond radius and the present optimal radius in this ported-incidence convention consequently answer different questions. [Fixed bond-radius theorem](../SAME-SIDE-READING-20261119-001/fixed_bond_range.md)


**Projected parity on the physical neighborhood.** Write \(L_w\) for the displayed holder set and \(A\) for the two holders of the mark. For \(d\ge w+2\) the wired complement of \(L_w\) is connected in both preparations. In particular, supplying the true global allowed states projected onto these visible ports preserves every smaller-radius equality above. The condition includes every subcritical \(w\) when \(d\ge2\), and the first distinguishing radius when \(d\ge4\).

To prove connectivity, take any outside ordinary neighbor \(x\) of the ball. Its distance from \(A\) is exactly \(w+1\). If its age \(t>w\), every descendant is still outside by age alone, and a child chain reaches a true loose distinction at the present end. For \(t\le w\), the distance-to-ancestor formula gives an original ancestor \(z\) exactly \(m=w+1-t\ge1\) old steps from \(A\), and no original ancestor nearer than \(m\). Since \(m\le w+1<d\), this \(z\) belongs to the same plain side as the marked holders. On the side of \(A\) away from the attachment choose the continuation of its path parity out through the physical end spare. On the side of \(A\) toward the attachment choose that parity going inward, and continue to the opposite physical end spare. If the loaded attachment interrupts this latter parity at \(b_{s+2}\), its released endless spare is the continuation instead. The other parity goes through the surviving pair on \(b_{s-1},b_{s+1}\). Reflect these directions for a left-side mark. In each case this gives an infinite route in the point presentation starting at a lift of \(z\), none of whose original holders is nearer than \(m\) to \(A\). Any finite cluster containing this lift can add the first point on the route it still lacks. The resulting knots remain actual children, keep \(z\), and add no nearer original ancestor. Their distance to \(A\) therefore rises by one at each step until they reach the present true loose distinctions. Every outside component either contains the wiring exterior or touches the ball in the connected whole presentation. The exit just proved joins every such component to that exterior. The parity projection criterion above now applies. [Proof of both parity continuations](../SAME-SIDE-READING-20261119-001/side_bonds_proof.md)
