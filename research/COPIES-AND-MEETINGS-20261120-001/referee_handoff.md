## Final report as sent

20 November 2026, 01:14 PM PST.

**Repeated copies.** A finite start cannot keep releasing independent copies of one fixed finite product in the class where every birth can be reconstructed from the start. This holds even if each copy can grow arbitrarily large after release. After finitely many early releases, each lasting copy must keep its own repeating sequence of parents or its own continuing chain. At a fixed launch width the start has only finitely many such sequences and chains. Beyond this class, the proof covers every one-knot release and every product that stays at three knots or fewer. The general case is open. [Proof of the bound](https://github.com/jscul/knotlab/blob/main/research/COPIES-AND-MEETINGS-20261120-001/bounded.md)

**Later meetings.** Yes. In the seven-knot source, two products released at cluster size 159 never meet directly. Both persist, and their first common descendant appears at size 308, **149 parent steps later**. On any growing end that continues to fund its births, two families first share a descendant after exactly as many parent steps as there were witnessed steps between them at release. One witnessed step connects two live knots that a present knot witnesses together. Products in separate witnessed parts stay independent by parentage forever. [Seven-knot example](https://github.com/jscul/knotlab/blob/main/research/COPIES-AND-MEETINGS-20261120-001/generator_delay.md) · [Timing proof](https://github.com/jscul/knotlab/blob/main/research/COPIES-AND-MEETINGS-20261120-001/propagation.md)

**One product can change what the other makes.** In a start with 21 knots and 60 distinctions, a descendant of the older product supplies an actual input to a child of the younger one. The two products never meet directly. Changing one holding at the start prevents the older product’s release. The same younger knot still makes 34 children, but the corresponding child receives **33 fresh distinctions instead of 34**, and the total across those children falls from 1,174 to 1,170. Keeping both products present in both starts, **one shared distinction** suffices to give their descendants a meeting. In another matched pair, one extra loose distinction changes the first common child from two fresh distinctions to three. One is the least positive edit under either count. [Matched receiving knot](https://github.com/jscul/knotlab/blob/main/research/COPIES-AND-MEETINGS-20261120-001/indirect.md) · [Both-products comparison](https://github.com/jscul/knotlab/blob/main/research/COPIES-AND-MEETINGS-20261120-001/start_edits.md)

**Checks.** Both parity and not-all-equal gave these results. The seven-knot start was exhausted through eight parent steps, reaching 765 knots and 4,212 distinctions. The size-308 result is proved from the parent map. Among initially separated pairs followed for at least two steps, a common descendant was observed in **140/1,634** pair observations under uniform labelled trees and **95/970** under fresh such trees with one uniform extra meeting, with 240 starts per weighting. The separate jump-selected archive contributed 14 lawful starts out of 16. A reader restricted to the growing end gets the meeting delay for marked parties, but an exact swap can hide which earlier product it sees.
