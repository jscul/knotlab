# What a live triple identifies when the allowed states agree

**Theorem.** Let a reachable source have the seed as its only three-place junction, and at most four additions making three fresh distinctions. Let any reachable rival have the same allowed-state relation after carrying the bit names by a fixed bijection. If the source has a legal three-parent addition, the bijection identifies every actual junction support. It also preserves every current three-parent permission, by the [full-state inverse](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/inverse.md). Thus a four-birth example with a changed site directly involved in a legal triple under an exact scalar-table match would have to change the allowed-state relation on that same correspondence.

The proof counts **unary additions**. In this source a unary addition has three outputs because a unary two-output addition would give another three-place site. Two- and three-parent additions making three outputs are allowed and use the same budget. The rival receives no bound on births or junction arities beyond its own full serial law. A physical site is compared by the literal set of distinctions it joins.

## The ordinary block containing the mark

Use the wired representation with one exterior for loose distinctions. Equality of allowed states identifies all labeled simple cycles of this representation, including parallel pairs as two-bit cycles. The three marked holders and their three distinct pair witnesses lie on an ordinary six-cycle. Write \(B\) for its maximal two-connected ordinary block.

Every cyclic ordinary block has at least two distinct edges born at unary additions. For its earliest multiple-parent addition, take two inputs and their earlier common witness. The four meetings form a cycle in that very block. Its two pre-existing edges cannot have been introduced by an earlier multiple-parent addition, since each input at such an addition already lies on a cycle with the other inputs. Each cyclic block has a multiple-parent addition, as the latest vertex on a cycle has two older neighbors. Each ordinary bridge also came from a unary addition. Distinct ordinary blocks have disjoint edge sets. [Unary-edge count](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001M/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001M-P001_unary_third/incidence.md#a-cyclic-ordinary-graph-has-at-most-one-leaf-outside-its-block)

We recall two consequences of the [labeled block proof](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/inverse.md#the-part-supporting-the-three-marked-inputs). For any cyclic ordinary block, retain its ordinary edges. At each vertex replace the group of all its loose edges and all branches attached there by one indexed connection to the exterior, omitting empty groups. The resulting simple augmented graph, the torso, is three-connected. Removing the exterior and one ordinary vertex leaves the two-connected block connected. Removing two ordinary vertices preserves all routes to the exterior after projecting the full lawful graph onto the torso. Its size is sufficient already for a four-cycle. The equal cycles and graphic ranks recover the same group partitions, which bits within a group are direct terminal-to-terminal edges, and all labeled vertex stars in the torso. Those steps use the cyclic block and the general two-ordinary-deletion lemma. The six-site legal mark is needed for the further conclusion that the exterior of **its own** torso \(B\) keeps its identity. A group with at least two bits always has the rival exterior as one of its two terminals. Two ordinary terminals either support a forbidden parallel pair or separate an interior vertex from the exterior when those two terminals are deleted. [Locations of the two terminals](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/inverse.md#locating-the-exterior)

If \(B\) is the only cyclic ordinary block, all other ordinary edges are bridges. Equal states give identical full scalar tables, so the [exact separator theorem](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/separator.md) matches the bridge bits and their literal partitions. Cut all such bridges, keeping their bit identities as local loose boundary bits on both sides. Projection gives equality of the local states on every piece, since any discarded branch can realize either value of its single connecting bit. Each one-site piece on the tree side has equation rank one and must contain one physical site in the rival as well. In a retained lawful piece the site equations are independent, by peeling off latest sites with fresh outputs.

In the central piece the original marked bits are genuine surviving loose bits. There the source consists just of the two-connected block, with its actual loose bits and the restored bridge bits. The [retained-block exterior argument](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/inverse.md#components-retained-after-genuine-single-distinction-cuts) fixes its exterior, even if the local first site originally had four places. In this piece every exterior connection is direct or a group of parallel direct edges. Directness is determined by the labeled cycles, so each one has its same holder in the rival. The ordinary edges of the block retain their two endpoints. Restoring the matched bridge bits to their two pieces now identifies every original physical star. This argument does not require a bound on the number of tree branches when there is only one cyclic ordinary block.

## Two cyclic blocks

If a second cyclic block \(C\) exists, the count above spends all four unary edges, two inside each block. These are all four three-output additions in the source. Every other addition makes two outputs. There are no ordinary bridges and no third block. By ordinary connectedness the blocks meet at a single articulation \(v\). Write \(o\) for the exterior.

Divide the labels into disjoint sets. Put the ordinary bits of \(B\) and all its loose bits, including every loose bit at \(v\), in \(X\). Put the ordinary bits of \(C\) and the loose bits at \(C-\{v\}\) in \(Y\). In each block at least two different holders away from \(v\) have loose bits. Otherwise deleting \(v\) and the sole possible such holder would disconnect an ordinary vertex from the exterior, contrary to the lawful two-ordinary-deletion lemma. The two wired subgraphs on \(X\) and \(Y\) are each two-connected, since in each case \(o\) attaches to a two-connected block at two distinct vertices. Their common vertices are exactly \(v,o\).

The restrictions of the common labeled cycle family to \(X\) and to \(Y\) each link all their edges by cycles. Thus both corresponding edge subgraphs in the rival are connected. For connected edge sets with connected union, the graphic-rank identity is \(r(X)+r(Y)-r(X\cup Y)=|V_X\cap V_Y|-1\). The ranks are common, so the two rival sets likewise have exactly two joint terminals. Adjoin to each side one indexed virtual edge between these terminals. The cycles of an augmented side are the cycles on its own old labels and its simple terminal paths closed by the virtual edge. Those paths are exactly its intersections with full cycles crossing between the two sides. Hence the two source and rival augmented sides have matching labeled cycle families, including their virtual edges.

Each source augmented side is three-vertex-connected. Deleting the exterior and an ordinary site leaves its ordinary block connected. After two ordinary deletions project the full graph's routes to the exterior onto this side, replacing any traversal of the other side by the virtual edge. The three-connected vertex-star test of D therefore recovers all vertex stars in each rival augmented side, including the unordered pair meeting the virtual edge. It applies to parallel as well as simple edges. Its proof recognizes a bond whose deletion leaves all remaining bits linked by surviving cycles as a star, and equal rank gives the same number of vertices. The three marked loose distinctions belong to \(X\) and meet three distinct holders. They remain jointly legal in the rival by D, hence are loose there. Their unique common vertex in the identified augmented \(X\) is the global exterior \(o\). The globally ordered terminals on \(X\) are consequently fixed. Matching the \(Y\) stars at the shared terminals now leaves exactly two orders. If the order agrees, every actual star agrees, including the union of the two local stars at \(v\) with the virtual edges removed.

Suppose the order reverses. Every vertex of \(C_0=C-\{v\}\) has at most one original loose bit, since two such bits would become parallel ordinary meetings in the rival. Conversely each such vertex has at most one original edge to \(v\), by ordinary simplicity, so the same loose bound holds after the reversal. Every vertex in \(C_0\) has precisely the same labeled incidences on the two sides once both types of bits are read as loose. The only possible ordinary exit of \(C_0\) is the vertex attached to \(B\) on the corresponding side. Its latest site has at least two outputs, at most one loose, and at most one directed to that ordinary exit. Therefore the exit follows every member of \(C_0\). Connectedness of every chronological prefix forces \(C_0\) to be the entire prefix before that exit, in each realization. In particular no site beyond this articulation could have been used as a pair witness for an addition on this prefix.

The two groups consumed by the respective exit sites are complementary sets of the same old loose distinctions. Both have to be legal on this identical physical prefix, regardless of differing birth orders. In the source all ordinary edges incident with \(v\) inside \(C\) are incoming and there are at least two, since \(C\) is two-connected. The source addition at \(v\) is therefore not unary. Both unary edges of \(C\) were born strictly in \(C_0\). All unary edges of \(B\) are later and every other addition makes two outputs. Thus \(C_0\) is a genuine history from the original ternary seed in the two-three-output family of 001J.

That family always has at least five loose bits. Each complementary group uses at most three. The source group has at least two, by the degree of \(v\) in \(C\), and the rival group has at least two because two distinct original holders in \(C-\{v\}\) have loose bits. Two groups of size two cannot cover the frontier. Sizes two and three would give a legal triple on five loose bits. Appending that triple with two outputs to this very 001J prefix would leave just four loose bits, also impossible. Finally the 001J fixed point excludes two complementary legal triples on six loose distinctions, including different selections from the same holder. [Exact six-loose exclusion](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J-P001_seven_to_six/family.md#all-histories-have-at-most-two-shrinking-joins) The reversal is impossible. The theorem follows.

## Exactly equal named states above the two width-32 splits

Take the actual complementary final choices of [001L](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001L/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001L-P001_binary_third/empty_witness.json) or [001M](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001M/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001M-P001_unary_third/examples.json). Write \(P,Q\) for the disjoint triples and \(x\) for their alternative cap, site 13. In both cases the three holders of \(P\) are the three holders of \(Q\), each supplying one distinct bit to either group. The two alternative caps make the same new bits 30 and 31. The original ordinary graph at width 32 is two-connected in both families. Adding at most one further unary edge cannot start a second cyclic block, since that would need two unary edges outside the existing block. Thus the one-block proof already excludes a live changing support under any state-preserving correspondence of any descendant, independently of the two-block argument.

More can be said on the identity of the original names. Suppose two final extensions have the same allowed states and the same fresh bits in birth order. Every later step extends every state on its prior bits, so projection yields equality at all common prefixes. The batch boundaries themselves can be recovered if just the order of fresh names is specified. The rank of equations supported on a prefix of those names increases by one at the end of each fresh batch. Forgetting any bit of a partially included batch removes its new parity equation, and later births are conservative.

At a common stage, adding the same fresh batch on old inputs \(A\) on the left and \(A'\) on the right gives equal states if and only if \(A\mathbin{\triangle}A'\) is in the old common equation space. Suppose the earlier additions have used identical names, all avoiding \(P\cup Q\). A word supported on currently usable input names on the two sides has one of just four intersections with \(P\cup Q\), namely \(\varnothing,P,Q,P\cup Q\). Indeed every internal bit of the old common prefix is already shared on both sides. Its absence from the word makes the selected prefix-site coefficients constant by ordinary connectedness. Selecting them all contributes \(P\cup Q\), the old cap can contribute \(P\), and no common later site meets these six bits. Any nonempty intersection would force one side to consume its entire residual triple in a single move. Its three holders still share \(x\), so this is forbidden. With empty intersection the difference is a word supported entirely on the *common* loose bits. Those form a proper subset of either side's loose frontier. Connectedness again implies that the only such parity word is zero. The inputs must be identical and avoid \(P\cup Q\).

The only common loose names initially are 30 and 31 at \(x\). The first further step is therefore unary on either one, and must be the fourth three-output birth. Each such choice preserves the equal states. Next the common loose bits lie at \(x\) and its child. Those two adjacent sites cannot be co-parents. With the fourth three-output birth spent, a further unary move is impossible. Both remaining menus are empty. The three old residual holders and the unary child all meet \(x\), and \(x\) alone lies on the other bipartition side among the loose holders. This classifies all equal-state continuations under the common fresh-name order. It allows different operands and different proposed times for the fourth birth.
