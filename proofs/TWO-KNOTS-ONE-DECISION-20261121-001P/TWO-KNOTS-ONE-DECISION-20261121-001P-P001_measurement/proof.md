## 1. Deriving the physical change masks

Give a mark e its actual bit in an ordinary word w. For every local identity supported on a subset t of the names record the required parity b. Stack the rows t into A. The finite carrier W is the kernel of A over the two-element field, so every bit is a physical mark in the *homogeneous* comparison. Distinct coded apparatus holdings remain lawful through its preparation. Write the prospective motion and sign as
~~~text
U_e |w> = (−1)^(w_e+s_e) |w XOR d_e>.
~~~
The preservation condition A d_e=0 permits the named flip throughout W. The square is the identity exactly when d_e[e]=0. Applying e then f instead of f then e changes only the sign bit d_e[f]+d_f[e]. The two orders agree throughout W precisely when these two matrix entries agree. These requirements apply for each pair requested in a single holder.

For a signed identity t, sequentially apply its e in increasing name order. The final ordinary word is w XOR the sum of the d_e. The phase is the sum of the s_e, of the w_e, and of d_e[f] over earlier e and later f. The middle sum is zero on W. Thus a scalar signed product needs a zero change sum and a phase equal to b. Conversely those two conditions give exactly the required product on *every* carrier word. Each step was an equivalence. These are binary **linear** conditions on the unknown real signed changes and ordinary sign bits, including the order-dependent term. ## 5. An all-history obstruction with a counted price

Let Alice sweep all her holders once. Bob chooses holder j and repeats it throughout. Every physical mark occurs exactly once on each side’s catalogue. Let M_j be the distribution of the *whole locally available Alice transcript*, N_j the number of mismatching actually covered bits with both words present, and B_bad the event of a missing, invalid or unstable Bob word. For Alice let A_bad include a missing or locally invalid sweep word. On a good Alice sweep assemble an ordinary assignment from her answers. Its product of all parity signs is the fixed product of Alice's legal signs. With odd total source charge at least one Bob parity fails. For each j let V_j be the event in that very same Alice transcript that its assembled word violates Bob's j-th parity; define V_j false on A_bad.

For every reference k, the union bound gives 1−P_k(A_bad) ≤ sum_j M_k(V_j). Each M_k(V_j) is at most M_j(V_j)+TV(M_k,M_j). In a j run with a valid Alice sweep, Bob complete and stable, violation of the assembled parity forces at least one actual bit mismatch. Hence M_j(V_j) ≤ E_j N_j + P_j(B_bad). Combining,
~~~text
1 ≤ P_k(A_bad) + sum_j [ E_j N_j + P_j(B_bad) + TV(M_k,M_j) ].
~~~
There is no independence assumption on any two misses. With complete legal words, repeat-stable Bob and no local signalling, the sum of the mismatch expectations must be at least one. With perfect agreement as well, for each k the sum of the total variations to *other* sweeps is at least one, so some variation is at least 1/(m−1) for m fixed holder choices. For even total charge the ordinary global assignment shows why the argument cannot be transplanted. A device that always lacks records pays in the explicit failure terms. These are bounds for fixed experiments, not a union over postselected traces. Exchanging the readers gives the same argument.
