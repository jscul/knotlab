# Which trees can close at any formation step?

**Under the registered loose-distinction rule, a connected tree on six or more starting knots can never close by ordinary parity growth.** The obstruction applies at every formation step. With four or five starting knots, a closure requires one spent knot meeting every other starting knot, each of which is a leaf. Exact weights for the four-step event then require only five funded arrangements. This argument uses the given cap of one extra loose distinction per holder. [Frozen source](../IDLE-PHASE-AND-TREE-CLOSURE-20261121-001B/preregistration.md) · [Full physical replay and funding check](tree_check.py)

**Persistence of separate groups.** Call an old knot live if it has a loose distinction. Relate two live knots when a third physical knot meets both, and take the connected components of that relation on the current live knots. A funded group contains only mutually related parents, or a single parent. Every live parent contributes to at least one birth because all its loose distinctions are spent. At the following step the only loose distinctions belong to the newborns, and two of these have a common witness only if one old parent contributed to both. Thus children of different old live components cannot become related. The complete physical holding stays connected because every new knot spends an old distinction. When at least two live groups remain, the funded roster has at least two births. No one of them uses every loose name of the complete connected holding. Its new parity equation is then independent of the older rows, so it receives at least one fresh name. Induction preserves at least one descendant from each live group at every funded step. Stalling also gives no closure. This distinction between connected physical holdings and connected *live* knots is essential. [Ordinary funded roster and whole-end exception](../ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/end_operator.md) · [Criterion for an implied closing equation](../ERASURE-AND-CLOSING-SIGNS-20261121-001B/closing_law.md)

**Restriction on the starting tree.** A tree has two classes of knots, with each direct meeting joining opposite classes. Related live knots share a witness, and so are always in the same class. For the initial live knots to be connected, they must therefore all be in one class. Every leaf is live since its initial supply is two or three. Every knot in that same class must also be live. Indeed a nonleaf is on the unique sequence of meetings between two leaves in different branches at that knot. Removing it disconnects those leaves in the live relation as well, because every live-relation walk expands to a walk of two meetings at a time with all its intervening witnesses in the opposite class.

Every knot in the opposite class is spent. Its degree is at least three because a holder of degree one or two must have loose distinctions. In a tree, every set of three or more knots pairwise sharing witnesses has one common witness. For three, two different witnesses on their unique meeting sequences would put the remaining pair four meetings apart. Any further member must share that same witness. Such a set supplies no unheld larger group. Each live knot therefore owes one opening for every *other* live knot sharing a witness with it. A live knot of original degree \(d\) owes exactly \(\sum_{w\sim u}(\deg(w)-1)\), since all the other neighbours of each witness are live and no two witnesses can contribute the same neighbour in a tree. This demand is at least \(2d\). For \(d\ge2\) the registered supply \(\max(0,3-d)+b\), with \(b\in\{0,1\}\), is at most two. The first cut would stall. Hence in a funded tree capable of closing every live knot is a leaf. Connectedness then entails a single spent common holder. With \(l\) leaves each leaf owes \(l-1\), and each has at most three loose distinctions. Thus \(l\le4\). This proof applies to every size of connected tree on at least four knots under the stated supply cap. The original unsigned rule separately keeps at least one fresh name at each funded birth in this class. [Original unsigned growth rule](../NAE-GROWTH-20261119-001/nae_growth.md)

**Sufficiency through four steps.** Replay the surviving arrangements using the named physical growth engine, retaining the full old incidence for the operator test. With three leaves and zero, one or two extra leaf openings, ordinary parity closes on steps 2, 3 and 4, on 8, 12 and 18 knots on 12, 20 and 33 distinctions respectively. Only the third admits the single-odd operator reading. With all three leaves given an extra opening it is still open after step four. With four leaves only the configuration giving all four an extra opening funds the first step. It closes on step three on 16 knots and 32 distinctions and admits the operator reading. The supplied odd measurement uses a different rule from ordinary growth. Ordinary growth closes at the implied even sign in every case.

Write \(p\) for the independent chance of one extra opening. At size \(n\) the possible common holder has \(n\) labelled choices among \(n^{n-2}\) trees. It must receive no extra opening. If \(k\) of its \(n-1\) leaves receive an extra, their factor is \({n-1\choose k}p^k(1-p)^{n-1-k}\). The five tree sizes receive equal weight. At \(p=1/4\), four starting knots therefore give closure \(189/1024\) and admitting closure \(27/1024\). Five give \(3/25600\) for both events. All larger sizes give zero. The resulting mixture gives \(591/16000\) for closure, \(339/64000\) for admitting closure and \(113/788\) for admission conditional on closure. The unsigned conditional is undefined. The finite check also verifies the formula for initial funding by independent label counts through nine starting knots. [Exact funded, closing, and admitted populations](tree_check.py)

## Check just beyond the frozen horizon

After the four-step population had been calculated, a boundary check asked about the single still-open arrangement permitted by the necessary condition. This supplementary check leaves the primary table at four steps. The older finite-cluster theorem already decides when this arrangement ends. The three leaves each have three openings and owe two. The one remaining opening at each gives one auxiliary point and no endless continuation under the theorem's floor. There is one finite component of six points. Its final cluster appears at step five and closes. The ordinary engine gave cohorts of 6, 7, 6, 3 and 1 births, with zero fresh distinctions at the last birth. The resulting 27 knots on 54 names admit the single-odd operator reading. Its source mass is \(3/5120\) under the registered mixture. Adding that one class gives all-step probabilities \(4803/128000\) for closure, \(753/128000\) for admitting closure and \(251/1601\) for admission conditional on closure. Under either law the probabilities reported as four-step results keep their original denominators and observations. **Changing the funding assumption.** The all-size bound has a sharp limit if the question is whether arbitrarily large trees can close. Allowing *two* extra openings still leaves a live knot of degree at least two with at most three openings against at least four calls. Only a spent common knot with leaves can qualify, now with at most five leaves. Allowing *three* extras suffices for unbounded starting sizes. Take a chain of \(m\ge2\) spent three-place holders, each successive pair meeting the same live degree-two connector on distinct names. Complete each spent holder's three meetings with leaves. Give each connector four openings and each leaf two. The resulting \(3m+1\) starting knots have one connected live component. Every opening is spent in the first cut, leaving the finite-cluster construction with \(2m+1\) points and no additional or endless points. The known identification applies because the original holdings form a tree, no pair has two witnesses, and every initial group is a pair under one witness. The full cluster closes at step \(2m\). The physical engine reproduced closure at steps four and six on the seven- and ten-knot starts respectively, and kept growing under the unsigned law on those same starts. This sensitivity control changes the initial funding without changing either growth law. These longer trees already admit the odd reading after closure. Choose a live connector between two of the spent holders. It has four distinct other live neighbours in the identified family, two through each witness. Each subset of the four together with the connector is a connected cluster retaining that original knot. Adding any one missing neighbour gives a genuine single-point parent relation in the cluster theorem. The sixteen clusters thus give 32 meetings, joining subsets of opposite cardinality parity. A planar simple bipartite graph on 16 vertices has at most 28 edges. The final physical incidence therefore contains a nonplanar subgraph, which suffices for the earlier signed criterion. This rules out the prospect that replaying successively longer examples of this particular family will uncover an opposite signed verdict. Where a source has an additional old meeting, the unique-witness argument for trees needs to be tested afresh.
