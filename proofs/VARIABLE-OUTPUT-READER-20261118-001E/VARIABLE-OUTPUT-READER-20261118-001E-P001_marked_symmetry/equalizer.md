# Unmarked continuations cannot equalize the width-22 pair

Start each side at D's literal width-22 arrangement. Keep the loose distinctions \(\{14,15,20\}\) on the legal side and \(\{14,15,16\}\) on the forbidden side. Each side may take its own finite sequence of legal serial moves on other distinctions, with two or three fresh distinctions at each move. No full marked-table isometry exists between the results. The same holds after restricting each table to the part retained by the single-distinction separator rule. [Starting history and measurements](equalize_rows.json)

All marks stay loose at their three distinct original holders. On the legal side the holders of 14 and 15 each have only their reserved loose distinction. Their common witnesses stay fixed. The holder of 20 cannot acquire a new meeting with either of those already present witnesses. The three pair witnesses persist, so the triple stays legal. On the forbidden side junction 7 remains a common witness.

## Why the original table is fixed on the forbidden side

The only initial holders available for its continuation are junction 7, with three loose distinctions, and junction 8, with two. These two junctions neither meet nor have a common witness. Inductively, every addition belongs to the descendants of just one of these holders, and the two collections of descendants have no meetings or common witnesses with one another. A join using parents from both would require an earlier common witness across them. An addition within one collection cannot create such a witness, because all its meetings are with its chosen parents. Each collection therefore attaches to the old arrangement at just its original holder.

Compute a bond as a minimal cut in the usual graph for the parity equations, with an exterior endpoint for every loose distinction. Both sides of a bond are connected. Let \(A\) be the set of original junctions on the side away from the exterior. If \(A\) is nonempty, any selected new junctions from one of the collections attach through a holder in \(A\). The old cut of \(A\) is already a bond. Its selected side stays connected when each excursion into new junctions is removed. On the other side, an original junction can use its route to the exterior in the extended graph. Whenever that route leaves the original graph through one of the new collections, replace the rest of the route by the holder's originally loose distinction to the exterior. This gives connectedness of the old complement. [Bond criterion and parity representation](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/separator.md)

Selecting new junctions attached at a holder can cancel at most one old distinction per selected junction, and at most three in total. One and two selected junctions contribute at least two and three new boundary distinctions respectively. With three or more, the last three selected junctions introduce at least six fresh distinctions. At most two can be taken within those three, since the ordinary meeting pattern is bipartite and two junctions share at most one distinction. At least four new boundary distinctions remain. So each nonempty selected collection strictly increases the size of the projected old bond. Any original distinction on the new bond was already on that projected bond.

If \(A\) is empty, a connected selected set is entirely within one collection. For its cut to contain two distinct old distinctions, it must select two different children of its holder. They have the same bipartition and therefore do not meet. Connectedness requires at least three selected junctions, giving at least four new boundary distinctions and a cut of size at least six. The original holder's star was a bond of size at most six containing those same two old distinctions. Together, these cases show that no old pair minimum can decrease on the forbidden side.

On either side, old bonds persist. A parity sum using any added junction contains a fresh distinction of its last included junction, so no new parity support lies wholly among the original distinctions. A smaller support contained in an old bond would therefore already have existed. Thus old pair distances cannot increase on either side. The forbidden side consequently preserves every old entry exactly.

## Why no marked map can mix original and fresh distinctions

In the original arrangement, every distinction is within bond distance seven of each mark on either side. Every originally loose distinction at holder 7 or 8 is at bond distance exactly seven from each of the forbidden side's marks 14 and 15. For a bond between either of those marks and a fresh distinction, the selected side contains the mark's original holder and some new junctions from the corresponding collection. It also contains that collection's original holder. The projected old bond contains the mark and an originally loose distinction of that holder, so has size at least eight. The strict increase just proved gives size at least nine in the new bond. [Initial margins and exact continuation](equalize_rows.json)

The set of distinctions within seven of **every** mark, with each distinction at distance zero from itself, therefore consists of exactly the 22 original distinctions on the forbidden side and includes all 22 on the legal side. Under a marked isometry these sets would correspond. They would have equal size, so the isometry would carry the original distinctions onto one another. The original forbidden-side table stays fixed, and the original legal-side entries can only decrease. Equality of the original tables up to a permutation would force equality of their sums and hence every legal-side entry to stay fixed. The initial marked tables already differ under an isometry invariant. At the mark opposite the unique shortest pair, count pairs of unmarked distinctions at bond distance five with one member in its distance-six set and the other in its distance-seven set. The count is five for the legal marks and zero for the forbidden marks. [Original shell comparison](equalize_rows.json)

The original ordinary arrangement has no separating shared distinction. Its original shared distinctions remain in their cycles, and the retained part contains all nine original junctions. It therefore retains every original distinction, including any originally loose distinction that has become a boundary distinction. The same radius and sum arguments apply to the retained tables, which use the restricted full scalar entries. ## Controls

A lawful width-36 continuation on the forbidden side leaves the 231 original entries fixed. One collection of new junctions survives pruning and the other is removed. Every fresh distinction is at distance at least eight from marks 14 and 15. On the legal side, one lawful join on 16 and 21 shows that original entries can decrease and fresh distinctions can lie within seven of all marks. A counterfactual join on 17 and 20 that ignores the parent test preserves the forbidden marks but breaks both properties on that side. These controls exercise the proof premises on the literal seed.
