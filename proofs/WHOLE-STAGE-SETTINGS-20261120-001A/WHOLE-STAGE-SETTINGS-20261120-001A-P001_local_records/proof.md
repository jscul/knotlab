# When an ordinary whole-stage decision reveals a free setting

**Standard statement.** A deterministic change in a visible local record distinguishes two interventions on one fixed preparation. In a setting-independent mixture the amount visible in a binary flag is the absolute conditional difference of its two success probabilities, averaged over the observer's prior information. We give an exact result for two independent private address labels, two local repairs, and a common decision to install a round only when both repairs succeed.

**Nearest known result.** Having four preassigned local answers with one common distribution gives the classical Bell bounds. Setting-dependent acceptance has also been studied in the literature. The operation here retains every trial. A stalled round is one of two locally observable events and has a defined output. We compare the four *unconditional* tables for actual ordinary joins with independent free edits. ## 1. What can be read on a fixed presentation

The prepared presentation and state precede the two independent free settings. A trial contains one **known attempted round**. In this protocol an observer can compare its holdings before and after that attempt, including absence of a birth that would have used a known persistent distinction. Counting across attempts supplies a reading convention. It supplies neither a clock inside the unedited logic nor a signal propagation speed. The accessible record includes the own edit, the incidence and values the observer can inspect before and after the edit and attempted round, and any disclosed preparation information. Compare records after transporting names of corresponding local distinctions. A global enumeration number supplies no local observation.

Fix one preparation *type* (the unweighted incidence and any source data designated public), an observer and its own setting. For two remote settings, let \(m_0,m_1\) be the two deterministic structural parts of that observer's record. If the records differ after transport, their two laws have disjoint support in this structural coordinate. Their total variation is 1 under **every** weighting of the allowed states. One actual local observation distinguishes the two alternatives. If the structural records agree, their contribution to total variation is zero. Value or history readings can still differ and need their own joint-state calculation.

For ordinary funding, a shortage anywhere cancels every proposal. A persistent, offered local distinction gains a second holder exactly on a funded round if it has a known local proposal on every funded arm. In comparison S the two designated holders are already live. Appending a loose distinction there leaves the live roster, candidate groups and obligations fixed. It can remove a shortage and cannot create one. A remote supply edit that changes the global funding decision therefore has TV 1 at a fixed type for the other reader with such a persistent mark. Bringing a spent holder into the live roster, altering which loose distinction is offered, or using an observer with no local proposal need separate reasoning. In particular an inert observer on an unchanged holding, with no locally visible target or nearby birth, may have exactly the same record despite a distant global stall. Choosing a fixed transposition of offered names calls for transporting the marked read as well.

For ordinary connected parity, a designated funded birth consuming every loose distinction is already implied, and in the closing configuration receives zero fresh. An otherwise identical nonimplied birth at zero next demand receives at least one. A reader with its offered mark and that child's complete fresh count distinguishes these conditions with certainty at a fixed type. It may also distinguish a missing child from one born with zero fresh. A predicate coding both as zero hides that distinction. Unsigned not-all-equal on the admitted arity-three-or-more, at-most-two-holder starts has no corresponding nonempty implied birth. Selecting one loose input per parent leaves at least two others unpinned at each parent, so one can make all selected inputs zero in an old solution. This removes the **parity closure** mechanism under not-all-equal. Global funding still applies under both laws. The stronger convention rejecting an implied two-place final parity birth changes which child exists and must be analyzed with its own record. [Ordinary operator and closure example](../../../research/ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/end_operator.md) · [Referee on the scope of shared descent](../../../research/COPIES-AND-MEETINGS-20261120-001/referee-R1/report.md)

For any binary value readings one sufficient Bell-local condition is a common prepared random variable \(L\) independent of the external indices, with each observed local record a function of its own index and \(L\), allowing local random coins pre-sampled in \(L\). The four potential answers then coexist. For one complete list of signs their canonical expression is \(A_0(B_0+B_1)+A_1(B_0-B_1)=\pm2\). Every odd-minus orientation has the same bound, and averaging preserves it. The absence of a common birth between two families alone does not give this factorization: the ordinary whole-stage rule still sees whether every proposed birth is funded. A missing local structural difference alone also does not prove factorization of stochastic value readings.

## 2. Exact visibility in a mixture

Let the original preparation be sampled with a common weighting independent of the settings. At a fixed own edit \(x\), let \(I\) be everything already known locally before the attempted round, including own source information and the own edit. Couple counterfactual remote choices using the same preparation, the same local edit coins and an observation time fixed in advance. Suppose \(I\) is unchanged by the far choice. Write \(H_0,H_1\in\{0,1\}\) for a locally distinguishable funding flag (or a designated closure event) on the two arms, and \(\Delta=H_0-H_1\). Then

\[
\begin{split}
d_{\rm TV}((I,H_0),(I,H_1))
 &= \mathbb E\,\left|P(H_0=1\mid I)-P(H_1=1\mid I)\right|\\
 &= P(\Delta\ne0)-2\,\mathbb E\min\{P(\Delta=1\mid I),P(\Delta=-1\mid I)\}.
\end{split}
\]

Indeed at each fixed value of \(I\) the two possibilities for the flag contribute the same absolute difference to the half-sum defining total variation. Writing that difference as the conditional mass changing in one direction minus the mass changing back gives the second formula. Both statements apply with the sides exchanged.

The TV of a complete record that decodes \((I,H)\) is **at least** that amount. It is equal if all further local data are supplied by the same conditional probability rule given own setting, \(I,H\), on both remote arms. We use this condition where claiming equality for the full record. If the pre-edit regions differ or a remote edit changes local values even while \(H\) agrees, compute those records directly. For a common coupling making records identical whenever \(H_0=H_1\), the full TV is at most \(P(\Delta\ne0)\).

If the two flags are ordered pointwise in a common direction, or are ordered with a direction determined by \(I\), the visible flag TV equals the fraction of preparations on which the remote choice changes it. This proves the monotone-mixture bound for the fixed-roster increasing-supply operation S. It equally applies to a closing family on which a fixed local target and a single order of exhaustion changes have been proved. It does not assert such an order when introducing a new live holder changes the group roster. Outside ordered strata, opposite changes can cancel **even in a complete, fully recorded local test**. The fact that an observer could have read the global source if it had been disclosed is different from that source being in \(I\).

## 3. Exact reduction for the two-address operation

Let \(U,V\) be fair independent preference labels fixed before the independent free settings \(x,y\). Editing the deficient address at each side clears all local shortages. The whole round is funded exactly when

\[
G=\mathbf 1\{x=U\}\mathbf 1\{y=V\}.
\]

Each locally knows its own label. A designated persistent local loose distinction has a proposal on every funded branch and gains a holder there. Thus both observers can decode the *same* flag from their full records. Their primary bit, the logical AND of a terminal value with that gain, erases the flag at a zero terminal. Giving every stalled trial output zero is a total read, with no discards.

For Alice write \(K_v\) for the law of her pre-attempt record conditional on \(U=x,V=v\). It includes every old bit and incidence in the declared region, rather than just the selected bit. For fair preferences the exact TV of Alice's initial record and her visible gain is

\[
d_{\rm TV}( (I,G)_{x0},(I,G)_{x1})=\tfrac12 d_{\rm TV}(K_0,K_1).
\]

Only the half of trials with \(U=x\) can change the gain. On that half the two laws for \(G=1\) have masses \(K_0/2\) and \(K_1/2\), while the masses for \(G=0\) are interchanged. This proves the formula. More generally, for independent weights \(\pi_x=P(U=x)\) and \(\tau_v=P(V=v)\), it is \(\pi_x\sum_i|\tau_0K_0(i)-\tau_1K_1(i)|\). When \(K_0=K_1\) but the remote preference is weighted 3/4 versus 1/4, this is \(\pi_x/2\), hence 1/4 for a fair own preference. The primary bit changes by TV 1/8 if the terminal is fair on the funded preparations. If the remote preference is publicly known each trial, the two conditional records are disjoint in that label. Then flag TV is 1/2 for fair own preferences, and 1 on the pivotal fixed types. These calculations separate an observer's prior information from the state-dependent fact that **half** the fair trials have their gain changed by switching the far setting.

For full records there is a direct check that makes no assumption about how fresh values are sampled. On \(U=x,V=v\), let \(F_v\) be the distribution of Alice's entire record when \(y=v\) and \(S_v\) its distribution when \(y\ne v\). If \(U\ne x\) always stalls with the same local record law at either far choice, fair preferences and local decodability of \((U,G)\) give

\[
d_{\rm TV}^{\rm full}=\tfrac14\big[d_{\rm TV}(F_0,F_1)+d_{\rm TV}(S_0,S_1)\big].
\]

The three classes \(U\ne x\), \(U=x,G=1\), and \(U=x,G=0\) are disjoint in the record, and have weights \(1/2,1/4,1/4\). Thus equality of the two *complete* funded laws and equality of the two complete stalled laws are together necessary and sufficient at this interface. Observing the far address edit itself changes that interface.

Now let \(Q_{uv}(a,b)\) describe the pair of all declared premeasurement payload records at the two endpoints under the actual prepared type \((u,v)\). These tables are to be computed from that preparation. In particular they are not a table indexed by the subsequent free settings. Suppose each local post-round record consists of its own label and payload, the visible common \(G\), and locally generated data given those values and its own edit. This last condition is satisfied when required old inputs are included in a fixed local buffer (or in a latent locally generated extension of the payload), the new local birth roster depends on the remote side only through \(G\), and fresh distinctions can be sampled locally given those inputs. For the common-source constructions below, any extra joint randomness used by the two local record generators must itself have a setting-independent shared-randomness representation. Separate one-side probability rules alone suffice for the marginal test but do not by themselves imply such a representation of all joint data.

* If \(Q^A_{u0}=Q^A_{u1}\) and \(Q^B_{0v}=Q^B_{1v}\) on the **complete declared payloads**, the full record on either side has the same distribution at fixed own setting for both far settings. First apply the gain formula, then the equal conditional local record rule. Conversely, if the initial payloads, preferences and common gain are all retained, equality of each side's full marginal forces these equalities. On \(U=x,G=1\), for example, Alice's initial payload has mass \(Q^A_{xy}/4\).
* The complete four tables of \((U,a,G;V,b,G)\) admit a Bell-local model **if and only if** \(Q_{uv}\), as four tables indexed by its two preference labels, admits one. For the forward direction, convert any local model to independent-of-setting latent randomness and local prospective records. Since the two flags are equal with certainty at every setting pair, all four prospective flags agree almost surely. This follows simultaneously over the four pairs because there are finitely many of them. Conditional on the resulting common event that the flag is 1, that latent model supplies a local model for \(Q_{xy}\). This conditional distribution is a step in a proof of necessity; the experiment continues to score all trials.
* For the reverse direction, write a local representation of \(Q\) using common independent randomness \(L\) and local functions \(a(u,L),b(v,L)\). Pre-sample two independent fair bits \(i,j\), independently of \(L\). At external settings \(x,y\), produce \(U=x\mathbin\oplus i\), \(V=y\mathbin\oplus j\), the corresponding two payloads, and the same flag \(\mathbf1\{i=j=0\}\) on each side. This reproduces the complete joint distribution at every setting pair. Local random extensions of the records preserve that statement under the joint-randomness condition above. This is a common explanation of the measured tables. Its alternative potential \(U\)'s can differ under contrary **own** settings. It does not say that the actual trial was prepared after seeing a free choice, or that a fixed physical preparation lacks a causal response.

The argument locates a way an ordinary veto can produce a Bell-violating *all-trial* response from correlated preparations. It also gives a control: independently prepared registers, or a separate shared source producing each endpoint's payload using only its own preference, yield no violation under this matched-address rule. Nothing in the equivalence says that an arbitrary setting-independent joint distribution on the actual tuple \((U,V,a,b)\) already gives a common distribution of all four *potential* payloads.

## 4. A wider control for independent local preparations

Suppose both sides see a common label \(C\) before the settings. Conditional on \(C=c\), let their complete local input records \(I_A,I_B\) be independent. Allow arbitrary local readiness functions \(f_x(I_A),g_y(I_B)\). Suppose the only nonlocal input to subsequent local outputs is the shared visible flag \(H=f_xg_y\), with locally simulable further records as above. Then full-record no-signalling implies a Bell-local reproduction of all four tables.

For \(C=c\), let \(\alpha_x=P(f_x=1\mid c)\) and \(\beta_y=P(g_y=1\mid c)\). The flag probability \(\alpha_x\beta_y\) must be the same at every pair of settings, since \((C,H)\) is visible on both sides. If it is positive, \(\alpha_x=\alpha\) and \(\beta_y=\beta\) are each constant. Draw common latent bits \(F,G\) independently at those probabilities. For each possible own index pre-sample a local input from its original conditional distribution given \(f_x=F\) or \(g_y=G\). The resulting pair has the original distribution on that context, its shared flag is \(FG\), and each local record uses only its own setting and the pre-sampled data. Inputs conditional on a never-drawn bit value need no definition. If the flag probability is zero in every cell at \(c\), set \(H=0\) and use the original local input marginals. Mixing these reproductions over \(C\) proves the claim. The label must be available on each side; otherwise full-record no-signalling need not hold within its strata.

A jointly uniform affine distribution of the complete pair of registers is in this class whenever those registers contain all prepared data used by the two readiness functions. Translate its support to a linear subspace \(T\) of the direct sum of the two registers. Two possible Alice values are in the same class if their difference can appear with zero Bob difference in \(T\). Define Bob's classes in the same way. The pairs allowed by \(T\) match the classes bijectively: once one pair is allowed, either coordinate can be changed within its class independently by adding an element of the corresponding kernel. Every pair of values in a matching pair of classes is thus allowed, with the same weight. The class is a common label visible to either side, and the records are independent conditional on it. This proves the assertion including degenerate registers. A mixture of *different* affine wiring relations selected by two private labels need not have this property, even when each type by itself is uniform affine.

**Supplemental test with a freely chosen message.** Suppose the sender knows its two local readiness values \(g_0(V),g_1(V)\) on the actual trial. Before receipt of an independent free message \(M\), fix two policies. For \(M=1\) choose an address attaining the larger value; for \(M=0\) choose one attaining the smaller. The receiver keeps its setting \(x\) and can decode \(H\). In the coupling on one preparation,

\[
H_+-H_-=f_x(U)\big(\max_y g_y(V)-\min_y g_y(V)\big)
=\mathbf1\{f_x(U)=1,\;g_0(V)\ne g_1(V)\}.
\]

Consequently the TV between the receiver's **flag** laws for the two messages is exactly the probability on the right. This is the largest possible difference in the probability of funding for any two sender policies, since every pointwise difference is bounded in absolute value by that same indicator. Any further receiver record containing the flag has at least this TV. Equality for the whole record follows if it can be coupled to agree whenever the flag agrees. No independence of the two readiness profiles is required. In the matched-address experiment the sender chooses the deficient or the other address. At fixed Alice setting and fair labels the flag and her complete guarded record have TV \(1/2\), and her fair-terminal-times-gain bit has TV \(1/4\). With Alice also choosing her deficient address every time, the funding flag recovers the free message with certainty. The two policies are additional local operations using the visibly prepared labels. They demonstrate signalling available through ordinary funding alongside no-signalling of the original four fixed-address tables.

**If both observers may use their known readiness.** Suppose no independent message implemented by these policies can signal in either direction, for either fixed receiver setting. Then outside one null set every possible Alice readiness of one implies Bob's two readiness values agree, and every possible Bob readiness of one implies Alice's two readiness values agree. This follows by applying the preceding identity four times. On any preparation where at least one setting pair would fund the round, both implications apply. All four local readiness values are one, and all four pairs would fund the round. On every other preparation all pairs stall. Thus the common funding decision itself is a function of the preparation alone. Conversely, an almost surely setting-independent funding decision cannot carry a message through its change. Provided that the entire subsequent record depends on the far side only through this decision, and that its further local draws have a common local simulation, one pre-sampled preparation and those local draws give potential records at all settings. The Bell bound follows even with arbitrary correlations between the prepared inputs. A Bell excess obtained solely through this visible product of two locally known readiness values necessarily allows a message under at least one pair of the additional policies. A general closing event or a readiness ordering unknown at its own site requires a separate argument.

## 5. Applying the test to the ordinary permutation construction

Each passive piece consists of two ternary constraints on \((l,s,t)\) and \((r,s,t)\). For even parity their equations force \(l=r\). There are two choices of \((s,t)\) at either common value. For not-all-equal, fixing equal terminals leaves three internal assignments, and fixing different terminals leaves two. The internal counting matrices, in bit order 0,1, are therefore

\[
K_{\rm parity}=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
K_{\rm NAE}=\begin{pmatrix}3&2\\2&3\end{pmatrix}.
\]

These counts include both internal distinctions. Along an unconstrained single line of \(n\) pieces with fair ends, the normalized not-all-equal transition has correlation \(5^{-n}\). When terminal triples also meet detector constraints, their local completion counts must be included before converting a transfer matrix into an outcome probability.

For parity the four correspondences compose in the written order \(P^uQ^vP^uQ^v\), with \(P=(0\,1),Q=(1\,2)\). If either exponent is zero the permutation is the identity. With both one it is a three-cycle, so terminal 0 arrives at a different channel. This conclusion uses two individually selected transpositions twice each and their order. A relabelling of all channel names transports the statement. Commuting involutions give identity in all four cases.

In a type with identity transfer, the two designated old parity terminals agree in every state. In the crossed type, write \(d\) for the probability that the two designated bits differ under the prepared allowed-state weighting. With fair end bits the two off-diagonal outcomes each have conditional mass \(d/2\). Only one quarter of all trials fund the round in each free-setting cell, because the two free settings must match the two fair private labels. All other trials contribute (0,0). At 00, 01, 10 the tables are consequently \((7/8,0,0,1/8)\) in order 00,01,10,11. At 11 the table is \(((7-d)/8,d/8,d/8,(1-d)/8)\). The correlators are \((1,1,1,1-d/2)\), so both the signed score and the largest odd-minus sum are \(2+d/2\). Each local bit still has probability \(1/8\) of being one. With freely variable, uniformly weighted terminal triples \(d=1/2\), giving \(S=9/4\). The required uniform projection is checked on the actual source in the construction record, alongside all other bits available to the observers. Reading only the unmodified old terminals under the same four-type preparation produces a constant correlation \(1-d/2\) at all external settings. Its signed score is \(2-d\).

Whenever \(d>0\), no quantum state and local binary measurements reproduce the four reported tables. For a quantum state \(\rho\), the real Hilbert–Schmidt inner products of \((A_x\otimes 1)\sqrt\rho\) and \((1\otimes B_y)\sqrt\rho\) are the correlators, and the vector norms are at most one. The three unit correlators force all four vectors to be identical unit vectors. The fourth correlation must equal one as well. The failure is visible in the full table even though \(9/4<2\sqrt2\).

At a guarded endpoint, invariance must hold jointly for the entire local region. The far address device with its two labels interchanged exchanges two equally attached ports. Its number of compatible completions as a function of the three incoming terminals is unchanged by any permutation of the three terminals when their attachment to the detector is symmetric. Each set of three identical passive pieces acts by the same one-line counting matrix on all three channels. Moving a hidden correspondence through this counting operation just permutes the triple arguments. Once the far completion function is symmetric, the incoming completion count seen at the boundary of an entire near guard is therefore unchanged by that hidden correspondence. This permits one to retain every internal bit on the near side jointly. It does not require three independent endpoint bits under not-all-equal. The argument requires checking that the far module really has that symmetry, that every visible local birth and fresh count is fixed by own edit and funding, and that every old input needed for its generation lies in the unexposed-switch buffer. Those checks are properties of the actual construction.

Uniform old states followed by a uniform draw *within each state's own extensions* furnish such local post-round probability rules. Conditional on all needed old inputs, fresh draws of distinct ordinary births are independent because the new distinctions are private and each old state extends. On a private distinction newly appended by the edit, parity carries an old allowed state by setting the new bit to zero. An existing satisfied not-all-equal constraint permits either value, and the registered lift gives them equal weight. Replacing this procedure by uniform sampling on the final not-all-equal presentation weights old states in proportion to their completion counts. Full-record equalities for that alternative require new calculations.

Access to additional named switch holdings can expose a remote preference. For example, in the parity case suppose Alice can see the two labelled bit triples immediately before and after one remote optional interchange, although its wiring itself is hidden. With three independent fair entering bits, the unchanged-triple law and the interchanged-triple law have TV 1/2, since interchanging two unequal bits is detectable. If this information is available jointly with her own label and gain, the formula in §3 gives TV at least 1/4 at each own setting. Direct observation of all named switch incidences can reveal the preference with certainty and gives the 1/2 mixed-type bound instead. Merely telling an observer that two guards are identically distributed does not establish that **both together** hide the remote choice. Extending fixed unchanging guards to shield any specified finite view gives a family of preparations for different radii, without promising privacy for an unrestricted view of one finite preparation.

## Evidence and scope

The companion check uses finite source tables to test the TV identity, the ordered-mixture bound, reconstruction under locally visible common-part/product controls and the matched-address reduction. It separately checks the passive single-piece counting from its two constraints, and the independent-setting table argument in §5. Full incidence, local access geometry, finite-radius buffer bounds and empirical results for each knot law belong to the construction and experiment records. The public, biased, ordered and privately mixed cases answer different operational questions. All new results are independent-referee pending.
