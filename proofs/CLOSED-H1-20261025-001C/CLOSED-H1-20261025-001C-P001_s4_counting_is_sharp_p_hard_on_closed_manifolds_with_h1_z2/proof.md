# S4 counting is #P-hard on closed manifolds with first homology Z/2

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

**Setting.**
- K ⊂ S³ is a knot with group π, meridian μ and preferred longitude λ.
- T(K) = |{ρ: π → S₄ : ρ(μ) is a transposition}|.
- For an integer p, S³_p(K) is the closed orientable 3-manifold obtained by p-surgery: the slope μ^p λ is filled.
  - Its group is π₁(S³_p(K)) = π / ⟨⟨μ^p λ⟩⟩.
  - Its first homology is Z/p.
- C_p(K) = |Hom(π₁(S³_p(K)), S₄)|.

**Theorem 1 (surgery identities).** For every knot K:
- **(i)** C₂(K) + C₄(K) = 8 + 3·T(K);
- **(ii)** 3·C₂(K) + C₂(K # 3₁) = 16 + 12·T(K), where 3₁ is the trefoil, of either handedness.

**Theorem 2 (hardness).**
- **Statement.** Counting homomorphisms π₁(M) → S₄ is #P-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions for closed orientable
  3-manifolds M with H₁(M) ≅ Z/2.
- **Input.** M is given by integral surgery on a knot diagram, or by a triangulation after the standard polynomial-time conversion.
- **The reduction.** It makes two queries per knot, by (ii).

**Corollary (the line with bounded homology).** On closed orientable 3-manifolds with H₁ ≅ Z/2:
- counting maps into a fixed group with an abelian normal subgroup and nilpotent quotient, every metabelian group included, is
  polynomial (`BOUNDED-H1-20261025-001B`);
- counting maps into S₄ is #P-hard.

## Nearest known result

- **Kuperberg and Samperton (2018).** #P-hardness for non-abelian simple targets on integer homology spheres.
  - A homology sphere has a perfect group, so a solvable target admits only the trivial map, and solvable targets need H₁ ≠ 0.
  - A map sending meridians to transpositions composes with the sign to a surjection H₁ → Z/2. So Z/2 is the smallest first homology
    in which the transposition colourings survive.
- **Galindo (2026).** #P-hardness for every non-abelian target when homology may grow.
- **Samperton and Weiß (2026).** Class-2 targets are polynomial with bounded H².
- **`BOUNDED-H1-20261025-001A`** (knot hardness) and **`CLOSED-H1-20261025-001B`** (peripheral identities) are the inputs.
- **Novelty.**
  - No bounded-homology hardness for a solvable target was found in the bounded scan (`research/CLOSED-H1-20261025-001/scan.md`).
  - The surgery bookkeeping itself is routine. Folklore risk is low to medium.

## Proof

**1. Surgery.**
- Hom(π₁(S³_p(K)), S₄) is the set of ρ ∈ Hom(π, S₄) with ρ(μ)^p ρ(λ) = 1.
- Since λ ∈ π′ commutes with μ, ρ(λ) ∈ ρ(π)′ ∩ C(ρ(μ)).

**2. Bookkeeping by the class of ρ(μ), for p ∈ {2, 4}.** The meridians generate π and are all conjugate.
- **Identity.** ρ is trivial: 1 map.
- **Double transposition z.**
  - The image is generated by conjugates of z, which lie in V₄. It is abelian, so ρ factors through H₁ = Z and ρ(λ) = 1.
  - Since z^p = 1, the three double transpositions give 3 maps.
- **3-cycle s.**
  - The image lies in A₄, so ρ(λ) ∈ A₄′ ∩ C(s) = V₄ ∩ ⟨s⟩ = 1, and the condition reads s^p = 1.
  - That fails for p = 2 and p = 4: 0 maps.
- **Transposition t.**
  - ρ(λ) = 1 by `CLOSED-H1-20261025-001B`(a), and t^p = 1.
  - So every transposition colouring survives: T(K) maps.
- **4-cycle c.**
  - ρ(λ) ∈ A₄ ∩ C(c) = {1, c²}.
  - For p = 2 the condition is ρ(λ) = c^{−2} = c², and for p = 4 it is ρ(λ) = 1.
  - Write F_{c²}(K) and F₁(K) for the numbers of 4-cycle colourings with each longitude.

So C₂ = 4 + T + F_{c²} and C₄ = 4 + T + F₁.

**3. Identity (i).** By `CLOSED-H1-20261025-001B`(c), the 4-cycle colourings are as many as the transposition colourings:
F_{c²} + F₁ = T. Adding gives (i).

**4. Connected sums.**
- π(K # J) is the amalgamated product of π(K) and π(J) over the meridian, and its longitude is λ_K λ_J (Eisermann, Prop. 2.5;
  standard).
- So the colourings of K # J with a fixed meridian value m are the pairs of colourings of K and J with meridian m, and their longitudes
  multiply.
- **Fixed-meridian notation.** Fix a transposition t and a 4-cycle c, and write:
  - x = T(K)/6, the colourings with ρ(μ) = t;
  - g = F_{c²}(K)/6, the colourings with ρ(μ) = c and ρ(λ) = c².
  - By B, F₁(K)/6 = x − g.
- **The trefoil's numbers** (check part 3), the same for both handednesses: 5 colourings with ρ(μ) = t, all with trivial longitude; 1
  colouring with ρ(μ) = c and ρ(λ) = 1; 4 with ρ(μ) = c and ρ(λ) = c².
- **The counts.**
  - C₂(K) = 4 + 6x + 6g.
  - C₂(K # 3₁) = 4 + 6·5x + 6·((x − g)·4 + g·1) = 4 + 54x − 18g.
  - Hence 3·C₂(K) + C₂(K # 3₁) = 16 + 72x = 16 + 12·T(K). ∎ Theorem 1

**5. Hardness.**
- By `BOUNDED-H1-20261025-001A` (replayed in `CLOSED-H1-20261025-001A`), computing T(K) for knot diagrams is #P-hard under Turing
  reductions.
- Given a knot diagram K, the surgery diagrams of S³₂(K) and S³₂(K # 3₁) have size O(|K|), and both manifolds have H₁ ≅ Z/2.
- By (ii), T(K) = (3·C₂(K) + C₂(K # 3₁) − 16)/12. ∎ Theorem 2

**The corollary.** `BOUNDED-H1-20261025-001B` covers closed 3-manifolds with bounded |H₁|. It works from any presentation of π₁, and a
surgery diagram gives one directly: the Wirtinger presentation plus the relation μ^p λ.

## Check

`check.py` uses the standard library only. Every count is a brute force over all S₄ homomorphisms of the knot group, kept when
ρ(μ)^p ρ(λ) = 1. Its output is `result.json`.

1. **Conventions.**
   - S³₂(3₁) gives 58. Its group is the binary octahedral group, and by hand 1 + 9 + 24 + 24 = 58.
   - S³₋₂(3₁) gives 58, S³₆(3₁) = L(2, 1) # L(3, 2) gives 90 = 10 × 9, and S³₁(3₁) gives 1.
   - The Smith normal form gives H₁(S³_p(K)) = Z/p for 3₁, 4₁ and 8₁₈ with p = 2 and 4, and Z for p = 0 (control).
2. **Bookkeeping.** On every knot, the counts split as 1 (identity) + 3 (double transpositions) + T + the 4-cycle terms, with no 3-cycle
   term. Transposition longitudes are trivial, and the 4-cycle colourings number T.
3. **Identities.**
   - The knots: braid closures 3₁, 4₁, 5₂, 6₁, 8₁₈ and 8₁₉; the pretzel P(3, 3, 3); the tripled alternating diagrams of the edge, the
     path, C₃ and the triangle with a pendant; a gadget knot; and 3₁ # 8₁₈.
   - (i) and (ii) hold exactly on all of them.
   - The trefoil's fixed-meridian numbers are (5; 1, 4).
4. **Controls.** Each of these breaks (i):
   - dropping the 4-cycle terms;
   - using the blackboard longitude instead of the preferred one;
   - a virtual knot (the trefoil word with one sign flipped).

## Scope

- **Covered.** Closed orientable 3-manifolds given by integral surgery on knot diagrams, with H₁ = Z/2; with Z/4 too for (i).
- **Kind of reduction.** Turing reductions, with two queries per knot on top of `BOUNDED-H1-20261025-001A`.
- **Cited, not proved.**
  - The longitude of a connected sum.
  - The conversion of surgery diagrams to triangulations (for example via Hass, Lagarias and Pippenger's triangulations of knot
    complements, plus a layered solid torus).
  - What `CLOSED-H1-20261025-001B` and `BOUNDED-H1-20261025-001A` cite.
- **Not covered.**
  - Other solvable groups, such as SL(2, 3) and GL(2, 3).
  - Homology spheres, which are trivial for solvable targets.
  - Approximate counting.
- **No overclaim.** No claim about P versus NP.
