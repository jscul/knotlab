## A. The readings, and what each is fair to

All are defined in D 7 and decided by a program; T is the last round every run of the reading reached (D 7.1).

- **(i)** C(r, base law) and CF(r, base law) fail for every rule r and base law (claim, section 5). This is fair to his
  first sentence on a registered class: it is **overturned** for that class by one first pattern, law and rule that is a
  copier for it (D 5.1), and **only supported** by failures, since the classes are finite and "all logic structures" is
  not. It is reported in two parts kept apart: **no copier at all** (no member of the class has two made by X under a rule
  that survives renaming), or **a copier for some members of the class and not all**. X is a copier for a member Y when,
  under the claim's law and one stated rule, run A with Y has two of Y made by X, surviving every renaming registered for
  the design, condition 3 holds, and (form) the run with each other member Y′ as the second pattern has fewer than two
  parts the same as Y after every round read. "Some and not all": this holds for at least one member and not for every
  member, the same X, law and rule. Only the second part is the outcome his first sentence describes; the first part is
  a degenerate case of it.
- **(ii) X ended with Y making births, Y still making births** (D 7.6). Two patterns changing each other's fresh counts can
  give it; in the cases seen, X is a copier of nothing. It is reported with its reverse and the controls with Y the same
  as X, and it is **never called support for his second sentence**, whose object is a copier.
- **(iii) One-way** (D 7.7): X's course differs with Y barred, through a meeting, and differs from X alone, while Y's
  course is the same with X barred and Y makes a birth in round T. Reported with its reverse and controls, no prediction.
- **(iv) A third pattern** (D 7.8): on every values case with two of Y under a stated rule or a registered take list,
  a third pattern Z there removes the two, Z barred gives them back, Z leaves no two of its own places, and Z makes a
  birth in round T. Reported, no prediction.

A bounded run cannot say "for ever": "makes a birth in the last round read" says only that a birth was made in round T,
and "not ended by round T" says only that.

## B. Three arguments for the referee

### B1. No place of a pattern, and no relation among its distinctions, is changed by another; its course can change

**Statement.** Under a law in which every birth either receives a fresh distinction or, under parity with IMPLIED, adds a
rule already in the span (COPY-LAW(t) (the fold study's name for (FLOOR1, t, RELEASE), not a law of copying), CAP-3(t)
and FBP(t), with MET or STEPS-1), from round s on, Y's births change:
- (a) **no relation among distinctions that exist**: the allowed states restricted to the distinctions existing after a
  round are the same after every later round (S1), so no tie and no relation among X's distinctions changes;
- (b) **no place**: a knot's places are fixed when it is made (FD 1.1);
- (c) **barred marks only through X's course**: a barred distinction is never unbarred, and under the registered laws a
  distinction of X is barred only when a member of X and its descendants retires (CANCEL-RETIRE), or by the paired runs'
  barring (B(i), B_X, Z barred), which is supplied. Y's births can make a member of X short, through its groups and
  owing, and so make it retire: they change which of X's loose distinctions are barred, through X's course. (FILLERS,
  which also bars, is not a registered law.)
- (d) **loose distinctions only through births with a parent in X**: a loose distinction is never eligible for a take
  (FD 2.2), so a usable loose distinction of a member of X is received only by a birth with a parent among X's members;
  no birth without such a parent takes it. Joint births (a parent in X and one in Y) and retiring still change them.

So: **nothing changes a place, or a relation among distinctions that exist; what changes is the holders of shared
distinctions and, through the course, which loose distinctions are taken or barred.** Under these laws what his second
sentence names can be read only as a change of X's course.

**Its edge (FLOOR0 and ONE-FEWER, run O).** Under these rations a join of three inputs can receive nothing, and then a
birth descended from Y only can change the relation on X's distinctions (D 7.9). Smallest seen (checked by hand by the
advisor): made-3 under (FLOOR0, CANCEL-RETIRE, RELEASE) with STEPS-1(NEWEST), parity: in round 3 Y's join 13 takes the
fresh distinctions 17, 18, 19 of Y's three round-2 joins, receives nothing and is not implied; its rule
x17 + x18 + x19 = x0 (the start knots' shared distinction) freezes 0 and ties 1 and 2, and both start knots then fail the
knot test. Counts: see C.6. The prediction (P, PO2) is that in every run with such a change some knot fails the knot
test. It bears only on PO2.

**Checked (seen).** Under COPY-LAW(t) and CAP-3(t), in 288 version-1 runs (144 each), the allowed states restricted to up
to ten distinctions held by X and its descendants after round s + 1 were the same after every later round; no birth
descended from Y only received a loose distinction of X.

### B2. The law favours neither pattern: at the rule, and with every choice

- **At the rule** (FD 2, 3.3): groups come from met sets, meeting is mutual, eligibility is a count of holders, MET's
  offshoot clause looks at the knots a parent meets and its join clause at a common knot of the parents, and demand and
  the ration look at the structure. No step refers to descent.
- **With every choice read in full, a proof**: a renaming of knots and distinctions carries the set of next structures
  onto itself (up to the names of the new knots), and so carries histories onto histories one to one. On a start with a
  renaming exchanging X and Y (made-2), the counts of (X's state, Y's state) at the last round read are then unchanged
  when X and Y are exchanged.
- **With drawn histories, a check**: the take-by-take weighting is carried along, the drawn counts are not. So PO8 is a
  check where every history is read (retiring, waiting) and a seeded replication under offshoots.
- **Not instance by instance.** Who takes from whom is decided by the structure (which knots make births in which
  rounds) and, under a stated rule, by the names: every stated rule is carried along only by renamings that keep the
  order of names.

### B3. A necessary condition, on the parts by descent

- **The statement kept** (P16): if every member of each of two parts the same as Y is descended from X only, and X meets
  condition 2 of D 5.1 (with Y, fewer than two of Y′ after every round read; with Y′, two of Y′ at some round read), then
  the counts of parts the same as Y′ differ between the replacement runs with Y and with Y′, at the round at which the
  run with Y′ has two (fewer than two against at least two), so the parts by descent (D 5.3) differ. This is **true by
  definition**, when both runs reached that round, and P marks it a check.
- **Why the trace alone cannot carry it at the last round read.** The trace restricts each met set to X and its
  descendants (FD 5.2), so a part wholly of X's descendants and the restricted remainder of a mixed part look the same
  in it.
  At the last round read, a birth of Y can take a place of X's common knot in that round, so that the parts differ while
  the traces are equal. At earlier rounds such a take shows in the next round's trace. The renamed trace is reported
  with the parts by descent (P17).
- **Nothing like it for values**, and that is no gap: what a made distinction is tied to is fixed by what its births
  took, which renaming by first appearance forgets. So a pattern that leaves two of a value need not have a course that
  depends on what Y is. A take from Y is **seen in the one case** (X takes from Y in round 2, before its made
  distinctions are tied to Y's place 16 at round 6); that a take is necessary is not proved.

## D. What the owner would be told

The plain sentences, one for each outcome (the advisor's, fitted to the counts above):

- **As seen**: "We have no copier yet, so there is nothing yet for what your second sentence describes to act on. No
  fixed rule leaves two of every pattern in the class; where a fixed rule leaves two of one pattern of the class, they
  are gone under some renumbering of the start, or the first pattern makes them with nothing to meet. Under these laws
  no place of a pattern, and no relation among its distinctions, is changed by another; only its course changes. In
  three runs the first pattern has ended while the second still makes births, and had not ended by the same round with
  the second barred. One of the three counts only because that run was cut off at round 5: with the second barred the
  first pattern ends two rounds later than with the second there. The other two are one start of two patterns of the
  same kind, under one law and both knot laws. The reverse is seen in nine. A relation among one pattern's distinctions
  changed by the other's births was seen only under rations we do not use, and there the knot test fails."
- **A copier for some members and not all**: "This is the outcome the first sentence of your inkling describes: a copier
  for these patterns and not for those. Here is the smallest pattern it fails on and what that pattern does to the
  copier's course. Whether that is what your second sentence means, the runs do not say."
- **A copier for the whole class**: "Overturned for this class; nothing said beyond it."
- **No case of (ii)**: "In no run had the first pattern ended, with the second still making births, where it had not
  ended with the second barred."
- **Reading (iii)**, as seen: "In 70 runs, 66 of them with a second pattern of more members than the first and short
  knots making offshoots, the first pattern's course differs with whether the second is there and the second's does not
  differ with the first; in 8 runs it is the other way. Under MET the comparison with the first pattern alone always
  differs, so it adds nothing there."
- **Reading (iv)**, as seen: "With a third pattern named before the second, the one case is gone, and it is there again
  when the third is barred. Named after the second, the case is gone too, and with the third barred the two are tied to
  a place of the third, not of the second. This goes with the names, as the case itself does. Under a fixed list of
  takes the third pattern removes nothing where the list still applies."

**What it would not show.** A finite list of failures is not an argument about every pattern, let alone every logic
structure: it supports his first sentence and cannot prove it. Readings (ii) to (iv) say what the runs did, at the
rounds read; none names a cause. The cases depend on supplied rules (the ration, the treatment, the choice and the
start's numbering), as the rest of the study does. No claim is made about living things, minds, physics or time.
