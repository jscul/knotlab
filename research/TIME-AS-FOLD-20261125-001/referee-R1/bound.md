# The bound on ticks that give parts off

The bound in item 2 holds. For any finite start under the first primary law, a series can give parts off at only finitely many ticks. Four such ticks would require four distinct start knots in the series' first part, acting in four different intervals between returns. The bound leaves that case open. [Registered argument, item 2](../../TIME-AS-FOLD-20261125-001A/preregistration.md)

**One interval.** At the beginning of a tick interval, mark the members of its part. Retain the mark on members that wait and mark each subsequent birth with at least one marked parent. Count the usable loose distinctions of marked members. A marked join spends at least one of those distinctions. Two parents supply different distinctions because each was loose and had a unique holder. Group payments and offshoots use each loose distinction at most once. An offshoot spends one of its parent's loose distinctions. Any extra distinction taken was already shared at the beginning of the round, so it differs from every loose input. The ration with distinct inputs gives a birth with two or more inputs one fresh distinction, or zero if parity closing applies. A joint birth contributes its fresh count once in this marked descent, however many of its parents were marked. Retirement and barring can only reduce the count. This uses the three registered treatments of a short member. [Round, steps 3–7](../../RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001/definitions.md) · [Eligibility and distinct inputs](../../PARTS-MEET-AGAIN-20261124-001/definitions.md)

**Why an offshoot takes.** Under the first law, consider a made knot and any of its parents. The parent spent all its usable loose distinctions in the round when it acted. Under these rules an acting knot has no barred places. Its places are now shared and stay shared. The made knot holds one loose input from that parent, and its extra take, if any, came from elsewhere. Each start has at least three places per knot. For a later birth the ration supplies enough places to reach three unless parity closing removes the fresh distinctions. A birth with no fresh distinction has no usable loose distinction and cannot act. The acting parent therefore has at least three places. There is therefore an eligible place of the parent outside the made knot. This also works for the rules that take from a knot the parent meets or from ancestors up to a stated number of parent steps above it. Under the parent-taking rule Q, the made knot's original loose input from one of its parents is now shared and eligible for its own offshoot. This works even when the made knot was a join or made no extra take itself.

For the rule that takes from exactly $k$ parent steps above a made knot, consider any ancestor there, for positive $k$. If it was also an immediate parent, the preceding argument applies. Otherwise none of the made knot's loose inputs belonged to that ancestor, and at most one extra take could belong to it. It has another shared place. The only parents whose offshoot can raise the count are therefore those with no ancestor exactly $k$ parent steps above them. This extension uses the same ration with distinct inputs.

**Why counts of ticks add.** The part followed at a return consists of marked members and has the same usable loose count as the preceding part by the registered renaming. Each further qualifying part contains a marked member, as does each part given off, including a mixed one. Such a member holds at least one usable loose distinction. Distinct parts have disjoint members, and a loose distinction has only one holder. Hence the marked count has risen over that interval. Some marked parent must then have made an offshoot without the extra take. Under the first law this is a start knot. It was already in the preceding part and hence in the series' first part. RELEASE spends it in that round. The intervals $(t_{i-1},t_i]$ are disjoint, so one such knot can pay for at most one tick in the series, even if it made many offshoots at once. For the rule at exactly $k$ parent steps the same proof charges an acting parent with no ancestor at that depth. [First returns and mixed parts, §2](../definitions.md)

**Scope of the four-tick observation.** After all the start knots in the part have acted, it can no longer give parts off at a return. At most three acting start knots in the part would also exclude four such ticks. The general definitions allow any finite start, and a short start knot can wait and act later. The pre-registration gives an explicit start whose waiting knot first makes an offshoot in round two. With the two treatments where short members retire or make offshoots, every start member acts or retires in the first round, so at most one tick interval can contain a start action. The waiting treatment has no such argument. The registered census found no clock giving parts off at four ticks on its twelve named starts under either primary law. A counterexample to the four-tick headline under the waiting treatment remains unknown here. [Disclosed waiting example and predictions P2–P3](../../TIME-AS-FOLD-20261125-001A/preregistration.md)
