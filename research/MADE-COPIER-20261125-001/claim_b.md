glossary's and those of [ticket A's definitions](definitions.md) ("D") and [ticket B's

## The owner's words

His second addition, 24 November 2026 (Pacific time; the record's tickets carry 25 November, its date in UTC),
answering the report that the scratch runs had no copier even for one pattern once the start is renumbered:

> "I have a trick maybe. What if instead of 'copying', we consume and output 2?"

His standing words for the study: "copying would not be a law. Laws are for how the knots interact. A copy function,
like other functions, would be a logical structure that has a meeting pattern with other things." And: "a law is the
rule the growth follows (which knots join, what a new knot receives, what a short knot does); a copier is a pattern the
growth makes." His inkling (A248) is kept apart from the evidence, as in ticket A.

## 1. How his words are read: two readings

His second addition changes what counts as "two of it", not the law. The first pattern X and the second pattern Y are one
part; births with a parent descended from each take loose distinctions of both; the knots descended from Y only end; and
two parts the same as Y are left, each descended from both. Ticket A counts two parts only when every member descends from
X alone, so this case lies outside its claim.

- **CB-1, the second pattern with the first** (DB 4.1). Read literally, his sentence asks that Y become two of itself
  with X and not without it. In the literature's words (from memory, not checked) this is **autocatalysis**, A + B → 2B
  with B the one that increases: here Y, increased on X. CB-1 asks for two parts the same as Y, each descended from both,
  the knots descended from Y only ended by that round, and fewer than two parts of Y's form, of any descent, at every
  round read with X barred (a), in Y alone (b) and in X alone (d).
- **CB-2, the first pattern for the class** (DB 4.2). The thread's stricter reading, the one ticket A asked of a copier:
  one X under one law gives CB-1 for every member of the class other than the control, and is not of the others: with
  any other member as the second pattern, fewer than two parts of Y's form (c).

Both are reported, so that a failure of the stricter reading is not all that is said. Descent from both says little by
itself: after the first births with a parent descended from X only and one from Y only, every birth with such a parent
among its parents is descended from both.

## 2. The law, fixed first

- **Arm 1, no taking rule.** The six base laws COPY-LAW(t) (the fold study's name for (FLOOR1, t, RELEASE), not a law of
  copying) and CAP-3(t), with no taking rule: no birth takes a shared distinction. (With no taking rule a birth's distinct
  inputs are its parents, so the fresh count by parents is COPY-LAW itself; version 1 counted it as a third ration.)
  Parts that share nothing never become one part without a taking rule (FD 3.1 says so for parts that came apart; the
  argument, that everything a birth takes is held by one of its parents, is the same for parts wholly apart from the
  start), so the meeting has to be supplied by the start, by one of four registered start's meetings, W1 to W4 (DB 2.3). **Arm 1 is a census of
  start rosters whose members are marked X or Y**, not a claim that could be made: by PB's roster statement, a start of
  arm 1 is one start roster with its members marked first or second, and its run is that roster's run.
- **Arm 2, MET.** MET under CAP-3(t) and under FBP(t), the fresh count by parents (the base law ticket A registered
  because ticket A's S4 fails there), with D's four stated rules, on ticket A's designs, where X and Y are made by the
  growth or are wholly apart at the start and meet only through takes; and on the starts of arm 1. **Arm 2 gives the
  numbering back**: takes choose by name, so every case must survive the renamings registered for its design (for a start
  of arm 1, D's n1, n2 and n4). MET under
  COPY-LAW(t) is one check row: by ticket A's S4 nothing of the classes can be left from both there.
- Both knot laws in every run. The law is never changed for a pattern, and no rule is added for ending one.

## 3. The patterns

- **X**, from a registered list of eight kinds: D's six (one member holding two or three; two members holding two and
  two, two and three, three and three; three members holding two in one met set) and pairs-3 and pairs-4 (three or four
  members holding two, each consecutive pair in a met set of its own; the form the record calls a "chain", made of the
  two-knot part by the cap of three). Two members holding two each is the two-knot part.
- **Y**, from the class 𝒴_B: the same eight kinds. For each X, Y of X's own kind is the control.
- **With it, the per-law class**: for each base law, the forms of parts the law leaves, with every member made, in the
  alone runs of the eight kinds (DB 5). It was fixed after the drafter's scratch lists were seen.
- In arm 1, X's part and Y's part are one part at the start by one of the four start's meetings (DB 2.3): a new dead knot meeting
  one member of each; X's first dead knot meeting one member of Y; Y's first dead knot meeting one member of X; a new dead
  knot meeting every member of both.

## 4. What is read, each decided by a program (DB 3)

- **Two parts the same as Y, each descended from both**: at least two parts of the roster, each the same as Y's form (h,
  k and maximal met sets), each with every member descended from both; parts with mixed descent reported apart.
- **The round t of a case**: the first round after which there are such two parts and the knots descended from Y only
  have ended (SD's "ended"). With it: how they ended (given to births, barred when retiring, or closed; barred is not the
  sense of his first verb); whether there are exactly two such parts and what the other parts are; whether the knots
  descended from X only have ended; whether X returns (from X only) or a part of X's form descended from both is left.
- **The conditions** (a) X barred, (b) Y alone, (c) each other member as the second pattern, (d) X alone: fewer than two
  parts the same as Y's form, of any descent, at every round read.

## 5. The claims, what is settled, and what is predicted

> **CB-1(L, W; X, Y).** Under law L and start's meeting W, run A has a round t (DB 3.5), and (a), (b) and (d) hold.

> **CB-2(L, W; X).** For every member Y of the class other than X's control, CB-1(L, W; X, Y) and (c).

In arm 2 every case must also survive the renamings registered for its design.

**Settled by argument (PB, "Settled by construction"; the runs check them):**
- **Renaming (arm 1).** With no taking rule, the run from a renamed start is a renaming of the original run, round by
  round, agreeing with the renaming on the start's knots and on its shared or barred distinctions, and on each member's
  usable loose distinctions up to a permutation of them. So no case of arm 1 can depend on the numbering of the start,
  the defect that removed every case of ticket A. (The record has the one-round step: which loose distinction a knot pays
  is a renaming, `research/REVIEW-LEADS-20261123-001E/result.md`.)
- **The roster statement (arm 1).** With no taking rule and RELEASE, every new knot receiving a fresh distinction unless it
  closes, and no extras, the marked roster after a round depends only on the marked roster before it; under parity, on it
  and on whether the round closes, and a round that closes leaves no member. The record states the unmarked form for its
  own laws (`research/RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001A/preregistration.md` lines 93–99); it is proved here for the six. So a start of arm 1 runs as its marked start roster runs; 256 starts
  give 78 rosters, 189 with marks.
- **The made-knot statement (arm 1, COPY-LAW).** Every knot made after round 0 holds at most two loose distinctions; one that holds two
  is an offshoot and meets no knot but its parent; so a made part whose members all hold two is one parent's offshoots,
  in one met set. Of the class only one member holding two (one-2) and the two-knot part (two-22) can be left from both.
  **So CB-2 fails under COPY-LAW for every X, by argument**: at least five members other than the control cannot be left
  from both.
- **MET with COPY-LAW (arm 2).** By ticket A's S4, a member descended from both holds at most one loose distinction, so
  nothing of the classes is left from both: **CB-1 and CB-2 fail there by argument.**

**What is then tested, and predicted (by census; computed, bounded):**
- **Arm 1 under COPY-LAW, CB-1 for one-2 and two-22.** Two-22 fails (b) under every treatment (Y alone leaves two of
  itself); one-2 fails (b) under offshoots and waiting. **The one undecided case is Y = one-2 under COPY-LAW(CANCEL-RETIRE)**,

## 6. The owner's inkling (A248), as it bears here

Each failure is reported as by argument or by census, the latter with the clause that failed (DB 4.4). Failures by
argument (CB-2 under COPY-LAW without a taking rule; everything under MET with COPY-LAW) are facts about the ration and
the taking rule, not about patterns, and do not bear on his inkling. A failure on (b) alone (DB 4.4: CB-1 for two-22 under every
treatment of COPY-LAW and for one-2 under offshoots and waiting, in all 36 runs seen) is a fact about the law and Y, not
about X, and does not bear on it either. The other failures by census (no round with two: the undecided case; everything under CAP-3; arm 2
under CAP-3 and FBP) support his first sentence on this class and cannot prove it. His second sentence is not read
here.

## 7. Verdicts, as three questions (DB 6)

Who made the two (descended from both, by definition; the first joint births they come from); what fixed any takes
(nothing in arm 1; a stated rule in arm 2, with its renamings); where X, Y and their meeting come from (in arm 1, marks on
one start roster, and the meeting is the start's, so no case of arm 1 can be called made).

## 8. Law and pattern, kept apart

The law is fixed in section 2 and never adjusted for a pattern; the rations are of the law, and a part is never said to
have one. X and Y are patterns, in arm 1 marks on one start roster; what is left are parts made by births under that
law. The meeting supplied by the start is supplied, never made. No claim is made about living things, minds, physics or
time.
