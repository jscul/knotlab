# Which blocks the groups cross

A block holds junctions that can all join in pairs. A large legal group consists of at least three mutually compatible junctions, is maximal, and escapes every single block. A group can nevertheless lie in the union of two blocks. To decide this, intersect the group with each block. If two blocks suffice, one holds at least half the group. For each such first block, test the blocks holding the first uncovered junction. This gives an exhaustive two-block test.

For the partnered comparison, fix an actual group. An outside junction can replace its member $u$ if it meets every other group member and misses $u$. To let several choices vary independently, the replacements must also meet each other. Any mutually compatible collection replacing different members gives a literal partnered part with those members and their replacements. Counting members with any replacement gives an upper bound. Greedily choosing compatible replacements gives a witnessed lower bound. A second upper bound is the number of junctions outside the largest block, since each independent partner pair uses at least one of those junctions. We call a group near-partnered for this measurement if at least three quarters of its members can be given simultaneous independent replacements. The cutoff is a reporting choice. Both numerical bounds are recorded. Direct subset tests checked 1,086 large maximal groups on labelled inputs of up to five junctions. ## The groups that made round eight of start-03

Each of the 28,008 large groups has equal weight in this exhaustive count. Exactly 24,955 fit in two maximal blocks and 3,053 require at least three. The median group has 34 junctions and meets 37 maximal blocks. Counting a block whenever it meets any member is a different question from asking how many blocks cover every member. The largest group has 109 parents. Block 102 holds 108 of them, and blocks 102 and 22 together cover all 109. Only one of its parents has a possible replacement.

At least one partner replacement occurs in 27,588 groups. The lower and upper tests establish that 21 groups are near-partnered, rule it out for 27,976, and leave 11 undecided. Every one of the 27,913 groups with eight or more parents is outside the three-quarter threshold. Thus a few choices can vary independently inside a much larger group. The registration did not assign a physical significance to the three-quarter threshold. ## Groups that actually make round nine

Blocks 35 and 0 contain a part with exactly 1,988,324,131,679,583 maximal groups. Sample an integer uniformly from that count, decode its group, then extend it in increasing order of the literal roster. Every result is a full maximal group. Two different integers remain different after extension because restricting the full group to the counted part recovers its original group. The sampling weight of each group in this specific family is therefore exactly the reciprocal of that count.

In 32 independent draws from this family, the full groups had 9,113 to 10,955 parents, with median 10,060.5. Every draw required at least three blocks. The groups met 150 to 168 blocks. The witnessed partnered parts had 3 to 12 independently replaceable members per draw. No draw allowed independent replacements of even one percent of its members. In every draw all junctions outside the counted part that could join its original group were mutually compatible, so the extension of that particular original group was forced. The 32 results concern uniform ranks of this one known family. The fraction of **all** round-nine groups with these properties remains a separate quantity.
