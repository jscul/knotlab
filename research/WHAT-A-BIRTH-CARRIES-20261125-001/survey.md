**What the record says on "which".** In the study of parts that meet again, 16 pairs under six laws, compared between
two starts that differ only in one start knot's holdings, and in none did the course differ with what the other part is.
In the copier study's first ticket the record has a contrary count. Its frozen pre-registration discloses, from its
drafter's prototype, that X's renamed trace differs between run A with Y and run A with another member Y′ of its
classes in **164 of 864 made-X pairs and 203 of 1,440 start-X pairs** ("So, unlike the fold study's 16 pairs, X's
course here depends on what Y is in 367 of 2,304 pairs"), most under CAP-3 (56 of 144, 116 of 240) and fewest under
FLOOR1 (6 of 144, 11 of 240): `research/MADE-COPIER-20261125-001A/preregistration.md:202-205` and `:434` (P17, reported).
Class: seen in that study's drafter's prototype, under rules that choose by name; that study's result note is not yet
filed. Three reasons it does not yet answer this epic: (i) the replacement run renumbers the start ("Names follow the naming
convention, so the start is renumbered", `research/MADE-COPIER-20261125-001/definitions.md:163-164`), and every
registered way of choosing uses names, so the difference can be the numbering's; (ii) FD's trace counts the
descendants of joint births as X's (`research/PARTS-MEET-AGAIN-20261124-001/definitions.md:210-218`), so Y's births
can be counted as X's; (iii) no masked run confirms the difference went through the meeting. So the sentence "in the
record a meeting passes on whether something is there and never what it is", as the first texts of this epic put it,
says more than the record does: it holds for 16 pairs in one study, and the copier study's first ticket has 367 of
2,304 pairs of runs that differ, under rules that choose by name, unconfirmed. **Changed** from the first version of
this survey, which called the copier count a contradiction of a brief; here it is stated against the record's own
wording.

## 2. The candidate reason, checked law by law

The sentence under test: "under these rations what a new knot holds depends only on how many inputs it has (and, under
the cap of three, on its demand), never on what its parents are."

| Ration | Formula | Uses | Where defined |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ORD | f = q = max(D, 3 − p, 1) | p, D | `research/RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001/definitions.md:59-61` |
| CAP-k | min(q, k) | p, D | same |
| RATION-k | max(k, 3 − p) | p | same |
| ONE-FEWER | min(q, max(3 − p, 0, q − 1)) | p, D | `:62` |
| FLOOR0, FLOOR1 | max(3 − p, 0), max(3 − p, 1) | p | `:62` |
| FILLERS | usable min(f, D) | D | `:67` |
| p with a taking rule | distinct inputs | the takes | `research/PARTS-MEET-AGAIN-20261124-001/definitions.md:100-102` |
| FBP | p = parents | parents | `research/MADE-COPIER-20261125-001/definitions.md:62-64` |

- **Per birth the sentence is right, and wider than stated**: ORD, ONE-FEWER and FILLERS use the demand too, not only
  the cap. Proved by inspection of the formulas.
- **As a reason for the negatives it is not enough**, as the copier study's ticket B already says: "a statement that
  'what a new knot holds depends only on how many inputs it has' is right for each birth and wrong as a statement about
  what is left" (`research/MADE-COPIER-20261125-001/claim_b.md:135-136`). The demand D is read from the next round's
  groups, which hold the other part's members; groups, shortness and takes all read met sets that hold the other part.
  The generator study has the oldest instance: under ordinary growth a knot receives 18 fresh distinctions where two
  packets are joined and 15 where they are kept apart (`research/GENERATORS-20261120-001/decision.md:9`, proved and
  computed between two prepared starts; referee status as recorded there).
- **Treatments** (`research/RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001/definitions.md:41-48`): every cancelling treatment acts on
  "short or not", never on how short. **S4** (one fresh distinction for each later birth under FLOOR1 with a taking
  rule, `research/MADE-COPIER-20261125-001A/preregistration.md:280-293`, argued, checked on 1,008 runs): under FLOOR1
  every made knot holds at most one usable loose distinction, so a member is short as soon as it is in two groups. That
  is the record's nearest reason for "one bit": a member that holds one usable loose distinction is short as soon as it is in two groups, not the ration's indifference to parents.
- **Rule R, rule Q** (`research/PARTS-MEET-AGAIN-20261124-001/definitions.md:75-81`, `:130-132`): satisfy H1, so they
  cannot make parts wholly apart meet (argument above). Any carrying they add cannot reach another part.

## 3. What the record has on numberings

- The renaming statement for laws with no taking rule (`research/MADE-COPIER-20261125-001/definitions_b.md:159-170`;
  `research/MADE-COPIER-20261125-001B/preregistration.md:183-200`), argued, checked on 5,184 renamed runs; "With a
  taking rule the step fails: the candidates of a take are shared distinctions, not interchangeable, and every stated
  rule picks among them by name" (`:199-200`). Under MET(OLDEST/NEWEST) 1,860 of 3,456 renamed runs differ (`:164-165`).
- **Not in the record**: a taking rule that picks nothing by name. One that takes every candidate, or only a sole
  candidate, would be one; the renaming statement would extend to it by the same induction (argued in this epic's first
  design, not refereed). Route 9 uses MET with a choice by names, where a sole candidate (S4) is the part that uses
  none.

## 5. How the question has been put to the public

the fuller sense: it takes one course or another according to what a second made pattern is" (`:4686-4689`); the
with-and-without test "settles whether, not what" (`:4640`, `:4830-4833`); "in none of the 16 pairs asked does it differ
with what the other is" (`:5028-5029`). Lesson 10.4 already says that a count following returns "would differ between
two clocks of different periods, which is a difference with what the other pattern is" (`:5003-5007`, reading).
