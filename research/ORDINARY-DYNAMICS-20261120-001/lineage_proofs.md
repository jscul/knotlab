# What a part of the start makes under ordinary growth

**Stage 1, proof on paper, referee pending.** L2 fails even for a path of three three-place knots. L3 fails for two disjoint parts, each connected by ordinary shared distinctions, whose current knots have different, permanently recognizable types. For P3 there are two lasting variants of a donor for which the least observation radius at the receiver is exactly its initial separation from the changed knot, at every layer. Increasing the waiting time never compensates for a smaller radius. This refutes a universal delay law. The examples use ordinary parity growth in the proved class and also work under not-all-equal. ## L2. A pair spends a distinction that used to make its own knot

Start with two three-place knots \(A,W\) sharing one distinction. Each has two loose distinctions. Add a third three-place knot \(C\) that takes one loose distinction of \(W\) and has two loose distinctions of its own. Removing \(C\) and retaining every distinction of a surviving holder recovers the exact first start. The entire support of \(A\), including its two loose distinctions, agrees in both starts.

| Growing end at the start | First groups with several parents | First children with the sole parent \(A\) |
|---|---|---:|
| \(A,W\) | None | 2 |
| \(A,W,C\) | \(\{A,C\}\), witnessed by \(W\) | 1 |

Every loose distinction funds one birth. Exchanging the two loose distinctions of \(A\) can change which one goes to the pair. It cannot match the two old single-parent births to the one surviving birth. Both starts fund every demand, have at least two loose distinctions, have at most one witness per pair, and have no three-parent group. Every source knot retains a loose distinction. After any one knot is removed, all the remaining knots still connect through their loose distinctions to the common exterior used to test parity. Each original star therefore supports its single full parity equation under projection and passes K1–K3. For not-all-equal, any nonconstant assignment on one star extends along this ordinary tree by choosing a different value on a remaining distinction of each next knot. Both starts have cumulative growth dimension two. In the first, each live map piece has two endless chains. In the second, the \(A,C\) piece has one chain at each end and the \(W\) piece has two chains at its single leftover point. [Identification with multiple meetings, Theorem 6](../../proofs/DOUBLE-IDENT-20261119-001A/DOUBLE-IDENT-20261119-001A-P001_doubles/proof.md)

There is also a strict extension preserving the **loose status of every old distinction**. Give \(W,C\) an entirely new shared distinction, leaving the two loose ones of \(W\) untouched. Then \(W\) has four places. The same table holds, the same class gates pass and the \(W\) piece again has two endless chains. These variants separate changing an old distinction's holder count from changing demand at an untouched knot.

The mechanism also applies to the ordinary ring of five with one spare. Give each ring knot two loose distinctions except \(v\), which has three. The five first pairs use two distinctions at each knot, so \(v\) makes one further single-parent child. Add an exhausted three-place knot \(w\) meeting \(v\) once and a new live knot \(C\) twice, and give \(C\) two loose distinctions. Use new distinctions at every added meeting. Since \(w\) has no loose distinction, every old witness still holds the same set of live ring neighbours. The sole new pair is \(\{v,C\}\) through \(w\). The five old pairs remain, but the number of first births made only from the ring drops from six to five. The resulting candidate piece is the five-cycle with one additional point joined to \(v\). It meets the same class gates. The wired complement of each star is connected, since even after removing one knot \(w\), if present, still meets a live knot with loose distinctions. Under not-all-equal, assignments on a prescribed live star extend over \(w\) using its meeting with the other live knot, and the other live knots can each take opposite values on two loose distinctions. A prescribed nonconstant assignment on \(w\) extends by the same loose distinctions. Both preparations grow exponentially. On the unrolled five-cycle, each turn independently allows a one-point attachment, either the original spare or the added \(C\). On an interval of \(5q\) points meeting \(q\) such attachments, choosing half of them gives exponentially many distinct clusters of one size. Translating the interval to the next turn identifies no two choices with that same fixed leftmost point. The identified maps have bounded degree, giving an exponential upper bound as well. [Five-ring controls and cluster counts](../EDDY-WORLD-20261119-001/theory_fate.md)

**What the fixed-map result preserves.** In the cluster identification, a birth's original ancestors are exactly the original knot labels represented in its cluster. Every parent deletion keeps only labels already present. Conversely, retain any chosen original point while successively deleting other terminal points in a spanning tree. These deletions realize a parent history to that point. To obtain the pure diagram of a set \(S\), restrict the existing witness blocks to \(S\) and keep the *existing* leftover points and their chains at members of \(S\). In an unrolled piece the inverse image of that incidence restriction is a forest. Each of its components covers the corresponding restricted piece universally. Two lifts of a cluster in a fixed component are equivalent precisely under transformations preserving that component. Thus the multiplicities and the one-point parent deletions are those of the restricted presentation. A relay has the additional prerequisite that the entire original finite piece has been taken. Its descendants are pure exactly when all the original labels of that piece lie in \(S\). [Source-restriction proof](../EDDY-WORLD-20261119-001/theory_fate.md) · [Finite-piece relay, Theorem 4](../../proofs/RELAY-IDENT-20261119-001A/RELAY-IDENT-20261119-001A-P001_relays/proof.md)

Consequently an extension preserving that restricted presentation, including its relay prerequisites, preserves the pure knots and all their parent sets. To also preserve their loose counts one must preserve the numbers of one-point extensions in the **whole** presentation. A pure knot can acquire a new mixed child. Recomputing demand and leftover points after adding to the raw start need not preserve even the pure parent diagram. This is the distinction between the L2 counterexample and the archived agreement on three joined starts. [Three original comparisons](../ROUND-FREE-20261119-001/round_free.md)

## A family with two identifiable ends

Fix \(D\ge5\). The live source knots are \(X_0,\ldots,X_D\). For each \(0\le i<D\) use a separate exhausted three-place witness \(W_i\) that meets \(X_i\) twice and \(X_{i+1}\) once, by three different physical distinctions. The complete candidate path has successive members \(X_i,X_{i+1}\). The two endpoints have one demand each and the others have two. Give all live knots two loose distinctions, with two additional loose distinctions at \(X_1\) and, in one variant, one additional loose distinction at \(p=X_{D-1}\). Write \(a=0,1\) for these two choices at \(p\).

This is a connected physical source. Deleting any one knot leaves each surviving exhausted witness attached to a live knot, and each surviving live knot retains loose distinctions to the exterior. Hence each star has full even-parity projection. Under not-all-equal an assignment on one live star can be extended across each incident witness separately, since that witness has a further distinction held by its other live neighbour. All other live knots have two loose distinctions on which to place opposite values. Prescribing a nonconstant tuple on one exhausted witness is still simpler, since the other witnesses have disjoint supports and the loose distinctions on all live knots remain available. These arguments continue to hold if one extra shared distinction is added directly between two live knots, as done below. In that case its value can be chosen first if the prescribed star has not already fixed it. Every star has at least three places. No pair has two witnesses, there is no unheld triple, and every demand is funded.

The identification gives one two-sided endless chain whose consecutive original points are indexed \(0,\ldots,D\). The two leftover points at \(X_1\) and the optional one at \(p\) are terminal points without further continuations. These integer indices establish the proof and are erased by the observation. A knot at layer \(t\) is a connected cluster of \(m=t+1\) points containing an original point. Its parents are its connected one-point deletions retaining an original. Its loose count equals the number of available single-point extensions. Two distinct knots at the same positive layer are one step apart precisely when they share such a deletion. At size one the declared common-witness relation gives the original candidate path. These starts have cumulative dimension two. There are exactly two endless chains and only finitely many terminal points on the original segment. Every cluster extends in both directions along the chain. Parity therefore never closes here, and ordinary not-all-equal gives the same growing ends and parents at every layer. [Identification with endless chains](../../proofs/CHAIN-IDENT-20261119-001A/CHAIN-IDENT-20261119-001A-P001_chains/proof.md) · [Not-all-equal growth, T1–T3](../NAE-GROWTH-20261119-001/nae_growth.md)

Let \(T_j\) be the rooted radius-one type with two loose distinctions at its root, exactly one neighbour, and \(j\) loose distinctions at that neighbour. On these two knots the pair has a common witness, so the full restricted witness data are specified. **All three types used here, \(T_2,T_3,T_4\), and their period-one tests were registered before the proof.** [Registration](definitions.md)

At every layer exactly two live knots have a single neighbour. They are the two extreme intervals \([-t,0]\) and \([D,D+t]\). To prove exclusivity, a cluster containing at least two originals has at least two distinct valid terminal deletions. Each resulting parent has at least two extensions along the endless chain. It therefore makes another child. Two different children cannot share two distinct deletions of the given cluster, since their union is the entire cluster. A cluster containing only one original can use its attached terminal points or its outward chain. Two selected terminal points give two deletions. One terminal point alone gives the original as sole parent, and that original has at least three children. The remaining single-original case is an interval on an endpoint's own chain. Its only parent has exactly two extensions and hence just one other child. This proves the assertion for positive layers. At the source it follows directly from the candidate path. The loose counts of those respective neighbours are \(4\) and \(2+a\), since their clusters first include \(X_1\) or \(p\) respectively. The root has two loose distinctions at both ends. Exactly one knot in the whole end has \(T_4\), and exactly one has \(T_{2+a}\), at every layer.

For denominators, let \(N_t\) be the total number of live knots. Its \(D+m\) straight intervals alone ensure a nonempty outside population in all comparisons below. If an exact denominator is desired, let \(F\) contain the two optional terminal points based at \(1\) and the \(a\) terminal points based at \(D-1\). For nonempty \(J\subseteq F\), write \(b(J)\) for the greatest minus the least of its base indices. Counting straight intervals of length \(m-|J|\) that contain all the bases gives

\[
N_t=D+m+\sum_{\varnothing\ne J\subseteq F}\max(0,m-|J|-b(J)).
\]

## L3. A nearby source knot can leave no pure child

Use \(a=0\) and add one physical shared distinction directly between \(X_0\) and \(p\), keeping all loose distinctions. Previously neither had any live ordinary neighbour. Each now has just one, so neither witnesses a new live pair. Every old pair still has its unique exhausted witness. The entire identified map, including its two chains and the two terminal points at \(X_1\), is unchanged.

The part \(A=\{X_0,p\}\) is connected by that actual shared distinction. The part \(B=\{X_D\}\) is a disjoint singleton. At layer zero their candidate-step separation is one, through \(p,X_D\). The two points of \(A\) are separate in the map of *candidates*. The point \(p\) has no leftover or eligible \(A\)-partner. It contributes no pure child of \(A\), nor ever reappears in a pure cluster. At every \(t\ge1\) the pure \(A\) and \(B\) lineages are precisely \([-t,0]\) and \([D,D+t]\). Moving between knots with a common parent can increase the greatest chain index by at most one, since it adds a single point to a connected parent. Shifting the straight interval one place at each step attains that bound, with an original in every common deletion. Thus the minimum separation is

\[
\delta_0(A,B)=1,\qquad \delta_t(A,B)=D+t\quad(t\ge1).
\]

There is one \(T_4\) hit inside \(A\) out of two live source knots at layer zero, and one out of one thereafter. There is one \(T_2\) hit inside \(B\) out of one at every layer. For either type the outside hit count is zero out of \(N_t\) minus the stated inside population. Thus every outsider fails, and recognition uses the current rooted end alone. The labels assign evaluation lineages and identify physical distinctions for the proof. They are absent from the pattern test. The first increment is \(D\), arbitrarily large, and all later increments are one. The two *pattern roots* themselves are \(D+t\) steps apart even at layer zero. The jump in the frozen lineage minimum comes entirely from the additional source member \(p\), which lies outside the matching roots and has no pure child. Inclusive descendants can continue to carry \(p\) through a mixed birth.

Two simpler formulas locate the qualifications. On a two-ended chain take a left and a right source interval, each including the corresponding endpoint with its endless continuation. If they are disjoint with \(g\ge1\) steps between their closest originals, their pure separation is \(g+t\). Indeed their extreme straight intervals toward the gap realize this, and a finite attachment point cannot shorten the passage. More explicitly, with the left restriction ending at \(j\) and the right beginning at \(i=j+g\), every route must first introduce the \(g-1\) intermediate chain points before it can enter the right restriction, then introduce the \(m\) points of its target cluster. Each step introduces at most one. If they overlap in \(q\ge1\) consecutive originals with no terminal points in the overlap, their minimum is \(\max(0,t+1-q)\). For \(m\le q\) they have a common cluster. Otherwise their clusters share at most \(q\) points. The two straight intervals ending and beginning at the near ends of the source restrictions attain the resulting exchange bound. Choosing both terminal decorations outside the overlap supplies the same unique endpoint types, for instance \(T_4,T_3\). This last example permits each source part to be connected in the candidate map and uses the frozen rule giving a shared original to both lineages. The disjoint counterexample uses only ordinary source connectedness.

## P3. The observation radius can matter for every layer

Compare the two variants \(a=0,1\) **without** the extra direct meeting of the L3 example. The donor consists of \(p,X_D\) and their exhausted common witness \(W_{D-1}\), so it is physically connected. The only changed distinction is the optional loose one at \(p\). The receiver is \(\{X_0\}\). Its initial candidate separation from the donor is \(d=D-1\). The donor's unique outer root reads \(T_2\) in one start and \(T_3\) in the other at every layer. There is one hit inside the donor for its own variant type and zero outside. Its inside population is two when \(a=0\), and \(2+\mathbf1_{t\ge1}\) when \(a=1\), counting the two straight intervals with leftmost point \(p\) or \(X_D\) and the optional interval containing the donor's terminal point. The receiver has one hit of \(T_4\) among its single pure live knot, and zero outside at all layers in both starts. Each calculation has at least one outsider and every outsider fails the relevant type. These are anonymous recurring variants, with unlimited recurrence under the stated law.

The entire induced witnessed ball of radius \(r\) around the pure receiver is the same in both variants for **every** layer when \(r<d\). At exactly \(r=d\) it differs for **every** layer, including zero. To see this, the receiver cluster \([-t,0]\) has greatest chain index zero. Each same-layer step increases that index by at most one. No cluster within \(d-1\) steps can contain \(p\), and using its new terminal point requires that it already contain \(p\). Thus all these clusters and their parent deletions are common. Their extension counts are also common, since the only new point attaches at \(p\). Every witnessed subset on this ball comes from the same shared parent in both histories. Conversely, every path within the bound in either arm uses only these common knots. At the initial layer this is simply the source path with the loose count at \(p\) changed.

In \(d\) steps the interval \([d-t,d]\) is reachable by successive shifts. Its required common parents all contain originals. Any \(d\)-step route reaching \(p\) must increase its greatest index at **every** step. From the initial straight interval that forces adding the next chain point and deleting the opposite endpoint, so this is the sole first cluster containing \(p\). No new cluster containing the optional terminal point occurs in this ball, as the step introducing that point cannot also advance to \(p\). The two radius-\(d\) balls therefore have the same knots and the sum of their loose counts differs by exactly one. This survives every possible renaming and holds for larger radii by restricting again to radius \(d\). The receiver's own birth and loose count agree throughout. The test uses its current anonymous root and evaluates its **present** neighbours, without a fixed old mark.

The minimum between the two **current pure lineages** is \(d+t\) in both arms, by the disjoint-interval formula. Thus even while the lineages separate, the radius at which the receiver can read the changed loose count stays at its initial value \(d\). At positive layers the first changed cluster contains \(p\) and a source closer to the receiver, so it need not itself be donor-pure. For any fixed observation radius, the donor can store a lasting, recognizable structural difference forever at finite initial separation without any registration there. At the threshold radius the difference is already visible at the start. This settles the universal P3 claim while leaving any existence claim for delayed registration on other sources open. All conclusions here are Stage 1 mathematical comparisons on built starts. No weighting or Stage 2 recurrence test was run.
