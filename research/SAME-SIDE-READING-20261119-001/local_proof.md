# What a same-side ordinary reading can detect

Fix two to four old shared path distinctions on one side of a once-loaded five-ring. **Every ordinary shortest-route count between those marks agrees at every completed age. A ball counting nearby knots can nevertheless lose exactly two knots before reaching the attachment.** This effect occurs at arbitrarily large original offsets if the radius also grows. A fixed radius has an all-age range limit. On a single ring compared with its quiet source, requiring every counted knot to have all its ancestry on the marked side makes the two counts equal. Distances below count actual knot–distinction incidences. The source is a finite path $b_0,\ldots,b_N$ with distinction $e_i$ between $b_i,b_{i+1}$. The attached five-ring is $s,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4,s$, writing $s$ for $b_s$ when its meaning is clear. All loose values start at the reference. Cold proposals pass, hot multi-parent proposals are vetoed, and old inputs go to distinct children. A113 makes every positive-age parent cold, so the ordinary complete law applies after the first plan. Funding supplies at least the ordinary next demand. The end spares provide continuing unary children with two loose distinctions, ruling out the sole implied closure on these starts. The seam index and ancestry serve only as proof coordinates. ## First-layer count at every radius

Let $M$ be the set of old path marks, and let $d(v)$ be the minimum source incidence distance from $M$ to an old knot $v$. At the first layer an ordinary child of $p,q$ is at distance $2+\min(d(p),d(q))$. Its two replacement unaries are at $2+d(p)$ and $2+d(q)$. Shortest distances to old knots persist under growth, as proved below. All other birth contributions cancel. Thus the loaded-minus-quiet difference in the number of knots within radius $r$ is exactly
\[
 \sum_{\{p,q\}\ {\rm vetoed}} {\bf1}\{\,2+\max(d(p),d(q))\le r\,\}.
\]
The formula also covers disconnected packets of marks, physical dose on a doubled ring and multiple full rings with disjoint veto sets. On the registered double attachments at least four old knot steps apart, every affected first pair meets only one donor. Even when two vetoes return different loose distinctions to the same cold parent, their knot counts add. [First proposals and the two-ring case](../STATE-FEEDS-GROWTH-20261119-001/locality.md)

Suppose the closest marked holder is $h\ge1$ original knot steps from the attachment, on the right. Write $\delta=2h+1$. The veto of $\{s,b_{s+2}\}$ contributes at $\delta+2$. At $\delta+4$ come five pairs of neighbours of $s$, each containing $c_1$ or $c_4$. At $\delta+6$ come the veto of $\{s,b_{s-2}\}$ and the four pairs witnessed by $c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4$. Thus the successive cumulative differences are $0,1,6,11$. Reflection gives the left side. If $h=0$, the seam already belongs to the radius-one ball. The near path-pair contribution is at radius three if the packet also meets $b_{s+2}$, otherwise at five. The other contributions remain as just listed with $\delta=1$.

For a partially active doubled ring, use indicators $a_0,\ldots,a_4$ for the activities of $s,c_1,\ldots,c_4$. For $h\ge1$ the three increments are
\[
 a_0,\qquad
 (a_1\lor a_4)+2(a_1+a_4),\qquad
 a_0+(a_0\lor a_2)+(a_0\lor a_3)+(a_1\lor a_3)+(a_2\lor a_4).
\]
Duplicating a physical ring meeting leaves the ordinary neighbour sets and path marks the same. The formulas count each distinct knot once. Which proposals see an active parent determines the result, even at equal total occupied weight. [Doubled-meeting preparations](../OLD-MARK-GEOMETRY-20261119-001/distances_proof.md)

## Which ages and which original holders can enter a ball

Write $O(x)$ for the set of original ancestors of a knot $x$ born at age $t$, including an original knot as its own ancestor at age zero. For every completed stage containing $x$,
\[
 d(M,x)=2t+\min_{a\in O(x)}d(a). \tag{1}
\]
Indeed a route through a latest knot between two different older parents can replace that knot by their earlier common neighbour at precisely the same cost. A return to a single parent can be deleted. Successively removing such latest excursions preserves shortest distances between older knots. Each old shared mark has the same two old holders permanently. At its birth $x$ is entered through one of its parents, so its distance is exactly two plus their minimum. Iteration proves (1). Descending to any two original ancestors from $x$ gives a path of $2t$ ordinary knot steps between them. Excursion removal therefore bounds their original knot-step distance by $2t$. [Ordinary route replacement](../OLD-MARK-GEOMETRY-20261119-001/ordinary_formula.md)

For $r\ge1$ set $W=\lfloor(r-1)/2\rfloor$. The entire radius-$r$ knot count has reached its permanent value by age $W$. At observed stage $T$, only cohorts $0\le t\le A=\min(T,W)$ can enter. In cohort $t$ we count the union of the descendants of all original knots at most $W-t$ original knot steps from any marked holder. At radius zero there are no knots.

The following coupling uses one fewer generation than matching all ancestors of the observed knot. Let $U$ be the original holders whose **entire first child roster** agrees in the two arms, including mixed births. In particular all original holders more than two original steps from the donor belong to $U$. If all original ancestors of a knot $q$ belong to $U$, its complete child roster agrees, with full parent sets. To check this by induction, a possible candidate of $q$ must be its sibling at one of its parents. Those parents have matched full rosters by induction. The names and complete parent sets of the siblings determine their pair meetings, unheld larger groups and residual pairs through $q$. This gives both the ordinary fresh count at $q$ and its complete roster in the ensuing all-cold plan. For consistent names use the recursively named parent set for a joint birth and a sibling index for an otherwise tied unary. The names couple incidences and have no role in growth. [Full-roster coupling](../STATE-FEEDS-GROWTH-20261119-001/locality.md)

In particular, if an original holder $a$ is more than $2t$ steps from the donor, every cohort-$t$ knot through $a$ matches, with its parent set. For $t\ge1$ it comes from a cohort-$(t-1)$ parent through $a$. Every original ancestor of that parent is within $2(t-1)$ steps of $a$, hence more than two from the donor. Match the parent's whole roster. Applying this in both directions matches exactly the union for any collection of such $a$'s. Consequently $h>W+A$ protects the radius-$r$ knot count. This suffices also if a second ring's activity is held fixed or if only some original ring holders are active. In particular $h>2W$ protects the same fixed reading at **every** age, no matter how long growth continues.

## An exact test for all counted ancestry on the marked side

For a single ring compared with its quiet source, the whole ordinary ball has all its original ancestry on the marked side in both arms **if and only if**
\[
 h>W+\min(T,W)
\]
for $r\ge1$. Whenever this condition holds the number of knots agrees. In the sufficient direction every original source knot whose level-$t$ descendants are counted lies more than $2t$ steps from the attachment. Co-ancestor separation confines all those descendants to the same side, and the preceding coupling gives equality of counts.

For necessity, a quiet descendant of the source holder $b_{s+n}$ first has an ancestor outside the right-hand component at age $\lceil n/2\rceil$, for $n\ge1$. The co-ancestor bound gives the lower bound. For even $n$, take every second original path knot from $b_s$ to $b_{s+n}$. For odd $n$, start instead at $b_{s-1}$. In the quiet source each consecutive pair on this list has a first child. For each interval of the list, build a common descendant of its entries by combining the descendants of the two subintervals missing the first or last entry. They share the already chosen descendant for their overlap. Every such candidate pair is covered by a child in an ordinary funded round. If the two descendants coincide, use any successor. The base consists of the original singletons and the stated first children. This realizes the upper bound and further successors retain both ancestors.

If $h\le W$, the original seam is itself counted. Otherwise at age $t=A$ choose the original holder on the path from the nearest marked holder toward the seam, $W-A$ steps along. Its offset is $n=h-W+A$. The failure of the displayed strict inequality gives $n\le2A$. The quiet nonconfined descendant just constructed lies inside the ball by (1). The same argument applies when measuring a single donor with another quiet ring present. For a second preloaded ring the sufficiency still holds, while the necessity proof requires the indicated initial chain of two-step pairs to survive. As a finite check, five packets on each of the original path and its four-per-side padding, at ages zero through four and all radii from one to $2N+1$, gave 2,050 quiet-arm purity comparisons. Every case obeyed the equivalence and every pure case also had an equal, pure loaded count. This calculation concerns actual ordinary knots. A least parity bond uses a different set of possible witnesses.

## An all-age bijection of shortest routes on one side

Consider two old path marks $e_i,e_j$ with $s\le i<j$. Any knot of age $t$ on a shortest route has original ancestors $a,b$ realizing its two minimum distances in (1). Its route length is
\[
 d_0(e_i,a)+4t+d_0(b,e_j).
\]
An original route from $a$ to $b$ uses at most $2t$ knot steps. Equality with the old minimum between the marks therefore forces $a,b$ onto the unique original shortest route in order, with exactly $2t$ original knot steps between them. In particular the farther ancestor $b$ is at least $2t+1$ steps from the donor because $a$ is at least $b_{i+1}$ along the right-hand path. At age zero the original routes already agree. At every positive age the full-parent-set coupling through $b$ matches this knot. The argument applies to every knot along every shortest route in both arms. Each parent–child meeting consumes exactly one physical distinction, and all original path meetings along these routes are single. The matching thus transports the entire literal route, with inverse furnished by the opposite coupling. This proves equality of route multiplicities at every completed age. It also matches the middle objects on these routes. Their larger surroundings can differ. [Earlier route counts and midpoint convention](../OLD-MARK-GEOMETRY-20261119-001/routes.md)

Reflect for marks on the left. In a double, change one donor at a time while fixing the other. The source path remains the unique old shortest route and the changed first rosters lie within two original knot steps of the changed donor. The argument proves equality of all routes for a pair lying on one side of each donor, including pairs between the donors. It also applies to arbitrary legal shared-only doses on a single doubled ring. All ordinary pair vectors and their associated route counts in a packet of two to four such marks therefore agree.

## A difference strictly before the old attachment, at arbitrary offsets

Let $k\ge2$. On the right take $m=2,3,4$ consecutive marks beginning at $e_{s+2k}$. It suffices to have $s\ge4$ and $N\ge s+10k+24$ with the usual four end spares. The original attachment is $4k+1$ incidence hops from the packet. At radius $2k+3$, from completed age $k+1$ onward, the exact counts are
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 (k+2)(m+2k+3)+6 &\text{quiet},\\
 (k+2)(m+2k+3)+4 &\text{loaded}.
 \end{array}
\]
The counts agree at every smaller radius for every age, and at this radius through age $k$. The radius is strictly below the attachment distance. The observation still includes ancestry from outside the right-hand component in both arms.

To prove the counts, follow the two lists of original path holders $v_j=b_{s+\epsilon+2j}$ with $\epsilon=0,1$. In each list denote the first binary child of $v_{j-1},v_j$ by $X_j^1$. For $j\ge2$ it has two loose distinctions. Its only candidates are its immediate neighbours on the list. For the even list, $X_1^1$ is the four-fresh quiet child of $b_s,b_{s+2}$. Under load use the two-fresh unary of $b_{s+2}$ in the same position. For the odd list $X_1^1$ is the four-fresh child of $b_{s+1},b_{s+3}$ in both arms. For the two four-fresh cases the respective parent supplies are four and two. The outer parent has just one other child, whose other parent cannot join the inner parent. This gives ordinary demand four. The two-fresh unary has one sibling and the unary floor of two.

At age $t$ each list contains knots $X_j^t$ for $j\ge t$ in the interior range used here. The first, $X_t^t$, keeps the loose count $H$ of $X_1^1$. Every knot beyond it has two loose distinctions, only its immediately preceding and following candidates, and two binary children. For induction join $X_{j-1}^t,X_j^t$ to form $X_j^{t+1}$ for $j\ge t+1$. At the first such pair let $x=X_t^t$, $y=X_{t+1}^t$. The outer $y$ has just two candidate neighbours $x$ and $z=X_{t+2}^t$, which cannot join one another. Thus $y$ has precisely the two binary children on $x,y$ and $y,z$. The first child's other candidates consist of $H-1$ siblings at $x$ plus that second child at $y$. The latter shares no parent with any of the $H-1$, since $x$ and $z$ cannot join. Any larger collection involving only the siblings at $x$ shares $x$. All $H$ candidate pairs therefore remain, and the first child again has $H$ loose distinctions. This calculation allows arbitrary larger joins on the inner side of $x$. Farther out, two successive ordinary two-open parents give exactly two fresh distinctions and the same two-neighbour pattern.

These are finite statements. Start the induction through $j=4k+8$ on both lists, leaving two further ordinary holders available for the base calculation. Dropping two outer indices per induction step supplies all candidate and demand checks just used. At age $k+1$ this still reaches $2k+8$. The largest needed descendant index below is at most $2k+3$. The displayed bound on $N$ leaves the initial segment and these checks strictly before the path-end spares.

For every $a\ge t$ in this segment the complete age-$t$ descendant set of $v_a$ is $X_a^t,\ldots,X_{a+t}^t$. This follows from its two successive children at each earlier step. For consecutive roots of one parity, their union therefore has the number of roots plus $t$ members. If the least root is instead $a=t-1\ge1$, it also uses the children of $X_{t-1}^{t-1}$. That knot has $H$ children, exactly one shared with the next parent on its list. Its other $H-1$ children lie outside the union already counted from farther roots. The union consequently gains $H-2$ relative to the preceding formula. The exceptional parents on the two lists cannot have a common child. Their own parent sets are disjoint and, at the initial stage, lie in different parities of the original path. The same disjointness separates their children from the ordinary children counted on the other list.

For radius $2k+3$, the original roots whose age-$t$ descendants enter the ball form the consecutive interval from $b_{s+k-1+t}$ through $b_{s+3k+m+1-t}$. This is (1) with $W=k+1$. At $0\le t\le k-1$, both parity lists have least root index at least $t$. Their combined count is the interval length plus $2t$, namely $m+2k+3$. At $t=k$ the odd list alone has least root $t-1$, adding $4-2=2$ in both arms. At $t=k+1$ both lists have least root $t-1$. Their additions are $2+2$ in the quiet arm and $0+2$ under load. Summing gives the formulas. At any smaller radius only odd knot-distance shells matter, so $W\le k$. All contributing roots then have parity-list indices at least $t$ in their cohort and match. After $k+1$ no new knot can enter this ball.

The even leading knot in the quiet arm already has the seam as an ancestor. In both arms the odd root $b_{s+2k+1}$ also has a level-$(k+1)$ descendant through $b_{s-1}$, by the chain construction above. Its initial odd path pairs all survive the ring load. Thus the two-knot difference is physically visible short of the old seam but fails the stronger ancestry test in both arms. A literal ordinary-incidence replay for $k=2,3,4$ checked the first differing radius and age for all three packet sizes. The two-mark quiet and loaded totals were respectively $42/40$, $61/59$ and $84/82$. These finite checks use the frozen source builder and full physical incidence.
