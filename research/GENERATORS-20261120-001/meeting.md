# Two released packets can make a joint knot and both keep producing

One connected source under ordinary parity or not-all-equal repeatedly produces **two distinct lasting packets at the same size**. On the very next parent step one member of each supplies an input to a joint knot. Both packets have new members at every subsequent size, and every parent of each such member belongs to its own packet. A matched construction keeps both packets and removes the joint output. Their pure parent diagrams persist, while the number of fresh distinctions at corresponding members changes.

There is a sharp timing restriction. Disjoint all-own-parent packets can meet directly only on the first step after simultaneous release. The parents of that encounter must already share a parent at launch. In particular this construction does not show two previously unrelated, independently continuing parties acquiring their first shared witness later. Its fixed ordinary source and its recognition rule are specified below. ## Why a later first direct encounter is impossible

Use a formation size as a cut of the parent relation. At a positive formation layer two distinct parents of any one new knot are candidates. They therefore have a **common immediate parent at the preceding layer**. This fact follows directly from the ordinary operation, including for a group of more than two parents, and does not require the cluster identification. [Ordinary operator](../ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/end_operator.md#the-operator-on-the-present-end)

Let \(P_t,Q_t\) be disjoint at every layer from their common release \(k\) onwards. Suppose every parent of every member at \(t>k\) is in its own family at \(t-1\). For \(t>k\), a common parent of a member of \(P_t\) and a member of \(Q_t\) would belong to \(P_{t-1}\cap Q_{t-1}\). Thus no birth at \(t+1>k+1\) can take inputs from both. At \(t=k\) the two launch members can have a common parent in the emitting source. If neither launch member shares a parent with the other packet, direct encounters are impossible at every later layer. When the two packets are released at **different** sizes by a still-disjoint source, even the later launch cannot make that first opportunity: the older packet member has parents only in its own previous packet, and the new launch has parents only in the source.

There is a corresponding one-step statement about indirect competition. In a complete witnessed end, remove live members \(B\), restrict all witness sets to the survivors and hold their loose supplies fixed. Suppose no removed member is a candidate of any member of \(A\). Every group containing an \(a\in A\) is unchanged. Each larger proposed group containing \(a\), its maximality, its common witnesses and the coverage of its pairs depend solely on \(a\) and its candidate neighbors. The number of unary births at \(a\) consequently stays the same. Moreover, for a birth with parents entirely in \(A\), every prospective sibling in the next demand calculation shares at least one of these parents. Its full parent set and its multiplicity were just shown unchanged. All candidate adjacencies and common-parent tests involving this birth in the prospective roster therefore agree. Its nonclosing fresh count agrees as well. This comparison assumes both steps are funded and the same nonclosing clause applies. A whole-step stall, a change of the parity closing condition, removal of witnesses or changes to the surviving loose supplies are different comparisons. Erasing all mixed descendants of a source can also change the intermediaries retained at the compared end.

## What the three-knot example already does

Take the three three-place knots \(A,W,C\) with ordinary meetings \(A,W\) and \(W,C\). The endpoints each hold two loose distinctions and \(W\) holds one. The initial pair \(A,C\) leaves only one unary birth at each endpoint. Removing \(C\), retaining every distinction at \(A,W\), gives two unary births at the untouched \(A\). The initial interaction uses an existing witness in the prepared source. The three-knot count is minimal for a pair through a distinct ordinary witness. [Earlier paid-slot calculation and source lawfulness](../ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/lineage_proofs.md#l2-a-pair-spends-a-distinction-that-used-to-make-its-own-knot)

The \(A,C\) piece is a doubly infinite sequence of points with adjacent originals at 0 and 1. Its size-\(m\) intervals meeting an original form an end path on \(m+1\) knots, all with two loose distinctions. Its two endpoints are respectively the single all-\(A\) and all-\(C\) descendants. At size \(m=1\) the separate \(W\) is a singleton. At every \(m\ge2\) its component is a path on \(m-1\) knots (one unary followed by two endless chains). Thus **the endpoints of the uniquely larger witnessed component** name exactly the enduring unordered pair from the current end, with neither a source label nor a size input. The reflection interchanges them. The radius-one test “two loose distinctions and exactly one candidate with two loose distinctions” also recognizes both, with further hits in the smaller component from \(m=3\). In the two-knot deletion control \(A\) has \(m\) pure descendants at size \(m\), whereas in the joined source it has exactly one. Both endpoints in the joined source persist. These are changes of pure outputs across prepared starts.

At size two the first joint knot \(J\) genuinely makes a further join with the pure unary child of \(A\), and another with that of \(C\). At every later size the first mixed interval next to each pure endpoint again joins that endpoint. These later mixed parents already share ancestry with the endpoint. The three-knot source does not repeatedly emit new disjoint lasting packets from the same continuing producer. Similarly the two unary children at the origin of a standard relay can each continue at its own end and jointly supply the next two-parent knot. This is a simultaneous-sibling control. The repeated emissions below address the additional condition. [Relay parentage](../../proofs/RELAY-IDENT-20261119-001A/RELAY-IDENT-20261119-001A-P001_relays/proof.md)

## One physical source, one reader, arbitrarily many releases

Use nine live originals denoted by \(v,r_1,r_2,r_3,s,t,b_0,b_1,b_2\). The ordinary candidate pairs, denoted by \(H\), are the consecutive pairs on the neutral cycle \(v,r_1,r_2,r_3,v\), the two pairs \(vs,vt\), and \(sb_j,tb_j\) for \(j=0,1,2\). Realize each of these 12 pairs by its own spent witness. Each witness has one ordinary distinction with one endpoint and two with the other. Orient towards the doubled endpoint round the neutral cycle in the displayed order and on

\[
v\longrightarrow s,\quad t\longrightarrow v,\quad
s\longrightarrow b_0,\quad t\longrightarrow b_0,\quad
s\longrightarrow b_1,\quad b_1\longrightarrow t,\quad
t\longrightarrow b_2,\quad b_2\longrightarrow s .
\]

Each live original gets as many loose distinctions as its number of neighbors in \(H\). This gives **21 knots and 60 distinctions**, namely 36 shared and 24 loose.

The source is connected. For parity, after removing a live original every affected witness still reaches its other endpoint, and every remaining live original has loose distinctions to the common exterior. Removing a witness also leaves all other holders connected through that exterior. Each star is its full projected parity relation. For not-all-equal, a prescribed nonconstant assignment at a live original extends across every incident witness using the distinction or distinctions at its other endpoint. The different witnesses share no positions, and each remaining live original has at least two loose positions on which to supply opposite values. Fixing a witness's allowed assignment leaves the other witnesses disjoint and the same free positions at every live original. This supplies the full projected local relations for both laws.

Each candidate pair has just its own witness, and the candidate graph has no three pairwise adjacent knots. The funds pay for exactly the incident pairs, with no unary at the first step. Consequently the fixed-map identification applies: the knots are the deck-equivalence classes of finite connected subsets of the unrolled tree of \(H\). Every point represents an original live knot. Parents are the connected one-point deletions and fresh distinctions are the available one-point extensions. Every finite connected subset has at least two extensions because the tree has minimum degree two. Thus no birth exhausts all loose distinctions, and both knot laws give the same nonclosing growth. Serial delivery respecting the parents recovers these same births whenever all enabled births are eventually delivered. [Identification with repeated ordinary meetings, Theorem 6](../../proofs/DOUBLE-IDENT-20261119-001A/DOUBLE-IDENT-20261119-001A-P001_doubles/proof.md#theorem-6-the-identification-with-multiple-touches) · [Fixed-map order qualification](../ROUND-FREE-20261119-001/round_free.md#the-answer)

**The reader.** Every original holder is permanently incident to a repeated meeting, including each spent witness. No later holder ever meets a single parent twice. Recover the original incidence and recover initial loose counts by subtracting all distinctions shared with another original from each original support. In \(H\), \(v\) is the unique degree-four knot adjacent to both other degree-four knots. The numbers of incoming doubles distinguish \(s\) from \(t\) (two versus one); the directions at \(v\) also distinguish them. Of the common neighbors \(b_j\), only \(b_0\) has two incoming doubles. The other two receive their double from \(s\) and \(t\) respectively. The directed neutral cycle fixes its orientation once \(v\) is identified.

This complete physical reader can recover the parental depiction afresh. Formation depth is the minimum ordinary holder-step count to the recovered originals, because every parent meeting changes depth by one and every nonoriginal has a descending route. Enumerate the fixed-map cluster classes of each present size from the now recovered \(H\), starting from the actual live originals. A cluster of at least two points has at least two deletion parents in this identification. Its physical knot is matched by its full parent set: the ordinary rule makes each multi-parent group once. In particular an interval is recognized as a straight path in its unrolled cluster and its word consists of the actual original types along it, up to reversal. This procedure uses potentially the whole accumulated presentation and the admitted identification theorem. The depth labels, word phases and named origin types are absent from its input. A reader given only the current witnessed end does not inherit this decoding result.

Let \(A\) be one traversal of the neutral cycle and \(P_j=(v,s,b_j,t,v)\). For each \(n\ge1\) take the two bi-infinite periodic walks

\[
W_n^j=(A^nP_0A^nP_j)^\infty,\qquad j=1,2 .
\]

They never immediately retrace a meeting. Every finite substring lifts to a straight interval in the unrolled tree. Consecutive visits to a marked type from \(\{b_0,b_1,b_2\}\) have separation \(d_n=4(n+1)\) holder steps on the word; the period in points is \(k_n=2d_n\). A length counts points, so a factor of length \(k_n-1\) between two positions \(k_n\) steps apart excludes both positions.

Use **one** source predicate at all sizes. A straight interval is in the source if it has no contiguous subinterval of \(2d\) points containing two marked positions \(d\) steps apart, for any positive integer \(d\). Deleting an endpoint preserves the predicate, so every parent of a nonsingleton source member is in the source. All singleton points and all two-point meetings in \(H\) are in it, since no two marked points are adjacent. Every accepted interval has a deletion route to a singleton. Hence the accumulated source is connected by actual parent incidences, and all-neutral intervals give productive members at arbitrarily large sizes.

In the joined frame the source members also form a **connected subset of the witnessed end at each individual size**. For a word of \(m\) points, the test is equivalently that every two marks have separation greater than \(m/2\): a pair at separation \(d\le m/2\) fits inside a window of length \(2d\) within the word. At size one all originals of \(H\) qualify. At larger sizes choose one endpoint of an accepted interval. It can be continued without adding a mark onto the neutral cycle, except possibly when it ends \(v,s\) or \(v,t\). In that case replace its last point by \(r_1\) or \(r_3\), choosing a different point from the predecessor of \(v\), if present. The two windows share their remaining deletion parent and the change is unmarked. At a marked endpoint continue through its other \(s,t\) neighbor to \(v\); at \(s,t\) reached from a mark continue to \(v\); at \(v\) choose a neutral direction without retracing. A neutral endpoint can stay on the neutral cycle. Now slide the fixed-length window along that continuation. Successive windows have a common parent, and deleting an old position and adding an unmarked one preserves the separation test. Eventually the entire window lies on the periodic neutral walk. Its phases are connected by shifts, up to reversal. This proves connectedness of the selected source. The complete physical reader is still used to select it.

For \(m\ge k_n\), packet \(j\) consists of **all** length-\(m\) factors of \(W_n^j\), read as interval knots. Every length-\(k_n-1\) factor is in the source: any two marks in it have separation at least \(d_n\), and it has too few points to contain a subinterval of twice that separation. Every length-\(k_n\) factor contains exactly two marks at separation \(d_n\) and is excluded. Thus the entire finite packet is launched at \(k_n\) from source parents only. Both endpoint deletions of each later member are in its own preceding packet, and they are its complete list of parents. The packets have members forever.

Every factor of a full period contains the physically different types \(b_0,b_j\). The two packets at a given \(n\) are therefore disjoint. Factors long enough to be in packets with two different values of \(n\) have different separations between consecutive marked positions. They cannot be the same interval. No packet member is in the source. There are exactly \(k_n\) phases in each packet at each of its sizes. A marked type occurs once per period, fixing a directed phase. Reversal cannot identify two directed phases: at least one mark lies strictly inside the word and all forward marked triples have the order \(s,b_i,t\), with \(s,t\) physically distinct. This argument also distinguishes the directed length-\(k_n-1\) deletions within each walk; with only one mark present it is an interior mark.

**A limit of the current witnessed-end reader.** Erase the accumulated original meeting multiplicities and keep the loose counts and full common-holder relation at the current size. Every permutation of \(b_0,b_1,b_2\) preserves \(H\) and its supplies. It induces an automorphism on all cluster classes, their parent incidences, boundary sizes and witnessed sets in every subsequent end. Interchanging \(b_1,b_2\) exchanges the two designated packets. Even the *unordered pair* of packets with common role \(b_0\) is not invariant: swapping \(b_0,b_1\) preserves the \(b_0,b_1\) packet by a phase shift but sends the \(b_0,b_2\) packet to one alternating \(b_1,b_2\). No anonymous predicate on the current witnessed end alone picks exactly the indicated pair. Recognizing all three alternating pairs as one symmetry-invariant family would be a different query.

## The mixed birth and the future of its parents

Between two consecutive occurrences of \(b_j\) in \(W_n^j\) lies the same interval \(I\) of length \(k_n-1\) for both \(j\). It contains the common \(b_0\). At launch there are actual knots \(b_1 I\) in packet 1 and \(I b_2\) in packet 2. They share the actual parent \(I\), using the identification modulo deck transformations. The joined word \(b_1 I b_2\) never retraces a meeting and is a straight interval. Its **only** connected one-point deletions are precisely those two packet knots. Hence the mixed birth occurs at \(k_n+1\), one parental step after release. It is in neither packet, since it contains both \(b_1\) and \(b_2\). Each parent also has its two ordinary pure periodic continuations; both parties continue indefinitely. The mixed birth witnesses an actual new holding of one distinction from each party. A cluster merely containing both types somewhere among its far ancestors would not suffice.

The packets can be constructed with different interleavings. Withhold all unrelated births after the launch while delivering just the packet's deletion-closed future, and its own continuations still have all their prerequisites. A parent-respecting execution can deliver a newly ready mixed birth before or after an independent pure continuation. These choices leave the roster and its fresh counts fixed. No observer or rule inserts a new meeting after the source has been chosen.

There are further outputs **after that first meeting in the parent relation itself**. Let \(T_r\) be the next \(r\) points on \(W_n^2\) after the final \(b_2\) of \(I b_2\), and put \(J_r=b_1 I b_2 T_r\). For every \(r\ge1\) its two parents are \(J_{r-1}\) and the still-pure packet-2 member \(I b_2T_r\). Thus the newly produced joint knot has actual later descendants repeatedly taking an input from a continuing party. They are absent in the separated comparison below. This mixed sequence has an outside parent at each step and is not a third all-own-parent packet. Moreover, once a mixed knot lies outside both packets, no continuing pure member of either can descend from it: tracing any such parent chain backwards stays in that packet. This distinguishes a change in the parties' **complete** output from a causal change to their pure diagrams.

**Changing the available return.** In the frozen deletion comparison remove \(b_2\) and its two spent witnesses, and set each surviving live original's loose supply to its new degree. All remaining originals still have a repeat, and the orientation of the meetings of \(v\) distinguishes \(s,t\). Lawfulness and the identification follow as above. All packet-1 words continue to exist with the same two pure children and the same parents. The mixed child \(b_1 I b_2\) is absent. For any connected \(m\)-point cluster \(C\) in this tree, its fresh supply in the full construction is

\[
 f(C)=\sum_{x\in C}\deg_H(x)-2(m-1)
     =2+\sum_{x\in C}(\deg_H(x)-2).
\]

Indeed each unused incidence at a point reaches a different one-point extension; the tree's \(m-1\) internal meetings each use two incidences. The identification counts these fresh positions at the physical knot. Every full-period packet-1 factor meets \(v\) exactly \(2n+2\) times, and \(s,t\) twice each. Its fresh supply is \(4n+14\). Deletion reduces each occurrence of \(s,t\) by one, giving \(4n+10\). Under both laws the missing four are previously possible one-point extensions; two pure periodic children survive. This comparison changes the prepared source and the original loose supplies at the two affected types.

**A matched comparison retaining both parties.** There is also an analytic preparation with one connected generating source in which both periodic packets exist, but no direct mixed birth between them is possible. Use one copy of \(H\) omitting \(b_2\), and another omitting \(b_1\). Join the remaining \(b_1\) in the first copy to the remaining \(b_2\) in the second through a new unmarked type \(q\), by two candidate meetings. Give every live original its candidate degree in loose distinctions. Realize each candidate meeting with a separate spent holder. To make the shared roles readable, choose distinct integers at least two for the roles \(v,r_1,r_2,r_3,s,t,b_0,b_1,b_2,q\), giving each witness that many ordinary distinctions on the side of the corresponding original. Copies of the same role use the same integer. This is an explicit physical typing choice. All old holders have a repeated meeting, the class has no candidate triple, and the connectedness and both-law projection proofs above apply. The two new candidate meetings have an unmarked endpoint, so they are in the same source predicate. It still gives one connected accumulated source.

Each packet occurs in its respective copy, with the same marker separations, launch, pure parents and pure children as before. Both still originate repeatedly from the common source. Their actual previous knots in the two copies are distinct even when some projected role strings agree. A designated packet word never traverses \(q\). There is no shared launch parent across the copies and thus no mixed birth on these two packets at any size. At a full-period packet-1 factor, the four unavailable additions at \(s,t\) are offset by one new addition at its single occurrence of \(b_1\), towards \(q\). Hence its full fresh supply is \(4n+11\), three less than in the first preparation. Its next pure child \(b_1 I b_1\) costs \(4n+14\) in the joined preparation and \(4n+12\) in the separated one: now there are two places to add the new type \(q\). Under the one-return deletion this same pure child costs \(4n+10\). These are differences in immutable prices across prepared sources. The pure child and the original mixed birth need not be causally ordered. The comparison preserves both parties' pure continuations while changing the other physical holdings that determine their total output.

**Ordinary replay of one packet alone.** In the induced current witnessed end, the phases of a single packet form a cycle of length \(k_n\): precisely successive phases have a common deletion. Each full-period member in the joined construction has only two within-packet children but \(4n+14\) fresh distinctions. If just that cycle is retained with its given loose supplies and incoming pair witnesses, ordinary growth uses its two pairs at every holder and sends the other \(4n+12\) slots to unaries at the first replay. It therefore does not reproduce the withholding comparison. A bare cycle supplied with exactly two opens per holder reproduces the two pure child patterns at successive sizes, with altered prices. Nor is the phase cycle itself a source of arbitrarily many disjoint all-own-parent subpackets: every continuing phase after \(k_n-1\) steps requires all launch phases, by repeatedly taking its two adjacent predecessors. Only the complete phase set can support such an indefinitely continuing subfamily. [One-walk controls](../RING-CORES-20261119-001/ring_cores.md)

The all-size positive uses a structural reader of unbounded range and a prearranged source, shared-parent opportunity at release and one positive parent step. The surviving impossibility is sharper for two already independent disjoint parties: their first direct encounter cannot occur after even one intervening all-own generation. A frame whose parties are already distinguishable from the live witnessed structure alone, or a later mediated change to pure outputs under a stronger matched history, remains a separate test. No global minimum on source size is proved here.
