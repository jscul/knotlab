# Ends supply open distinctions that the knot test cannot tell from loops

Status: Proved

Ticket `HOLES-AT-INFINITY-20261020-001B`, 2026-10-20. It uses:
- the canonical K1–K3 knot test (`KNOT-FOUNDATION-20261019-001Z`) through
  `tools/knotlab`;
- the tangle formula of Theorem 1 of `SOS-SIZE-20261020-001B-P001`.

## Standard statement

Let G be a connected, locally finite, infinite graph and c any {0,1}-valued
function on its vertices. The Tseitin system T(G, c) has a variable for each
edge and requires the edges at each vertex v to sum to c(v) mod 2. It is always
satisfiable, and its solutions form a coset of the space of even-degree edge
sets. This proof shows four things.
- A parity of finitely many edge variables is non-constant on the solutions in
  exactly two ways: a finite cycle meets it oddly, or it is a finite edge cut
  with infinitely many vertices on both sides.
- Projections onto edges near a vertex cannot tell the two ways apart.
- For each end, the uniform measure on the solutions whose odd charge "went to
  that end" has as its moments Grigoriev's tangle pseudo-expectation, with the
  end as the tangle.
- A computable tree can carry a satisfiable system with no computable solution.

## Nearest known results

- **Compactness.** An infinite parity system is satisfiable when its finite
  parts are (folklore). Tseitin's criterion for finite graphs is Urquhart's
  lemma.
- **The topological cycle space.** Diestel and Kühn (*On infinite cycles I,
  II*, Combinatorica 2004) define it as the edge sets of circles in the
  Freudenthal compactification. They show it consists of the edge sets that
  meet every finite cut evenly, and that it is generated by thin sums of finite
  circuits.
- **End parities.** Bruhn and Stein (Combinatorica 2007) and Berger and Bruhn
  (Combinatorica 2011): an edge set lies in that space exactly when every
  vertex and every end has even degree in it.
- **Ends as tangles.** Diestel (*Ends and tangles*, 2017) shows that ends are
  tangles of infinite order.
- **Grigoriev's pseudo-expectation.** Grigoriev (2001) gave it for Tseitin
  formulas on expanders. In tangle form it is Theorem 1 of
  `SOS-SIZE-20261020-001B-P001`.
- **Computability.** Kleene's tree is a computable infinite binary tree with no
  computable path (Kleene 1952; Jockusch and Soare 1972).

**Classification: known with a delta.**
- Lemma 2, Theorem 1 and Proposition 2 restate the classical results above in
  this proof's terms. They are proved here only because the new statements
  need them in this form.
- The delta is:
  - Theorem 2, the knot reading;
  - Theorem 3, that no finite ball decides whether a parity is opened by a loop
    or by the ends;
  - Theorem 4, that the tangle pseudo-expectation is a genuine measure at an
    end.

  I found none of these stated. Proposition 5 is folklore-level.

## Setting

- G = (V, E) is connected, locally finite and infinite, hence countable.
  {0,1}^E carries the product topology and is compact. For a finite F ⊆ E,
  p_F(x) = Σ_{e∈F} x_e mod 2, and y_F = (−1)^{p_F(x)}.
- Sol(c) is the set of solutions of T(G, c). Z = Sol(0) is the group of
  even-degree edge sets, possibly infinite. Z_fin is its finite elements, the
  sums of finitely many finite circuits, and Z̄_fin is the closure of Z_fin.
- δ(X) is the set of edges with exactly one end in X. A finite cut is a finite
  δ(X).
  - It is **end-separating** when X and V∖X are both infinite.
  - 𝔅 = {X : δ(X) finite} is a Boolean algebra. 𝔅 modulo the finite sets is the
    algebra of clopen sets of the end space (Freudenthal; Specker). Below this
    is only language.
- An **end** is a class of rays, where two rays are equivalent if no finite
  vertex set separates them. For each finite cut δ(X), every end has a tail in
  exactly one of X and V∖X; say it **lives** there.
- **Terms used below** (each defined here in standard words):
  - a parity p_F is **open** if it is non-constant on Sol(c);
  - it is **loop-supplied** if some finite circuit meets F in an odd number of
    edges;
  - it is **end-supplied** if F is an end-separating finite cut.

## Statements

**Lemma 1 (satisfiability; folklore).** Sol(c) is nonempty and equals
x₀ + Z for any x₀ ∈ Sol(c).

**Lemma 2 (cuts).**
- A finite F ⊆ E meets every finite circuit evenly if and only if F = δ(X) for
  some X.
- If moreover X is finite, then p_F = c(X) on Sol(c). If V∖X is finite, then
  p_F = c(V∖X).

**Theorem 1 (two sources of openness).** Let c be any charge and F ⊆ E finite.
Exactly one of the following holds:
- **(a) Fixed.** F = δ(X) with X or V∖X finite, and p_F is constant on Sol(c).
- **(b) Loop-supplied.** p_F is open. For every solution x, the solution
  x + C, where C is a circuit meeting F oddly, differs from x in finitely many
  edges and has the other value.
- **(c) End-supplied.** p_F is open, yet any two solutions whose difference
  lies in Z̄_fin give it the same value. In particular, so do any two solutions
  that differ in finitely many edges.

In a finite graph, case (c) cannot occur. So passing from closed finite systems
to infinite ones adds exactly one source of openness, the end-separating cuts.

**Proposition 2 (where the hole goes; classical in substance).** Let c have
finite support. For x ∈ Sol(c) and X ∈ 𝔅, put μ_x(X) = p_{δ(X)}(x) + c(X).
- μ_x vanishes on finite sets, is finitely additive on 𝔅, and has total mass
  μ_x(V) = c(V).
- μ_x = μ_{x′} if and only if x + x′ ∈ Z̄_fin.
- Every finitely additive μ: 𝔅 → Z/2 that vanishes on finite sets and has
  μ(V) = c(V) equals μ_x for some x ∈ Sol(c).

So Z̄_fin is the set of z ∈ Z that meet every finite cut evenly; this is the
Diestel–Kühn space. Sol(c)/Z̄_fin is the space of such measures on the end
space. When c(V) = 1, μ_x records where the single hole drained.

**Theorem 2 (knots at vertices; knots without loops).** Let v have degree
d ≥ 3, and let R_v be the projection of Sol(c) onto the d edges at v.
- R_v passes K1–K3, with any one edge as contact and the others as loops, if
  and only if no nonempty proper subset F of the edges at v has constant
  parity. Then R_v is the full parity relation "the d values sum to c(v)".
- For d = 3 this holds if and only if each edge at v is open, that is:
  - it lies on a finite circuit; or
  - it is a bridge with infinitely many vertices on both sides.
- **Consequence.** In a locally finite tree in which every edge has infinitely
  many vertices on both sides, such as the 3-regular tree, every vertex of
  degree at least 3 carries a knot. Every open distinction involved is supplied
  by ends, since a tree has no circuit. A finite tree carries no knot.

**Theorem 3 (no finite ball decides the source).** Setup:
- B ⊆ V is finite, E_B is the set of edges incident to B, and F ⊆ E_B is
  end-supplied in G.
- u and w are vertices outside B ∪ N(B), on opposite sides of F, each in an
  infinite component of G − (B ∪ N(B)).

Then G′ = G + uw is connected, locally finite and infinite, and:
- its constraints at B are those of G;
- Sol_G(c) and Sol_{G′}(c) have the same projection onto E_B;
- F is loop-supplied in G′, although G′ has the same ends as G.

K1–K3 verdicts on variables in E_B depend only on that projection. So no
finite part of the system, and no knot verdict, tells whether an open
distinction is supplied by a loop or by the ends.

**Theorem 4 (an end is a tangle of every order, and its pseudo-expectation is
a measure).** Setup:
- c has finite support with c(V) ∈ {0, 1}, and ω is an end.
- 𝒯_ω = {X ∈ 𝔅 : ω does not live in X}.
- Sol_ω = {x ∈ Sol(c) : μ_x(X) = c(V)·[ω lives in X] for all X ∈ 𝔅}, the
  solutions whose hole went to ω.

Then:
1. 𝒯_ω satisfies axioms (T1)–(T3) of `SOS-SIZE-20261020-001B-P001` for every
   order: it orients every finite cut.
2. Sol_ω is nonempty and is a coset of the compact group Z̄_fin. Let ν_ω be its
   translated Haar probability measure.
3. For every finite F ⊆ E, E_{ν_ω}[y_F] = s_X if F = δ(X) with X ∈ 𝒯_ω, and
   E_{ν_ω}[y_F] = 0 if F is not a cut. Here s_X = (−1)^{c(X)}.
4. If G has one end, then Sol_ω = Sol(c). The same formula, with "finite side"
   for X, holds for the uniform measure on all solutions, for every charge c,
   finitely supported or not.
5. Under the uniform measure on all of Sol(c), every end-separating cut has
   moment 0.

Item 3 is the formula of Theorem 1 of `SOS-SIZE-20261020-001B-P001`, with no
bound on |F|. On a finite graph with a tangle of order k and c(V) odd, that
formula is consistent only up to degree k − 1 and is no measure, because the
system is unsatisfiable. At an end it is the moment sequence of the uniform
measure on actual solutions, because the end gives the hole somewhere to go.

**Proposition 5 (the escape route need not be computable; folklore-level).**
There is a computable binary tree, with computable degrees, whose Tseitin
system has charge 1 at the root and is satisfiable, but has no computable
solution.

## Proofs

**Proof of Lemma 1.**
- **Finite parts are satisfiable.** Let U ⊆ V be finite. Every component K of
  G[U] has an edge e_K to V∖U, since otherwise K would be a finite component of
  G. Set every variable outside a spanning tree of K plus e_K to 0. Then fix the
  tree edges from the leaves inward, and let e_K absorb the remaining parity.
- **Compactness.** The closed sets Sol_U = {x : the constraints at U hold} are
  nonempty, and any finitely many of them meet in Sol_{U₁∪…∪U_k}. By
  compactness of {0,1}^E their intersection Sol(c) is nonempty.
- Differences of solutions have even degree everywhere. ∎

**Proof of Lemma 2.**
- If F = δ(X), every circuit crosses between X and V∖X an even number of times.
- Conversely, suppose F meets every finite circuit evenly. Fix a vertex r and
  let X be the set of vertices v such that some r–v path meets F oddly.
  - X is well defined: two r–v paths form a closed walk, whose edges mod 2 are
    a sum of finite circuits, so the two paths meet F with the same parity.
  - Edges outside F join vertices of equal status, and edges in F join
    vertices of different status, so δ(X) = F.
- Summing the constraints over a finite X counts the edges inside X twice and
  the edges of δ(X) once, so p_{δ(X)} = c(X). The same argument applies to
  V∖X. ∎

**Proof of Theorem 1.**
- **Case (b).** Suppose a finite circuit C meets F oddly. Then x + C ∈ Sol(c)
  and p_F(x + C) = p_F(x) + 1, so p_F is open. By Lemma 2, F is not a cut, so
  neither (a) nor (c) holds.
- **The remaining split.** Otherwise F = δ(X) by Lemma 2. Either X or V∖X is
  finite, which is (a) by Lemma 2, or both are infinite, which is (c).
- **(c): p_F is open.** Let S be the set of endpoints of F.
  - G − S has finitely many components, since each contains a neighbour of S.
  - X∖S is a union of these components: an edge from X∖S to (V∖X)∖S would lie
    in F with an endpoint outside S.
  - X∖S is infinite, so it contains an infinite component C₁. Likewise
    (V∖X)∖S contains an infinite component C₂.
  - By König's lemma, C₁ contains a ray R₁ from some u₁, and C₂ a ray R₂ from
    some u₂. Let P be a u₁–u₂ path, and z = R₁ + R₂ + P (mod 2).
  - Then z ∈ Z, since every vertex has even degree in z. The rays avoid F,
    since F's edges have both ends in S. P crosses from X to V∖X an odd number
    of times. So |z ∩ F| is odd, and p_F(x + z) ≠ p_F(x).
- **(c): rigidity.**
  - For y ∈ Z_fin, |y ∩ δ(X)| ≡ Σ_{v∈X} deg_y(v) ≡ 0; the sum has finitely
    many nonzero terms.
  - For y ∈ Z̄_fin, some y′ ∈ Z_fin agrees with y on the finite set F, so
    |y ∩ F| is even.
  - Two solutions that differ in finitely many edges differ by a finite
    even-degree set, which lies in Z_fin.
- **Finite graphs.** A finite graph has no end-separating cut, so (c) cannot
  occur. ∎

**Proof of Proposition 2.**
- **Measure.** If X is finite, μ_x(X) = 0 by Lemma 2. If X = X₁ ⊔ X₂ in 𝔅,
  then δ(X) = δ(X₁) Δ δ(X₂), and charges add, so μ_x is additive. Finally,
  μ_x(V) = p_∅ + c(V) = c(V).
- **Fibres.**
  - First, μ_x = μ_{x′} if and only if z = x + x′ meets every finite cut
    evenly. For the forward direction, cuts with a finite side are covered by
    Lemma 2, since z ∈ Z.
  - Such a z lies in Z̄_fin. Take a finite A ⊆ E and let W_A be the restriction
    of Z_fin to A. By Lemma 2 the annihilator of W_A in {0,1}^A is the set of
    cuts contained in A, and z annihilates them. So z|_A lies in the double
    annihilator, which is W_A. Hence some element of Z_fin agrees with z on A.
  - The converse is Theorem 1(c).
- **Onto.** Fix a target μ. Let S be finite and contain the support of c, and
  let K₁, …, K_m be the infinite components of G − S.
  - The set A_S = {x ∈ Sol(c) : μ_x(K_j) = μ(K_j) for all j} is closed.
  - A_S is nonempty.
    - Take x₀ ∈ Sol(c) and put d_j = μ(K_j) + μ_{x₀}(K_j).
    - Both measures have total mass c(V) and vanish on finite sets, so
      Σ_j d_j = 0.
    - Pair up the j with d_j = 1. For each pair, add a z built as in
      Theorem 1(c): rays into K_j and K_j′ joined by a path. It changes μ on
      K_j and K_j′ and nowhere else.
  - The family is directed. If S ⊆ S′, each infinite component of G − S is, up
    to a finite set, the union of the infinite components of G − S′ inside it.
    So A_{S′} ⊆ A_S.
  - Every X ∈ 𝔅 is, up to a finite set, such a union for S the endpoints of
    δ(X).
  - So any x in the intersection of all A_S, which is nonempty by compactness,
    has μ_x = μ.
- **The identification.** By the fibres, Z̄_fin is the set of z ∈ Z meeting
  every finite cut evenly, and Sol(c)/Z̄_fin is the space of these measures. ∎

**Proof of Theorem 2.**
- **The hyperplane.** R_v is an affine subspace of {0,1}^d contained in the
  hyperplane H = {Σ = c(v)}. The linear functionals on {0,1}^d are the p_F with
  F a subset of the edges at v. On H:
  - p_F and p_{F′} for the complementary set F′ differ by the constant c(v);
  - p_F is constant on H only for F empty or all edges.

  An affine subspace is determined by the functionals that are constant on it,
  so R_v = H exactly when no p_F with F nonempty and proper is constant on R_v.
- **If R_v = H.**
  - K1 holds: H allows 2^{d−1} of the 2^d states.
  - K2 holds: forgetting any one variable, the others take every value.
  - K3 holds.
- **If R_v ≠ H.** Some nonempty proper F has constant parity. Forgetting an
  edge outside F leaves that constraint in place, so K2 fails.
- **d = 3.** A pair's parity equals the third edge's parity plus c(v). So all
  proper subsets are open exactly when all three single edges are. By
  Theorem 1, a single edge {e} is open exactly when either:
  - e lies on a finite circuit; or
  - {e} = δ(X) with both sides infinite, that is, e is a bridge with infinitely
    many vertices on both sides.
- **Trees.**
  - In such an infinite tree, every subset F of the edges at v is a cut, since
    there are no circuits. Each of its sides contains one of the infinite
    branches at v, so it is end-separating. Hence every R_v is a knot, and
    every open parity is end-supplied.
  - A finite tree has Z = 0, so it has at most one solution, and every R_v is
    at most a point. ∎

**Proof of Theorem 3.**
- **Choosing u and w.**
  - Write F = δ(X). Its edges have both endpoints in B ∪ N(B).
  - So X∖(B ∪ N(B)) is a union of components of G − (B ∪ N(B)). It is
    infinite, so it contains an infinite component, and we choose u there.
  - Likewise we choose w in an infinite component on the other side.
  - u and w lie in different components, so they are not adjacent.
- **G′ and its constraints.** G′ is connected, locally finite and infinite. The
  edge uw is not incident to B, so the constraints at B, and the set E_B, are
  unchanged.
- **Same projection.**
  - The projection of Sol = x₀ + Z onto the finite set E_B is an affine
    subspace. It is determined by the parities p_{F′} (F′ ⊆ E_B) that are
    constant on it and by their values. By Theorem 1, these are exactly the F′
    of type (a), with the values given by Lemma 2.
  - **Type (a) in G stays type (a) in G′.** Let F′ = δ_G(Y) with Y finite.
    - The infinite component of G − (B ∪ N(B)) containing u avoids every
      endpoint of F′, so it lies inside Y or outside Y. As Y is finite, it lies
      outside, so u ∉ Y. Likewise w ∉ Y.
    - Hence δ_{G′}(Y) = F′, with the same value c(Y). If instead V∖Y is finite,
      apply the same argument to V∖Y.
  - **Type (a) in G′ stays type (a) in G.** If F′ = δ_{G′}(Y) with Y finite or
    co-finite, then uw ∉ F′ because uw ∉ E_B. So δ_G(Y) = F′.
- **F becomes loop-supplied.** A u–w path in G crosses F oddly. Closed by uw,
  it is a closed walk meeting F oddly, so some finite circuit of G′ meets F
  oddly.
- **The ends are unchanged.** Adding one edge merges no ends, because a finite
  separator together with u stays a separator. ∎

**Proof of Theorem 4.**
- **Item 1.**
  - (T1): ω lives in exactly one side of each finite cut.
  - (T2): suppose X₁ ∪ X₂ ∪ X₃ = V with every X_i ∈ 𝒯_ω, and let S be the union
    of their cuts' endpoints. The component of G − S in which ω lives lies
    inside or outside each X_i. It meets their union, so it lies inside some
    X_i, and then ω lives in X_i, a contradiction.
  - (T3): ω lives in V∖{v}.
- **Item 2.** By Proposition 2, with μ = c(V)·[ω lives in ·]. This μ is
  finitely additive, vanishes on finite sets and has mass c(V).
- **Item 3.**
  - On the coset x_ω + Z̄_fin, the function y_F is (−1)^{p_F(x_ω)} times a
    continuous character of Z̄_fin. The Haar integral of a nontrivial character
    is 0.
  - The character is trivial exactly when F meets Z_fin evenly, that is, when
    F is a cut (Lemma 2; then also on the closure by continuity).
  - For F = δ(X) with X ∈ 𝒯_ω, p_F = μ_x(X) + c(X) = c(X) on Sol_ω.
- **Item 4.** With one end, every finite cut has exactly one infinite side, so
  no end-separating cut exists. Then Theorem 1 gives Z = Z̄_fin, and the same
  computation applies to any charge.
- **Item 5.** An end-separating p_F is open (Theorem 1(c)), so its character
  is nontrivial on Z. ∎

**Proof of Proposition 5.**
- **The tree.** Let T be the set of finite 0/1 sequences σ satisfying: for
  every e < |σ|, if the e-th partial computable function on input e halts
  within |σ| steps with output b ∈ {0,1}, then σ(e) ≠ b.
  - T is decidable and closed under prefixes: a computation that halts within
    m steps also halts within more.
  - T is infinite. For each n, setting σ(e) = 1 − b when such a b appears
    within n steps, and 0 otherwise, gives an element of T of length n.
- **No computable path.**
  - A path f satisfies f(e) ≠ φ_e(e) whenever φ_e(e) halts in {0,1}.
  - If f were computable, then f = φ_e for some e, total and 0/1-valued, and
    f(e) ≠ f(e), a contradiction.
- **The graph and its system.** As a graph, T is connected and locally finite,
  with computable degrees. Lemma 1 makes the system with charge 1 at the root
  satisfiable.
- **Every solution computes a path.**
  - At the root, the child edges sum to 1. The root has at most two children,
    so exactly one child edge has value 1.
  - At a vertex entered by an edge of value 1, the child edges sum to 1, so it
    has a child, and exactly one child edge has value 1.
  - Following these edges from the root computes an infinite path of T. ∎

## Checks

`check.py` calibrates the uniform statements on declared graphs. It rewrites
`result.json` and replays it.

**Graphs.**
- the ray;
- the line;
- the tripod, made of three rays at a centre;
- the tripod and the line *folded* beyond level N: their rays joined by
  triangles, or rungs, at every level after N;
- the one-way and two-way ladders;
- the 3-regular tree;
- the square grid.

**Truncations.**
- The open truncation at radius R keeps the constraints of the ball B_R and
  every edge incident to it. Its projection onto the edges incident to B_r is
  the infinite system's projection once R passes the finite sides of the cuts
  involved; this follows from the proof of Theorem 1. The checks confirm that
  the projection is identical at three consecutive radii.
- The closed truncation at a larger radius supplies the finite circuits and,
  through its boundary sphere, tells which sides of a cut are infinite.

**Results.**
- **Theorem 1.**
  - Every parity of the edges incident to B_r was checked, from 8 to 65,536
    parities per graph, under two charge patterns. That is r = 2, or r = 1 for
    the tree and the grid.
  - The computed constant parities and their values agree with the
    classification with zero mismatches.
  - End-supplied parities occur exactly on the graphs with more than one end:
    the line, the tripod, the two-way ladder and the tree.
  - Closed finite truncations have only loop-supplied openness.
- **Theorem 2.**
  - At all 28 vertices of degree ≥ 3 checked on six graphs, knotlab's K1–K3
    verdict on the projected relation equals the prediction from the
    classification.
  - All 10 checked vertices of the 3-regular tree carry knots whose edges are
    end-supplied. The same 10 vertices in a closed finite tree carry none.
- **Theorem 3.**
  - Tripod against folded tripod, and line against folded line, for N = 2, 3
    and 4: the projections onto the ball of radius N − 1 are identical, and the
    root edges are end-supplied in the first and loop-supplied in the second.
  - Adding one far edge across each of 152 end-supplied parities on four graphs
    left the projection unchanged and made the parity loop-supplied every time.
- **Theorem 4.**
  - On the ray, the one-way ladder, the grid and the two folded graphs, the
    uniform moments equal the tangle formula with finite sides: 65,536 moments
    on the grid, no mismatch, no end-separating cut.
  - On the line, the tripod, the two-way ladder and the tree (96 clusters of
    ends at the truncation), the moments of the solutions whose hole went to a
    chosen end equal the formula for that end's tangle.
  - Unconditioned, every end-separating cut has moment 0.
- **Proposition 5, finite shadow.** In 300 random binary trees of depth 6 with
  free edges only at the horizon:
  - the 66 trees without a horizon are unsatisfiable;
  - in 1,872 sampled solutions of the others, the path of value-1 edges from
    the root reaches the horizon.

**Controls**, each a mathematically wrong input that the checks reject:
- calling end-supplied parities constant: 384 mismatches;
- the infinite tree's prediction applied to the closed tree: rejected;
- an added edge inside the ball: the projection changes in 10 of 10 cases;
- an added edge on one side of the cut: the parity stays end-supplied in 10 of
  10 cases;
- the infinite side's charge in the one-ended formula: 32 mismatches;
- another end's tangle: 32 to 256 mismatches;
- a free edge at a dead end of the tree: 328 failing paths.

## What this does not show

- **Small mathematical additions.** Lemma 2, Theorem 1 and Proposition 2 are
  the classical theory in other words. The additions (Theorems 2–4) are short
  consequences of it.
- **Whether ends count as native loops is not decided.** That is open question
  1 of `docs/KNOT_INTUITION.md`. Theorem 3 shows why it is a decision: the
  canonical test cannot see the difference.
- **No knot exists only at infinity.** Every relation here is already the
  projection of a finite stage with free boundary edges, as
  `INFINITE-CNF-001A1-P001` predicts. What infinity adds is the source of
  openness, not new relations.
