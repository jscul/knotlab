# What a live triple fixes at every size

**Theorem.** Take a source satisfying the [one-spent hypotheses](../../../research/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001O/preregistration.md) with at least one legal triple. Under any correspondence of the pair table through bond distance five to any reachable mixed-law presentation, the two remaining outputs of the fourth site are loose at the same site. If there are two six-place sites, all physical supports agree. If there is just one, every other physical support agrees, on the actual transported names. The only possible change is which four names attach the six-place site to these already identified sites. Two different completions must have at least one attachment in common. No distinction that is loose on both sides can change its sole holder.

The statement compares a specified source with arbitrary rivals. The reader sees the current table and the proposed unordered triple. The names \(H,F,Z\) below designate objects in the proof of invariance. They do not mark any row for the reader.

## Recovering the small equations on every live source

Use \(K=D+1\). A junction is an ordinary site. To measure a cut, connect each loose distinction to the exterior. Each ordinary site contributes an independent parity equation on its incident distinctions. A nonzero cut is a sum of these equations. A bond is a nonempty cut minimal under inclusion. The rank is the number \(n\) of ordinary sites. In this family the width is \(2n+5\), and any reachable rival matching \(D\le4\) has rank at most \(n\). A shrinking join takes three parents and produces two distinctions. The [exact all-depth formation closure](frontier.md) proves that a live one-spent source has at most two shrinking joins. If it has two six-place sites, it has zero shrinking joins and the [nonshrinking inverse](low_stars.md) identifies every site. If it has just one six-place site and at most one shrink, the same proof fixes every smaller physical site.

It remains to treat the single six-place site \(H\) and exactly two shrinking joins. Write \(F=\{a,b\}\) for the two loose outputs at \(H\). No cut containing \(H\) on its ordinary shore has size below six. Such a cut contains \(a,b\) and partitions into bonds. Any bond containing either loose sibling must contain both. Their minimum bond size is six on a live source. [Six-minimum proof](holders.md#when-the-one-spent-source-supplies-a-minimum-six-pair)

Call a shore large if it contains at least three ordinary sites. Its first three chronological sites have total degree at least eleven and at most two internal meetings. Subsequent increments are \(h-p+2(p-j)\), for \(h\) outputs, \(p\) inputs and \(j\) parents already inside. Thus every large boundary has at least five bits. All source equations of size at most four are the actual three- and four-place sites and transfer to the rival. In the absence of a large five-boundary, the existing short-shore theorem also forces every five-place site as a word and excludes any false rival small site. [Four-bit transfer](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F-P001_finite_interval/structural.md#recovering-every-equation-through-size-four) · [Five-bit transfer](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J-P001_seven_to_six/inverse.md#five-bit-equations-at-the-first-shrink)

Suppose there is a large five-boundary. In every such shore the first three sites are the seed and two four-place sites with two internal meetings. Both shrinking sites must come later and each must take its full three parents inside. Every other subsequent increment is zero. No binary three-output site is present, since it has arity five and adds at least one. The six-place \(H\) is excluded by its loose pair. The shore is parent-closed. Indeed at least five sites are inside and the latest three export at least four bits. If one input entered, it would enter one of the first three, since all later selected sites take every parent inside. A directed path from the selected seed to its outside parent would first leave the shore from one of the first two selected sites. That earlier exit would be in addition to the four later exits and the entering bit. The count exceeds five. The parent-closed shore can be built first. Its first two additions must therefore both be unary and introduce three bits. Every further site there makes two. [Parent-closed formation](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001E/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001E-P001_marked_symmetry/frontier_exchange.md#the-shore-can-be-built-first)

Each input of a shrinking site is now internal to every large five-shore. In chronological order choose at each such site one input whose other holder differs from the seed. Its other holder is a four-place site or an earlier shrinking site, since neither \(H\) nor a binary three-output site is inside. This forces both shrinking equations by the protected-bit theorem. If the remaining three-output birth was binary its three outputs cross no large five-shore, since it lies outside each and none has an incoming bit. At most one of those outputs meets \(H\). An output avoiding \(H\) is loose or meets a four-place site or a shrinking site whose equation has just been forced. The same theorem forces the binary birth equation. This uses every possible five-place source site. [Protected-bit argument](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001L/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001L-P001_binary_third/structural.md#one-protected-bit-forces-a-five-place-site)

We have forced the \(n-1\) independent low equations in every rival. Write \(L\) for their span. Their supports avoid \(F\) and cover all other bits. Some rival equation uses \(a\), so the rank bound is attained and the rival has exactly four three-output additions. Restriction to \(F\) has rank one, and some rival equation uses \(b\) as well. The only possible nonzero restriction is \(11\). Ordinary sites cannot share two distinctions. Hence the rival holds \(a,b\) loose together at one site, which has six places by their row minimum. The equations vanishing on \(F\) form exactly \(L\).

If a second rival site had six places, the two obligatory unary births and the two six-place sites would exhaust all four three-output additions. The rival would have \(7-t'\) loose bits, where \(t'\) is its number of shrinking joins. All loose bits form a bond containing \(a,b\), hence \(t'\le1\). Apply the reverse short-shore transfer with this *rival* as source. All \(n-1\) actual small sites of the original must then be among its \(n-2\) small sites. This excludes a second rival six-place site. [Reverse argument](low_stars.md#reading-the-smaller-junctions)

## Recovering the actual sites from their common span

Temporarily delete \(H\) and \(F\), keeping every other distinction at its remaining holder or holders; call the resulting ordinary graph \(X\). The two presentations have the same low equation space \(L\), generated by their remaining \(n-1\) actual sites. The source \(X\) is connected. Before \(H\) this follows from the ordinary prefix, and every later site has some parent other than \(H\). The following proof does not assume that \(X\) can itself be grown by the original rule.

First all three- and four-place sites are physical on both sides. The small-word transfer already identifies their words, and on the rival side a large four-boundary would have to consist entirely of outgoing bits from the latest three sites. Thus it is parent-closed and can be built first with four loose distinctions. It omits its six-place site because any cut including it contains \(F\) and has size at least six. If the resulting prefix has only the two mandatory unary three-output additions, the minimum-width theorem rules out a four-loose prefix. Otherwise that prefix contains every birth with positive net increase of the loose count: apart from the six-site's zero increment only three of the four three-output births can increase it. No later step can reach the rival's at least six loose bits. All source four-words are therefore ordinary rival stars. Two distinct sites cannot share a pair of the common seed's bits. [Minimum-width theorem](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J-P001_seven_to_six/family.md)

Any remaining rival small site has five places and belongs to \(L\). In the true source a word of weight five is either a physical star, a sum of the seed and an adjacent four-place site, or the boundary of a large five-shore. The seed sum cannot be a rival star, since it contains two of the rival seed's bits. Work modulo the common span of the physical three- and four-place sites. Each actual five-place star contributes a separate basis vector. Every large five-shore contains both shrinking sites and omits the binary three-output birth, if present, so every such boundary has the same vector modulo that common span, the sum of the two shrink vectors. The rival low stars must be an independent basis of \(L\). Its binary three-output star, when present in the source, must therefore be kept as a physical support. The only possible alternative collection deletes one shrinking star \(Z\) and inserts one large five-boundary \(\partial U\) containing \(Z\) on its shore.

This exchange is impossible. The boundary of \(U\) is the same named set in \(X\): each meeting to the deleted \(H\) is now wired to the exterior. The whole \(X\) has at least eight loose bits, the source loose count plus two, so \(U\) is proper. Every ordinary crossing of \(U\) in \(X\) would acquire a third ordinary holder when \(\partial U\) became a site, unless it already met the omitted \(Z\). Thus every such crossing meets \(Z\).

Build \(U\) first. It is a minimum-width prefix with only its two unary three-output additions, five loose bits and no legal triple. The two shrink sites are both in \(U\); after reordering there is at most one additional unary birth before \(H\). Any first low site outside \(U\) before \(H\) can use only \(Z\) as a parent, so it must be this extra unary birth, say \(q\). A second outside low site before \(H\) is impossible. It could draw parents only from \(Z,q\), which are adjacent and cannot form a parent pair. Without \(q\), \(H\) has no legal triple in \(U\). With \(q\), the additional site is just a leaf at \(Z\). It makes no new common witness for two distinct old holders, and it cannot be one of three legal parents since its sole neighbor would have to witness all three. Thus \(H\) could never be born in the source. Every low physical site is recovered.

## The possible four attachments

In the single-six case the only undetermined support is \(F\cup A\), where \(A\) consists of four distinct names each occurring once among the recovered small sites. Their four low holders must be distinct and of the same color in the connected bipartite \(X\). Let \(T\) denote all the names occurring just once. Then \(|T|=\ell+2\), where \(\ell\) is the source loose count. With a live triple \(8\le |T|\le11\). There are at most \(\binom{11}{4}=330\) literal candidates, and at most \(\binom94=126\) in the two-shrink case. This bound is independent of width. Order and labels do not enter it. Enumerating the legal histories on each candidate and remeasuring the table gives a finite procedure to compare all their menus; the bound is on candidate supports, not on computation time. Once low sites are fixed, any distinction loose on both sides has the same holder, whether at \(H\) or a low site.

Suppose a rival chooses \(A'\) disjoint from \(A\). Each member of \(A'\) is loose at a small site in the source and reads at most six to \(a\) in the rival. Choose for it an attaining source bond. Its shore contains \(H\) and this loose member's low holder and has size exactly six. Shores of size six containing \(H\) are closed under union: their intersection and union contain \(H\), every such cut has size at least six, and cut sizes are submodular. The union's six boundary bits must be exactly \(F\cup A'\). This makes the rival high star a source equation. Since the low span and ranks already agree, both full allowed-state relations coincide. The live equal-state rigidity theorem applies to the source, which has at most three unary additions, and requires the physical supports to agree. Thus disjoint alternatives are impossible. [Equal-state rigidity on a live source](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001M/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001M-P001_unary_third/incidence.md#a-cyclic-ordinary-graph-has-at-most-one-leaf-outside-its-block)

For every retained \(i\in A\cap A'\) and every new \(z\in A'\setminus A\), the source has a large five- or six-boundary crossing both bits. Indeed the new bit is source-loose at a small holder; its small holder and the small holder of \(i\) are distinct and of the same color because both attach to the proposed rival site. Yet \(K(i,z)\le6\). A singleton shore cannot contain both bits as crossings at one site. A two-site shore using \(H\) costs at least seven, and a connected two-site shore through only the two small holders would require them to meet. The pair therefore requires a larger shore. This provides a test of an actual possible change of relation. Whether an overlapping attachment can satisfy the entire table while changing one legal triple remains open.
