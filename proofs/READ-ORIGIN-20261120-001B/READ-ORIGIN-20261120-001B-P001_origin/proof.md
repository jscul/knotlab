# Which ordinary continuations identify their origin?

Fix a nonempty connected finite lawful start under parity or not-all-equal. Each starting knot holds at least three distinctions and each pair meets at most once. The physical present reveals all holdings and the chosen knot law. A partial cohort contains correctly funded ordinary births on their physical loose inputs. The completed-boundary comparison takes only entire nonempty cohorts. No supplied origin, names, ordering or past values enter a present reading. The conclusions concern the project’s stipulated finite growth operator. [Frozen definitions](../../../research/READ-ORIGIN-20261120-001/preregistration.md)

1. **The smallest failure of intrinsic recognition has two knots and five distinctions.** One three-place knot can produce a second with one shared distinction. The second knot has two pending single-parent births if both knots were original. If it was the first child, its two loose distinctions instead go to births with its two siblings. The entire present is identical within each knot law. The possible futures even differ on whether deleting the shared distinction disconnects the original pair. To change the immutable fresh count on the same ready marked event takes two knots and seven distinctions. Its common unary demands three versus two. The corresponding minima for a common pair are three knots and seven distinctions with parent-closed release, and four and nine inside both unfinished cohorts. Arity has no upper cap in the lower proofs. [Witnesses and minimality](../../../research/READ-ORIGIN-20261120-001/minimum.md)
2. **One bit can say whether any birth has been written.** On a fixed presentation admitting both interpretations the two-valued answer has exactly that information cost. Identifying *which* knots were original, or reconstructing the whole remaining roster at a proper prefix of one cohort, has worst-case cost proportional to the number of displayed knots. For every $m\ge2$, one connected display on $4m$ knots and $9m$ distinctions hides $m$ independent choices that change counts on identified ready pairs. They remain distinguishable under renaming. Source-membership flags reconstruct the whole roster, including already-used events, and give an upper bound of one bit per holder. With knot and distinction budgets $J,D$, the worst-case order is $\Theta(\min(J,D))$. Declaring only the size of the true origin and that the stage is a middle still leaves a linear worst case. Coding claims are conditional on the given display and the identification convention. A physical pointer may be needed to name a particular member of an orbit. [Counting, coding and construction](../../../research/READ-ORIGIN-20261120-001/information.md) · [Independent family checks](../../../research/READ-ORIGIN-20261120-001/family_checks.md)
3. **A complete boundary has a different inverse.** It carries its entire future ordinary growth without origin bits. On the promise of completed layers, identifying the historical origin up to renaming has worst-case order $\Theta(\log J)$. Open positive boundaries have one possible preceding layer. A spent parity boundary can have multiple possible last holders. An unending five-knot ring yields a matching lower bound from its nested sources of different sizes. [Proof](../../../research/READ-ORIGIN-20261120-001/information.md#complete-boundaries-separate-prediction-from-recovery) · [Executable boundary checks](../../../research/READ-ORIGIN-20261120-001/boundaries.md)
4. **A physical mark can work in a promised family.** Ordinary births never create a repeated meeting with a parent. Source-wide coverage by repeated meetings must be supplied by the starting arrangement. In an unbounded single-meeting family every original has five places and growth always produces four-place knots. There arity recognizes every original without an added label. Elsewhere a single known original, even with a known old loose distinction and the true source size, fails. An externally supplied bit on each holder always recovers the source membership. [Intrinsic and attached marks](../../../research/READ-ORIGIN-20261120-001/marks.md)
