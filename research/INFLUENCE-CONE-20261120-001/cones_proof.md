# What a witnessed neighborhood determines after several parent steps

On a finite, connected ordinary presentation, suppose the next \(n\ge1\) complete steps are funded and nonclosing. Mark a set \(A\) of present live knots. Its inclusive descendants are all knots with at least one ancestor in \(A\) at the marked cut. Their complete \(n\)-th birth roster is determined by the original witnessed ball of radius \(n\). Their fresh counts are determined by radius \(n+1\), or by radius \(n\) in the proved identification class. Under both parity and not-all-equal the two general bounds are sharp, including at arbitrarily large depths. A different result applies when every ancestor must be in \(A\). The first witnessed neighborhood then suffices at every depth to recover the entire pure roster and its fresh counts. ## The two parent maps

At cut \(t\), distinct live knots are one end step apart when some physical knot witnesses them together. At cut zero this witness can be an exhausted source holder. At every new cut the witness is an actual parent. Write \(B_t^r X\) for all live knots within \(r\) end steps of a set \(X\), including \(X\). Retain on a displayed ball the entire family of witnessed subsets **restricted to that ball**, and the stated numbers of loose distinctions. A witness whose roster extends outside is displayed by its intersection. Equal witnessed subsets may be identified. The view supplies neither counts of unseen participants nor physical arities of unseen witnesses.

Write \(I_t X\) for all children with a parent in \(X\), and \(P_t Y\) for all parents of children in \(Y\). Both maps take unions. For each continuing step,

\[
 P_t I_t=B_t^1,\qquad I_t P_t=B_{t+1}^1.
\]

Two distinct old knots sharing a child are witnessed neighbors because all pairs in a proposed group are witnessed. Conversely every witnessed pair is covered by an actual group. An uncovered pair makes a binary birth, and a covered one lies in a larger birth. Every live old knot has a child. This gives the first identity. Two distinct new knots have a common witness precisely when they share an old parent, and every child has at least one parent. This gives the second. Consequently \(I_t B_t^r=B_{t+1}^r I_t\) and \(P_t B_{t+1}^r=B_t^r P_t\). Compose the matching identities over \(n\) steps. With \(I_{[n]}\) the successive forward map and \(P_{[n]}\) its successive backward counterpart,

\[
 P_{[n]} I_{[n]} X=B_0^n X,\qquad
 I_{[n]} P_{[n]}Y=B_n^nY,\qquad
 P_{[n]} B_n^rY=B_0^r P_{[n]}Y .
\]

For example the union of the cut-zero ancestors of **all** \(n\)-step descendants of one receiver equals its whole ball of radius \(n\). The exact ancestors of **one selected** later knot are \(P_{[n]}\{y\}\), computed from its actual parents. Their pairwise separations are at most \(n\). A unary chain can have just one original ancestor. In particular two initial releases first have a common inclusive descendant after exactly their initial separation in parent steps, if that many steps take place. Infinite separation gives none. In reverse, two later knots have a common ancestor \(n\) cuts back precisely when their current separation is at most \(n\). Distances from a release formed partway through the run count from that release. A change between two physical starts and the set of ancestors in either one are distinct observations. ## The calculation on matching input balls

The uncharged data of a newly formed knot consist of its actual parent set, its parent count, its identity up to the interchangeable unary copies of a parent, and all displayed witnessed subsets at its cut. On the initial input the *given* loose counts \(h\) can be arbitrary subject to funding. At later cuts let \(p(x)\) count the parents of \(x\), and let \(D(x)\) count the proposed groups of two or more through \(x\) at its current cut. The ordinary lookahead gives

\[
 h(x)=\max\{D(x),3-p(x),1\}.
\]

Thus, when writing the following cut, the number of unary children through \(x\) is \(\max\{0,3-p(x)-D(x),1-D(x)\}\). At the initial cut the unary remainder is \(h(x)-D(x)\). These identities allow intermediate cuts to be computed from parent counts and witnessed subsets alone. Recomputing the two-step fresh-count reading at every intermediate cut would pay for information the roster already supplies. [How the lookahead counts the groups](../FRESH-DEMAND-20261120-001/demand_proof.md)

To enumerate every group through one pivot \(x\), its closed one-step neighborhood is sufficient. Every member and every possible enlargement must be adjacent to \(x\). An entire proposed subset there is held by one witness exactly when it is held by a restricted witness there. Take the maximal complete groups of at least three which are held by no one witness, then take all pairs uncovered by those groups. This includes all potential competitors through \(x\). At a set of pivots combine the results, listing each multiparent set once. Give each parent its own separate copies of the unary remainder. A comparison can match these copies by any bijection within the parent. The law does not assign names to interchangeable loose distinctions.

For \(r\ge0\), the ball \(B_{t+1}^r I_t X\) equals \(I_t B_t^r X\). To compute it, enumerate through the pivots \(B_t^r X\) using the witnessed data on \(B_t^{r+1}X\). At the initial cut only the pivots' loose counts are needed. At later cuts only their parent counts are needed. Every retained child now has its **full** parent set, including any parent outside the pivot set. For each old parent collect the displayed children containing it, even if some other children of this parent were not displayed. These intersections supply exactly the induced witnessed family on the output ball. This is why a witness which crosses the boundary can shrink without losing any later comparison on the interior. Having only the graph of witnessed pairs can lose whether a larger group is held by one witness.

Iterating gives the following guarantees. They apply to rooted identifications of the stated balls and to the full corresponding multisets.

| Desired record after \(n\) steps | Required cut-zero witnessed radius about \(A\) | Cut-zero loose counts sufficient on |
|---|---:|---:|
| All births in \(B_n^r I_{[n]}A\), their parent sets and induced witnesses | \(r+n\) | \(B_0^{r+n-1}A\) |
| The same record with each actual fresh count | \(r+n+1\) | \(B_0^{r+n}A\) |
| The same charged record in the identification class | \(r+n\) | \(B_0^{r+n}A\) |

For the second row compute the uncharged ball of radius \(r+1\) at the **same** final cut. Its data determine \(D(x)\) for every retained \(x\). For the last row the identified one-step formula computes a unary demand as \(h(u)-1\). For a birth with parents including \(u,v\), its demand is \(h(u)+h(v)-|W|\), where \(W\) is their unique common witness block. Every member of \(W\) is visible from \(u\) in one step. The proved formula covers finite pieces and relays. Thus one step recovers the charged successor radius \(r\) from the charged old radius \(r+1\). Induction gives the stated row. For sharpness take the identified comparison with sender at distance \(d=r+n\) on the straight witnessed path and receiver at its other end. Descendants of the radius-\(r\) receiver ball first contain the terminal sender on a straight interval of \(n+1\) points, at depth \(n\). That matching interval has one or two fresh distinctions according to the sender's spare. All other matching intervals have two, and the old radius \(r+n-1\) agrees. These are sufficient shields for arbitrary edits outside them. Inside the shields a particular distinction can be irrelevant, a changed roster can retain the same size, and matching births can retain the same fresh count. The identified fixed map supplies another reading. In it the ancestry of a particular ordinary cluster follows legal point deletions, and its openings on a proper cluster count permitted outside extensions. Giving an observer the whole fixed map therefore gives information about other original points before a local marked lineage reaches them. A comparison of two actual changed starts must construct the appropriate map or growth in each. Ordinary complete steps and a serial schedule writing an already known set of births give the same incidence after all those writes. Recomputing the permission after every write is a different rule. ## Knots with every ancestor inside the mark

Let \(K_0=A\) and \(K_{t+1}=\{y:P_t\{y\}\subseteq K_t\}\). Every born knot has a parent. This is the maximal all-parent-pure family from \(A\). Starting with the induced witnessed family on \(B_0^1A\) and the loose counts **on \(A\) alone**, one can determine, at every depth, every pure member, its fresh count and the full parent sets of all its immediate children (including mixed ones).

Indeed, from \(B_t^1 K_t\) enumerate \(I_t K_t\) with full parent sets. One can select \(K_{t+1}\) by exactly the subset test above. Its entire next neighborhood is

\[
 B_{t+1}^1 K_{t+1}=I_t(P_t K_{t+1})\subseteq I_t K_t .
\]

The set on the right of the equality is obtained by taking the actual parents of the now known pure children and retaining every enumerated child through those parents. Restrict the displayed parent rosters to these children. This recovers the whole induced neighborhood for the induction, its parent counts and the demand needed to price each pure center. The statement includes the case where purity ends. It applies as well to a matched selected subfamily when the choice at each step is supplied or can be made from the displayed data. An external rule choosing a different subfamily using distant data requires its own comparison.

More generally \(r\ge1\) is a time-independent radius for recovering the uncharged \(r\)-neighborhood of this pure family, with initial openings needed only on \(B_0^{r-1}A\). Indeed

\[
 B_{t+1}^r K_{t+1}=I_t\!\left(B_t^{r-1}(P_tK_{t+1})\right)
                  \subseteq I_t(B_t^{r-1}K_t).
\]

All children on the right can be enumerated using the supplied old radius \(r\). The displayed old parents determine the particular subset in the equality. This also shows that original radius two and the original openings on \(B_0^1A\) protect **the prices of all immediate children** through a pure member, even when that child has outside parents. Such a child's full one-neighborhood consists of children through any of its parents, all of whom are in \(B_t^1K_t\). Enumerating through these parents requires at most \(B_t^2K_t\). A pure-family reader knows the initial mark or its ancestry. An anonymous present observer who has forgotten both faces a separate recognition question.

## Why both general radii are sharp at every depth

Here a single additional **shared** distinction changes the witness family while leaving every original loose count unchanged. Write \(u,a,b,z\) for four live knots. Give each of \(ua,ub,ab,az\) a separate spent witness. Attach a path of live knots \(e_0=u,e_1,\ldots,e_N\), with a separate spent witness for each consecutive pair. Each of these witnesses meets each of its two endpoints twice, using a different physical distinction every time. Add a spent knot \(t\) meeting \(b\) twice and the witness of \(ua\) once. The sole change in the second preparation is one new distinction shared by \(t,z\). Give \(u,a,b\) two loose distinctions each, \(z\) one and each \(e_i\) for \(i\ge1\) two. Thus the only new witnessed pair is \(bz\). All original physical distinctions and their holders match by identity. These sharp bounds use permitted original preparations as the comparison cut. Sharpness with the extra requirement that both origins have positive age is a further question. The spent holder \(t\) has no end-step distance as a live mark. The witness change has live members \(b,z\), and only \(z\)'s physical star among the live stars changes.

Both connected presentations are lawful under both knot laws. For parity, wire the loose distinctions to the common exterior for the projection test. On deleting one ordinary holder every surviving live holder reaches the exterior by a loose distinction. Each ordinary pair witness retains at least one live endpoint. If \(b\) is deleted, \(t\) still reaches the witness of \(ua\). If another knot is deleted \(t\), when present, reaches \(b\). Hence the complement of each star is connected. Its only supported nonzero equation is its full star. For not-all-equal, fix any nonconstant assignment on one star. Every pair witness can be satisfied by choosing opposite values on its two distinctions to an unpinned endpoint, unless that witness is itself the prescribed star. Its available pair is private to that witness and its live endpoint. The special knot \(t\) can use its pair to \(b\) when \(b\) is unpinned. When \(b\) is prescribed, choose the shared distinction toward the \(ua\) witness to satisfy \(t\). That witness still has its doubled pair to an unpinned endpoint. If \(t\) itself is prescribed, the \(ua\) witness still has both doubled pairs available. Assign all remaining shared values, then use a private loose distinction at each unpinned live holder to complete it. This proves that every physical star projects to exactly its declared knot relation.

For the first birth let \(Y=\{u,a,b\}\), present in both preparations. Through the path write \(E_i=\{e_{i-1},e_i\}\). In the first preparation \(Y\) has three neighbors, \(E_1,\{a,z\}\) and a unary through \(b\), and their parent sets are pairwise disjoint. In the second its only neighbors are \(E_1\) and \(\{a,b,z\}\), again with disjoint parent sets. Hence \(Y\) receives respectively three and two fresh distinctions. In either preparation the path births away from its far end have two. All initial groups are funded. Thereafter the preceding step pays the next demand. A sufficiently long untouched part of the path has multiple births at every step considered, so neither law takes a closing exception.

The same calculation propagates through a plain path under the actual lookahead. Suppose \(V,X_1,X_2,\ldots\) is such a path, where \(X_i\) have exactly two openings and their only neighbors, in the portion used, are their two path neighbors. Other groups can meet \(V\) from the opposite side. The pair \(V^+=\{V,X_1\}\) is residual and is a birth. The only neighbors of \(V^+\) are the \(h(V)-1\) other children of \(V\) and the birth \(\{X_1,X_2\}\). The former are all held together with \(V^+\) at \(V\). None has parent \(X_1\) a second time, and none can have parent \(X_2\), which is not a candidate with \(V\). Thus none meets the latter birth in the new witnessed relation. Every group through \(V^+\) is a separate pair, so \(D(V^+)=h(V)\). Since \(V^+\) is binary it receives exactly \(h(V)\). Farther along, each successive binary path birth again has two neighbors and two fresh distinctions. This establishes the induction on the actual birth rosters, including all possible simultaneous outside births. Taking \(N\ge3(n+r)+4\) leaves a sufficient path segment for all \(n\) steps below.

**Fresh counts.** At cut zero mark \(s=e_{n+r-1}\). The two witnessed balls of radius \(n+r\) about \(s\) agree including all openings. Indeed the change \(bz\) meets \(b\) at distance \(n+r\) and \(z\) at distance \(n+r+1\) in both preparations. Projecting the special witness onto the smaller ball yields only \(b\) in either. The old radius-\(r\) ball of \(s\) runs from \(e_{n-1}\) to \(e_{n+2r-1}\). Each step adds one to the number in an inclusive interval of path descendants. At cut \(n\) its closest member to the edited part is \(Y\) advanced \(n-1\) times by the binary calculation just proved. All other members still come from the plain path. By the commutation identity this is the ball \(B_n^r I_{[n]}\{s\}\). In the first arm its fresh counts comprise one three and \(n+2r\) twos. In the second all \(n+2r+1\) counts are two. The special birth matches recursively by its parent sets. The difference even survives forgetting all its names.

**Rosters.** For \(n\ge2\) instead mark \(e_{n+r-2}\). The old radius \(n+r-1\) agrees. At depth \(n-1\) the marked output ball comprises the advanced \(Y\), denoted \(V\), followed by \(\ell=n+2r-1\) consecutive ordinary two-open path members. Its inclusive children number \(h(V)+\ell\). They consist of the \(h(V)\) children through \(V\) and the \(\ell\) further path pairs through the listed path members. The latter include the pair extending away from \(V\). No other child through \(V\) can also touch a retained path member. The output therefore has \(n+2r+2\) births in the first arm and \(n+2r+1\) in the second. For \(n=1\) attach the path at \(e_0=a\) instead and use \(h(u)=1,h(a)=3,h(b)=2,h(z)=1\). Mark \(e_r\). Its old radius \(r\) agrees. The first output ball includes the binary \(\{a,z\}\) in one arm and the ternary \(\{a,b,z\}\) in the other, as well as the same \(Y\) and the same path pairs. The number of binary births drops by one and the number of ternaries gains one. When \(r=0\) the unchanged opening count forces the total number of immediate births of \(a\) to agree, making their arities essential for this particular one-step test.

**Finite check.** Using the older complete operator directly on the physical stars, take \(N=24\), both laws, \(0\le r\le3\) and \(1\le n\le5\). Test equality of both sets of named input-ball members and of their fully restricted witnessed ends at each asserted smaller radius. Follow actual parents for \(n\) steps, then measure the output ball and compare the displayed multisets or counts. All 40 fresh-count comparisons, 32 multi-step roster comparisons and eight one-step arity comparisons give the stated values. The two main preparations give birth counts \(28,29,31,33,35\) and \(27,27,27,27,27\) after 1 through 5 steps. For a separate source check set \(N=4\) for both attachment points in both preparations. Each has 17 knots and respectively 50 or 51 distinctions. Exact elimination of the parity equations and exhaustive pinning of each single star in the not-all-equal relation gave the full expected star projection in every case. The canonical K1–K3 test passed on all 68 projected stars per law. These finite checks corroborate the path induction. ## Other rulers and the two global decisions

Giving a step to **every pair of co-inputs** in any actual funded birth reproduces the witnessed ruler exactly. Counting only actual **two-parent** births can lose even connectivity. Take three live holders with two loose distinctions apiece. Give each pair a different spent witness meeting each endpoint twice. The same source projection arguments above apply. Every two live knots have witnessed separation one and a common ternary child at the first step. There are three additional unary children. There are no two-parent births at this cut, so every different live pair has infinite separation on the restricted ruler. This mechanism also occurs on the simple physical six-cycle control. [Physical six-cycle and matched edit](physical_edits.md)

Each witnessed step can be followed by two shared-distinction steps between ordinary holders, through its witness. A short ordinary route need not give any route in the witnessed end. An ordinary contact between opposite parts of a physical bipartite presentation, for instance, cannot connect those parts by witnessed steps, all of which stay in one part. As a sufficient physical reading, a ball of \(4R+2\) **alternating holder/distinction incidences** about a marked live set recovers its witnessed radius \(R\) with loose counts. A path of \(R\) witnessed steps uses at most \(4R\) such incidences. Two extra incidences show all distinctions of each retained holder and their possible other holders, and therefore its true loose count. They also identify every common witness of any displayed subset by intersecting those holder lists, including witnesses outside the displayed witnessed ball. For independent global decisions this conversion supplies no information beyond the given physical ball. A minimum common **bond** measured on the projected allowed states is still another reading. The theorem uses growth incidences and makes no conversion from scalar bond minima.

A shortage at one initially supplied holder cancels the whole planned step in either law. At a connected funded parity cut, a sole child consuming all loose distinctions is already implied and receives zero. The second test can depend on a different live component in the same connected physical presentation. Not-all-equal keeps that child live. A canceled plan, a formed last knot and a live child are different outcomes. The local and sharp-radius statements require the matched continuing cuts they compare. They give no duration in physical time, and they do not require an edited origin to be selectable by an operation within a run.
