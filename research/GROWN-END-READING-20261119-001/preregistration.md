# Which reading carries the growing end?

The experiment concerns a specified permutation of a finite reached arrangement. The growing end is fixed pointwise. Record every pair bond distance. Add the reading on all pairs of whether those two distinctions can flip while every other one is held fixed, or the minimum bond size on every unordered triple with a distinct value for no common bond, or both. The outcome is whether some naming of the fresh distinctions carries the whole next pair table with that same old permutation. The equation space and the next triple table answer stronger questions.

## Before the comparison

1. The pair flip reading is expected to repair the six-junction start with repeated meetings. On meet-once starts its nontrivial pairs have identical holders, which can only be loose twins already fixed at the growing end. We therefore expect it to leave the old meet-once failures intact.
2. The three-distinction reading is expected to eliminate every bad permutation of the four named arrangements and the two exceptional stages of the starting census. A stronger question is whether it eliminates every permutation that changes the equations, including on stages where the old pair table already extended. We do not assume that outcome.
3. For the all-size question, try recovering the equations from the smallest bonds, and try hiding competing equations at more than two junctions connecting a closed piece to the growing end. The possibility that a reading carries one round without carrying the equations stays open. A size bound on the starting census is not a bound on the largest reached distinctions count.

## Checks

The earlier study exhausts 33,430 admissible connected meet-once starts with at most 15 distinctions. It tests every positive-age open stage with at most 16 current junctions, through the greatest observed age of six. On any stage where the entire pair-table group already extends, each subgroup extends. Classify every permutation or an exact generating set of each refined group in the remaining cases. Preserve the distinction between checking every member and selecting generators of the old group that happen to survive.

Run the three inequivalent minimum meet-once stages separately, together with the published six-junction repeated-meeting example. Keep the identity, a false input permutation, a failing old permutation, and literal next readings on already shared distinctions. For a positive all-round claim check equation transport and that the next growing end is carried. State the exact limitations of finite tests, extra samples and sufficient lemmas.

The closest results are the earlier meet-once and repeated-meeting examples and the accepted equation-transport theorem. They supply the population and comparators.
