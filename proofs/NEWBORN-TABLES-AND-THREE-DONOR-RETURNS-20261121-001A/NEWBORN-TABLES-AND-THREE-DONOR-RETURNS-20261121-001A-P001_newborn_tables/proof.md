# The complete three-reader table on a newborn knot

**Theorem.** On every finite lawful ordinary newborn with three loose distinctions held by that knot alone, one distribution on six predetermined binary answers gives all eight joint setting tables. This holds under even parity and not-all-equal, both for a positive uniform full-word preparation retaining the spent distinctions and after their coherent all-plus erasure. The three readers choose only between the ordinary value and the single-distinction change, independently after any herald. Other loose distinctions stay unread. Disclosing an exterior value can change the conclusion on the very same newborn.

## The actual states and the reader

Let the indicator of an allowed complete written word be \(A(s,x,r)\), where \(s\) lists every twice-held distinction, \(x\) the three chosen loose distinctions, and \(r\) the remaining loose ones. Write \(n_r(x)=\sum_s A(s,x,r)\), \(N=\sum_{s,x,r}A(s,x,r)\), and \(W=\sum_{x,r}n_r(x)^2\). On nonempty support the two reduced states are
\[
 D=\frac{\sum_{s,r} A(s,\cdot,r)A(s,\cdot,r)^T}{N},
 \qquad Q=\frac{\sum_r n_r n_r^T}{W}.
\]
The genuine retained records distinguish different \(s\); an unexamined loose word \(r\) is orthogonal to every different word in that bank for both readings. Neither a discarded record nor an ordinary paid round adds the amplitudes of its distinguishable alternatives. If a separate coherent character instrument measures all \(k\) spent names, its all-plus port has probability \(W/(2^k N)\). Port \(h\) replaces \(n_r(x)\) with \(\sum_s(-1)^{h\cdot s}A(s,x,r)\), with common per-launch denominator \(2^k N\). Summing all port matrices gives \(D\) by orthogonality. An unreturned orthogonal copy of every \(s\) leaves even the selected all-plus table equal to \(D\), with port chance \(2^{-k}\). Returning that copy coherently before measurement recovers \(Q\). These probability and state formulas were proved in the earlier erasure study. On one measured distinction, \(Z\) assigns the sign \((-1)^b\) to ordinary value \(b\) and \(X\) reverses \(b\). The supplied two-outcome projector is \((I\pm O)/2\) for either choice \(O\). Complete settings are products on distinct names. In particular, summing unasked outcomes leaves every other outcome probability unchanged. Write \(Z=(Z_1,Z_2,Z_3)\) and \(X=(X_1,X_2,X_3)\) for the two *separately observed* triple laws. A single distribution on their six signs, independent of the actual settings, is the proposed common explanation. Each reader may look up only its own entry. The literature describes the finite local-distribution test by the convex hull of deterministic lists and gives 46 inequality classes for three readers with two binary-outcome settings each. Here the physical support and its complement symmetry give a direct construction. [Śliwa, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0305190v1)

## What the not-all-equal holder implies

Fix every other loose value \(r\). A chosen distinction occurs nowhere else. After imposing the other holders, each compatible spent exterior allows all six mixed triples. It can exclude at most one of 000 and 111, because the newborn has at least one further value on a paid input. If the other values at that knot already contain both values, both homogeneous triples remain possible. Thus, for some \(C>0\), the eight clean amplitudes at this exterior are
\[
 C(a,1,1,1,1,1,1,b),\qquad 0\le a,b\le1,\quad a+b\ge1.
\]
A zero \(C\) gives no probability. For the retained state fix \(s\) as well. Its nonzero vectors have \((a,b)=(0,1),(1,0)\) or \((1,1)\). The previous study derived this representation and the scalar score bound \(25/13\). The question here is the entire distribution. [Predecessor score theorem](../../SPENT-PASTS-AND-TWO-SHARED-NAMES-20261121-001A/SPENT-PASTS-AND-TWO-SHARED-NAMES-20261121-001A-P001_spent_pasts/proof.md)

Three consequences matter.

1. Interchanging any of the three fresh names preserves every amplitude. Complementing the *entire* ordinary word under unsigned not-all-equal also preserves its weight. This matches \(r\) with its complement and \((a,b)\) with \((b,a)\) at the same \(C\) and squared norm. In the retained case it also complements \(s\). A fixed complement orbit of size one already has a symmetric vector or mixture. Physically averaging all exteriors sums their unnormalized matrices, so each sector has weight proportional to \(C^2(6+a^2+b^2)\). On the answers, ordinary complement reverses all queried \(Z\) signs and leaves every \(X\) sign unchanged.
2. For any distinct \(i,j,k\), \(P(X_i=-,Z_j\ne Z_k)=0\). At unequal ordinary values of the other two names, both possible values on name \(i\) have equal amplitude for every exterior. Their negative-change amplitude is the difference and vanishes. This holds before considering complements and survives arbitrary convex averaging of these sectors.
3. Let \(m=P(ZZZ\text{ is mixed})\) and \(u=P(XXX=+++)\). In a sector with \(L=6+a^2+b^2\), \(m=6/L\) and \(u=(6+a+b)^2/(8L)\). Hence \(m\le(48/49)u\) in every physical sector and after their trace-weighted sum. Every eligible **four-place** newborn has precisely one place outside the chosen three. Each spent exterior excludes exactly one of the two homogeneous triples, so \(a+b=1\) at each nonzero sector and equality holds in that ratio for both preparations. With further places, seeing both values in those places can increase the supply of all-plus change outcomes.

## An explicit explanation on all 64 lists

Draw a triple of change signs \(x\) with its measured \(XXX\) probabilities \(p_x\). If \(x=+++\), choose a mixed ordinary triple with conditional probability \(m/u\), equally among its six words. In every other case choose 000 or 111 with equal chances, independently of the common-bit orientation. In signs 000 means all ordinary signs plus. In bit order \((z_1,x_1,z_2,x_2,z_3,x_3)\) the complete weights are
\[
 w(z,x)=
 \begin{cases}
 m/6 & z\text{ mixed},\ x=000,\\
 0 & z\text{ mixed},\ x\ne000,\\
 [p_x-m\,1_{x=000}]/2 & z\in\{000,111\}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The weights are nonnegative and sum to one. At \(XXX\) and \(ZZZ\) the measured distributions follow directly. For each of the three settings with one \(Z\), global complement makes the single ordinary bit fair at every fixed pair of change outcomes; ignoring that bit recovers the corresponding marginal from \(XXX\). These are exactly the generator's probabilities. For a setting with two \(Z\) answers, their marginal is already fixed. Unequal \(Z\) answers force the change to be plus. The observed single-negative-change probability therefore all lies on equal ordinary answers, and ordinary complement splits it equally between 00 and 11. The generator has the same marginal and exactly those zero and symmetry properties. This accounts for every one of the 64 entries. The replay also verifies all 64 polynomial identities on the paired symbolic sector before normalization. It enumerates the distinct lists in each of the ten permutation-and-complement orbits and checks their multiplicities. [Symbolic and numeric proof checks](../../../research/NEWBORN-TABLES-AND-THREE-DONOR-RETURNS-20261121-001A/replay.py)

There is an exact boundary for any normalized, non-signalling table with these same permutation and complement symmetries and the three stated zero events. Let \(p_1=P(XXX\text{ has exactly one minus})\). A common explanation exists **if and only if** \(m\le u+p_1\). Indeed on a mixed ordinary word only the minority position can have a negative change sign, for either other position would see two unequal ordinary values. Necessity also gives an observable inequality valid on *all* deterministic lists, without symmetry:
\[
 P(ZZZ\text{ mixed})\le P(XXX\text{ has at most one minus})
       +\sum_i P(X_i=-,Z_j\ne Z_k),\quad\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}.
\]
For sufficiency, allocate mixed mass first to all-plus, and any remaining mass to the three single-negative change words in proportion to their observed chances. There put the ordinary minority at the single negative position, and choose its ordinary orientation fairly. All remaining words receive equal 000/111 orientation. This again gives all four kinds of settings by the preceding marginal argument. The symmetric pure-sector pair criterion is thus \((6+a+b)^2+3(a-b)^2\ge48\), since a single-negative change word has probability \((a-b)^2/(8L)\). For illustration the abstract pair \((4/5,0)\) needs some one-negative mass and still has a common explanation, whereas \((2/5,2/5)\) violates the inequality. Both lie outside the newborn restriction \(a+b\ge1\). Neither is offered as an attained counterexample.

## Parity

On any nonempty finite parity structure write the allowed full words as the binary affine space \(v+C\). Independently choose an ordinary word uniformly on \(v+C\) and a change-instruction word uniformly on the orthogonal space \(C^\perp\). The signs on any disjoint queried sets \(U\) of ordinary reads and \(T\) of changes have product expectation zero unless \(U\in C^\perp\) and \(T\in C\), when the value is \((-1)^{U\cdot v}\). This equals the full positive uniform-word expectation under the two supplied projectors, so every subset and the retained triple has the claimed distribution. For clean erasure every supported loose word has the same number of spent completions, the size of the projection's kernel. The loose support is itself affine, with a positive uniform coherent preparation. Apply the same construction to that projected affine space and then restrict to the three named readers. This covers disconnected components and inconsistent support gives no launch. Parity needs no global ordinary-complement symmetry. ## What changes if the exterior is readable

On the first growth of the isolated four-place not-all-equal source, each of its four children holds one paid input and three fresh names. For the retained preparation, reading the paid bit as an extra measurement gives each orientation with probability \(1/2\). The corresponding conditional triple is the equal positive superposition of seven words, with only the homogeneous word of that orientation excluded. The same conditional triple can be obtained for **either** preparation by disclosing that all nine fresh names at the other three children are zero. Ordinary legality forces the old bits to 0111 in focal-first order. All nine ones give the opposite orientation. For either specified orientation the ordinary retained event has chance \(1/4802\), its chance conditional on clean erasure is \(1/50018\), and its chance together with that clean port per initial launch is \(1/76832\). These numbers come from seven supported triple words, each with one compatible old word; the initial normalizers are \(N=33614\), \(W=350126\), and \(2^k=16\). Conditioning is on a pre-settings event.

Let \(c\) be the excluded ordinary bit value. On any predetermined list the following inequality holds:
\[
 P(XXX\text{ has at least two minuses})\le P(ZZZ=ccc)
  +\sum_i P(X_i=-, (Z_j,Z_k)\ne(c,c)).
\]
Indeed, if two positions have negative changes and neither associated summand occurs, both complementary ordinary pairs must be \((c,c)\), forcing all three ordinary bits to \(c\). On the seven-word preparation every event on the right has probability zero. Every three-bit change word containing at least one minus has probability \(1/56\), giving the left side \(4/56=1/14\) and a strict violation. Its signed score remains \(12/7\). Mixing the two equally weighted orientations without disclosing which occurred gives a common explanation again. These are *attained conditional tables*. They show why solving an arbitrarily selected exterior alone would have given the wrong answer to B298.
