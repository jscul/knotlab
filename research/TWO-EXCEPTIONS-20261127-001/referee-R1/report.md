1. **Block on a population statement.** The sentence in README.md is “**Under `grow`** every accepted start from seven to eleven knots is in the proved class (one ten-knot start is over the cap), most with chains: there the late view is a theorem.” The five `grow` cells establish 774 certified of 775 accepted draws across the five sizes, with one unresolved. The conditional theorem stands. The summary “So under both weightings, the reading's failure on starts it can check is almost always chained twins, and the main limit as size grows is the size of the core, not either exception.” also needs the two denominators. The fresh `grow` table has zero finished exceptions from which to estimate the mix. 3. **Narrowing of the two family descriptions.** The twin definition in README.md reads “a **twin family**: two lifts (T_1, a), (T_2, a) of two core parents on one vector, with two or more members one chain step lower, on one vector, whose cores are common children of T_1 and T_2 (Lemma D's chained-twin case; the lower members may be pure twins that hold no chain base)”. Its next entry is “a **lift split**: two families inside one parent set P(y), each holding one of two lifts that share no parent (N2's failure).” A twin family can have **two or more** such lifts. Of the 154 gained twin families, 18 on three original starts have three or four lifts. One eight-knot sample alone has a family with three pure members and four lifts. For the split, use the shortenings together with a maximal mutually compatible set of lifts. A finished 44-knot construction first splits at child round 8. There is one shortening and there are three lifts. The third lift meets the other two and occurs in both gained triples. This contradicts the more specific sentence in shapes.py, “two extra families, each one lift with all shortenings, whose union is one missing P(y)”. One 122-knot construction gives **three** gained families at round 8. Its core remained unresolved at the search limit, so it shows what the local rule permits without settling multiplicity on all finishing cores.    **Can a third kind receive one of the labels?** For genuine round-rule outputs, the twin test's vector pattern forces the stated parent incidences by Lemma A. A gained family strictly inside a proposed parent set also forces an incompatible pair of lifts. If all members of the proposed set met in pairs, a gained group inside it would fail maximality. An uncovered pair inside it would be covered, since a proposed family of three or more in this case has no common parent and extends to a group. Only two lifts can be incompatible. Thus the labels distinguish the broad mechanisms on their stated inputs. The strict-subset test alone does not establish the exact number of lifts or families, check their union, or account for a discrepancy on another piece. A family witnessing N3 at a single vector with no common core child cannot pass either test. The independent check compared both directions of the family difference, including parent-set multiplicities. ## Independent checks and stops

The family check starts from the holders of each distinction and the loose distinctions at each live knot. It forms each core rank from maximal pairwise-sharing families without a common parent and from uncovered pairs. It indexes each extension by its core and its sorted chain indices. Shortening a positive length or lifting the same lengths gives its entire proposed parent family. For each successive round I compared this multiset with the round rule on the previous round, including every piece. Exhaustive subset checks on 25 small witness ranks and reversal of the knot and distinction names on all seven witnesses reproduced the rosters and verdicts. [Code](rebuild.py) · [Controls](control.json)

The broader bounded search covered 935 earlier rows, of which 492 had no chains, 358 agreed through child round 11 or exhaustion, 70 failed by twins and 15 reached the width guard. Edits to the loose distinctions of four supplied witnesses gave 249 candidate starts. Of these, 139 had a shortage, 12 reached the width guard, 17 agreed through round 15 or exhaustion, and all 81 first failures had a named mechanism. These counterexample searches overlap the original samples. The constructed search tested 45,540 one-chain settings with repeats for a single-vector first failure with no common child. Of these, 6,235 finished with no obstruction, 13,890 encountered N1 or N2 first, and 25,415 reached a search cutoff.
