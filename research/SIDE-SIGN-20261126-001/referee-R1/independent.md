# Answers before opening the study

1. For a specified involution g on distinctions, compute x+gx over the two-element field. The all-zero and all-one differences distinguish two disjoint subsets. Any other difference lies in neither subset. A fixed distinction excludes the all-one difference. Not-all-equal admits complementation; an even parity rule of odd arity prevents a state and its full complement from both passing. Within the two subsets the difference itself reads the label. Selecting g, restricting the population and giving the two sectors signed mass are separate operations. Equal positive mass on every allowed assignment gives a common joint distribution and CHSH at most two for ordinary distinction readings.
2. Where the relevant knot pairs act freely, a chain of paired knots and paired shared distinctions can lift to a chain from a knot to its partner. Changing which representative is called first changes the names at both ends of each step and cancels on a closed chain. For example, taking half a turn around an even ring ends at the partner while closing on pairs. Fixed and complement-fixed assignments respond differently when comparing the values of exchanged distinctions. A general assignment need not give one common response. The structural exchange persists independently of any assignment or weighting.
3. My first trial on the ordinary five-knot census input at index 3097 already attains 4 before and after one growth under parity, using an invariant of a given swap. 4. Let S be the parity state set, and H the span of all tied shortest allowed changes through each distinction. A distinction permutation g maps the minimizing changes bijectively and so acts linearly on S/H, fixing the zero class. With exactly two classes it fixes both; for larger quotients one must compute the action on nonzero classes. A permutation on the set of classes assigns no relative mass or sign. When it fixes two classes, constant weightings on them allow either relative sign. This can be tested by transporting each class's indicator separately.
