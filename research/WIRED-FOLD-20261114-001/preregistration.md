## Bounded learning procedure

1. Initialize both branch accumulators to a single fitted bit. Visit every source in its given order. A shared four-input Boolean program updates one accumulator from its old state, that record's membership value, and its two individual inlet matches. The same program acts at both branches and all four source positions. Fit an elementary expression of at most four gates and the initial bit from downstream training accuracy with two available payload bits. A training-only majority decoder maps the two bits and the actual query mask to an answer, sharing all wirings and menus. It gets no input-pair label. Break ties by fewer gates and a deterministic syntax order.
2. Fit a single Boolean program on the four public menu bits to choose whether a second bit is sent. For two bits the packet contains the final two accumulators. For one bit a separate shared expression may read the two accumulators and the first query port's two mask bits. Search width expressions of at most three gates and one-bit expressions of at most four gates, with AND, OR, XOR, NAND, constants and raw inputs. Formulas can reuse a value for unary inversion. Semantically duplicate expressions can be collapsed. Fit the one-bit decoder across all training episodes actually choosing one bit. Its only keys are the bit and the eventual mask. Choose first by total training correctness, then by fewer actual bits sent, then by fewer gates and deterministic ties. This explicitly values communication, with no class-count or correct-width labels. The supplied two-lane memory, fixed scan, computation budget and choice between one derived output or two accumulator bits are architectural priors.
3. Before consulting any held-out answers, serialize **one** policy containing all gates, initial state and both parts of its shared decoder. Freeze its digest. Count labels, widths and fixed-world counterfactuals independently. A complete positive result requires all answers correct and the smallest possible fixed width for every public menu in the stated panel. Partial accuracy, excess width or lost distinctions are separate outcomes. Always-two is an attainable accuracy comparator. Always-one cannot distinguish all the required future answer patterns.
