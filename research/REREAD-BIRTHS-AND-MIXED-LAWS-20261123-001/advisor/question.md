Cold design review before two small studies are frozen. For each: (1) the strongest objection, (2) one concrete change, (3) anything the predictions get wrong by plain reasoning. At most 350 words in all. Do not praise.

Setting. Structures: two-valued distinctions; knots hold 3 or more of them (parity or not-all-equal). Ordinary growth, per round: a knot is live if it holds a loose distinction (one held by it alone); two live knots are adjacent if some one knot meets both; groups are the maximal sets of 3 or more pairwise adjacent live knots held by no single witness, plus each adjacent pair in none of those; each live knot owes one loose distinction to each group containing it; if any knot owes more than it has, the whole round stalls; otherwise each group gives one birth, and every remaining loose distinction gives a one-parent birth, so every loose distinction is spent every round. A birth gets max(D, 3 - p, 1) fresh distinctions (D a one-round lookahead of demand, p its number of parents); under parity a sole birth taking all loose distinctions gets none and closes the whole. Births, parents and fresh counts never depend on values. Under ordinary growth no distinction ever gets a third holder. Recorded: 744 of 1,024 sparsely supplied random starts stall at once; the recorded sources never stall; a Bell-type table of 9/4 was once produced by edits that create or repair a shortage (stalling as a joint response), with weights fixed on the start.
Known from the preceding study: with every finished word weighted equally and a supplied "setting birth" that takes distinctions already held twice, a 7-knot structure mixing both laws gives a hidden CHSH of 19/9; with loose inputs nothing with at most three holders was found.

Study A (a growth law with re-reading). Rule R: as ordinary growth, except that a knot owing more than its loose distinctions pays the excess with distinctions it shares (giving them a third or later holder); the round stalls only if it owes more than it holds in all. Which shared distinction is paid is a supplied choice: all choices enumerated on small cases, two fixed conventions (oldest, newest) elsewhere; never one the newborn would hold twice. Tests: (a) every old word extends at every birth, both laws, exact on small cases; (b) the recorded sources keep their histories (they never stall); (c) the 744 stalled starts per law: growth kind, largest number of holders, first failing step of a planarity reading, dependence on the convention; (d) the two smallest stalling set-ups behind the 9/4 table rerun under R with the same edits and start weighting: CHSH; (e) pace: one loose distinction added at a random knot of a start; the earliest round at which a birth changes at each holder-distance from the edit, under ordinary growth and under R, 200 starts. Predictions: (a) holds; (b) identical; (c) no start stalls at once, some distinction reaches four holders, the two conventions differ on some start; (d) at most 2; (e) under R no birth changes farther than t + 1 holder-steps from the edit by round t, while ordinary growth has instant changes through stalls.

Study B (reachability). Target: the old part of each structure with a hidden excess found by the census (the 7-knot one and 291 others of up to 8 knots, both laws mixed). For every connected proper subset of a target's knots taken as founders (their distinctions held by non-founders become loose), run ordinary growth and ask whether some stage equals the target as a pattern of holders. Since every loose distinction is spent each round, the knots of a reachable target fall into cohorts with shared distinctions only between consecutive cohorts or inside the founders. Predictions: the 7-knot target is reached from no proper founder set; pencil reasons: two knots sharing two distinctions must both be founders (a newborn takes one distinction per parent), a not-all-equal newborn needs a fresh distinction, and the last knot's two parents have no common witness. Reported: for each target the largest part reachable, and whether any of the 292 is reached.
