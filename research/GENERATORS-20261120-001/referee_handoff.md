## Final report as sent

2026-11-20 12:48 PST

**Yes. Ordinary growth can keep a source active, repeatedly produce things that continue on their own, and let two of them make something together while both survive.** The constructions work under parity and not-all-equal. The result is referee pending.

**The source.** Seven knots and 25 distinctions suffice. The first products leave at cluster sizes 16, 24 and 32, then every eight sizes. At release, every parent belongs to the source. From then on, every parent of every continuing member belongs to that product. This is the smallest start, counting knots first, in the class where the full parent structure is proved from the start. The unrestricted minimum is open. A single catalogue ring can persist indefinitely, but its surviving descendants share ancestors that prevent it from repeatedly releasing separate lasting products. [Source proof](https://github.com/jscul/knotlab/blob/main/research/GENERATORS-20261120-001/source.md)

**The meeting.** In a 21-knot start, two products emerge at size 16 and make a shared child at size 17. Both keep growing at their previous rates. Descendants of their shared child can keep taking inputs from one surviving product forever. In a matched start that keeps both products apart, the shared birth disappears, and a corresponding knot receives **15 fresh distinctions instead of 18**. The opportunity for direct contact must already be present at release. After one intervening generation in which each product supplies all its own parents, a first direct meeting is impossible. [Matched preparations and timing proof](https://github.com/jscul/knotlab/blob/main/research/GENERATORS-20261120-001/meeting.md)

**A product can itself be a source.** One released family keeps taking either of two returns. Within it, a less frequent return gives rise to independently continuing products of its own. The limit for the generator analogy is that successive releases use larger patterns. Whether one source can emit the *same finite thing* indefinitely is open. [Nested construction](https://github.com/jscul/knotlab/blob/main/research/GENERATORS-20261120-001/nested.md)

**Evidence.** The size check exhausted 996 connected starting shapes and 117,142 choices of live knots through seven knots. In the separate sample, 75/240 uniform labelled trees funded a first step, compared with 32/240 fresh such trees given one uniformly chosen extra meeting. All 480 passed both knot laws. Fourteen of 16 archived explosive starts qualified under each law. These panels tested the growth controls. The frequency of generators remains unknown.
