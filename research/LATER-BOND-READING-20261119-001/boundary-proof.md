# When a fall at two attachment junctions reveals a triple

Use the classical wired incidence representation. Let two connected parts H and Q meet exactly at the distinct vertices A and B and have disjoint distinction sets. Write K(X) for the minimum size of a bond containing the nonempty marked set X, using infinity if there is none. Pair distance is K−1. A later complete round changes only Q, whose new version is Q′. A terminal means A or B. Every result below refers to this supplied decomposition. ## The exact test, including a free fresh naming

Let L_H(X) be the minimum size of a cut of H separating A from B, containing X and having both shores connected. The empty marked set gives the ordinary minimum separating cut size. Let λ=L_Q(∅), let λ′=L_Q′(∅), and let B_H(X) be the minimum bond size through X in H with A and B identified. A loop created by identification belongs to no cut. Both λ and λ′ are positive finite integers when their graphs are connected.

A bond meeting both parts must separate the terminals. When the terminals are apart, each global shore contains just one terminal. Any component of its restriction to one part missing that terminal would also be disconnected in the full shore. Conversely a connected separating cut in each part gives a bond on aligning the terminal sides. If nonempty X is in H and nonempty Y in Q, this proves

$$
K(X\cup Y)=L_H(X)+L_Q(Y).
$$

For X confined to H there is a second possibility. With the terminals together, a bond through X keeps all of Q on that side and Q connects the terminals there. Therefore

$$
K(X)=\min(B_H(X), L_H(X)+λ).
$$

These formulas also hold with Q′. In a lawful round confined to Q the terminal cut cannot increase. Place the new junctions on the old exterior side of any old separating cut. Each former loose distinction keeps its crossing status and no fresh one crosses. Minimizing gives λ′≤λ, even if that particular extended cut has a disconnected shore. In a closing round the sole new junction replaces the exterior. For any later sequence confined to Q the same inequality holds from the original stage.

**Criterion.** Suppose a permutation φ of H, fixing Q pointwise, preserves all present pair and triple readings. Extend it by the identity on all Q′ names. Every later pair is preserved. For triples the only possible failure is on three old distinctions in H. For such a set X write t=K(X) and s=L_H(X), and write s′=L_H(φX). If t is infinite, the later reading stays infinite. If s=s′, the later readings agree. In every other case the later readings differ exactly when

$$
                \min(s,s′)+λ′ < t.
$$

Indeed choose any q in a minimum separating cut of Q. It has L_Q({q})=λ, so a mixed query on X and q gives L_H(X)+λ. The current readings through order three determine L_H(X) for all X of size one or two. Every mixed future pair or triple therefore agrees by the first formula. Pure Q′ sets are fixed. For any old internal X the second formula and λ′≤λ give

$$
               K_{\rm later}(X)=\min(t,L_H(X)+λ′).
$$

Taking this same expression at X and φX proves the criterion. An infinite present reading in H forces both B_H and L_H to be infinite. If just one of s,s′ is infinite, the strict inequality above still gives the correct test. A failed internal triple uses only old names, so no allowed fresh naming repairs it. Conversely when all internal triples pass, the displayed identity extension works, including on triples with new names.

For present finite t define the slack g=L_H(X)+λ−t, which is nonnegative or infinite, and the drop d=λ−λ′. Then the later value is t−max(0,d−g), interpreting the subtracted term as zero for infinite g. For this particular drop the statistic min(g,d) is necessary and sufficient on each pair of matched internal triples. A one-unit fall asks just whether some minimum present bond through the triple separates the terminals. This is a test on one internal triple's minimum witnesses. It does not assert that their existence was already given by the unaugmented triple table.

**Four current marks suffice.** The same single exterior distinction q works for all internal triples X, including those for which no separating cut exists. Reading the four-set X∪{q} yields L_H(X)+λ. The later internal triple is therefore the lesser of its current reading and that four-reading minus d, with infinity minus finite taken as infinity. In particular the all-size implication holds simultaneously for all future times confined to the same exterior. This sufficient condition does not claim that fourth-order data are necessary. More generally, present readings through order j+1 suffice to carry later readings through order j in this setting, by the same formulas.

## Relative presentations

For two supplied decompositions and name bijections carrying H to the other interior and Q to the other exterior, absolute separating costs need not have been provided. In each presentation put η=L_H(∅), τ=η+λ, h(X)=L_H(X)−η, and q(Y)=L_Q(Y)−λ. All normalizations subtract finite numbers. The least pair reading with one name in each part, using bond size, is τ. The minimum is attained by one crossing from a minimum separating cut of each part. Such a pair maps to another minimum pair whenever the current mixed pair readings agree. Fix its exterior name. Mixed readings with that anchor determine τ+h(X) on matched interiors, through three marks when present readings through order four are available. The corresponding interior anchor gives τ+q(Y) on matched exteriors. This step determines the normalized costs even when neither absolute terminal minimum is agreed in advance.

Choose a proposed matching of the future exterior names extending their prescribed old matching. In each presentation set τ′=η+λ′ and q′(Y)=L_Q′(Y)−λ′. The formulas for later readings become

- mixed X,Y give τ′+h(X)+q′(Y),
- an internal X gives min(K(X), τ′+h(X)),
- an exterior Y gives min(B_Q′(Y), τ′+q′(Y)).

Consequently, given matching current readings through order four and a lawful nonincreasing exterior cut on each side, the proposed extension carries the readings through order three exactly when the two values of τ′ agree, the normalized exterior costs q′ match on one and two marks, and the last displayed exterior expression agrees on sets through size three. Necessity of the first condition follows by minimizing a mixed pair, and necessity of the second by using a minimizing interior name. The other direction follows by substitution. Unary readings follow from the pair table in the admissible open arrangement because every bond has at least two distinctions. An equivalent statement can include them explicitly. For an existential fresh matching, require one bijection satisfying these conditions simultaneously. When the initial and later exteriors on the two sides are isomorphic under the specified transport, the absolute exterior minima agree and so does η; all exterior conditions follow. If the two exterior minima fall by different amounts then τ′ differs despite initial agreement. Equal falls alone do not identify the relative profiles of two unrelated new exteriors.

## Every integer fall has a lawful realization

Fix integers m≥k≥1. A static ordinary core H may be joined to the construction below at A,B if closing H by one auxiliary AB link is two-vertex-connected and every nonterminal ordinary site has arity at least three. The final start also requires both terminal arities at least three. For a simple core and m≥2 each terminal with at least one ordinary core distinction meets that requirement in the construction. No core distinction is loose. The constructed exterior has terminal minimum m after round one and k after round two. All new sites lie in the exterior. The graph may therefore be used to turn a verified numerical obstruction on such a core into an actual positive-age obstruction. It does not prescribe the fate of an arbitrary exterior already chosen.

A unit branch at a pole P consists initially of P–U, U–V, U–W and V–W, with one loose distinction at each of V and W. Only V,W are live. Their common neighbor U backs their pair. Its junction takes both loose distinctions and receives one fresh since the rest of the construction also has loose distinctions. At the next round its private one-parent offshoot gets two fresh distinctions. The original P–U link keeps the capacity to the exterior at one. P has no initially live neighbor in this branch.

A branch of size b≥4 has one initially live R_ij for each unordered pair of b indices, with two loose distinctions apiece. For each three indices add a closed T_ijk meeting the three R sites indexed by its two-element subsets. Join P to every T. Two R sites are candidates exactly when their index pairs meet. A maximal family of pairwise meeting index pairs is either an entire index-star or the three pairs of one triple. To see this, take ij and ik. Any member omitting i must be jk, and then a member meeting all three must be one of the three. T_ijk meets precisely that latter family. No ordinary witness meets all b−1 pairs at one index, including at b=4. Thus the first round adds one site P_i for the star of i. Every candidate pair belongs to such a star, so the fallback rule adds no pairs. Each R_ij supplies exactly the two stars at i and j, with no spare offshoot.

The P_i are pairwise backed by the corresponding R_ij and none of their old neighbors meets more than two of them. Their whole set is the single maximal group next time. Each P_i therefore has demand one, took b−1 parents and consumes a proper subset of the starting loose set. Its cheapest fresh count is exactly one. The second-round group takes all b tips of this branch, but there are loose distinctions on the other side. It has demand zero for the following round because each P_i has only this child, and it gives one fresh. At age one the b routes P–T_{i,i+1,i+2}–R_{i,i+1}–P_i–outside, with subscripts modulo b and sets unordered, have distinct distinctions. The b tips separate, so the terminal cut is b. At age two the unique fresh distinction separates and the branch stays connected, giving value one.

At b=3 give three sites meeting P two loose distinctions each. P backs their three pairs and meets them all, so there is no unbacked triple in the first round. Each pair joins and receives one fresh. The three new sites form the sole second-round triple and give one fresh. The three first-stage tips and three disjoint routes certify 3→1. At b=2 use closed U,V meeting P, with U meeting S,T and V meeting S,W, and join T to W. Give S two loose distinctions, T and W one each. The only pairs are S,T and S,W. They each receive one fresh and then join with one fresh, giving 2→1 through separate routes via U,T and V,W. At b=1 use another unit branch.

On the A side install one branch of size b=m−k+1 and k−1 unit branches. On the B side install m unit branches. No ordinary initial witness sees live sites from two different branches. Every unit branch meets its pole through a closed U, including when b=3 on the same side. After round one all new sites meet parents within their respective branches, preserving independence at the next round. Every fallback pair and every offshoot has been accounted for. The two sides meet each other only at the exterior, so the terminal minimum in Q is the lesser of their two total branch capacities. These totals are m and m, then k and m. In the largest branch all initial R sites have arity b and T sites have arity four. This construction permits growing arity and makes no fixed-arity claim.

For admissibility, add a temporary link from each individual branch pole to the outside. A unit branch then has no cut vertex. The two-parent branch has a spanning cycle P–U–S–V–W–T–outside–P. For a three-parent branch each leaf meets both terminals. In the index construction every T meets P, every R meets the outside, and the T,R meetings permit a route across after deleting any one internal site. After deleting either terminal all sites connect to the other terminal. Hence the augmented branch survives every single vertex deletion. Distinct parallel branches share only their two terminals. Between the A and B parts the exterior provides a series connection, and the static core supplies the alternative connection between A and B. Deleting the exterior leaves each branch attached to its surviving ordinary pole. Deleting an ordinary pole leaves its branch sites attached to the exterior and the core attached to the opposite pole. Deleting any other site preserves a route between its surviving terminals in the full graph. These facts and the core hypothesis give connectedness after deletion of any site. Each starting support is a bond, so its simultaneous projection is its full parity relation. The construction preserves single meetings between ordinary junctions whenever H has that property.

## Any finite number of attachment junctions

The following profile computes the exact answer for any finite set T of shared terminals. For each nonconstant bipartition of T, each piece records the least cost for the marked set in that piece and the indicated pair of terminal partitions. Each partition says which terminals on that shore are joined inside the piece. Every component of that shore must contain one of its terminals. An impossible record has cost infinity. For a mixed query, align the two terminal sides. On each side the connectivity partitions from the two pieces together must connect all terminals, meaning one can pass between any two by alternating their two recorded equivalences. The global minimum is the least sum of costs over these compatible states.

To prove this, restrict a global bond to each piece. Marks in both pieces force terminals on both shores. A component of a restricted shore which misses the terminals cannot join the rest of the global shore. Global paths across the shared terminals are exactly the alternating connections described in the compatibility test. Conversely compatible local cuts give a global cut with both shores connected. For a nonempty internal query one also permits a bond inside the interior with all of T identified. This supplies the constant-terminal alternative, since the opposite shore contains a marked interior endpoint and cannot meet the exterior piece. The exterior-only formula interchanges the roles of the pieces. Thus supplying all these profiles and a compatible future relative naming is sufficient, including when some minima are infinite.

For three terminals one is alone, while the other two must connect inside at least one piece. Each piece has its strict cost when they must connect there and its relaxed cost when they may connect through the other piece. The mixed minimum ranges over the three possible lone terminals and the two choices of which piece guarantees the connection. A single ordinary set of exterior anchors need not force a specified strict state. In the three-edge exterior star, the strict cut {q_a} is contained in each of the two relaxed cuts {q_a,q_b} and {q_a,q_c}. No positive marking of its crossings excludes both alternatives. ### Three terminals and an unbounded number of positive marks

Even when marks may be placed in both pieces, the number of shared terminals gives no bound on how many positive marks suffice to force a particular terminal bipartition. More precisely, for each k≥1 there is a simple positive-age source with three supplied terminals and a fixed interior distinction x such that every feasible marked set of size ≤k through x has a bond for *each* of the three nonconstant terminal bipartitions. This concerns feasibility for positive marks. The three conditional minimum sizes need not be equal.

Use two cycles of layers in the classical graph. Each cycle has even length, every layer has 4k+10 sites, and each pair of successive layers is completely joined. The cycles share precisely the three terminal sites. In both cycles place them in distinct layers more than k steps apart along either route. On the exterior cycle also select k+1 branch poles in layers more than k steps from one another and from every terminal. For example, an even cycle with k+4 locations spaced 2(k+1) apart supplies all required locations. Attach a private unit branch at each such pole, with their common outside, and take one lawful round. Distinct branch starts share no witness for their live sites. Every V,W pair produces exactly one binary junction with one fresh distinction. Each cycle remains connected after one vertex deletion, since every layer has multiple sites. The two cycles share three terminals, and another pole keeps the outside attached after deleting any one pole. Together with the unit-branch deletion check above this gives full starting projections. All ordinary meetings are single.

On either cycle, any at most k marked distinctions form a bipartite subgraph. No connected marked component meets two of the specified sites in that cycle since their layers are farther than k apart. Thus one can prescribe either shore at every specified site independently and assign two colors to each marked component to cross all its distinctions. At most 2k marked endpoints and k+4 specified sites have been assigned. There remain at least two free sites in every layer. Put one on each shore, then assign all other sites arbitrarily. Both shores within this cycle are connected. The representatives on either shore are joined from layer to layer, and every site meets a representative of its own shore in the next layer.

Fix any distinction x of the interior cycle and any feasible set through x of size ≤k. Every marked branch has a mark outside it. Hence its pole and the outside are separated in any witnessing bond, and the part of that witness inside the branch supplies a connected separating cut through its local marks. Put the outside on side zero, every marked pole on side one, and one unmarked pole on side zero. Such an unmarked pole exists since there are k+1 branches. Prescribe any given nonconstant bipartition at the three terminals, using matching choices in the two cycles. Complete both cycles as above and attach the stored cuts in the marked branches. An unmarked branch with pole on side one uses any connected separating cut. With pole on side zero put the whole branch there. At least one such whole branch connects the outside to the cycle on side zero. The two cycle shores connect through their common terminals on each side, and all branch pieces connect to their respective pole or outside. The resulting bond contains every mark and has the prescribed terminal bipartition. This proves the stated limitation for all k.
