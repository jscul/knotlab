# What one added meeting can change

**A connected tree with one extra simple meeting and six or more founding knots never closes under ordinary parity growth, at any finite age, if each knot has at most one opening beyond the three-place minimum.** An initially funded source with a single connected group of live knots must be a bare odd ring with precisely two loose distinctions at every knot. Its paid groups repeat forever. All other sources either lack funding or have live groups that cannot combine. Ordinary not-all-equal gives a positive fresh grant at every funded birth. The proof below applies to all finite founder sizes at least six. The separately enumerated probabilities of funding cover sizes six through eight. ## Source and funding

Use the holder-to-holder meeting diagram of the founder. The literature calls this a connected simple unicyclic graph. Its only ring consists of \(l\ge3\) distinct knots. The rest consists of trees attached to individual ring knots. Each meeting holds just one shared distinction. A knot of degree \(d\) receives \(p=\max(0,3-d)+b\) loose distinctions, for a flag \(b\in\{0,1\}\). A live knot has \(p>0\). A spent knot has \(p=0\), hence degree at least three. Every leaf and every degree-two knot is live. A live knot of degree at least two has at most two openings. One of degree at least three has just one.

Every such start has the full local projection of each supplied rule. Fix an arbitrary word on any one knot \(H\) and temporarily omit its own rule. Every component \(D\) after deleting \(H\) has a holder with a private distinction. For a tree component of \(s\) holders there are at most two meetings back to \(H\), since each after the first makes a ring. Its sum of original degrees is at most \(2(s-1)+2=2s\). For a component containing a ring there is one meeting back, and its sum is \(2s+1<3s\). Each component thus has an original degree of at most two at some holder. Root a spanning tree there and reserve one private distinction at its root. Choose unused distinctions freely, then work inward from the leaves. At every nonroot satisfy its row using the sole reserved meeting with its parent. At the root use the reserved private distinction. For parity there is exactly one choice at the last free place. For not-all-equal there is at least one. Consequently any permitted \(H\) word extends, and erasing the \(H\) rule permits words it forbade. This argument also proves the fixed-private-supply deletion comparisons whenever their altered sources meet the minimum arity and retain a private distinction in each described component. The actual named controls separately check both local projections. Two live knots are related if a physical holder witnesses them both. Every maximal group of at least three pairwise related knots that has no one common witness is a multi-parent proposal. Each related pair outside those proposals contributes one pair proposal, even if two physical knots witness it. A live knot owes one loose name per proposal containing it. If any is short, the whole cut stalls. Otherwise every leftover loose name makes one unary birth. A nonclosing birth with \(a\) parents receives the maximum of its following demand, \(3-a\), and one.

Two facts persist at every funded cut. The complete physical holding stays connected because each child takes a loose distinction from an older knot. Distinct connected groups in the relation on live knots cannot merge. Every live point supplies at least one child, and two new live knots share a witness only if an old parent took part in both. With at least two groups the funded cut has at least two births. Every child then receives a fresh distinction under both laws. Indeed, a sum of even parity rows containing only loose names has constant holder-inclusion coefficient along every meeting of the connected physical holding. Its unique nonzero possibility contains all loose names. Thus every group keeps a live descendant, preventing any later whole closure. Funding a nonclosing step supplies at least the whole next demand, so it prevents a new shortage there. ## Which discounts on a call are possible?

Two different witnesses for the same pair give a four-knot ring, with the pair opposite. Three mutually related knots with no one common witness give a closed walk of six meetings through their three distinct witnesses. The three knots are distinct, the three witnesses are distinct, and the walk has no immediate retracing, even at its start. In a one-ring diagram every such closed walk follows that ring an integral number of times. A tree attached to it has no route out without eventual immediate retracing. An unheld triple therefore lies on a three- or six-knot ring. It consists of all three knots or of three alternating knots on the six.

This accounts for every unheld larger proposal. Away from a four-knot ring every related pair has a unique witness. If every triple in a pairwise related group is held, choosing one pair forces the same witness for the rest. On a four-knot ring a group of three or more still has a pair with a unique witness. For each of the two alternating classes at most one pair has two witnesses, and all related knots are in one class. In the three- and six-knot cases no fourth live point can be related to all three members of an unheld triple. An off-ring knot has two-meeting routes to at most two ring knots, and in the six-knot case another ring knot is in the other alternating class. Hence a duplicate witness saves at most one charge on its pair, and an unheld triple replaces exactly three pair proposals by one triple, saving one charge at each member.

## Even ring length

The original knots split into two alternating classes. Related live knots are always in the same class. A connected live group must therefore lie entirely in one class \(A\), and every knot in the other class \(B\) is spent and has degree at least three. Every \(A\) knot must be live as well. A missing off-ring knot in \(A\) would have degree at least three. At least two outward branches then have compulsory live leaves which cannot connect through it. A missing ring knot in \(A\) has an outward branch with a live leaf. Elsewhere on the ring a \(B\) knot has its own outward branch, hence a second leaf. Paths of live points with witnesses move from \(A\) to \(A\) in two steps, passing through \(B\). They cannot cross the missing \(A\) to join those leaves.

Take a ring knot in \(A\) with original degree \(d\ge2\). Every adjacent witness in \(B\) sees at least two other live \(A\) knots. Counting with multiplicity gives at least \(2d\) calls. On the four-knot ring the two witnesses for its opposite partner save one. On the six-knot ring the one unheld alternating triple saves one. At every other even length there is no saving. Thus the ring knot owes at least three, exceeding its available two. A connected live end on an even ring cannot pay the first cut.

## Odd ring length of at least five

For bookkeeping use a point copy and a witness copy of each original holder. Join a point copy to the witness copy of every original neighbour. Retain a point copy only when that physical knot is live. Live connectivity is exactly connectivity of the retained points via these witnesses. Over an odd ring, before removing spent points, this diagram has a single alternating ring with \(2l\) positions. Every original attached tree has two separate copies, respectively attached at its root's point and witness positions. No other connection exits either tree. This construction reads the already supplied meetings.

Write \(l=2m+1\ge5\). Going around that alternating ring write \(p_i,w_i,p_{i+1}\), with indices modulo \(l\). If physical ring knots in original order are numbered modulo \(l\), \(p_i\) is knot \(2i\) and \(w_i\) is knot \(2i+1\). The witness copy of the physical knot at \(p_j\) is therefore \(w_{j+m}\). Every spent physical ring knot has an attached branch with a live leaf. Its leaf must reach the ring in a connected live end. A live ring point lies outside the branch. If all ring points are spent, another ring knot has a required live leaf in a different branch. Its leaf cannot reach the ring through the missing point copy. It must reach a **live immediate branch neighbour** through the witness copy, which in turn must meet a retained ring point. If no ring point is live, branches at the different spent ring knots have no route between them.

Consequently there is at least one retained ring point, and a proper subset of retained points must be a single consecutive run \(p_0,\ldots,p_{s-1}\). Attached trees cannot bridge a gap. Every witnessed pair here is charged separately. If \(s\ge3\), an interior point already owes two calls to its two ring neighbours in the point sequence. None of the witnesses \(w_0,\ldots,w_{s-2}\) between successive retained points can be the copy of a spent knot. Such a witness must also see a live immediate branch neighbour, adding a third call at an interior point. Spent knots' witnesses can therefore occupy only the two boundary positions \(w_{l-1}\) and \(w_{s-1}\). At most two points are omitted. But the omitted \(p_{l-1}\) has its witness at \(w_{m-1}\), strictly between two retained points for both \(s=l-1\) and \(s=l-2\). This is impossible.

With one retained point there are only two incident witness positions for \(l-1\) spent knots. With two retained points there are only three incident positions for \(l-2\) spent knots, leaving only \(l=5\). There, each of the three positions must have its own live branch neighbour. Each of the two retained points would owe the pair linking them, a pair to the branch at their common inner witness and a pair to the branch at its outer witness, again at least three. Every possibility for a proper nonempty run has now failed.

Thus every ring point is live and owes its two distinct ring partners. Any attached tree at a ring knot gives it degree at least three, cutting its capacity to one. A fundable connected live system must be the bare odd ring and every knot must have both available openings. Its first births are precisely its \(l\) distinct witnessed pairs. The children form a ring in the relation through their old parents, each with two related neighbours. The next demand is two at every child, and the required positive grant is exactly two. This reasoning repeats on each child cohort. No child ever receives zero.

## The three-knot ring

Let \(s\) be the number of live ring knots. A spent ring knot has degree at least three and an attached branch. Where the root point is missing, any live point at even physical depth in a branch could reach the rest only through this missing point. Thus none is live. The point copies of odd-depth leaves reach the root through its witness copy. Every outward subtree has a leaf, so every required odd-depth ancestor on its route is live. In particular every immediate branch neighbour is live. If such a neighbour is a nonleaf, a depth-two child must be spent, so it has at least two outward children of its own, both live. The first neighbour thus receives at least two additional partner calls through the spent child. These off-ring pairs have unique witnesses and can occur in no unheld triple.

* With no live ring knot, branches at distinct spent knots cannot join.
* With one live ring knot, the two spent knots each have a branch and its immediate live neighbour. Each costs a distinct call at the sole live ring knot. That knot can afford at most two calls, so both spent knots have exactly one branch and the live knot can have none of its own. Each branch neighbour already owes the one partner call to the live ring knot. A nonleaf neighbour would owe at least three against capacity at most two. Both branches therefore reduce to single leaves, giving five founding knots.
* With two live ring knots, the third is spent. It witnesses both live ring points and each immediate live neighbour of an attached branch. Each live ring knot thus owes its ring partner and one partner per attached branch. The funding cap forces exactly one branch at the spent holder, two openings at each live ring knot and no branches there. The branch neighbour already owes both live ring partners. It must be a leaf, giving four knots.
* With three live ring knots, their unique unheld triple costs one opening each. Any ring knot with an attached branch has degree at least three, so this triple consumes its whole supply. A live point at even depth in its branch would need a call through that root's point copy to connect to the ring. The only unheld triple is the original ring, so this would be an additional call. Every branch must instead join through a live immediate neighbour at the root's witness copy. It charges a separate pair to each of the other two ring knots. Each must then have degree two and two openings. The attached knot can have just one such branch. The immediate neighbour already owes both other ring points and must be a leaf by the depth-two argument. The source has at most four knots.

These cases exclude a funded connected live end on a three-knot ring with six or more founding knots. Combining all lengths proves the claim. The result depends on simplicity, one extra meeting, the stated supply and whole-stage funding. It derives a consequence of the two supplied growth laws.
