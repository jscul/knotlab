# A 23-knot counterexample to the executable two-flag criterion

Ten live knots supply six members of a kept block and one private knot per group cell. The four groups' subsets of the kept block are {0,1,5}, {2,3,5}, {1,2,4} and {0,3,4}. The other thirteen starting knots witness the kept block and the twelve pairs joining a private knot to one member of its group. Every starting knot has three to six places. All shared distinctions have two holders and the start is connected. Each live knot starts with exactly its demand. Both laws accept a single piece with no chain. The cell intersections have size one.

On this start the reading's size formula gives ten sets of rank zero, eight of rank one, and none of rank two. The whole finite piece has rank one by that formula. The split test is therefore false. In ordinary growth, round one makes seven knots under each law. Three have two parents and one fresh distinction, four have four parents and three fresh distinctions. The graded reading makes eight births, including one with all ten live knots as parents. Both signatures differ already at round one. Direct enumeration of actual inclusion covers gives ten sets at rank zero, seven at rank one and the whole at rank two. Three pairs of parents of the whole have no common parent. Thus the start distinguishes the implemented rank test from a test on actual inclusion covers.

[Exact starting stars, both signatures, both laws and three pairs](challenge_witness.json). The parity growth closed at round five. Not-all-equal reached the declared horizon of seven rounds.

## Coverage

The first construction examined every connected simple graph on two through seven live knots up to relabelling, as represented by the graph atlas. Every maximal clique of at least three could either be a kept block or be a group cell. Every clique of size two was a kept block. Among the 1,860 marked presentations passing the numerical cycle test, all 627 with neither flag were genuinely graded and matched the bare-core join rosters at every positive rank. Markings related by an automorphism can appear more than once. Of 166 marked presentations realized and grown under each law, one clean control agreed, 164 flagged starts differed at round one and one flagged start differed at round two. [Raw growth comparisons](challenge_atlas.jsonl) and [all static counts](challenge_atlas.time).

The second construction sampled one kept block on five through ten knots, with group cells sharing at most one knot and each containing one private knot. Its 8,000 trials included one fixed first case. The remaining cases used seed 715291. Of these, 1,813 had neither flag and genuinely graded orders and matched the bare join rosters. In 2,314 the flags were absent, the rank formula failed to grade the order, and the structural comparison raised a discrepancy. Of six selected starts grown under both laws, three clean starts disagreed in both readings at round one. The 23-knot witness was the smallest of these tested. A 30-knot witness had one set at rank two, so the executable split test was nonvacuous. [Raw growth comparisons](challenge_anchor.jsonl) and [all static counts](challenge_anchor.time).

Single-process pilots at the same settings took 0.018 CPU seconds for the first 16 accepted atlas markings and 0.014 for the first eight marked-block trials. The full searches took 1.008 and 29.074 CPU seconds inside the programs, peaking at 47,968 and 213,068 KiB. Commands from this directory use the repository interpreter with bytecode writing disabled and the arguments `challenge.py atlas 5000 7` and `challenge.py anchors 8000`. The seven-round horizon and 1,000-knot guard apply to direct growth. Other sample markups were checked as bare static rosters and were not all grown. Starts with additional loose distinctions, true grading beyond the sampled cases, and larger marked families were not exhaustively tested.
