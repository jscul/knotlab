# The double covers of S4 are #P-hard on knots

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

**Setting.**
- K is a knot with group π.
- T(K) counts the homomorphisms π → S₄ sending the meridian to a transposition.
- R(K) counts those sending it to a 3-cycle.
- G̃ is GL(2,3) or the binary octahedral group 2O. Each is a central extension 1 → Z/2 → G̃ → S₄ → 1.

**Theorem.**
- **(a)** Every homomorphism π → S₄ lifts to exactly two homomorphisms π → G̃. Hence:
  - |Hom(π, G̃)| = 2|Hom(π, S₄)|;
  - the maps sending meridians to lifts of transpositions number 2T(K). These lifts are involutions in GL(2,3) and elements of
    order 4 in 2O.
- **(b)** |Hom(π, S₄)| = 4 + R(K) + 2T(K).
- **(c)** Counting homomorphisms from knot groups is #P-hard under Turing reductions for S₄, GL(2,3) and 2O, with or without the
  meridian condition. Knot complements have H₁ = Z.

**Not established.** The analogue for closed manifolds with bounded first homology, for GL(2,3) and 2O. The identity
|Hom(π₁ S³₄(K), G̃)| = 8 + 2T(K) holds for some knots and fails for others (check part 3).

## Nearest known result

- **For S₄ itself:** `BOUNDED-H1-20261025-001A` proves (c) with the meridian condition. `CLOSED-H1-20261025-001A` replays it
  independently.
- **Livingston 1995, Theorem 9.** The 4-cycle colourings are as many as the transposition colourings, as recorded in
  `S4-LIFT-PRIOR-ART-20261025-001`. It is used for (b) and re-proved in `CLOSED-H1-20261025-001B`.
- **Kuperberg and Samperton** treat simple groups; GL(2,3) and 2O are solvable.
- **Novelty.** (a) and (b) are routine, with folklore risk high. Only (c) is a new consequence, and it rests on
  `BOUNDED-H1-20261025-001A`.

## Proof

**(a)**
- **Existence.** The obstruction to lifting ρ: π → S₄ through the central extension lies in H²(π; F₂). That group vanishes: knot
  exteriors are aspherical and have the homology of a circle.
- **The lifts.** The lifts form a torsor for H¹(π; F₂) = Hom(π, Z/2) = Z/2: ρ̃ and ρ̃·χ, where χ(μ) = −1.
- **Meridians.** Both lifts send the meridian into the preimage of its class. The preimage of the transpositions consists of
  involutions in GL(2,3) and of elements of order 4 in 2O (check part 1). ∎

**(b)** Sort by the class of ρ(μ). The meridians are conjugate and generate π.
- **Identity:** 1 map.
- **Double transpositions:** the image lies in V₄ and is abelian, so all meridians have the same image: 3 maps.
- **3-cycles:** R(K) maps.
- **Transpositions:** T(K) maps.
- **4-cycles:** T(K) maps, by Livingston's Theorem 9 summed over the S₃ colourings.

So |Hom(π, S₄)| = 4 + R(K) + 2T(K). ∎

**(c)**
- **Hardness of T.** T(K) is #P-hard to compute (`BOUNDED-H1-20261025-001A`).
- **R is easy.** R(K) counts maps into A₄ (the subgroup generated by the 3-cycles), which is abelian-by-nilpotent. So R is
  polynomial (`BOUNDED-H1-20261025-001B`).
- **The reductions.**
  - (b) gives T = (|Hom(π, S₄)| − 4 − R)/2.
  - (a) gives |Hom(π, G̃)| = 2|Hom(π, S₄)|.
  - The meridian-restricted counts are 2T. ∎

## Check

`check.py` uses the standard library only and runs in about 2 s. Its output is `result.json`.

1. **Groups.**
   - GL(2,3), and 2O generated inside SL(2, 9) by (1 + i)/√2 and (1 + i + j + k)/2 (with √2 = i in F₉), both have order 48 and
     centre {±I}.
   - Conjugation on the four Sylow 3-subgroups maps each onto S₄, with kernel the centre.
   - The lifts of transpositions have orders 2 and 4 respectively.
2. **Knots.** For 3₁, 4₁, 5₂, 6₁, 8₁₈, 8₁₉, 10₈₂, 3₁ # 3₁ and 3₁ # 4₁, by brute force over the Wirtinger presentations:
   - |Hom(π, G̃)| = 2|Hom(π, S₄)|;
   - the maps on lifts of transpositions number 2T;
   - |Hom(π, S₄)| = 4 + R + 2T.
3. **Controls and the open closed case.**
   - On S³₂(3₁) the lift counts are 62 (GL) and 50 (2O), not 2 × 58 = 116. Closed manifolds obstruct lifts.
   - On S³₄, the identity 8 + 2T holds for 3₁, 8₁₉ and 3₁ # 4₁ but fails for 3₁ # 3₁ (500 against 308).

## Scope

- **Covered.** Knot groups given by diagrams, which are compact manifolds with torus boundary and H₁ = Z.
- **Not covered.** Closed manifolds for GL(2,3) and 2O. The lifted longitude signs vary, and a proof would need their Fourier
  analysis.
- **Cited, not proved.** Asphericity of knot exteriors, and Livingston 1995 (re-proved in `CLOSED-H1-20261025-001B`).
- **No overclaim.** No claim about P versus NP.
