# Common bonds of original marks on one side

The two far original marks \(e_8,e_{12}\) have bond distance **9 at every reached age** on the one-spare five-ring attached at \(b_4\), quiet or once loaded. The same proof covers arbitrarily padded paths and sets of up to four marks. There is also a positive same-side reading. Between rings attached at \(b_4,b_8\), the three marks \(e_4,e_5,e_6\) have least common bond **size 14 or 16 after one layer**, according as the first ring is quiet or loaded, at either setting of the second ring.

Write \(e_i=b_i b_{i+1}\) for the physical shared distinction on a path \(b_0,\ldots,b_N\). The four end spares and each locally funded attachment are as in the frozen sources. All old loose values are quiet. The rule passes cold groups, vetoes hot multipart groups and uses the fewest fresh state changes. Use \(K(M)\) for the least **size** of a bond containing every mark in \(M\), infinity if none exists. Subtract one to obtain pair bond distance. In the wired parity presentation these bonds are exactly cuts with both shores connected. The shore away from the exterior contains an ordinary endpoint of every mark. A once-existing bond stays a bond on the same distinctions at all later layers by conservative extension. ## The source and the first layer

At the source a connected ordinary shore has a single interval of path holders. Excursions to a ring return only at its attachment. Thus three or more distinct path marks have no common bond. Two always do. A single extra layer already gives every same-side set a finite common bond on a singly attached path, even if every mark on that side is prescribed.

The following exact first-layer calculation allows an arbitrary marked set. Remove the young knots first. With old holders \(S\) inside, their cost is the old shared boundary plus their original opening counts \(o_v\). The only children needed inside are binary children with both parents in \(S\), at extra cost \(f-2\) for \(f\) fresh distinctions. Children hitting only one inside old holder can be removed as leaves. All relevant binary children have \(f\ge2\). The exterior side is connected because every outside old holder has an outside child with fresh access to the exterior. Eliminating an attached ring is independent of the number of path marks. When its attachment is inside, it already connects its retained path neighbors. When it is outside, the excursion can only connect those two neighbors, and the direct child between them is affordable from the openings saved on removed ring holders. For a doubled physical ring, any such excursion uses a four-open holder and the direct child costs at most four. These reductions were proved from the actual roster. [First-layer cut reduction and doubled-ring costs](../OLD-MARK-GEOMETRY-20261119-001/distances_proof.md) · [Arbitrary padding](../OLD-MARK-GEOMETRY-20261119-001/padded_path.md)

The retained path holders have a span \([L,R]\). Its full cost is

\[
 {\bf1}_{L>0}+{\bf1}_{R<N}+\sum_{v=L}^R w_v,\qquad
 w_v=o_v+2{\bf1}_{v=s}
\]

for a simple single ring at \(s\). The doubled ring uses \(o_s+4\). Omitted internal holders must be isolated, since the only longer path connection available goes from \(g-1\) to \(g+1\). Omitting \(g\) adds \(f_g-w_g\), or is impossible when that child is vetoed. Prescribed marks must each have exactly one retained holder. For a simple single ring the penalties are as follows.

| Omitted holder | Quiet | Fully loaded |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(s\) | −2 | 0 |
| \(s\pm1\) | 0 | Unavailable |
| \(s\pm2\) | 2 | 2 |
| All other internal holders | 0 | 0 |

Indeed a child through an ordinary two-neighbor witness takes \(o_{g-1}+o_{g+1}-2\) fresh distinctions. At \(s\), the two four-open path parents have two additional paired siblings in the quiet ring, reducing six fresh distinctions to four. Loading removes both reductions. For a partially active doubled ring only the potential saving at \(s\) and the availability at its two neighbors can differ between preparations. At \(s\) the saving is at most four.

**All arities on one side of a single ring agree after one layer.** Consider the right side and put \(i=\min M\), \(j=\max M\), referring to indices. First trim the span up to its first holder of \(e_i\) and beyond its last holder of \(e_j\). Only extraneous initial or terminal pieces are removed. No necessary internal connection or marked crossing disappears. The contribution from removing any retained holder is favorable. In the span formula the only negative omission that could disappear is at \(s\); extending leftward to make \(s\) an internal omission had already added a four-open flank on its far side. This pays even the doubled-ring saving of four. Reaching a physical end costs at least two openings for the one boundary crossing lost. Thus a minimum exists with endpoints chosen among the extreme marks' holders.

For a single mark, trimming leaves its one chosen inside holder, so the minimum is the size of the smaller incident star. Now take at least two marks. If \(i\ge s+1\), the shortened span has neither \(s\) nor \(s+1\) as an internal omission. All its costs agree between preparations. If \(i=s\), consider the two possible roles of \(s\). Starting at \(s+1\), retain every other holder through the last mark, choosing the appropriate endpoint of that mark. This crosses every prescribed mark and uses only available bridges. Compared with the unbroken interval \([s+1,j]\), it adds at most two for the omission at \(s+2\) and two for a possible extra far endpoint. Starting instead at \(s\) adds its full weight of at least six to that same interval. Every further possible omission there has nonnegative cost, and extending the far end cannot improve the cost. For the two nearest adjacent marks the single holder \(s+1\) suffices. Hence a minimum can start at \(s+1\), where the costs agree. Reflection gives the left side. This covers all allowed shared-only loads on the singly attached doubled ring as well. It permits the far endpoint of an optimal alternating shore to lie outside \([i+1,j]\).

**Two nearby attachments.** For seams four apart take \(s,s+1,s+2\) as the marked indices. The old endpoints force one of two alternations. Keeping \(b_s,b_{s+2}\) puts \(b_{s+1},b_{s+3}\) outside. Its bridge through \(s+1\) exists exactly when the first ring is quiet. Its cost including the old boundary is \(2+(6+4+2)+0=14\). Keeping \(b_{s+1},b_{s+3}\) puts \(b_s,b_{s+2}\) outside. Its bridge goes through \(s+2\) between two four-open parents, so it is always available with six fresh distinctions. The cost is \(2+(4+2+4)+(6-2)=16\). In either case extending the span cannot improve it. Every newly accessible saving at a quiet attachment is at most two and requires a further four-open flank. Ring excursions can be removed separately since their three-holder attachment neighborhoods are disjoint. These two orientations exhaust the possibilities. The answer also holds under arbitrary external padding, with four old path steps separating the attachments, and is independent of the second load. All marks lie between the attachments, with nearest-holder offsets zero and one from them respectively. The unrestricted least bond uses a global minimization.

## Exact exterior readings at all later ages

Take the marks to the right of the rightmost attachment \(\sigma\). Reflection gives the exterior on the left. The proof applies to one ring with arbitrary padding, the registered doubles with even separation at least four and either independent full load, and the single doubled-meeting source with any of its allowed activity masks. For distinct marks \(M\) of size two, three or four write \(i=\min M,\ j=\max M,\ L=j-i+1\) in mark indices. Suppose \(i\ge\sigma+4\). At **every age at least one**, the exact common bond size is

| Original marks | \(K(M)\) |
|---|---:|
| Three consecutive marks, or four with offsets \(0,1,3,4\) from \(i\) | \(2L+2\), or \(2L+1\) when \(j=N-1\) |
| All other sets of two to four marks | \(2L\) |

The one-unit end correction on the reflected side occurs when \(i=0\). The formula requires no extra distance from that physical end. For **pairs** alone, \(i\ge\sigma+3\) already suffices. With \(i=8,j=12,\sigma=4\) it gives \(K=10\), as claimed. Between two rings the next proof does not automatically supply an exterior tail. The preceding 14/16 example shows why that distinction matters.

### A representation of the actual births

The established cluster description of complete cold growth uses the original live knots as points. Neighbor sets of old witnesses give blocks of mutually adjacent points. An unused opening gives a pendant point, with an endless continuation when its holder had two opens. Growth at age \(t\) consists of connected clusters of \(t+1\) points in the unrolled point–block presentation, up to its covering symmetries. A cluster has to contain an original point. Deleting one point while retaining connectedness and an original point gives precisely a parent. An admissible one-point extension gives a child and its physical parent distinction. End spares ensure continuing full layers. These are proof coordinates for actual knots. [Identification, Theorem 6](../../proofs/DOUBLE-IDENT-20261119-001A/DOUBLE-IDENT-20261119-001A-P001_doubles/proof.md) · [Identification of the frozen path and ring arms](../EDDY-WORLD-20261119-001/theory_fate.md)

Here is why that description also applies to the loaded histories. At the source all multipart groups are pairs. A surviving pair has two cold parents which belonged to a common old witness block. Restrict each block to cold members and discard sets of size below two. Keep every original live point, even a hot one with only unary children, and keep its original number of openings. This gives the actual first groups and unary completions. For a literal equivalent cold start, make a separate spent witness for every retained block, connected to precisely its live members. Give each live point one further private spent neighbor. Connect all spent witnesses together so that they are connected and have at least three incident distinctions, and preserve the stated openings at each live point. The private witnesses add no live pair. The ordinary presentation and the complement of each single knot are connected after wiring the loose distinctions, so its stars are genuine bonds. No pair has two live-pair witnesses and no new unheld larger first group is possible: restricting a block to cold members preserves the common witness for any triangle of its surviving pairs. All first groups are funded and the sources have more than one loose distinction. Thus the cold identification applies. The first child's fresh count reads only the actual roster, which agrees with the loaded history. All future parents of a live knot and all subsequent fresh counts agree inductively, since the original spent witnesses never meet a new child. Old-to-old meetings in the scored graph are always those of the **original** source. A retained wired end is available at every finite age, in particular along the two-open end spares. ### Integer levels and disjoint crossing certificates

Put \(B=\sigma+2\). Assign a coordinate \(m=\max(k,B)\) to the original point \(b_k\) and \(B\) to other original points. Beyond \(b_N,b_{N-1}\), give the two outward virtual chains their respective endpoint coordinate plus \(2,4,6,\ldots\) in order, beginning with the pendant. Give every other pendant, continuation or relay coordinate \(B\). These assignments lift to every copy. Adjacent map points differ by at most two. Above \(B\), each component is a single right tail, with coordinates increasing by two and a single inward entrance to a point of coordinate at most \(B\). This follows from the ordinary two-step path blocks and the two end spares. No source veto changes any block strictly within either tail.

Each such tail also has an infinite inward continuation that stays at coordinates at most \(B\) once it reaches the core. To check this in a single source, when \(\sigma\) is cold the path parity through it runs to its left end and its endless spare. When \(\sigma\) is hot, \(b_{\sigma+2}\) loses its unique pair with \(\sigma\); this two-open parent then has a spare with its own endless continuation. The other parity crosses via the cold pair \(b_{\sigma-1},b_{\sigma+1}\) and runs to its left end. These arguments work for partial activation on the doubled physical ring. On two-ring sources with the last seam quiet, its intact five-ring lifts to an endless inward path accessible to either right tail. With the last seam loaded, the other parity avoids both attachment knots because the separation is even. The spare at \(b_{\sigma+2}\) still exists. Consequently a finite cluster entering a tail can always be extended without increasing its maximum coordinate: extend toward the entrance if wholly inside the tail, or take the first unused point of an inward continuation if the cluster already contains the entrance.

For a cluster \(C\) put \(h(C)=\max_{x\in C}m(x)-t\) at age \(t\). At old knots use the coordinate of the one-point cluster. This is independent of lifts. Every parent–child meeting changes \(h\) by at most one, because a single adjacent point is added. Each original meeting also changes \(h\) by at most one.

Fix \(\sigma+3\le k\le N-1\). Let \(W_k\) consist of the distinctions between heights \(k\) and \(k+1\), including old \(e_k\). For an opening at the current top, use the height of the actual child in a fixed next complete layer to assign its distinction to \(W_k\), then end its route at the current exterior. Different \(W_k\) use disjoint physical distinctions. Each ordinary knot of height \(k\) or \(k+1\) has **two distinction-disjoint routes to that exterior in \(W_k\)**.

The details of this last assertion are essential. Write \(M_*=h+t\) for the maximum coordinate. At these heights the maximizing point belongs to a unique lifted right tail. Two distinct tails attaining at least \(M_*\) and \(M_*-1\) would require a path inside the cluster through coordinate at most \(B\), of length at least

\[
 \left\lceil (M_*-B)/2\right\rceil+
 \left\lceil (M_*-1-B)/2\right\rceil=M_*-B>t .
\]

A connected cluster of \(t+1\) points cannot contain such a path. The outermost point on a single tail is unique. Extending it outward increases the maximum by two. A different extension which keeps the maximum exists by the inward continuation above. Thus any knot of lower height \(k\) can ascend one age to higher height \(k+1\), and any one of higher height can ascend to lower height.

At positive age the reverse continuations also exist. At higher height \(k+1\), the maximizing point has coordinate at least \(B+3\); it is a leaf, and its inward neighbor in the cluster has coordinate exactly two less. Removing it leaves a cluster containing an original point. If the cluster had only one original point and used this high tail, all its nonoriginal points would have to come from the outward end spare of that original point; in that case the maximum itself is nonoriginal. At lower height \(k\), remove a leaf other than the maximizing point from a spanning tree rooted there. If there are two original points this retains one. With only one original point in a cluster reaching above \(B\) at positive age, such a cluster lies on an outward end spare starting at \(q=N-1\) or \(N\), giving \(h=q+t\ge N\). That excludes lower height \(k\). These removals are actual parent steps.

It follows that a knot in \(W_k\) can ascend to the current exterior, or descend to its source in \(W_k\), cross \(e_k\), then ascend from the other source endpoint. The first path rises immediately in age, while the descent falls. All birth steps within \(W_k\) preserve the parity of \(t+h\); the old \(e_k\) switches it. The second ascent therefore uses the opposite parity from the first path. This proves distinction-disjointness, including the last physical openings. At the physical end, knots of height \(N\) still have **one** ascending route within \(W_N\), by the same outward-then-inward extension rule. No old mark \(e_N\) is needed for that assertion.

**Replay from a physical roster.** The maximum coordinate needs no covering-space data to compute. Initialize it at the original knots as above. For a child with two or more parents use the maximum of the parent maxima: two distinct one-point deletions already cover the child cluster. For a unary child use its parent's maximum, adding two precisely if it continues the all-unary chain of an original \(b_{N-1}\) or \(b_N\). Those are the only single-parent additions that introduce a larger coordinate. Subtract the age to get \(h\). For each \(k\) check that the only original meeting crossing its level is \(e_k\), each later level knot has a crossing parent and each knot below the last retained age has a crossing child. For \(k=N\) check only the crossing child. The quiet and loaded one-spare starts, all four loadings of the four-separated double, and doubled-meeting inputs w00, w01, w03, w32 were checked this way through six completed layers. All 26,051 literal birth links had the required height increment, including a unit increment on links with both ends above \(B\). There were 1,064 crossing-parent, 956 crossing-child and 110 physical-end continuation checks. The proof of all later ages uses the two continuations, independently of this finite check. ### Lower bound, attainment and the two exceptional patterns

In a bond through the two extreme marks the side away from the exterior contains a holder of each. Its connectedness and the height increment bound force it to meet every \(W_k\) for \(i\le k\le j\). For intermediate levels a connecting path between the two inside holders passes from height at most \(k\) to height at least \(k+1\). At the extreme levels a marked endpoint itself suffices. The two disjoint continuing routes at each level force at least \(2L\) crossings. If this shore also contains a knot at height \(i\) or \(j+1\), apply the same argument at \(W_{i-1}\) or \(W_{j+1}\) for two additional crossings. At \(j+1=N\) one additional crossing is guaranteed. This is where the extra left margin in the higher-arity theorem is used.

An age-one shore attains \(2L\) whenever there is a zero-one assignment to \(b_i,\ldots,b_{j+1}\) starting and ending at zero, alternating on prescribed marks, and with no consecutive zeros. Keep the one-valued old holders, using the intervening plain binary children to bridge the isolated zeros between them. The span is \([i+1,j]\); each internal omission costs zero in this plain portion. Both shores are connected by the first-layer construction. If \(L\) is even, alternate throughout. If \(L\) is odd, start by alternating and insert a single \(11\) at any unmarked transition an odd offset from \(i\), then resume alternating. This again ends at zero. With at most four marks, the only cases left are three consecutive marks or four at offsets \(0,1,3,4\): an odd-length span with both extremes and all odd internal transitions marked has at least \((L-1)/2+2\) marks. A single mark is outside the stated theorem.

For three consecutive marks their old outer holders must have opposite shore membership. The inside outer holder forces the additional crossings just proved. For the exceptional four, either an outer holder is inside or the inside old holders in the strip are exactly \(b_{i+1},b_{i+4}\). These two have opposite \(t+h\) parity. If a connecting path stayed in the height band \([i+1,j]\), every birth step on it would change \(h\) by exactly one. Indeed an added point that changes the maximum by one at these heights would have to come from a distinct competing high tail (excluded by the same path-length bound), or lie at the entrance near \(B\) (below the band). Changes of zero or two in the maximum give a unit change in height. Hence a parity switch requires an original meeting with both holders inside. No such old meeting exists in this band for the forced pattern. The path must reach height \(i\) or \(j+1\), giving the same extra crossing bound. In both exceptional patterns the bound is attained already at age one: alternate starting at \(b_i=0\), ending with \(b_{j+1}=1\). The span adds that one plain holder, at cost two, or at cost one when it is the physical end. Conservativity carries every attaining bond to each later age. This completes the proof.

### An adjacent pair even at the attachment

For a single quiet or fully loaded ring, **every** adjacent pair of original path marks on one side keeps its source bond distance forever. If the shared holder \(b_p\) is adjacent to the attachment this distance is five. At any other such holder it is three. The source star of \(b_p\) gives the upper bound.

Here are disjoint-route lower certificates for the right side. Along either parity of the right path, an interval of map points can grow indefinitely to the right via its end spare, keeping its leftmost anchor fixed. It can grow to the left along that same path parity as well. If a loaded attachment cuts off this direction at \(b_{s+2}\), its new spare gives the required inward continuation. For \(p\ge s+2\), if \(b_p\) is inside, send two routes from it along intervals growing in opposite directions. If \(b_p\) is outside, send one rightward-growing interval from each of \(b_{p-1},b_{p+1}\). The distinct fixed starting points distinguish those routes. Together with the two old marked crossings this proves the four-cut bound.

At \(p=s+1\), when \(b_p\) is inside, four routes start on its four openings. Two follow the path in opposite directions. Under quiet growth the other two begin with the distinct pairs of \(p\) with \(c_1,c_4\). Extend them along the right path from \(p\), retaining their respective ring member. Under load they begin with the two separate unary children of \(p\); retain their distinct pendant points while extending along the right path. These four routes share no physical distinction. In the opposite orientation send the two interval routes from \(b_{s+2}\) and send two from \(s\) through its unmarked old meetings with \(c_1,c_4\). Under load these ring holders each have their own disjoint unary-descended part reaching the exterior. Under quiet growth continue from \(c_1,c_4\) into the respective pure-ring children on \(\{c_1,c_3\}\), \(\{c_4,c_2\}\), then extend both intervals in one fixed direction on the unrolled ring. Their fixed ends are distinct modulo five at every age. These routes do not use the interval routes through \(b_{s+2}\), which retain that background ancestor. This gives four additional crossings in either orientation, hence the six-cut bound. Reflection completes the claim.
