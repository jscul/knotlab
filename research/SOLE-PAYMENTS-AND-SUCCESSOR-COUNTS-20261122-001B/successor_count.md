# Does the next knot carry the signal count?

**Under either law, the next knot can hold exactly the same four marks and values after zero signals or one.** This happens on the same fully quiet founding word, at the same age, with the same receiver and both of its real payers. It also holds for arbitrarily many different totals if the earlier signals are far enough back in the retained payments. Follow the first two returns of the marked five-founder receiver. At the first, knot 29 receives two zeros on the quiet history and receives 01 on the other. The two histories can agree on every newly written value from the next cut onward. At the second return, knot 50 receives 11 and writes 00 in both. It gives its actual zero-valued mark 108 to its next enrolled child, knot 55. Another payer also gives 55 a zero, and 55 writes 11. The companion zero from 50 goes to knot 56. Even pointing out these identities, the number of returns and all four values at 50 and 55 gives the same local evidence for both earlier totals. The differing old receipts remain available through the older holdings. [Named payments](verification.json)

**Parity preserves the latest signal in the two fresh values together.** If the incoming pair disagrees, the fresh pair must disagree as well. Either particular mark can still be the one that changes. In the first signal history, mark 66 pays another child and mark 67 pays enrolled child 34. Knot 29 can lawfully put the changed value on 66, leaving 34 exactly its quiet word 1100. Putting it on 67 instead makes 34 receive 10. Under not-all-equal the same two choices are lawful and 29 can also keep both fresh values unchanged. A pair of equal fresh values only tells us that at least one incoming value differs from them. There is a physical way to recover that most recent signal. Starting at 34, visit its known continuing parent 29. All of 29's marks now have two holders. One more step distinguishes 29's two old payers, who have spent every loose mark, from its two newborn children, who each hold two loose marks. Reading the pair paid by the old payers recovers that one signal. Two steps suffice on every such payment and a matched comparison shows that one can fail under either law. Giving the reader a pointer to the original pair makes one step sufficient. Each read of that pair recovers the same event.

For any fixed access limit and any number of earlier returns, the two laws each permit signals on any chosen subset of those returns and allow later fresh marks to agree completely. An older receipt keeps its actual values at its original holders. A sufficiently late receiver and its actual next child then have the same accessible holdings for all those totals. Recounting through the whole retained presentation recovers every signal. A supplied writer can also carry the count *modulo two* on a known next payment by copying its continuing parent's input between returns and its other input on a return. This requires the parent roles, phase, outgoing payment and the added filling choice. Ordinary growth allows either value on that same payment. All comparisons use lawful histories without odds.
