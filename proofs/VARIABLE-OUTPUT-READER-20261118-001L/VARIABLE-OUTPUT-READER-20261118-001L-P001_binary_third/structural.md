# Recovering all equations through three shrinking joins

Write \(B\) for the binary three-output site, \(T\) for the seed, and \(t\) for the number of three-parent, two-output sites. Write \(S_v\) for the star of an ordinary site \(v\). The ordinary side of a cut excludes the exterior. The width of a source with \(n\) ordinary sites is \(2n+4\), and its whole ordinary side has \(8-t\) boundary bits. Use \(K=D+1\) for the smallest size of a bond on two named bits. The theorem below uses every pair reading through \(K=5\) and permits every reachable mixed rival under the supplied identification of names.

## The small shores

In a shore with at least three sites, the first three selected sites in birth order have boundary at least seven. They have total arity at least eleven and at most two internal meetings. Each subsequent selected site with \(p\) parents, \(h\) outputs and \(j\) selected parents changes the boundary by \(p+h-2j\). Only a shrinking site can save one, and then all its parents are selected. Thus when \(t\le1\) every such shore has boundary at least six. The existing transfer theorem forces all site equations through size five. [Five-bit transfer](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J-P001_seven_to_six/inverse.md#five-bit-equations-at-the-first-shrink)

Whenever the source has at least five loose bits, no four-bit boundary can enclose three or more sites. The ordinary side of any such boundary can be lawfully built first, giving four loose bits. If this prefix omits \(B\) it belongs to the minimum-width branch, whose loose count never falls below five. If it includes \(B\), all subsequent additions produce two and take at least two, so the loose count cannot return to five. Thus at \(t\le3\) the source has only the physical seed and four-place stars through size four. The short-equation theorem makes these same words the rival's full family through four. For \(t\le3\), every five-bit boundary on three or more sites is parent-closed and contains the seed and its first two unary additions. Indeed the latest three selected sites give at least four outgoing crossings, so at most one input enters. If the seed is absent, the first selected site must be unary and use that sole entering bit. A second selected site would have to be its unary child, and these adjacent sites cannot be the parents of any third site. Suppose the seed is present and precisely one input enters. Then all four outgoing crossings leave the latest three sites. The shore contains at least five sites, since reducing seven to five requires two subsequent shrinking sites. The seed is earlier than the last three, so all its children are selected. This includes both initial unary sites when they are siblings. In the chain case the first unary site is also earlier than the last three and includes its unary child. The net number of outputs minus inputs for this selected set is therefore \(7+\mathbf 1_{B\text{ selected}}-t_{\rm selected}\ge4\). Four outgoing and one incoming would give three. This excludes any entering input. A parent-closed set of at least three sites is itself a lawful prefix, and its first two additions must be the two unary sites. The prefix and later replay preserve the pair witnesses by taking the earliest common neighbor of a pair, as in the four-shore reordering proof.

Call a five-boundary on at least three sites a large five-boundary. When \(t=3\), these boundaries are exactly the minimum cuts with the first three sites pinned inside and the exterior outside. The minimum is five because of the preceding lower bound and the five loose bits of the whole source. These minimum shores are closed under union and intersection by cut submodularity. All their boundary bits point outwards in birth order.

## One protected bit forces a five-place site

Suppose a bit \(e\) at a five-place site \(v\) lies on no large five-boundary. Suppose \(e\) is loose or its other holder \(a\) has a previously forced four- or five-bit equation. Then the five-bit equation \(S_v\) is forced. A distinct bit \(f\in S_v\) has \(K(e,f)=5\), since all through-four words are physical smaller stars and no two ordinary sites meet twice. If \(e\) is loose, its row through five is contained in \(S_v\), which immediately forces the star.

Otherwise a source bond through \(e\) of size at most five comes from a single site or the sum of the seed and an adjacent four-place site. The whole row through \(e\) lies in \(S_v\cup S_a\), with possibly the additional seed bits when \(a\) is four-place and meets the seed. A source five-bit equation through \(e,f\) can only be \(S_v\). In the exceptional seed-plus-four case \(v\) and the seed have the same ordinary color, so \(f\) meets neither the seed nor \(a\).

Consider any rival five-bit word \(W\) through \(e,f\) outside the source space. Every pair in it has reading at most five because a five-word is a bond. Two outsiders from \(S_a\setminus S_v\) would give \(|W\cap S_a|\ge3\). Adding the forced \(S_a\) would leave a common word through four, making \(W\) source-true. In the exceptional case two outsiders on the seed give the same contradiction by adding the seed. For an outsider on each of \(S_a\) and the seed, either the common meeting belongs to \(W\) and one of those previous tests applies, or \(W\) meets the forced five-word \(S_a\mathbin\triangle S_T\) in both outsiders and \(e\). Its sum again has size at most four. Thus any rival word outside the source space can only replace one bit of \(S_v\) by one outsider. Two distinct replacements of this kind have difference of size two, which is impossible, or size four with two bits of \(S_v\), whereas no true four-place star can contain that pair. Consequently at most one such rival word exists through \(e\). The four pair minima \(K(e,f)=5\) must cover every other bit of \(S_v\), which forces \(S_v\) itself.

## Two shrinking sites

Every large five-boundary must include both shrinking sites and all their parents. The initial three selected sites must have boundary seven. The site \(B\) cannot be among those three, which have arities \(3,4,4\). If selected later it would add at least one. Thus every such shore excludes \(B\). Both shrinking sites have all their inputs protected. If large five-boundaries exist, they are the minimum shores pinning the seed and both shrinking sites inside and \(B\) outside. A union of any two remains a minimum shore. In fact parent-closure gives the stronger fact that no output of \(B\) can appear on any of them. If an output crossed with \(B\) outside, it would enter the shore. All three outputs of \(B\) are therefore protected.

There is also no dependency cycle in forcing the three five-place stars. If such a shore exists, build it first. Its two shrinking sites have protected inputs, at least two held away from the seed. Force them in birth order by the protected-bit lemma, since their older nonseed holders are four-place or an already forced shrinking site. Every possible opposite holder of a protected output of \(B\) now has a forced four- or five-bit star, or the output is loose. This forces \(B\). When no large five-boundary exists the five-bit transfer theorem applies directly.

## Three shrinking sites

Let \(\mathcal M\) be the pinned minimum shores above. If every member contains \(B\), each contains all three shrinking sites, since its net frontier has size \(7+1-3=5\). All five-place sites then have every input protected. Each has at least one nonseed input holder. Force the sites chronologically.

Otherwise let \(U_-\) be the union of all members excluding \(B\). It is itself a member. As a lawful minimum-width prefix with five loose bits it contains exactly two shrinking sites. Every other member omitting \(B\) has these same two. Every member containing \(B\) contains all three. Write \(C\) for the remaining shrinking site. The first two shrinking sites are pinned inside every minimum shore and have every input protected. Build \(U_-\) first and force both of their stars in birth order. All four-place stars were already forced. The two sites \(B,C\) have the same membership throughout \(\mathcal M\), and \(B\) occurs earlier than \(C\) in the reordered history. Otherwise a lawful minimum-width prefix would have three shrinking sites. Reordering a parent-closed prefix preserves the direction of every ordinary meeting.

At least one input of \(C\) is protected. Otherwise union three minimum shores exposing its three respective inputs. They all exclude \(C\), and the union includes all three input holders. Adding \(C\) to that shore reduces the cut by at least one, resulting in a forbidden boundary through four on at least three sites. If a protected input has the seed as its old holder, the seed is pinned, so \(C\) too must be pinned and every input of \(C\) is protected. Moreover at least one output of \(B\) must be protected. If all three appeared on minimum boundaries, intersect three corresponding shores. The intersection contains \(B\), all three shrinking sites and none of the children on its three outputs. A loose output needs no child exclusion. Removing the childless \(B\) produces a lawful parent-closed prefix with three shrinking joins and no third three-output addition, contradicting the minimum-width closure.

Thus a protected nonseed input for \(C\) forces it immediately unless its holder is \(B\). A protected output at \(B\) likewise forces \(B\) immediately unless its other holder is \(C\). Once either is forced, the other's protected bit suffices. An additional protected input at \(B\) also settles both, since its older holder cannot be \(C\). A protected seed-held input would make all inputs at \(B\) protected and its other holder differs from the seed.

It remains that the protected bit is the meeting \(e\) from \(B\) to \(C\), both inputs to \(B\) occur on minimum boundaries, and both other inputs to \(C\) also occur on minimum boundaries. All four named input bits lie on the boundary of \(U_-\). Indeed their witnessing shores exclude the respective site, hence exclude both \(B\) and \(C\). Their old holders remain inside their union. Denote the fifth bit of \(\partial U_-\) by \(z\). This whole five-bit equation is already forced, as the sum of the previously forced sites of that prefix.

A rival five-word through \(e\) must be supported inside \(S_B\cup S_C\), since \(e\) is protected and neither endpoint can participate in a seed-plus-four sum. Outside \(e\), each of these two stars has two input bits and two output bits. An input on one star and an output on the other cannot occur together in any source bond through five. A minimum shore contains \(B,C\) together or excludes them together, so it cannot expose the input and output together. Their other ordinary endpoints have opposite colors and hence cannot coincide. A seed-plus-four sum would require the old holder of the input to be the seed and the later child on the output to meet it. The seed has at most one unused output before \(B\), since the two unary sites already take two seed outputs if they are siblings, and in the chain case the fourth site completes the first four-cycle using another seed output. The reordered prefix \(U_-\) contains both shrinking sites and this first four-cycle. If the seed supplies the named input to \(B\) or \(C\), it has no further output for any child of either. No one- or two-site bond can therefore witness such a cross pair.

It follows that the only possible five-words through \(e\) are the two genuine stars, \(e\) with the four external inputs, and \(e\) with the four external outputs. The all-input word differs from the forced \(\partial U_-\) by only \(\{e,z\}\), so it is impossible. The all-output word cannot coexist with either genuine star. Their difference would be a four-bit equation having two bits of a genuine five-site and thus cannot be a common four-place star. The observations between \(e\) and all eight neighboring bits have value five. To supply the four input readings the rival must use both genuine stars.

The source's \(n\) independent site equations are now present for every \(t\le3\). A reachable rival with \(r\) sites has width \(2r+1+m\), where \(m\) is its number of nonseed three-output additions. Its unique ternary star forces the first two additions to be unary with three outputs. The width makes \(m\) odd, hence at least three. Thus \(r\le n\), and the forced equations give \(r\ge n\). The two equation spaces coincide, so every marked triple has the same verdict, including comparisons to rivals with other arities or a different chronology. ## The positive prefix before the fourth shrink

For the fourth shrink use the short-shore characterization in the main proof. Write \(P\) for the unique large five-shore, \(X=\partial P\) for its five bits, and \(C\) for the shrinking site that produces the least large four-shore \(W_0=P\cup\{C\}\). The other large four-shores, if present, are obtained by successive binary additions to \(W_0\). In particular none of these cuts, nor \(X\), contains any bit with both ordinary holders in \(P\). The prefix \(P\) has an eligible triple and five loose bits. Its last site is the third shrinking site, since the unique positive five-loose record has no incoming binary transition and \(B\) precedes all shrinking sites on a history reaching four. The last site owns the unique pair of loose bits at one holder. [Four- and five-shore characterization](proof.md) · [Positive prefix and its predecessors](frontier.md)

Every actual junction equation of \(P\) transfers to an unrestricted rival. At each of its two-parent four-place sites both inputs are internal to \(P\). At each initial four-place unary site its input and at least one output in the initial four-cycle are internal. For a pair of such internal bits the four-neighborhoods only see ordinary stars, and no bit outside the target star can meet both distinct same-color opposite holders. The common minimum is four. This forces every four-place site of \(P\), in addition to the seed already recognized. At each of the first three shrinking sites, choose two of the three internal inputs as follows. If the seed is a parent choose the other two, which have its color. Otherwise the seed cannot meet all three parents. Choose one parent avoiding the seed and one other. Apart from the target star the short row of either internal input can only reach its old holder, or the seed when that old holder is a four-place neighbor of the seed. No outside bit fits both rows. The internal pair has minimum five and forces that shrinking star. The same argument works for two internal outputs of \(B\). It has at most one loose output in \(P\), since the third shrinking site owns its only double loose holder. Its other two outputs go to different nonseed children, of which at most one can meet the seed. Indeed the seed used at least two of its three outputs by the birth of \(B\), by the initial four-cycle. This forces all site equations of \(P\), and hence their sum \(X\).

The fourth shrinking site \(C\) is itself forced at every suffix length. Write \(I\subset X\) for its three inputs and \(O\) for its two outputs. Choose an input \(e\in I\) whose old holder \(a\) differs from and avoids the seed. If the seed is a parent, the other two have its color. Otherwise the seed cannot meet all three. For either \(f\in O\) the input-output theorem gives \(K(e,f)=5\). Outside \(X\cup S_C\), the row of \(e\) through five reaches only bits at \(a\). Indeed the only large five-word is \(X\), no large four-word contains the already-consumed \(e\), and \(a\) avoids the seed. Any such outside bit \(x\) has both holders in \(P\), since its only loose bits there are \(X\). No large short boundary can contain \(x\). Neither holder of \(x\) is \(C\) or a later child on \(f\). A seed-plus-four sum containing \(f\) could only use its later child and the seed, while \(x\) is neither at that child nor at the seed. Hence \(x\) cannot occur with \(f\) through five. Any rival five-word attaining this pair lies in \(X\cup O\). It cannot use four members of \(X\), since adding the forced \(X\) would give a two-bit word. It must use both outputs and three members of \(X\). Its sum with \(X\) is then a four-word containing both outputs and the other two members of \(X\). The latter cannot be inputs of \(C\), as each input reads five to either output. The rival five-word is precisely \(I\cup O=S_C\). This also forces the first four-bit boundary \(\partial W_0=X\mathbin\triangle S_C\). ## Physical sites in every matched presentation with a legal triple

For \(t\le3\) the spaces agree by the preceding proof. The ordinary source graph begins with a four-cycle and each further site has at least two distinct earlier neighbors. Thus it remains connected after any one ordinary site is removed. The wired graph stays connected after any two vertices are removed, by this observation when one is the exterior and by the two-ordinary-site deletion theorem in the other case. In the source, each augmented vertex star is therefore a bond whose deletion leaves all surviving bits connected by surviving cycles. In any graph representation a bond with two vertices on each of its two connected sides leaves an edge on each side and no cycle linking them. Thus each source augmented star must be a rival augmented star. Equal rank gives the same vertex count and consequently the same complete augmented collection. [Recognizing augmented stars](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J-P001_seven_to_six/family.md) · [Two ordinary deletions](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001D-P001_separator_inverse/inverse.md#two-elementary-source-restrictions)

If the two presentations select different exteriors, a bit is loose in both exactly when it joins those two candidate vertices. All these common loose bits therefore have the same ordinary holder in each presentation. A transported legal triple would require three different holders. Equal allowed states transfer every legal triple, so a source with a legal triple has the same exterior and exactly the same physical junction supports in every matched rival. A four-shrink source has only four loose bits and an empty menu. The main proof rules out a positive unrestricted rival there as well.
