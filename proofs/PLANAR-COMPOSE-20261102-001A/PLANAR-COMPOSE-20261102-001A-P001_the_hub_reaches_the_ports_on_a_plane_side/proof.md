# The hub reaches the ports on a plane side

Status: Proved (items 1, 2 and 4 by proof; items 3 and 5 by exhaustive computation; three open steps stated)

Ticket `PLANAR-COMPOSE-20261102-001A`, 2026-11-02.

## Question

- **From HANDLE-EMERGENCE.**
  - **Shape of new knots.** Every new knot at an opened cut was a three-point knot {h, a, c}: a hub point, a near point and a far point.
  - **How they arise.** The hub and the near point pin the gluing line's pattern, and the far point separates it.
  - **Where they arise.** Plane removed sides never produced one.
- **The question.** Does a plane side always offer a route through a single port, so that such knots are generated?

## Dependencies

- **`HANDLE-EMERGENCE-20261102-001`.**
  - A: the planar spaces.
  - B: pin and separate, Lemmas 1–2.
  - C: the skip model.
- **`SPLICE-TREE-20261102-001C`.** `elim_lib.py`.

## Setting

- **The cut.** A knot space is glued along a simple 3-edge cut with ports X, and a near line M is removed. The interface I is
  assumed to have six patterns: all but an all-equal pair {σ, σ̄}.
- **The near side.** Its solutions are then exactly those of the near contraction without M. This holds because I ⊆ J, the
  far side's admissible patterns.
- **Odd port.** A pair {t, t̄} ⊂ I has one odd port y₁, where t departs from σ or σ̄. The other two ports are its non-odd ports.
- **Pinning.** A pinning is a value (v, w) of the hub h and a near point a with T(h=v, a=w) = {t}.
- **Separating.** A far point c separates t when one of its values excludes t.

## 1. The hub's tie class on a plane side (proved)

**On a plane near side with Kasteleyn signs, every point on the boundary of one of M's three faces is tied to the hub.**

*Proof.*
- **Matchings.** By the skip model (HANDLE-EMERGENCE C), the solutions up to complement are the perfect matchings of the line–face
  incidence graph B_M: lines other than M against faces not touching M.
- **Cycles in the plane.** Two matchings differ by disjoint alternating cycles. Draw each line at its vertex and each face at an
  interior point, and each edge L–f inside f. Each cycle is then a simple closed curve in the plane through line vertices and face
  interiors.
- **What a cycle flips.** Where a cycle passes through a line L, it changes L's matched corner from c_a to c_b, so L's odd point moves
  from the incidence opposite c_a to the one opposite c_b. The incidence r between c_a and c_b therefore flips relative to L's other two
  points. This is exactly the incidence the curve crosses at L. So the two solutions differ by flipping exactly the points the cycles
  enclose.
- **Points on an M-face are never separated.** Let g be a face of M. No vertex or edge of B_M lies inside g. So any two points on the
  boundary of g are joined by a path through g's interior that meets no cycle, and no cycle separates them. Their relative value is
  the same in every solution.
- **M's own points.** They lie on M's faces. ∎

Checked: 325 removals on 30 plane spaces; the tie class is exactly the set of points on M's faces at 321 of them.

## 2. The route lemma (proved, for any near side)

**Suppose (h, a) pins t, with odd port y₁ and non-odd ports y, y′, and suppose one of these holds.**
- **(i)** The hub is tied to a non-odd port; call it y.
- **(ii)** The hub is tied to no port, and the near knot {h, y, y′} excludes, at the pinning hub value v, a pattern that differs from
  t in exactly one of y, y′.

**Then every crossing knot K = {h, a, c} whose far point separates t is generated by eliminations from one-sided knots, whatever the
far side.**

*Proof.*
- **The key fact.** Inside I, fixing the two non-odd ports to t's values leaves one completion. The other completion is t with y₁
  flipped, which is σ or σ̄.
- **The far knot is automatic.** By HANDLE-EMERGENCE B Lemma 1, when c's value γ excludes t, the pattern (t_y, t_y′, γ) never occurs.
  So {y, y′, c} is constrained, and it is a far-side knot.
  - Its pairs are free: the ports are free because I has six patterns.
  - If c were tied to a port, K would be tie-equivalent to a one-sided set, so K would lie in the base. This trivial case is set aside.
- **Case (i).**
  - **The near knot.** K₁ = {h, a, y′} is a near knot: the pinning forces y′.
    - {h, y′} = {y, y′} is free.
    - {h, a} ⊂ K is free.
    - A tie {a, y′} would make K tie-equivalent to the far knot.
  - **The far knot.** K₂ = {y, y′, c}.
  - **The elimination.** With h ~ y, eliminating y′ from K₁ ∪ K₂ gives K.
- **Case (ii).** Say the excluded pattern (v, t_y, t̄_y′) differs from t at y′.
  - **The far knot.** K₂ = {y, y′, c}.
  - **A crossing knot.** Eliminating y′ from {h, y, y′} and K₂ gives K₃ = {h, y, c}. It is constrained: given h = v and y = t_y, the
    near knot forces y′ = t_y′, hence the pattern t, which c's value γ excludes. Its pairs are free: the hub is tied to no port, and the
    other pairs are subsets of K and K₂, or trivial ties.
  - **The near knot.** K₁ = {h, a, y} is a near knot: the pinning forces y.
  - **The elimination.** Eliminating y from K₁ and K₃ gives K. ∎

## 3. The access lemma (checked)

**On a plane near side, every pinning is of type (i) or (ii).**

| Near side | Pinnings | Type (i) | Type (ii) | Neither |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 30 plane spaces (6 planar census, 24 of HANDLE-EMERGENCE A) | 4560 | 3280 | 1280 | 0 |
| Fano plane | 168 | 0 | 0 | 168 (hub tied to the odd port) |
| Census 58 | 168 | 0 | 0 | 168 (120 tied to the odd port; 48 with the near knot's pattern two steps from t) |

The lemma separates plane from non-planar exactly on every space tested.

## 4. Consequence (proved from items 2 and 3)

- **The statement.** On each of the 30 plane near sides, at every six-pattern removal, no pinned-and-separated three-point knot through
  the hub is new, whatever knot space is glued on the far side.
- **Why.** Items 2 and 3 involve only the near side and the interface.
- **Confirmed knot by knot.** The planar census and 10-point spaces were spliced into K4^(3), census 6, the Fano plane and census 58.
  - **Six-pattern removals.** All 2142 such knots are produced by the predicted routes: 954 by route (i) and 1188 by route (ii).
  - **Four-pattern removals.** All 8981 knots there, which are outside the lemma, are generated.

## 5. The premise, on larger far sides (checked)

- **The test.** The Fano plane and census 58 were glued into every census space and the eight 10-point plane spaces.
- **The result.** All 1884 new knots are pinned-and-separated three-point knots through the hub.

## What is open

  is to show that a pinning never selects the odd port of a hub-tied port, and that otherwise the two-port near knot points the right
  way.
- **(b)** A proof that every new knot is a pinned-and-separated three-point knot through the hub.
- **(c)** A proof for four-pattern removals, where no creation has been found.
