# Can a single live pair get the original eight aligned?

**No.** With one temporary pair, the current bond distances can identify a correct neighbor on a favorable test. They cannot guarantee reaching the second permanent junction. The proof allows the rule to move the probe directly using what it currently sees, to test either fresh output of the first parity junction, and even to recognize which distinctions are temporary. [Proof](../../proofs/ONE-PROBE-ALIGNMENT-20261118-001/ONE-PROBE-ALIGNMENT-20261118-001-P001_serial/proof.md)

For example, call the starting original 000 and test it with 011. The two endpoints have bond distance two to four originals. Besides 000 these are 001, 010 and 011. The first two are genuine neighbors. The last is the other end of the diagonal. Swapping 001 and 011 leaves **the entire distance table** unchanged and fixes both test endpoints and all three permanent root ports. Thus the table cannot use that swap to choose safely. These names let us check what happened. The rule never receives them. [Exact tables](../../proofs/ONE-PROBE-ALIGNMENT-20261118-001/ONE-PROBE-ALIGNMENT-20261118-001-P001_serial/first.json)

Trying a fresh test does not guarantee escape. There are 45 possible probes after placing the root. An adversary can keep the process among 36 of those probes and the state with no probe. In each, any proposed next original has an indistinguishable nonneighbor, and any proposed replacement has a resolution that leaves the uncertainty in place. Before the root is placed, a test on two opposite originals already gives bond distance two on all 45 pairs among the ten distinctions. The proof therefore applies from the original seed. A separate calculation checked all 397 named positions needed to decide this first commitment. [Adversarial choices](../../proofs/ONE-PROBE-ALIGNMENT-20261118-001/ONE-PROBE-ALIGNMENT-20261118-001-P001_serial/game.json)

Two controls show what the one-pair rule can do. A test of the root against a uniformly chosen other original has a 3 in 7 chance to certify a true neighbor. Independent retries eventually find one with probability one. With one extra piece of current information, the rule can guarantee the same prefix. If the instrument identifies the **two originals it just tested** as well as its endpoints, at most two probes suffice. On a four-member interval, the two originals besides the tested pair are both neighbors of the root. On an eight-member interval, the tested original is the unique opposite and the next trial can exclude it. These controls establish the second junction only.
