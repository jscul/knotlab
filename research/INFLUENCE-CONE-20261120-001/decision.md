**What changes first.** Let $d$ count steps between live knots, with one step when a present knot witnesses both together. Compare matched starts that fund every birth and stay open through the steps being compared. Count a receiver birth when it has at least one ancestor in the receiving family.

- **Change only the loose count at a still-live sender.** A matched receiver birth can first receive a different number of fresh distinctions at step $d$. The first difference in which births occur can come at $d+1$. Both bounds are attained for every $d\ge 1$.
- **Change what is witnessed together.** At every $d\ge 2$, a matched receiver child can receive **three versus four fresh distinctions at step $d-1$**. It has no sender ancestor in either start. At step $d$, one side produces one extra receiver birth. Both bounds are sharp under both knot laws. Separation is measured to the changed witness data in the two starts. **The cone in both directions.** Within $n$ continuing parent steps, a knot shares descendants with exactly the original knots within $n$ witnessed steps. Looking backward, two later knots share an ancestor within those $n$ steps exactly when they are within $n$ witnessed steps of each other. For a receiver’s *entire* family, the initial witnessed groups and loose counts through radius $n$ determine which births occur. Radius $n+1$ determines their fresh counts. Both radii are sharp in general. In the class with the proved identification of births from the start, $n$ suffices for both. An old knot always keeps its written holdings. The differences appear in its new co-holders or descendants. [Forward and backward proofs](https://github.com/jscul/knotlab/blob/main/research/INFLUENCE-CONE-20261120-001/cones_proof.md)
