**Standard statement.** A stationary rule observes a finite binary relation through an anonymous, symmetric matrix. The entry for two distinct variables is the minimum cardinality of a minimal nonfree projection containing them, minus one. The entry is infinite if there is no such projection. Can the rule extend the eight-variable cut relation to the first complete aligned round under every resolution of its choices?

**Nearest known result.** One live betweenness test cannot guarantee the second connected junction, even with several roles marked. The present question allows two live tests and commits one permanent junction at a time. A fortiori they cannot guarantee the first aligned round. This includes a direct replacement chosen while the old test is still visible.

Opposed ends extend every permanent row. Equal ends extend exactly those rows in which both tested bits equal the common end value. Erasing both ends therefore recovers the old relation exactly, including the other probe if present. The two tests may overlap or repeat the same two middles.

The first source junction chooses any original $r$ and forms the even-parity relation on $r,u,v$, with $u,v$ fresh. Necessarily the second connects one of these three ports and a true neighbor of $r$ among the original eight. Grant any of the three ports, any correct neighbor and immediate success with both probes still live. A rule for the requested complete assembly would win this easier game. The necessity follows both from the source law and from the census on the final allowed states. ## Complete finite decision

The reduction preserves both the state relation and which commitments are legal.
2. For each type, generate its allowed rows. A projection is free if all binary words on it occur. A nonfree projection is minimal exactly when deletion of each of its bits gives a free projection. A support with a nonfree proper subset can be pruned. After the largest size at which a proper projection could still be free, every later support is pruned. Taking the minimum size minus one gives the entire matrix including infinity. There are 73 distinct matrices up to simultaneous renaming of rows and columns.
3. Within each matrix consider every action up to permutations preserving the matrix. Transfer every member of each action orbit back to every physical position with that observation. A wrong permanent commitment, or specifying an original as a temporary end to erase, is a failing resolution. For an add or replacement all permitted new middles are included. Observation-equivalent histories share one decision whenever both are reachable. An invariant policy allowing several action orbits could be reduced to one at each observation, since every resolution of a winning policy must succeed.
4. The finite test assigns one action to each needed observation. Reachable physical types have positive integer rank at most 225. The initial rank is positive. The chosen action must terminate successfully or lead only to positive, strictly smaller ranks. The conditions are necessary by ranking each reachable type by its longest possible remaining run. They are sufficient by descending induction. In particular an adversary cannot be required to break a tie differently on a later visit. Unreachable histories put no constraints on the policy.
