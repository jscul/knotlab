# The first structural difference short of the attachment

For a single original path mark or an outward consecutive packet, let $d$ be the nearest marked-holder offset in original knot steps. The attachment lies $\delta=2d+1$ ordinary incidence hops from the marks. Compare the quiet one-spare five-ring to its once-loaded copy under the same pass-cold, veto-hot law. All original loose values are quiet. Every later newborn is cold by the minimum fresh extension. Work at a completed layer $n$ with the old path, all actual knots and distinctions, and no auxiliary exterior. Radius is measured from the nearest supplied mark. The primary observation is the induced bipartite ball, with the two kinds and the marks preserved by isomorphism. Its boundary shows only the incidences actually inside. [Frozen definitions](definitions.md) · [Minimum extension and complete rosters](../STATE-FEEDS-GROWTH-20261119-001/selection.md)

**Exact radii.** For $d\ge2$, $n\ge1$, put $k=\lfloor d/2\rfloor$. The smallest radius distinguishing the full induced ball and the smallest radius distinguishing the number of knots in it are respectively
\[
 F(n,d)=2d+2-2\min(n,k),\qquad
 C(n,d)=2d+3-2\min(n-1,k).
\]
At every smaller radius than $F$ the rooted, typed balls are isomorphic. At $F$ even the number of distinct physical distinctions differs. At a smaller radius than $C$ the knot counts agree. The formulas hold up to the physical path ends, provided the stated marks exist on that side. Reflection exchanges sides.

| Reading short of the attachment | First layer at exact offset $d$ | What happens later |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full induced ball | One for $d\ge2$. None for $d=0,1$. | Radius $2d$ continues distinguishing at every $n\ge1$. The smallest distinguishing radius reaches $d+2$ for even $d$, or $d+3$ for odd $d$. |
| Number of knots alone | Three for $d\ge4$. None for $0\le d\le3$. | At layer two its first difference is at the attachment radius $\delta$ for $d\ge2$. At layer three and $d\ge4$ it first differs at $\delta-2$. |
| Full knot degree supplied at the boundary | On the first layer the difference appears at $2d-1$ for $d\ge2$. | The general first radius is $F(n,d)-1$. Supplying the unseen incidences changes the instrument. |

At age zero the structural sources agree. For $d=0$ the only radius strictly below the attachment distance is zero. For $d=1$ the permitted radii are zero through two. They expose the old root distinctions, their old holders and incidences at those holders. A new knot costs at least three hops. The resulting induced balls agree at every age. The root marks and all distinctions in a strict ball have zero relative value in both arms. Each of the five occupied distinctions has only original ring holders, hence lies beyond the nearest such holder. If one may choose **any** offset at least $d$, the first strict full-ball example is already at layer one by taking an available offset at least $\max(d,2)$. The corresponding knot-count example uses an available offset at least $\max(d,4)$ and layer three.

## The two outward sequences

On the right write $v_j=b_{s+\epsilon+2j}$, using $\epsilon=0$ or $1$. Use the identified cluster representation only as proof coordinates. A knot of age $u$ is a connected cluster of $u+1$ points containing at least one original. Removing an end while retaining such a cluster gives a parent, adding an adjacent point gives a child and its physical distinction. On each outward sequence the points beyond the last genuine $b_N$ or $b_{N-1}$ continue by twos along its virtual end spare. A cluster must still contain an original knot. These are the finite paths with the funding and four end spares of the frozen source. All first proposals are pairs. The loaded first roster, its open counts and the subsequent ordinary growth have the cold representation with hot members removed from the original blocks and the released inputs given their own unaries. [Identification, including the loaded surrogate and physical end chains](../SAME-SIDE-READING-20261119-001/side_bonds_proof.md)

The part at indices $j\ge1$, including both outward continuations past the path end, is the same in both arms. A cluster through an original $v_a$ can first reach the inward end at $v_0$ when its age is $a$. The only such cluster at that age is $[v_0,v_a]$. In the even loaded sequence, read $v_0$ here as the first spare point at $b_{s+2}$. That holder has two opens and its inward joint birth was vetoed. The spare continues as a single inward sequence. Thus the leading even cluster has four outward or inward extensions when quiet and two when loaded. At age one it has respectively two or one parents. At age at least two it has two parents in either arm whenever its rightmost point is the physical root under consideration. The leading odd cluster has four extensions and matching parents in both arms. Every interval wholly on the common part has equal numbers of extensions and deletions, including a one-original interval at an outer physical end. One can derive the two leading counts without the representation by successively joining the leading knot to the next two-open neighbour. [Straight-sequence induction](../SAME-SIDE-READING-20261119-001/local_proof.md)

One more inward step on the odd sequence is needed for the odd-offset boundary. Write $v_0=b_{s+1}$ and $L=b_{s-1}$. On the quiet side its inward choices are $L,c_1,c_4$ in the same four-point block with $v_0$. Each of these three points has one further choice away from that block. Under load the inward choices are $L$ and two terminal spares at $v_0$. The latter do not continue because $v_0$ has four opens. Beyond $L$ one can choose the next original path point or either of its own two terminal spares. Consequently:

- An odd interval $[v_0,v_j]$ with $j\ge1$, enlarged by one inward point, has two parents. The three choices have fresh counts $4,4,4$ quiet and $6,3,3$ loaded. Each count includes one extension at its right end, the two other choices at $v_0$, and the onward choices of its chosen inward point. Each side gives total fresh count twelve.
- The number of odd clusters obtained by adding exactly two inward points to the interval is six in either arm. There are three choices of two of the first three points. There are three choices using one first point and one further point beyond it.
- On the even sequence one inward point beyond $v_0$ has three choices quiet and one loaded. Zero extra points has one choice on both sequences in either arm.

These counts apply when the right end of the interval is the last old point. Its one further extension uses the physical end spare. All clusters compared here touch a specified original on the outward unbranched sequence. Two lifts of their chosen original determine the same orbit exactly when the corresponding rooted subclusters agree. This prevents any overcount from the ring's unrolling.

## Proof for the full induced ball

For a cohort-$u$ knot $x$, its distance from the old marks is $2u$ plus the smallest source incidence distance to an original ancestor. Upward steps give one inequality. To prove the other, replace a latest knot between two earlier parents by their earlier common neighbour, then recurse to a parent of $x$. Consequently a ball of knot radius $2W+1$ contains in cohort $u\le\min(n,W)$ exactly the descendants of original holders within $W-u$ original steps of a marked holder. The source holder of least offset in this window is at $d-W+u$ when that number is nonnegative. Attaining this least offset uses the closest marked holder on the source path. An old shared mark has the same two holders throughout. [Distance and descendant formula](../SAME-SIDE-READING-20261119-001/local_proof.md)

Set $t=\min(n,k)$. First take $W=d-t$, so $2W+1=F-1$. Call the resulting set of observed knots $S$. The least source offset in cohort $u$'s window is $t+u$. For $u<t$ this exceeds $2u$. Hence on either parity an observed cluster stays on the common interval, or is the matching odd leader at $u=t-1$. At $u=t$, its least possible even original is $v_t$ and its least possible odd original is also $v_t$. The two leading clusters can occur, with matching ordinary knots. Every other cluster through one of these original holders is an outward interval in the common part. Farther roots, even if the packet is long, introduce only matching intervals. At age zero the visible original path meetings are fixed.

There is just one additional cohort to consider. It occurs when $d=2k+1$ and $n\ge k+1$, since then $W=k+1$. At $u=k+1$ the least even index is $k+1$ and the least odd index is $k$. On top of the ordinary intervals and the new even leader, the odd boundary consists of the three one-inward-point clusters described above. Each has exactly one parent in $S$, namely $[v_0,v_k]$ on the odd sequence. Its other parent has largest genuine outward offset $2k-1$ and distance at least $2k+5$ from the marks, beyond this knot ball. Thus the three boundary knots may be matched in any bijection. All these knots are at distance $F-1$.

More explicitly, match the ordinary knots of $S$ by interval endpoints, with this last three-way matching when necessary. Every ordinary parent–child incidence whose endpoints are both in $S$ matches. It adds or removes an interval end, or is one of the three links just identified. For the first even cluster the extra quiet input from $s$ has its old holder outside $S$. Each of the few degree changes occurs on the outer shell $F-1$. Indeed the age-$t$ even leader is reached there through offset $2t$. In the additional odd case both the previous even leader and the new one are on this shell, reached respectively through offsets $2k$ and $2k+2$. The two parity lists have no common parent inside these intervals. The corresponding old original incidences and the designated marks match.

All distinctions shown in the radius-$F-1$ ball are incident to a knot strictly inside its knot boundary, apart from the supplied original roots. A distinction between two final-shell knots alone would require one more hop. Each interior knot has its whole degree matched. Match its private displayed distinctions arbitrarily after matching the ones between two displayed holders. This gives a rooted isomorphism and restricts to every smaller ball. At radius $F$ the set of knots is still $S$ and **every** distinction held by a member is shown. Its count is the sum of their full degrees minus the number of physical meetings with both holders in $S$. The latter number matches. The leading even knot contributes three fewer distinctions under load if $t=1$, otherwise two fewer. In the additional odd cohort its three exceptional odd degrees have the same sum, and the new even leader contributes two further missing distinctions. This proves the full-ball formula, including nonisomorphism after forgetting names and ages. An isomorphism at a larger radius would restrict to this ball, so the original layer-one radius $2d$ remains distinguishing at every later stage.

If full physical degrees are added at the boundary, the same unequal sum is already visible at $F-1$. For a smaller radius all those degrees can be read inside the ordinary ball one hop larger, where the preceding isomorphism applies. The same bound holds if the actual shared/loose status of a displayed distinction is also supplied. An external-holder identity or its entire hidden support would supply a different observation.

## Why counting knots alone takes longer

The first layer has one extra knot at radius $\delta+2$. This follows by replacing each vetoed source pair $\{p,q\}$ with its two unaries. Its contribution to the cumulative difference is one when $2+\max(d_0(M,p),d_0(M,q))$ enters the radius. Only the near pair $\{s,b_{s+2}\}$ contributes at that first radius for $d\ge2$. [Exact first-layer profile](../SAME-SIDE-READING-20261119-001/local_proof.md)

For $n\ge2$ put $q=\min(n-1,k)$. At the proposed radius $C$ take $W=d-q+1$. The least source offset in cohort $u$'s window is $q-1+u$. For $u<q$ its two parity indices are at least $u$, so no interval can extend inward beyond $v_0$ and every count matches. At $u=q$ the odd window can use one inward point, with three choices in both arms. At $u=q+1$ the even window can use one inward point, with one loaded versus three quiet choices, and the odd counts still match. This gives two fewer knots. If $d$ is odd and $n\ge k+2$, there is a last cohort $u=q+2$. The even window again allows one inward point and the odd window now permits two, with the same six choices in each arm. This cohort also loses two. No later birth enters this ball.

In this enumeration a cluster extending inward is counted once by its largest outward index and its additional inward choices. When that index is above the smallest relevant index there is one fewer inward point available for each extra outward step. At these thresholds that leaves only the equal zero-point case, or the equal one-point odd case. The smallest index on each parity is actually attained in the window of the nearest mark's two holders. Additional marked holders cannot remove these clusters or add an earlier inequivalence. If the outer physical end is nearby, intervals can continue across its spare and still contain the required physical marked holder. At a smaller radius it suffices to check odd shells with $W\le d-q$. Their least offset is at least $q+u$, and the largest possible cohort is $q+1$. At most one odd inward point is then available, and every count matches. This proves the stated minimum. For $d=0,1$ the earlier strict null applies irrespective of the knot-only formula's domain.

## A separately supplied remote set

Let $A_d$ consist of the outward original path holders beginning at offset $d$, with any ring-exclusive and inward originals omitted. Suppose a region retains the selected knots and every distinction they hold, with holders outside the region hidden. If **all** origins must be in $A_d$, the entire region agrees forever for $d\ge3$. Every original member then has its complete first child roster unchanged. The pure-roster induction matches all later pure knots, all their incidences and counts, including connections to hidden holders. For $d=1,2$ the pure region already adds respectively three and one first births under load. At $d=0$, which includes the seam itself, the increase is six. Each vetoed pair touching $A_d$ gives one extra pure birth. The changes persist in the rooted observation. At radius three around $e_{s+d}$, the two old holders have respectively five, three or one additional pure first children for $d=0,1,2$. No later knot can enter this radius. [Which first pairs see a load](../STATE-FEEDS-GROWTH-20261119-001/locality.md)

If **at least one** origin is required in $A_d$, the first structural difference for $d\ge1$ occurs at $n=\lceil d/2\rceil$. At earlier ages every represented cluster lies on the common outward part, or is the odd leader whose structure still matches. Specifically $d>2n$ gives lowest even index greater than $n$, and lowest odd index at least $n$. The full incident stars and their mutual meetings are then matched in every cohort, with hidden predecessors ending as stubs. At $d=2k\ge2$ the age-$k$ even leader gives the first difference, while at $d=2k+1$ its successor through the physical holder at offset $2k+2$ gives it at age $k+1$. These are detections of changed structure. The current fresh values remain quiet everywhere.

With the supplied original mark $e_{s+d}$ this difference persists at all later stages. Use its fixed region radius $2\lceil d/2\rceil+2$. Every lowest-age route to an indicated leading cluster stays in the region by descending through that mark's remote holder. Before the first differing cohort, all region knots and their incidences match. At the differing cohort, the only even leader in this region has its changed total degree, and every new knot in this ball is at the final knot shell. In the odd case the three additional odd knots have the same total degree for $k\ge1$, as above. Each has its single remote predecessor displayed and its inward predecessor hidden. Every other observed interval and internal meeting matches. The deficit is three for $d=2$, and two for $d\ge3$. At $d=1$, the first-child degrees on the odd side are $6,6,6$ quiet and $8,4,4$ loaded on the three inward choices, since a first unary has just one parent. Together with the first even leader this gives a deficit of five. No later knot enters this fixed region ball. For $d=0$ the joint-region first birth count increases by one, from the veto with both parents in $A_0$. Its rooted radius-four ball also distinguishes permanently: the loaded joint child of $b_{s-1},b_{s+1}$ has degree eight. The quiet first children at the two holders of $e_s$ have degree at most seven on the one-spare source, and the common old degrees agree.

When the original set $A_d$ itself is marked, incidence distance to the nearest of its original holders within the joint region is exactly $2u$ on every cohort-$u$ knot. The ancestor descent stays inside the region and each birth step can change age only by one. Thus the earlier cohort can be read from the retained structure. On the full physical stage the closest currently live knots to $e_{s+d}$ are exactly the current descendants of its two holders. They lie $2n+1$ hops away, and every older knot is exhausted. At first detection, summing their loose distinctions gives two fewer under load for every $d\ge1$. An even-offset holder contributes the four-versus-two leading count. For a single odd-offset holder its three inward counts change from $4,4,4$ to $6,3,3$, giving a different histogram at unchanged total. Each group of siblings here includes the marked holder in its ancestry, and the same-parity outward intervals contribute equally. These readings of true loose distinctions require real holder multiplicity; a one-ended display in the restricted region can also indicate a hidden holder.
