2026-11-15 (Pacific). Goals G3 and G16.

## The question

Goals G3 and G16 ask which proof steps cause work. Keeping the five products and both reports fixed, can a checked proof that learns an affine consequence and uses it in still-live branches beat the cheapest proof that derives its steps afresh when binary questions, local checks and citations are charged comparably?

Questions can mix all nine visible coordinates and have unit cost, including both child continuations. At any cell one paid source check may add all its affine consequences or refute it. A proof of an affine implication costs all checks and questions used to refute the guard with the opposite literal. Retyping that refutation as its conditional consequence costs zero; retain a separate line count. Each named use costs one and includes verifying algebraic inclusion in its guard. Any new guard facts require their actual derivations; a raw set of surviving traces on both sides is no free source. Structural child continuations are part of the split. Cover arbitrary placement of one-side checks or identify exact exclusions.
