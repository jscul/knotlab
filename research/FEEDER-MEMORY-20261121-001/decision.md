# Can a bot's knot memory change its next eat or pass?

Under both laws a prepared memory knot carried a past meal receipt into the next actual action. It wrote whether the feeder that last paid a ration had one control or several. In matched games, exchanging only those memory marks changed the action emitted by a separate knot at the same current observation. That small address also confused distinct feeders, and the keeper survived fewer turns than the stronger current-only script on held-out starts. The three bot-side knots, input writer, output coupling, reset and offer schedule were supplied. The game world used the unchanged ordinary growth law. ## A lawful write and an action at the same frozen game

The previous menu size, preceding issued ON and two public energies are sufficient to trigger a write. A change from one energy to four records a true meal; an empty attempt always costs one. Scheduled guarded transpositions then write 101 (last paid feeder had one control) or 011 (last paid feeder had multiple controls) into a four-place memory knot. A private slack completes the holder. A coupling knot can see the shared memory marks and the marks of a separate four-place action knot. The actuator always begins at 100. After the same sequence of offers its third name, physical distinction 6, gives the one action bit. OFF produces 010, ON produces 001. The writer exports the previous word into a supplied ready-token exchange. A fixed schedule, raw input encoding, initial words, clearing and the game’s wiring of that distinction to eat/pass all enter the construction. No input bank contains current stock, a feeder ID or an externally scored answer. The three prepared holders have 55 total names. Their unrestricted local projections are canonical under their own law. The 53-place coupling holder uses an exact extension argument; the finite small holders also admit direct canonical checks.
