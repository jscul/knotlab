# Which starts can hold the three six-pair choices?

Every connected start that reaches one of the three kinds after a birth needs at least **13 start distinctions**. The minimum number of start knots is six for kinds (a) and (b) and five for kind (c). The ordinary complete-round rule attains both minima together for each kind. These bounds concern the holding of a choice and apply to whole choices and to the restricted set of birth choices. ## What a complete round spends

A birth takes one loose distinction per parent. Every loose distinction that its knot keeps after serving the joins gets a one-parent birth of its own. Thus every funded round spends **all** the loose distinctions available when it begins. Each fresh distinction of that round has one newborn holder. At the next funded round it acquires its second holder. Hence two knots sharing a distinction are either both start knots or have birth rounds that differ by exactly one. In the latter case they share only one distinction, since the younger knot takes at most one from its older parent. These statements also hold at a later closure because existing holdings persist. [Engine funding and unique-parent checks](../../../experiments/FORBIDDING-JOIN-20261120-001B/FORBIDDING-JOIN-20261120-001B-E001_free_readings/rival_growth.py)

Among three mutually meeting knots, at least two must be start knots. If the three were all born after the start, each meeting would join an odd round to an even round, which is impossible for three pairwise meetings. If one of the three is a start knot, every neighbor of it born later comes in the first round. Two first-round knots cannot meet each other. Applying this to each triple, at least three of four mutually meeting knots are start knots.

## The three holdings

- **(a)** The three knots sharing two distinctions pairwise must be start knots. The other four meet pairwise, so at least three of those are also start knots. Every one of the 12 choice values consequently has a holder in the start. With only those 12 distinctions the start's holdings fall into two disjoint groups, the three knots of the doubled meetings and the start knots of the four. A connected start needs another distinction to join the groups. An outside start knot would also need a distinction of its own, because both holders of every one of the 12 are specified by the choice.
- **(b)** Every one of the six knots belongs to a doubled meeting. All six must be start knots, and each of the 12 values is already held by its two start knots. A start with only those distinctions is already closed. To close *after* a birth it needs at least one more distinction. The one-loose placements in the table use the engine's rule that gives zero fresh distinctions to an implied one-parent birth of demand zero.
- **(c)** Number the knots as in the five-knot start B3, with the missing knot labelled 5. Knots 1 and 4 share two choice values and must be start knots. Each of the remaining knots 0, 2, 3 and 5 meets 1 or 4. Every one born later must therefore be a first-round knot. Every choice value is already present in the start, since no two first-round knots meet. With only those 12 distinctions, a first-round knot's complete parent set must be precisely the knots it meets in the choice. There are no spare distinctions for an outside knot or an extra parent.

For (c), the required parent set for 2 is `{0,3,4}`, all of which meet start knot 1. For 5 it is `{0,1,3}`, all of which meet start knot 4. The common-knot veto bars either group when only the 12 choice distinctions are present. The required parents of 0 and of 3 are both `{1,2,4,5}`. If just one of 0 and 3 is born, its partner in this last pair is a start knot meeting all four parents. If both are born, the same four-parent group would have to be used twice. The rule uses each multi-parent group once. If all six knots are present in the start, their 12 choice distinctions are already fully held and the start is closed. This covers every case at 12 distinctions. The table of all 15 nonempty subsets of the four possible first-round knots also records which subsets contain a required meeting between two first-round knots. [Assignment check](built_counts.json)

The minimum of five **start knots** for (c) also holds when additional distinctions are allowed. Among 0, 2, 3 and 5 the meetings are 0–2, 0–5, 3–2 and 3–5. At most two of these knots can both be born. With a total of four start knots, two must be born, either 0 and 3 or 2 and 5. In the first case both would require all four available parents. In the second case 2 must add parent 1 to get past its veto and 5 must add parent 4. Both again need the same four-parent group, which forms a single birth. With fewer start knots there would have to be three mutually nonmeeting first-round knots among the four, and no such three exist.

## What changes the five-knot start

The ordinary join rule selects maximal groups of three or more live knots whose pairs have common neighbors and whose members as a whole have none. An eligible pair lying in no such group is a two-parent group. At B3 the three eligible pairs are `{0,1}`, `{0,3}`, `{1,3}`. Each of knots 0, 1 and 3 has two demands and one loose distinction. The start stalls. B4 makes knot 4 live with a thirteenth distinction. The four live knots qualify as a single group, every demand is funded, and the closure holds four labelled choices of kind (c). Of the five single-distinction additions to B3, only the one at 4 closes. The other four stall before any birth. ## The small hand witnesses

For a connected choice covered twice, let $V$ count its knots after separation and $F$ its failing pairs. There are $2F$ different cross pairs and the Euler count is $V-F$. If this count is at most −1, then $2F\leq\binom{F-1}{2}$, forcing $F\geq7$. At Euler count one, the separated sign is even exactly when the cross pairs are two-colourable. Two classes on $V$ knots can supply at most $\lfloor V^2/4\rfloor$ cross pairs. Comparing this with $2(V-1)$ gives $V\geq7$. Equality at $V=7$ requires every cross pair between classes of sizes three and four. At each of the four knots, the three neighbors occur in all three pairings. Hence every pair in the class of three holds two values, and each such pair splits the class of four into two pairs. The three splits must differ, since repeating one would give two separate cycles at their common knot of the class of three. [Separated sign criterion](../../CELL-SIGN-20261127-001/referee-R1/hand_check.md)

At Euler count zero there are $2V$ cross pairs, so $V\geq5$. At five knots every one of the ten knot pairs is a cross pair. Each knot appears in four failing pairs and is left out of one. Every pair is present together three times, twice as a cross pair and once as holders of a choice value. Of the $3^5=243$ assignments pairing the four knots at each omitted knot, six use all ten holder pairs once. Those six form one class and all admit balanced cyclic orders. This rules out five failing pairs. The three six-pair fixtures each have connected cycle incidences, coverage two, even sign, and no balanced orders. Their separated Euler counts are 1, 0 and 0. Completeness at six uses the separate abstract enumeration. [Five-knot check and three fixtures](built_counts.json)

The two choices of Euler count zero also pass an independent test of their claimed odd-cycle property. Join copies of their cyclic orders so every shared cross pair has opposite traversals. In each case the resulting orientation double cover is connected and two-colourable, on 12 knots with 24 cross pairs and 12 failing pairs. Exchanging the two copies interchanges the colours. A closed walk of the original choice therefore has even length when its lift returns to its starting knot and odd length when its lift ends at the other copy of that knot. The same construction for kind (a) gives 14 knots and preserves the colours when the copies are exchanged. [Cover counts](built_counts.json)
