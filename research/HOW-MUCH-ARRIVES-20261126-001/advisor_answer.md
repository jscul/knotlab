# The advisor's answer to the design question

Asked before the full run ([question](advisor_question.md)); a colleague's view, checked before use. Model: the Fable model this host serves.

1. **The measure cannot weaken by construction.** Births are named by their parents' names recursively, so a birth whose ancestor differed is itself "different" even when its kind, counts and places are identical. Damage is hereditary: c(t) is at least the count of living descendants of the first differing births, and that grows with the growth. "No case weakens" (expectation 1) is then a tautology, not the lamp's second half tested. Fix: compare births by an ancestry-free signature, the kind, parent count, fresh count, place count and the shape of the map of joins within r steps (r = 1 and 2), and take the multiset difference of those. Report both measures; the gap between them is the heredity, and the ancestry-free one is the only one that can show a lamp.

2. **A mark is a source, not a flash.** One extra loose distinction is conserved and makes an offshoot every round; an offshoot gets f ≥ 2 fresh distinctions, so the mark seeds a growth of its own. The lamp is a fixed quantity spread over a growing sphere. The transient analogue is a pulse: mark at round s, remove the extra loose distinction at round s + 1 (or let its first offshoot be struck), then compare. Add this as A4 to every start. A move keeps the inventory but still changes the kind of growth at a start, so A1 and A2 answer "which start" and should be labelled as such, not as disturbances. A3 is the right fix for the source question only if read ancestry-free and against the descent share; apply the descent share to every change, not only A3.

3. **Read damage by distance from the mark, not by round.** The lamp's claim is radial. For A3, round-t births sit at varying steps from the marked knot in the map of joins; bin c by that distance. "Weakens" then means the differing births per distance shell stay bounded while the shell's count grows. Round-only reading mixes the shell with everything that was never within reach.

4. **Setting B as written is forced.** A jam's end or a switch changes the receiver's whole future; share goes to one under either measure. Two readings can still show weakening with d: the share in the first round of difference, before heredity compounds; and the fraction of far changes (every mark at the far site, every move) that produce any difference in the receiver at all, as a function of d. The second is the lamp in reverse: how many sources are heard. B0 adds nothing as a control unless the ancestry-free measure is also zero there; check that first, it validates the measure.

5. **Thresholds.** Four rounds is too few for generator7, which releases packets: c(T) ≤ c(T − 3) can hold by phase alone. Use the whole series from round 4, require c bounded over every later round, and compare at equal phase for the generator. On ring5 the share is quantized to fifths, so 0.95 means 1.0 and "holds" above 0.8 means 1.0; state thresholds in counts for rings. "Dies out" needs more than four zero rounds under the one-fewer ration, where a jam can end later than round 12.

6. **The comparison.** Boolean-network damage spreading assumes fixed topology; here the medium grows and the disturbance is a conserved token, so the nearer analogue is a defect carried by a growing crystal (a persistent source) versus a pulse. GF(2) cancellation needs linearity, which not-all-equal joins lack. Cheapest discriminator: two marks at once; if c(both) < c(first) + c(second) there is cancellation, if equal the medium is additive, if greater the marks interact. Run it on two-touching and chain3.

7. **Expectations to change.** Rewrite 1 and 4 under the ancestry-free measure, else they are not falsifiable. Expectation 5 should name the first-round share and the heard fraction, both of which can fall with d. Add: under the pulse (A4) the ancestry-free damage dies out on every start, or the model has memory the lamp lacks.

8. **Compute.** Round 16 on the eddy is about 8,000 knots and the generator pairs at d = 8 are larger; time one case first and cap memory, per the standing rule.
