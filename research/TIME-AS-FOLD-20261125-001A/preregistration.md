## The question

The owner asked on 24 November 2026 (Pacific time; the record's tickets carry 25 November, its date in UTC): "Is time
itself a fold like intelligence/life through DNA?" ([The owner's request, as given](../TIME-AS-FOLD-20261125-001/owner_request.md).)
Nothing else here is the owner's. The thread takes the owner's fold of emergence (A223) to mean a pattern the rules make,
where what it makes depends on another pattern, and that makes a pattern of its own, and asks for the least such thing:
a made count of returns, one pattern counting another's returns.

The stop filed on the ticket (its objective, and sub-goal B329, `docs/RESEARCH_GOALS.md` line 2690), quoted exactly: "Stop
at one made part whose births follow another's returns at a fixed ratio over at least four returns and cease when the
other is stopped, with the reader and the law's choice said plainly to be supplied or made; or at a reason no law of that
kind holds both a pattern that returns and one that meets it at each return." The thread revised it before the freeze to
claim C (the claim, section 5): the same test, made decidable, with the count of X's own knots, a lag, rounds grown after
the bar and every numbering of the start. The revised wording is the thread's. **Its reading here**: a verdict is given
per test (TD 4.6), and a law in which some tests are undecided gives "none among n decided, m undecided", never "none";
the stop rule's "or none is" (below) is "or none is among the decided".

## In standard terms

In the literature's words, not the program's: a synchronous hypergraph-rewriting system (the pre-registration of the
study of parts that meet again, "In standard terms"). A made clock is a connected component of the live sub-hypergraph
whose isomorphism class recurs along descent: an oscillator. A part that reads it at each return is a second component
whose evolution depends on the first's presence in each period, tested by intervention (the oscillator barred); a counting
pattern also stores the number of periods, the literature's counter driven by an oscillator (a frequency divider). Under
the hypotheses H1 and H2 of the study of parts that meet again, components evolve independently (a context-free
substitution), so nothing can be driven; the laws studied here break H1 by a back-reference into the record of descent
(STEPS-k) or by a reference to a neighbour of the parent (MET).

## Prior work outside the program

Each from memory, the literature's, not checked:
- Coupled oscillators and entrainment: Huygens on two pendulum clocks on one beam (1665); Kuramoto's model of phase locking
  (1975). The two-knot case below, in which one part's form recurs at a second part's rounds only while the second is
  there, resembles the entrainment of one oscillator by another.
- A counter driven by an oscillator (a frequency divider; a ripple counter) is the electronic picture of one pattern
  counting another's returns.
- In Conway's Life a glider gun (Gosper, 1970) emits a glider every 30 updates of the board's supplied clock; patterns that
  count gliders, such as the sliding-block memory of Berlekamp, Conway and Guy, *Winning Ways* (1982), are placed by hand.
- Lamport's logical clocks (1978) count events along happened-before; the order of births is that relation here, and the
  thread keeps it apart from a clock.

What this would add: whether, on a structure whose every piece passes the canonical knot test and whose places are made by
the growth, a pattern the growth makes is driven by another made pattern's period and leaves a count of it, under which
law, at what cost, and how much of it is the scaffolds'.

## Settled by construction (stated, not tested)

Arguments for the referee; the runs check them. Checked by the drafter by hand and on the prototype; not by anyone else.

1. **The control reads nothing.** With no taking rule every law here satisfies FD's H1 and H2, so by the argument of the
   study of parts that meet again (its pre-registration, items 1 and 4) a part wholly apart from Y never becomes one part
   with Y's descendants, and its course is the same with Y barred, except through a round-wide trigger (parity's IMPLIED
   closing). So the control has no case of C or C-iii under not-all-equal.
2. **The count.** **The laws of the count**: the ration FLOOR1, the fresh count by distinct inputs, RELEASE, no FILLERS or
   NO-OFFSHOOT, FD 2.2's eligibility, and a taking rule under which every offshoot of a made knot has a candidate (Q, MET,
   MET's offshoot clause alone, STEPS-1, UP-TO-k). (a) An offshoot o of a made knot g has a candidate. Under MET, MET's
   offshoot clause alone, STEPS-1 and UP-TO-k: g's parent q (any parent, if g is a join) acted when it made g and, under
   release, then gave every usable loose distinction to a birth, so every place of q is shared; g holds exactly one of
   them (the one q gave it; g's own take, if any, is held by no parent of g); q has at least two places; so a place of q
   not held by g is a candidate (the advisor's argument, which replaces the first draft's). Under Q the candidate is the
   place g took from q. (b) So every birth that is not an offshoot of a start knot has at least two distinct inputs, at
   least one of them a usable loose distinction of a member, and receives max(3 − p, 1) = 1 fresh distinction or none
   (IMPLIED); an offshoot of a start knot that does not take receives two. (c) Hence the number of usable loose
   distinctions held by the members descended from a part (a waiting member descends from itself; a joint birth's fresh
   distinction is counted on each side whose input it took) never rises in a round unless a start knot among them makes an
   offshoot; retiring and barring only lower it. (d) A tick that gives parts off or has two or more qualifying parts needs
   a rise: the returned part is the same as the part before, so it holds the same number, and each part given off, and
   each further qualifying part, holds at least one more. (e) Under release a start knot acts in one round only. So a
   series has at most as many ticks that give parts off or have two or more qualifying parts as there are start knots that
   act during it, on every start, treatment, choice and knot law of these laws. For STEPS-k the bound is the number of
   acting knots with no ancestor k parent steps above them. It rests on the fresh count by distinct inputs: under the
   fresh count by parents an offshoot that takes receives two, and (b) fails.
3. **Where C is ruled out by the treatment.** Under RELEASE, a member that is not short gives every usable loose
   distinction to a birth (its kept joins, then offshoots, the distinctions of its cancelled groups included; SD step 5),
   and under CANCEL-OFFSHOOT a short member makes an offshoot from each. Under not-all-equal without FILLERS every birth
   receives at least one usable fresh distinction (FLOOR1 and CAP-3 give at least one), so it is a member. So under
   (CANCEL-OFFSHOOT, RELEASE, not-all-equal, no FILLERS) X and its descendants make knots in every round; they are own
   knots unless every such birth has a parent among P_j and its descendants. The verdict "ruled out by the treatment" is
   given to a test in which B(r) makes no joint knot after the bar; any other test is run in full. Elsewhere X and its
   descendants make no further knot only in one of the three ways of TD 5.2: they have no member (under FILLERS a birth with
   demand 0 holds only barred fresh distinctions), every member is short and waits or retires, or a birth receives no
   usable fresh distinction (IMPLIED under parity; the route of the first draft's 13 parity triples, a round-wide
   trigger). Under HOLD (a swap of run 6) a member that is not short can make no birth.
4. **What B(r) bars.** A knot can descend from both X and Y through a joint birth, so the part of Y's series at a tick can
   contain X's descendants; B(r) never bars them (TD 4.1). A member descended from X lying in one part with members
   descended from P_{r−1} makes that part not all descended from P_{r−1} unless it also descends from P_{r−1}; so a second
   part not descended from Y can share a part with the series' own part only between ticks.
5. **D, the half that is construction.** Under STEPS-k with RING-LAW on Ring(n,3), D2(n,3) and DW(n,3), as long as the
   ring's members are made as under the control, the first offshoot of a ring member that can take is made in round
   2k + 1: a ring member made in round 2i − 1 has the start knots exactly i parent steps above it, and its offshoots are
   made in round 2i + 1. Not settled: that the births of the parts given off, which can take from about round k + 1,
   leave the ring alone until then (false for k = 1 under every choice, 56 runs, and under BY-COUNT in 22 runs with
   k ≥ 2), that the take at 2k + 1 prevents the return at 2k + 2, and that no later return occurs (false under NEWEST in 4
   runs).
6. **STEPS-k for offshoots of start knots is the control**: a start knot has no ancestor k ≥ 1 parent steps above it, so
   nothing is taken. The family by the kind of the parent therefore has three members for each k (TD 1.4); this holds for
   STEPS-k only.
7. **L2, round 1 only.** On Ring(n,3), n ≥ 5, in round 1 the offshoot of start knot i has exactly two candidates (the
   distinctions that knot i + 1 shares with i + 2 and that i − 2 shares with i − 1) and a join has none (its common knot's
   shared places are held by its parents). Groups and shortness are fixed before the takes, so the round-1 births are the
   control's in their parents and loose inputs, every new knot is a member, and takes only add holders, so the parts after
   round 1 are unions of the control's, with the same members. The fresh counts are not the control's: demand is taken
   after the takes and the takers have two inputs. From round 2 the comparison is lost (groups and demand are not
   monotone in joinable). That the ring cannot return under L2 is seen per start and choice, by two routes: the would-be

## Engine and stop rule

- **Verdicts.** Each test has the verdict of TD 4.6. The result gives, per law, the tests, those failed, those held and

- **Stop** when (a) every primary law has a verdict on C and C-iii, with its reason (items 2, 3 and 7; P3 to P6); (b) every

  decided; (c) every registered run is done and every prediction has a verdict.

  reported as unfinished, never as a negative. Any check expected to take over ten minutes goes through the shared runner
  with a row in the long-running index.
- **Deviations** are recorded in `deviations.md` in the experiment family before the affected run.
