# Referee R1 — What produces the recorded excesses?
**Confirmed with fixes — Block on inventory completeness and on three interpretations. Theorem K stands. No checked construction requires a fourth kind.**

The seven-knot structure gives CHSH **19/9**. Throughout, TV means total variation, the largest change in probability of any event in the stated record. Alice can change Bob's complete record by changing her address on one visible old word. She can also use a different, nonconstant policy that leaves his whole distribution unchanged. The theorem requires immunity to **every** policy in **both** directions. The literal claim that each individual adaptive choice must change the far record is false.

The three kinds classify how a table was made. Among the historical examples I could trace, every excess has a concrete source in separate completion counts, a joint response, or a supplied reading, weighting, sign or order. The published inventory misses tables. Where a signed scalar whole has no allowed word, its probability law must be specified separately. The actual examples use supplied part counts or operator readings. The fixed-address whole-stage 9/4 is outside the quantum set. Its signal can be exposed by inspecting holdings alone. The supplied classical 17/8 control is inside the quantum set and passes every compulsory policy allowed by its declared records.

### Proof audit

Write $i,j$ for the visible old records, and $m_x(i),n_y(j)$ for their local completion counts. A single nonnegative old weighting and a product of the two local completion measures are essential. H5 means that each old value has a completion at both own settings or at neither. Discard values with zero count at both. All four fixed tables have positive total. The claimed implication follows even if one tests only single-old-value changes against address zero in each direction, with the receiver at zero. [Independent proof written before opening the study](independent.md)

| Step in the study's proof | Independent check |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Identify varying counts | Let $p_{ij}$ be the normalized old law at settings 00, with marginals $u_i,v_j$. Let $I,J$ contain the setting-independent counts on the respective sides. |
| 2. Change one address choice | Switching Alice only at $i$ multiplies that row by $r_i=m_1(i)/m_0(i)$. Keeping Bob's marginal unchanged gives $(r_i-1)(p_{ij}-u_i v_j)=0$ for each $j$, by cross-multiplication. His old record is an event of his complete record. This also covers a singleton support. |
| 3. Exchange readers | The column identity gives $p_{ij}=u_i v_j$ outside $I\times J$. H5 permits the same supports at both settings. An empty intersection of the varying sets' supports does not force the entire table to be product. A shared fair bit and constant counts give an immediate correlated example. The proof's fallback to its shared-variable case is needed. |
| 4. Check the unvarying part | On $I\times J$ the old law changes only by the common normalizer. The difference $p_{ij}-u_i v_j$ has zero row and column sums and vanishes off this part. Its total contribution to the normalizer at any settings is therefore zero. This gives the product normalizer and both marginals claimed for the unvarying part, including zero-mass cases. |
| 5. Produce the local answers | Membership in $I,J$ has independent probabilities depending only on the respective settings. Pre-sample a shared pair from $p$ conditional on $I\times J$ when it has mass. Each side draws its own membership. On membership it takes the corresponding shared value, otherwise it samples its own setting-weighted marginal outside. Then sample each private completion locally. This realizes every joint probability. Pre-sampling the local draws for both settings gives four potential answers, each deterministic CHSH sum having magnitude 2. |

The proof gives a common distribution of answers and hence all CHSH orientations. Its contrapositive guarantees *some* revealing choice. Immunity in just one designated direction, with the reverse direction left unconstrained, is insufficient. The two-old-bit example in the independent proof has CHSH 8/3 while every Alice policy leaves Bob's full law intact. Likewise a correlated fresh probability rule need not factor merely because the nominal fresh distinctions have different names. [Independent argument and one-sided counterexample](independent.md)

**The second normalization.** A226 pools finished words. If instead one splices the four fixed tables *after* dividing by their joint totals $Z_{xy}$, the same implication holds under H5. Fixed no-signalling implies that $P_{00}(i)=P_{01}(i)$, so $Z_{1y}/Z_{0y}=\sum_i P_{0y}(i)m_1(i)/m_0(i)$ is independent of $y$. Thus $Z_{xy}=Z_{x0}Z_{0y}/Z_{00}$, and its inverse is absorbed into the two local factors. Without this argument, a joint normalizer cannot simply be assigned to Alice. The two readings can give different signal sizes. If both readers see the same fair bit and Alice's counts are identically 1 at address 0 and identically 2 at address 1, keying to that bit changes Bob's chance of one to 2/3 under pooling and leaves it at 1/2 under per-setting normalization. [Original two normalization rules](../../THIRD-HOLDER-20261123-001A/preregistration.md)

**The suggested removal of H5.** For pooled counts this follows with all four fixed totals positive, comparing policies whose totals are positive. At fixed $y$, start with the Alice policy that chooses the larger count at every old value. Changing one varying row forces its Bob profile, if it has weight, to equal the common Bob marginal by the same cross-multiplication. If switching removes every word, that row was already the entire distribution. The fixed policies have that common marginal. Consequently, at each fixed pair, every varying row factors into the two actual marginals, and the reverse test gives the same for every varying column. The unnormalized law on the remaining constant-count block is identical at all four settings. Its mass after normalization is the product of the two membership probabilities by summing the already factored entries. If the block is empty in weight every fixed table is product. Otherwise its normalized law provides the shared pair of step 5. Indeed its first marginal equals the setting marginal restricted to the constant rows and renormalized, by subtracting the factored contributions of all varying columns. The same subtraction gives its second marginal. This verifies the conclusion suggested by the second review without relying on random trials. The three-reader rejection game is outside this two-reader argument. [Draft review, §§1–2](../advisor_answer_2.md)

## Fixes

1. **Block — a value-reading sender is unnecessary for the funding signal.** The decision, item 4, says “Only an agent outside the model, keying a setting to a value, makes them signal, and the classification's force rests on that standard (A296).” In the 9/4 apparatus the short holder has **one loose distinction** and the other has **two**. Bob can supply the short holder or the other holder using just this structural difference. With Alice fixed, her birth probability is 1/2 versus 0. The external choice of which instruction to use is still supplied. Nothing in this result makes the chooser native, and nothing requires it to read a distinction's assigned value. H0 prohibits value-dependent births but permits a decision based on holdings. The fixed-address hiding result stands. The 20/9 example has a value-keyed test, separately. Readiness labels and private payload bits are sampled in advance. Payload is disclosed only on a jointly funded trial, otherwise both return zero. The flag and bit give identical far marginals under every compulsory policy on the constant pre-records. Correlations are $(1,7/8,7/8,5/8)$, CHSH 17/8. Row 8 also obtains a quantum-compatible 8/3 by a supplied 5-to-1 weighting, with endogenous selectors. Row 23 *does* record an adaptive test, giving zero for the coherent apparatus and 1/4 for the uniform central rule with identical fresh probabilities. The claim that positive ordinary pair counts reproduce a particular quantum prescription remains scoped to the stated positive uniform preparation and real questions. 3. **Block — absence of a common allowed word does not characterize probabilistic nonlocality.** The README says “In the literature a table is nonlocal exactly when its local pieces fit together pairwise but admit no state of the whole (Abramsky and Brandenburger 2011, the sheaf-theoretic form of Fine's theorem).” The four-type 20/9 binary table alone refutes the assertion at the level of allowed words. Every cell is positive. All 16 deterministic four-answer lists are supported, yet no nonnegative distribution on them has the four given marginals. The literature distinguishes absence of a global *probability distribution* from absence of any globally supported assignment. H1 is a further distinction still, concerning an old or finished physical word in the actual protocol. Even a PR table has outcomes at each input. A296's “(2) is the form every recorded table has” cannot describe the very part-counting rows that the inventory excludes. Its “the whole-stage 9/4 is kind (b) only under (1)” should say that its *signalling verdict* changes with the allowed policies. Funding depends on both settings under either convention. I then read its README, decision, inventory, both reviews, code, both output files, A240, A296 and the source records. The first review proposed the three explanations.
