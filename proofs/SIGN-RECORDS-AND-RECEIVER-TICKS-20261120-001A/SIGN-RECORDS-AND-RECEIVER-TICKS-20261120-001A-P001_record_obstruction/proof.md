# A physical history record and the obstruction to reading a relative sign

Status: Proved for the classes below. Independent scientific referee pending.

## Standard statement

With two equally probable ordinary words $u,v$ and the fixed change $t=u+v$, the supplied real sign rule distinguishes $(|u\rangle+|v\rangle)/\sqrt2$ from $(|u\rangle-|v\rangle)/\sqrt2$ with certainty. An ordinary interaction among four lawful knots can separately encode the order of earlier changes in a memory and deliver it to a fixed ordinary reader under either knot law. An exact four-preparation encoding even gives both signed targets and both fair data-only singleton readouts in the prescribed one-read tests. At fixed mathematical sign its reader follows the retained memory. At fixed physical history externally reversing the signed input leaves the reader distribution unchanged. For the named ordinary-history and complete-instrument classes below this failure is necessary. The exact remaining requirement for a real pair and an affine event is a nonzero real off-diagonal matrix entry. The experiment and proof do not exclude a different knot interaction with a different source of such entries.

None of these results tests temporal correlations with an actual retained memory, a fresh signed comparison at fixed history, or the complete set of positive response effects compatible with the observed ordinary records. The proof below is elementary. Known resource-theoretic characterizations of operations that reveal coherence are contextual comparisons, and the proof uses no external theorem. ## 1. A physical order bit under both laws

Give D, M, W and R two ordinary marks apiece. The selected preparation sets the first and second of each to $(d,1-d)$, $(m,1-m)$, $(w,1-w)$ and $(r,1-r)$, and prepares three further marks $j=k=l=1$. Place the holdings

$$ (d,\bar d,j),\quad(d,\bar d,m,\bar m),\quad(m,\bar m,w,\bar w,r,\bar r,k),\quad(r,\bar r,l). $$

The bars in this display name different physical marks. Their complementary values describe a preparation. They are not additional equations in the ordinary laws. Every one of the sixteen encoded $d,m,w,r$ assignments satisfies even parity and not-all-equal. The four rows have respectively 2, 2, 4 and 2 ones on the code. Outside the preparation each named pair occurs together in exactly the same holders. Swapping the two marks therefore preserves every old law on every word. Conditioning such a swap on another ordinary mark in the same holder remains an involution whenever the control is itself unchanged. The conditional swaps and their addresses are supplied gates. The canonical growth law does not choose when to fire them.

Let $F$ swap D. Let $C$ swap M when $d=1$, and $B$ swap D when $m=1$. Define $P,V$ to swap R when $m=1$ or $w=1$, respectively; each is a separate gate. Let $L$ copy M into an initially zero W by the analogous controlled swap. On the selected preparations these give

$$F(d,m)=(1-d,m),\quad C(d,m)=(d,m+d),\quad B(d,m)=(d+m,m),\quad P:r\mapsto r+m,\quad V:r\mapsto r+w.$$

All Boolean sums are modulo two, and operations in a listed sequence are chronological. Then $FFCC$ is the identity and $FCFC$ gives $(d,m+1)$. Each uses exactly two offers of each gate. Both return D, so the first data mark retains its full original distribution. With $d$ initially fair and $m=w=r=0$, the primary event “R's first mark is 0 before the read and 1 afterwards” under the common reader $P$ has probabilities zero and one. The ordered control reaches M through the second knot, and reaches the R mark through the third. The R pair also belongs to the fourth knot. No sign, program identifier or scoring convention is present in that read. The difference was already observable at M at the cut. This is a constructive example of an ordinary history causing a readable state change. It does not give identical full ordinary word frequencies at the cut. The initials, holdings, actuator roster and the reader are the same.

If the initial M is independently fair and W is left at zero, both programs act by permutations of the four equally weighted $(d,m)$ words. Every single full snapshot, including intermediate ones and the reader after $P$, has the same law. The two-time ordinary property $m_{\rm initial}=m_{\rm cut}$ nevertheless has probability one under $FFCC$ and zero under $FCFC$. Thus equality of all one-time laws is insufficient for equality of a history. An observer's transcript can calculate this property. To give it a physical carrier instead, first apply $L$ with $w=0$. At the cut each individual M and W mark is still fair in both arms, but their joint support consists of equal pairs in one arm and opposite pairs in the other. Applying $P,V$ to the same ready reader realizes $r=m_{\rm cut}+w$. It reads out this history difference perfectly. Without W or access to the old M, the corresponding deterministic response to the full current word has equal laws. With W, all one-mark marginals of the data and apparatus at the cut can agree while a joint ordinary read distinguishes the arms. This is the retained information being tested.

## 2. An exact encoded imitation, and a test it fails

The exchange $CBC$ maps $(d,m)$ to $(m,d)$. On the common initial two-word mixture it makes $d=0,m$ fair. A further $F$ makes $d=1,m$ fair. On these data-only singleton preparations the same reader is fair. The sole random bit came from the original D. If the complete data and memory word is fixed, the reader is deterministic: it fires if and only if $m=1$. An independently supplied coin would give another result. Initializing R at one also changes the observable operation: its final occupation may switch, but “zero before and one after” then never occurs.

There is a genuine finite operational correspondence. With one fixed read, $m=0,1$ and $d$ fair reproduce the two reference change eigenstates, and $d=0,1$ and $m$ fair reproduce the two ordinary eigenstates. $F$, $FCFC$ and $CBC$ act on these four preparation classes as the usual real two-word $X,Z,H$ do, up to global phase. This follows by applying their displayed Boolean maps: $F$ exchanges the ordinary eigenstates, $FCFC$ exchanges the change eigenstates, and $CBC$ exchanges the two readings. Their compositions give eight distinct permutations of these four preparations, including both independent sign flips and the exchange of the two readings. In elementary counting terms there are two choices of which complementary pair of two preparations comes first and two choices of the first member in each pair. In contrast, $F,C,B$ together permute the four individual $(d,m)$ words in all 24 ways: $C,B$ generate the six invertible binary linear maps, and conjugates of $F$ generate the four translations. Exactly 8 preserve the chosen four preparation classes. The physical actions match both independent mathematical one-read probabilities in all $8\times4\times2=64$ comparisons. This is a restricted encoding. A law for sequential measurement disturbance is not supplied by this correspondence.

To tell this encoding from the signed weighting on *the same two ordinary words*, fix $m=w=r=0$ and let $u,v$ differ only by the D swap. Fresh externally supplied plus and minus weights have exactly the same ordinary word frequencies. Acting on basis words with either $FFCC$ or $FCFC$ returns D unchanged. On the two externally signed inputs the same gate sequence simply transports the two nonzero coefficients to the corresponding words with a common fixed memory. Their relative sign remains the one supplied initially. In particular a uniformly positive initial pair leaves a positive pair under **both** preparations, even though R subsequently writes different ordinary marks. Reversing the relative sign at a fixed schedule changes the signed answer without changing the pointer. Holding the sign fixed and changing the schedule changes the pointer without changing the signed answer. Negating both amplitudes together changes neither. There is therefore no independently generated relative minus in this pair experiment. A different preparation rule relating that sign to an ordinary register would require its own physical test.

## 3. Exactly what the whole-history obstruction assumes

Let the retained ordinary record $h$ at a cut include every accessible prior state, fired or refused action, member of the apparatus, current holding, past observations and correlation with any randomness that can enter a subsequent response. Histories that need not be retained can equivalently be represented by their conditional distribution given this record. Let $\mu_+,\mu_-$ be the two distributions of $h$. For a fixed future setting policy and output event $A$, denote the conditional probabilities by $k_+(h),k_-(h)$. Then

$$\Pr_+(A)-\Pr_-(A)=\sum_h (\mu_+(h)-\mu_-(h))k_+(h)+\sum_h\mu_-(h)(k_+(h)-k_-(h)).$$

Versions at zero-mass records can be chosen consistently or the formula read on the union of the two supports. When the same ordinary reader acts conditionally on that complete record, $k_+=k_-=k$. The greatest possible absolute event difference is then at most $\|\mu_+-\mu_-\|_{\rm TV}$. If a reader can implement all indicators of the accessible record, the bound is attained by selecting those records with positive difference. If only the new fixed pointer is allowed, its own weighted sum is the exact answer. If the retained record laws and common conditional continuation agree, the entire law of future observations agrees. This remains true under a finite adaptive sequence. Condition on its previous recorded answers, include the selected ordinary policy and use the same transition kernel to extend the joint measure one step at a time. A pre-existing correlation with a hidden random seed is part of the initial record for this claim. Selective conditioning requires reporting the probability of success and the joint success/answer probability first. Their ratio may be undefined if the selection never occurs.

This statement does not assume that equal current word distributions imply equal histories. The physically executed two-time tests above show exactly where the extra record is retained, forgotten or unavailable. An alternative physical law referring to retained history or unmodelled information could distinguish the programs. Any claim that it distinguishes the *relative sign* also needs an independent preparation mechanism and an identified coupling from that difference to its ordinary event.

## 4. An exact boundary for the coherent two-word comparison

Suppose the final binary result is an affine quantum event on a fixed input pair, so that a single effect $0\le E\le I$ gives its probability. This assumption is imposed only on the mathematical comparison. Write $p=E_{uu}$, $q=E_{vv}$ and $z=E_{uv}$. Its two event chances on the real plus and minus preparations are $(p+q)/2\pm\operatorname{Re}z$. Their difference is $2\operatorname{Re}z$. Both $E$ and its complementary effect must be positive. The two determinants give

$$ |\Pr_+(A)-\Pr_-(A)|\le 2\min\{\sqrt{pq},\sqrt{(1-p)(1-q)}\}. $$

This interval is sharp for fixed $p,q\in[0,1]$. Every real $z$ between the negative and positive smaller square roots gives valid effects on the pair. A purely imaginary $z$ does not distinguish these real preparations. A nonzero contrast does not force fair singleton answers. The real projector with entries $(4,2;2,1)/5$ has signed probabilities $9/10,1/10$ and singleton probabilities $4/5,1/5$. If one signed input gives certainty and the other impossibility, positivity gives $E|+\rangle=|+\rangle$ and $E|-\rangle=0$ for the chosen event orientation. Thus it is the plus projector on their span and it gives exactly $1/2$ on **both** basis singletons. Reversing the event interchanges the two eigenstates. This fairness is forced by one independently fixed affine effect acting on all four preparations. Ordinary memory-dependent encodings may instead change the physical preparation of their hidden register between those cases.

A deterministic ordinary event table gives an especially strong obstruction. If either singleton probability is exactly zero or one, the bound forces $z=0$ on this pair, whatever the rest of an affine realization does. In a native switch that takes one loaded donor to an already loaded receiver's identical terminal word, the terminal singleton event is certain for both initial words. Its effect cannot become sign-sensitive on their span while honoring both ordinary point-mass calibrations. The entire chronological record from the already preloaded input is one fixed transcript. For any event of those transcripts its probability on that input is zero or one. The same argument excludes sign sensitivity for each such event, including probabilistic schedules from the other input, when one normalized affine event respects those basis data. This pair provides a native merging test. Its receiver was loaded initially in one arm, so it is distinct from the matched-ready-reader preparation in section 1.

## 5. Which ordinary operations can compose

For a full instrument with classical outcome $a$ and matrices $K_{a,j}$, a future ordinary-event effect $D$ is pulled back to $\sum_j K_{a,j}^\dagger D K_{a,j}$. If this is diagonal for every diagonal $D$ and every accessible outcome, the same remains true after a finite sequence, including adaptive choice by previous ordinary outcomes. Start from the final diagonal output test and work backwards, summing the branch contributions. A signed ordinary-word permutation has at most one nonzero entry per output row. It has this property, including any weighting that merely multiplies a word by a phase. Ordinary projective inspections preserve it. A classical stochastic transition can always keep its old/next alternatives as separate branches and therefore also has it even when several old words reach the same new word. Growth retaining the identities and values of all old distinctions has disjoint sets of output words for different old words. A coherent lift with arbitrary coefficients *within those disjoint sets* preserves the condition. Ordinary forgetting or incoherent deletion of the old labels cannot make the effect off-diagonal. This argument includes arbitrary ordinary ancillas through their joint word distributions and private randomness. An independent coherent ancillary preparation followed exclusively by monomial operations still cannot feed data phase into an ordinary pointer. Initial system–apparatus correlations not fixed by the stated preparations must be declared before applying the result.

There is a useful strengthening for complete nonnegative linear instruments. If **all** entries of a full family of real Kraus matrices $K_s$ are nonnegative, trace preservation requires $\sum_{s,y}K_s[y,u]K_s[y,v]=0$ for $u\ne v$. Every summand is nonnegative, so no common pair $(s,y)$ has positive contributions from both old words. For the resolved paths each output row of each matrix therefore has at most one nonzero old-word entry. The diagonal pullback property just proved applies. A sole trace-decreasing positive matrix can evade this conclusion. For example $K=|y\rangle(\langle u|+\langle v|)/\sqrt2$ by itself has success probabilities $1,0,1/2,1/2$ on the two signed states and two singletons. Its complementary operation is essential. One complete realization has a separate pointer outcome with row $(\langle u|-\langle v|)/\sqrt2$. This supplies an interfering operation, not an ordinary merge deduced from observing equal terminal words. Without the normalization, mapping both input words coherently to $|y\rangle$ gives total mass 2 on plus and zero on minus, despite unit mass at each singleton. Reading only accepted runs could conceal that failure. The recorded exact comparisons keep unconditional and joint selection probabilities as well as any defined conditional answers.

For an ordinary apparatus whose accessible conditional rules are sign-blind, the complete joint record is generated from the distribution of old words and any actually retained records. When the external change is solely a sign on otherwise identical physical input and history, these distributions are equal, and every fixed ordinary output remains equal. This is a proof for the stated record class and a separate diagonal-effect criterion for stated coherent extensions. It is not a restriction imposed on all conceivable knot laws.

## 6. Transfer and an explicit operation beyond the boundary

On the four-word preparation with D and M both initially in equally weighted pairs, let their independent supplied relative signs be $s$ and $b$. The coefficient of $(d,m)$ is $s^d b^m/2$. Measuring neither word, extend the actual conditional swap $C$ by ordinary linear transport. Its output coefficient is $s^d b^{m+d}/2=(sb)^d b^m/2$. The data's new relative sign is therefore $sb$. This transfers the sign of a previously supplied negative weight on M. Positive input on both registers stays positive. The ordinary pointer $P$ still fires on half the words in all four signed preparations. A rule permitting such coherent transport alone has thus neither prepared minus from this positive source nor detected the transferred sign as an ordinary event. This is the literature's phase kickback, realized on these particular legal word pairs. [Cleve, Ekert, Macchiavello and Mosca](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9708016)

A separately supplied normalized sum-and-difference operation on D maps $(a,b)$ to $(a+b,a-b)/\sqrt2$. Start M and R ready. The physical $C,P$ gates then copy the resulting ordinary D orientation through M to R. The ordinary pointer fires with probabilities $0,1,1/2,1/2$ on plus, minus and the two singletons. This is a valid calibrated positive comparison. The new sign-sensitive ingredient was the mixing operation, identified before the read. The earlier signed-witness expansion also obtains a knot indicator from algebraic cancellation, without generating or reading that relative sign in an ordinary trajectory. The mathematical background on channels detecting coherence distinguishes creating, transmitting and detecting it. Here the additional evidence is the actual dual-law wiring, complete history comparisons, and the bounds that the archived native merger imposes on an extension of its ordinary event. [Theurer, Egloff, Zhang and Plenio](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07332)
