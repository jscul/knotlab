# Counting from a clock’s own origin

On a complete funded cut take parent order as the integer grading by actual growth. An original holding or a later, physically recognizable cut can anchor a pair of returns. On the named class a clock has a recoverable pair of nonretracing return words A, B with the same ordered overlap in the two copies, a distinguished original occurring exactly once in B and absent from A, and two distinct endpoint deletions as the **entire** parent list of every interval in their concatenations. Its source excludes a doubled word whose half contains that mark. The growing all-A intervals keep the source productive. The reached cut and the intrinsic word test are part of this class. Unmarked unlabeled coincidences or an age supplied by an observer cannot qualify a packet. [Earlier physical return proof](../GENERATORS-20261120-001/source.md)

Let a and b be the lengths of the two returns in original occurrences and h the order of the anchor. A clock tick is the first appearance of all phases of the repeating word AᵏB, k≥1. Its primitive length is p=ka+b. Its private mark appears once in each period. No earlier window of length 2p−1 can contain a doubled word through the private mark. At length 2p every phase does. Each of its two actual parents still belongs to the source. The freshly released phases last by their two genuine deletion parents. At parent order t the tick exists precisely when t≥h+2(ka+b)−1. The number already released is

$$
C(t;h,a,b)=\max\left(0,\left\lfloor\frac{t-h+1-2b}{2a}\right\rfloor\right).
$$

At equal age t−h, identical words and event give identical counts. After startup, raising the age by 2a adds one tick. At one common cut, write the difference of anchor orders as 2ak+r, 0≤r<2a. The difference of counts is k or k+1, with only k attained if r=0. A shift of the age argument by the true difference of origins aligns every tick. Whole numbers displayed simultaneously can alternate. There is no growing discrepancy on this class. The same argument applies to the nested Qₛ with a=b=s and gives the earlier 16- and 24-step periods. Count differences between *unequal* clocks can grow linearly. ## Which separation changes by one

Let d(X,Y) be minimum witnessed separation over the actual present members on the same cut. Give empty or disconnected pairs infinite separation. Write I for all actual children of a set, P for all actual parents, and B for one witnessed step including staying at a member. On a complete funded continuing step, PI=B on the old cut and IP=B on the new one. A witnessed pair is covered by an actual group, and a next-cut witness between different children is an actual common parent. Consequently a route between old members compresses by one on taking *all* their descendants, and a subsequent route can be lifted through its intervening common parents. These are the previously proved end identities. [Ordinary witnessed identities](../INFLUENCE-CONE-20261120-001/cones_proof.md#the-two-parent-maps) · [First common descendant and the one-pure-party inequality](../COPIES-AND-MEETINGS-20261120-001/propagation.md)

- **Two disjoint pure continuing sets.** A route of k>0 between their new members supplies k successive actual shared parents. Its first parent already belongs to the old first party, its last to the old second. Consecutive such parents coincide or occur in one genuine group and so are witnessed neighbors. Hence d(old)≤k−1. Coinciding children would force an old overlap. If an old minimizing pair each has a child in its own continuing family, take those children and a shortest old route. Choose one real co-child of each neighboring old pair. These co-children and the chosen two ends make a new route of at most d(old)+1. Thus **d increases by exactly one** under this productivity condition and finite positive separation. Without it only the lower bound follows. Infinite separation stays infinite. Old overlapping sets can still overlap or separate; all-parent persistence subsequently forbids reconvergence after separation.
- **One pure party and the other’s inclusive descendants.** Exactly d(X′,IY)=d(PX′,Y), since a new route of at most j into IY is equivalent to meeting I(BʲY) and hence to a parent in BʲY. Purity PX′⊆X gives the published nondecrease. If every old X participates in X′ the distance stays exactly constant. With no purity, following both old parties by all their descendants gives exactly max(d−1,0), also through repeated steps. A closed final parity knot can itself be a joint output without a future live end.
- **Two lasting open parties.** Internal parents at each new member and a continuing child for every old member give max(d−1,0)≤d′≤d+1. The upper bound uses the same old shortest route and internal children. For the lower bound prepend and append the selected old internal parents to the shared-parent witnesses of a new route. The two ends of each old link are actual candidates. Equality cases −1,0,+1 occur among the *current members* of physically identifiable returning rings; see the literal phase proof. - **Two present Q parties.** At each common size m≥2 max(s,r) the all-U₀ members of both survive. For the four-step neutral return and distinct spacings s,r, a shared word has at most one marked role. Without a mark it has at most min(s,r)−1 letters. At an interior common b₀ the straight forward extensions on either side first disagree min(s,r)−1 positions away; the shorter word reaches s or t while the longer remains on its neutral return. Reversing one orientation already disagrees beside an interior mark. The maximal common word, attained on all-U₀ in both, has L=2 min(s,r)−3 letters. This is an upper bound even allowing arbitrary binary choices before the exclusion. Lifting a witnessed route through its real shared deletion parents can remove at most one original occurrence per step. Intersections of two straight lifted words are consecutive: at least m−L steps are needed even through branched knots.
