## Do pair counts or rates supply the same datum?

When all defining not-all-equal rules are ternary, the sum of three pairwise disagreement rates is 2 exactly when the three distinctions are never all equal: every mixed triple contributes two disagreements, while every constant triple contributes zero. All such triples, together, recover the allowed set. Rates suffice at every width in that class, and raw counts suffice from four distinctions by the earlier theorem. The six-name example shows that even full recovery of the state set can leave the physical holdings ambiguous. [Ternary theorem and proof](../../proofs/DISTANCE-ORDER-20261117-001A/DISTANCE-ORDER-20261117-001A-P001_betweenness/proof.md)

For **wider lawful irredundant rules**, eight names give a concrete failure for both raw counts and uniform rates. Use the four supports \(0123,0456,1457,2367\), and compare their images after exchanging names 1 and 6. Each preparation allows 154 words; they differ on 16 words in each direction. For instance, the word whose ones are at 0, 1 and 2 belongs only to the first. Every one of the 28 named pair counts is unchanged, hence every uniform disagreement rate agrees. Both are closed and the permutation explains their identical unmarked structure. The full census found 630 named pair tables with two different named state sets here, all renamings of this example. At the other checked widths both raw counts and rates determine the named state within this population. Therefore either pair reading happens to suffice for **growth through the stated initial size bound**, including the closed six- and eight-name cases. The wider-arity example already defeats full reconstruction of named states from pairs alone.
