# Eliminate a bounded number of fresh Boolean product names

Status: Proved

## Standard statement

Consider syntactic resolution over degree-at-most-two formal polynomials over \(\mathbb F_2\), with the Boolean domain axioms and multiset disjunctions. A refutation may introduce \(k\) fresh names in order, each with its defining axiom \([t+fg=0]\), where \(f,g\) are affine forms in the original \(n\) variables and earlier names. There is a refutation of the same CNF with no names, still in degree two, of at most \(128\,2^k(n+k+1)(S+1)\) nodes for an \(S\)-node named refutation. The exponent counts declarations, including unused ones. Syntactic weakening may append a finite list in a single node, and a contraction removes one repeated occurrence.

## Nearest known result

Conditioning on a Boolean choice and joining the complementary cases are standard proof transformations. Pang's system provides the underlying deductions, and the earlier local affine simulation constructs Boolean excluded middle for affine forms. Algebraic extension systems in the literature include substantially different rules, fields and size measures. Here the delta is an explicit unnamed degree-two conversion of reusable affine product declarations through branch-dependent substitutions, with a bound on DAG nodes independent of the widths of the disjunctions. ## Proof

Write \([a]\) for \([a=0]\), and \(E_a=[a]\lor[a+1]\). A case cut on opposite equations in two copies of the same multiset \(H\) takes one addition producing the false constant, one deletion, and \(|H|\) contractions. This count holds even if either selected equation already occurs elsewhere in \(H\). A variable's \(E_x\) is an axiom. To obtain \(E_{f+g}\) from \(E_f,E_g\), choose \(f+i\) and \(g+j\) in the two parents for each \(i,j\in\{0,1\}\). Add the chosen forms and weaken to \(E_{f+g}\) with the two opposite choices as side equations. Cut first the two alternatives for \(j\), then those for \(i\). No multiplication is needed. Thus the Boolean disjunction for an affine form on \(n\) inputs takes at most \(32(n+1)\) nodes. A constant uses the true axiom and weakening. A constant term on a nonconstant form exchanges its two disjuncts.

Construct a binary case tree in declaration order. Along a path all earlier names have been replaced by affine forms in the base variables. At the next declaration replace those names in the factors, obtaining affine \(f,g\). Split on \(f=c\) for \(c=0,1\) and replace \(t\) by \(cg\) on that branch. Each child has a different substitution, and later splits may accordingly concern different forms. The boolean condition under which a branch fails is the equation \([f+c+1]\). Let \(H\) be the list of the \(k\) such failure equations on a complete branch. The splits cover all original assignments even when one branch is inconsistent or two split forms coincide.

On a leaf replay every original proof node, with all names substituted, under the common disjunction \(H\). The image of a defining axiom is \([(f+c)g]\). Multiply the equation \([f+c]\) of the derived \(E_f\) by \(g\), retaining \([f+c+1]\), then weaken by the remaining members of \(H\). A Boolean axiom for a substituted name is now a Boolean disjunction for an affine form, handled above. The original CNF axioms and the true axiom weaken directly. Every unary deduction commutes with the substitution under \(H\). For a binary linear combination use the chosen occurrences from the two replayed premises. Contract one additional copy of \(H\), preserving all other multiplicities. Ring homomorphism gives the specified rule result, including formal squares and constants. Each leaf derives \(H\) because the last original line was empty.

Merge sibling leaves on their opposite last condition, keeping precisely their common prefix. Repeat to the root, which derives the empty disjunction from the base CNF alone. Per leaf an input, declaration or Boolean row costs at most \(32(n+1)+3\) nodes, while an ordinary deduction costs at most \(k+1\). Fewer than \(2^k\) internal tree nodes each cost at most \(k+2\). These bounds imply the displayed estimate. The construction may reuse input nodes. Substitution expands each displayed monomial to at most \((n+1)^2\) terms before cancellation and adds only \(k\) disjuncts per leaf line. Conversion costs \(2^k\) times a polynomial in the explicit named proof and base input lengths. A very wide line costs more bits to write even while counting as one node. ∎

The degree-two lower bound admitted for \(8n\) independent uniform strict ternary clauses is \(\exp(cn)\) with high probability for a constant \(c>0\). The bound here therefore implies \(S\ge \exp(cn)/(128\,2^k(n+k+1))-1\). In particular \(k=o(n)\) preserves an exponential node lower bound. At \(k=O(\log n)\) the conversion itself has polynomial overhead. These are sufficient conditions measured in original source variables. Applying them to guarded names uses the separate literal-transport theorem and preserves the declaration count. A large value of this upper bound for elimination cannot prove the existence of short proofs.
