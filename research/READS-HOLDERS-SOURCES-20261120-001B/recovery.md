# Reading irredundant holdings from states

Let \(S\) be the allowed words on a fixed named set of distinctions. An ordinary not-all-equal holder is lawful only if its projection is **all** the nonconstant words on its support. Call a holder essential if deleting just its rule enlarges the allowed set. The carrier, including labels newly held nowhere, is fixed throughout.

## What the full relation can read

For each set \(E\) of at least three names, retain it as a candidate exactly when \(S|_E\) consists of all nonconstant words. This uses no upper limit other than the number of names in \(S\). Every physical holder is a candidate, and every candidate’s rule holds throughout \(S\). Thus imposing all candidates gives precisely \(S\). Every candidate is inclusion-minimal among sets that can never be constant: for any proper subset, a mixed word on the candidate can keep that subset all zero. A minimal set that cannot be constant may have a stronger projection, which is why the full-projection test is used.

Remove one rule from the complete candidate family, allowing that temporary family to exceed the physical two-holder limit. If a forbidden word now becomes allowed, that rule is **mandatory in every physical presentation**: every other candidate allows that word. If the mandatory rules by themselves give \(S\), an irredundant presentation must consist of exactly those rules. Additional holders would be implied. This is a sufficient test for a unique presentation. The physical multiplicity and connectedness gates can rule out alternatives even when the test returns no verdict. It also explains why counting saturated presentations answers a different question. An essential presentation may be recovered exactly when the states imply additional candidate rules. [Previous saturation study and correction](../STATES-GROWTH-20261119-001/referee-R1/report.md)

One can sometimes read a mandatory support from a single forbidden word \(w\). Write down precisely the names at which flipping that one bit makes the word allowed. If the resulting set \(H\) is a candidate, every candidate violated at \(w\) must contain \(H\): otherwise some one-bit repair would leave it violated. Candidates cannot properly contain one another. Hence \(H\) is the unique candidate violated at that word and is mandatory. The reverse need not hold. At seven, eight and nine distinctions the deletion test recovers cases where the one-bit tests alone leave some candidate unforced. Across 690 state classes up to renaming, deletion of candidates certifies the unique physical presentation in 689. The remaining class is the actual six-distinction named ambiguity. ## Two classes at every finite size

**Each holder has a private distinction.** Pin an arbitrary set \(T\) of names to zero. If \(T\) contains no whole holder, set every other shared distinction to one. A holder all of whose private names were pinned already has a zero and an outside shared one. At every other holder, a still-unpinned private distinction lets it take both values. If all its places are unpinned and private, assign two of them different values. These choices are independent because private names belong to just one holder. Thus \(T\) can be constant exactly when it contains no full holding. The physical supports are incomparable and are exactly the minimal sets that cannot be constant. They can be reconstructed from the states alone. More directly, each is essential: start with an allowed state in which a private distinction is the only one of its value on that holder (full projection guarantees it), and flip it. Only that holder fails. No competing lawful irredundant presentation can omit it or add another rule. The sufficient private-distinction condition can disappear after one birth step. The four ternaries reached from one ternary still have four mandatory supports, although the original holder has no private distinction and the six new names supply an extra candidate. **A class that ordinary growth preserves.** Suppose no three distinct holders pairwise meet, and each holder keeps at least two distinctions outside every one other holder. This includes all ordinary holder trees having one distinction per meeting. It also admits repeated shared distinctions if each holder has enough other names. Then the allowed states determine every physical holder among *all* lawful irredundant presentations. The same conclusion holds at every later stage, even when only the states of that later stage are supplied.

Here is the slightly more general sufficient test behind the proof. Fix one holder H. Erase its entire support temporarily. Require each remaining holder to retain at least two distinctions. Treat a remaining loose distinction as having an extra unconstrained individual holder only for this calculation. Suppose no resulting connected part consists solely of an odd closed sequence in which every holder has exactly two distinctions. Assign the two values to the remaining distinctions so that each original surviving holder sees both. The literature’s two-edge-colouring lemma gives exactly this assignment. A direct proof pairs consecutive distinctions in a walk using each once. If some holders have odd counts, introduce one temporary holder meeting each of them once. All counts become even. Begin the walk at that temporary holder and alternate values. Removing an added distinction from an originally odd holder with at least three leaves an intact opposite-valued pair. If all counts were even to begin with, start at a holder with at least four places, where an intact opposite-valued pair remains even when the first and last colours agree. If all counts are two, an even closed sequence alternates and the odd one is precisely the exception. Components with a temporary loose endpoint of count one fall under the first case. This uses established mathematics. What it adds here is a certificate for recovering the original physical rule. [Bondy and Murty, *Graph Theory with Applications*, Lemma 6.1.1](https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/teaching/WS1314/BondyMurtyGTWA.pdf)

Restore the names of H and give them all zero. This word violates H alone. Flipping any one of its names repairs H; every other holder retains both values away from H. Flipping a name outside H leaves H violated. Thus the single-word test recovers H. Under the stated no-three-pairwise-meet condition the exceptional component cannot occur: every surviving holder of count two there must have lost a distinction to H, since originally all counts were at least three. Two consecutive holders of the component meet each other and both meet H. This contradicts the hypothesis. Therefore every H is recovered.

The class persists. All existing physical supports keep their literal distinctions. A newborn meets its old parents once each and has at least three places under not-all-equal. Thus all old–new pairs retain at least two distinctions at either holder outside the other. Distinct newborns share no distinction. If two parents of one newborn already met each other, their required common witness in the old structure would give three pairwise meeting old holders. Hence adding that newborn introduces no such triple. A stalled source stays as it was. Induction establishes the state-only reading at every reached stage in this class. It supplies a guarantee even when the observer has forgotten the seed. The extra sufficient class was identified after the freeze from the advisor's candidate test and the coordinator’s one-bit repair test, with the original definitions retained. ## What one complete step preserves more generally

For one rule \(C_H\), write the conjunction of all others as \(T\). A new global word after erasure is exactly a word in \(T\) whose projection violates \(C_H\). Such a local word belongs to \(T|_H\) but not \((T\wedge C_H)|_H\). Conversely, a new local word has a full completion after erasure, and that completion was forbidden before. Global and local irredundance agree under either knot law, at any size, with this fixed-carrier definition. They are not the separate question of whether the original projection was the full elementary law.

**Not-all-equal preserves irredundance.** Every actual nonempty birth receives at least one fresh distinction, and there is an allowed old word in which all the old inputs to any one birth are equal. Give those inputs and all fresh positions of that birth the same value. Every other newborn can be satisfied independently at a fresh position. Thus the new rule is essential. For an old essential rule, take an old word violating it alone. Keeping only that old rule erased, satisfy every new rule at its own fresh position, extending the same witness. An old implied rule remains implied because the remaining old rules already suffice. This proves preservation at every parent step of the ordinary law, independently of whether the initial presentation is recoverable. The equal-input lemma uses at most one loose input per parent and leaves it at least two old positions. [Earlier nonimplication proof](../NAE-GROWTH-20261119-001/nae_growth.md#why-it-holds)

**Parity.** A zero sum of physical parity equations must take both holders of each shared distinction or neither. Connectedness forces the selected holders to be all or none. Selecting all leaves exactly the set of loose names. As long as there is a loose distinction, all rows are independent and each is essential. If there are none, their sum is zero and *each* rule follows from the others. A single ternary grows three one-parent births and then three two-parent births; its next sole three-parent birth closes. In its eight-holder closed structure every rule is implied. This gives an explicit failure of preservation for parity. Previously proved independent switches have loose distinctions in every arm, so the unbounded lower bound on additional bits for parity growth survives this restriction.
