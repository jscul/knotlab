## The answer

The direction asked two things of the structures the recorded laws grow: how the number of knots within r steps of a
knot grows with r, and whether the growing end carries a distance of its own.

1. **What exists at one round is of degree 1.** The members of a part within r steps of a member number about a
   multiple of r, or the whole part is within reach, in every run read under the three capped laws (1,700 and 169
   readings; 283 undecided; none of power 2 or more).
2. **The count over the whole history adds one degree, which is the rounds.** Counting every knot ever made within r
   holder steps, no run passes the registered test for one power from every knot examined at 2 or more with a range
   that lengthens. On 21 runs the test is passed at power 1 (the bare rings and a few small starts, all with constant
   births). On 54 runs of the capped laws the count reads power 2 from every knot examined at the longer length
   grown, and under looser thresholds 111 runs pass at power 2. Where the number of parts is fixed, a power of the
   history count is the roster's power plus one from the rounds, so this 2 is the degree-1 roster read through its
   history. The negative answer at 2 rests on the registered thresholds, which are supplied.
3. **Births per round are of degree 1 on most starts once short knots retire, and the cap stops only the faster
   ones.** Under the ordinary count 145 of 469 starts end and 23 have births of degree 2 or 3. With retiring and no
   cap 112 of the 145 grow at degree 1 and the 23 keep their degree. The cap of three leaves 9 at degree 2 and none
   at 3; a cap of 2 leaves none above 1. So retiring does most of "births in step with the round", and the cap bounds
   the rest. Two predictions about this failed: a cap of three does not remove every start of degree 2 (9 remain),
   and the share of degree 1 without a cap is 0.666, a hair under the two thirds predicted.
4. **The growing end has grading but no order of its own.** Under parity two loose distinctions are tied or
   unrelated (argued; checked on 29,599 stages), so there is no grading at all between single pairs; under a taking
   rule a rule among a few loose distinctions does appear (1,602 stages). Under not-all-equal, how strongly two
   members' loose distinctions are related is graded, and with loose counts held equal it still differs; but it does
   not keep the order of the members' arrival delay, of witnessed steps or of holder steps, from a stage of 7 knots
   up. Under the ordinary count the arrival delay equals the witnessed steps on all 74,327 pairs compared.
5. **Laws with a taking rule make no hubs that hold a structure together**: in no run does the knot count double
   while the greatest holder steps stay still.

Fifteen predictions were judged: ten hold, four fail (two about caps, one clause about the bare rings that fails by
arithmetic, one about which power the capped laws read at the shorter length), and one is undecided because a clause
is tested on no stage.
