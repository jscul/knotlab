# The ordinary parent map when disjoint triples have different witnesses

**Claim.** In every enrolled clock start the finite objects below, of rank n, are in bijection with the ordinary live knots of original age n−1. Its true parents are all its connected immediate predecessors containing an original live point. Its loose supply after birth is its number of connected immediate extensions. Both knot laws give this same map. The original single-gate C case is already covered by the earlier held-block identification; the mixed cases have a true triple in the first round and another unheld triple among some children in the next. This proof establishes the map for those mixed presentations too. The point and rank representations below serve to derive the ordinary groups. They introduce no growth or payment rule. ## Original roles and allowed holdings

Use one point per live original. Each ordinary two-donor pair is a block of two. Both ways of connecting u,v,w give a *single marked block of three* in this mathematical representation. For C, one spent witness physically meets all three. For S, three spent witnesses meet the separate pairs. The two presentations give the same candidate pairs. The rank rule below retains which presentation was used. All three-point blocks are disjoint sets of donors, and their w points meet no other such block. Apart from branches carrying extra loose names, replace each successive block by a node adjoining its member points and unfold this incidence structure to its universal covering tree. Every nontrivial closed reduced route of blocks has length at least four: erasing the terminal w at a marked block recovers the original triangle-free candidate system and never shortens an original route. The minimum is attained on a four-role return in either clock. Names at unrolled points project to their original roles. Points above one role need not be the same occurrence.

The first ordinary groups are three pairs at C, one three-parent group at S and a single binary group at each other binary block. Distinct blocks do not provide additional unheld triples because their footprint has no three-block cycle. At each donor attach one auxiliary terminal point for every unused original loose name after its groups. The ordinary rule creates precisely these unary children. If the donor originally had two loose names, append a one-way infinite chain to that terminal. For three or more append none. All clock starts have at least two loose at every original live donor. This is the established surplus construction in the rank-one coordinates; the floor and its count are checked below. A support must contain a genuine original. [Earlier surplus and floor theorem](../../proofs/CHAIN-IDENT-20261119-001A/CHAIN-IDENT-20261119-001A-P001_chains/proof.md)

Take finite, nonempty sets of points connected through their blocks. Require that any two points in an S block bring its third point too. No such condition is imposed at C. The rank of a support is its number of points minus its number of complete S blocks. Two supports define the same member when one translate of the cover takes the entire first to the second. Every S block has the five possible restrictions empty, any one of its three points, or all three. To count joins and intersections, represent the three distinct singleton restrictions by the three one-dimensional subspaces of a two-dimensional binary vector space. For every point outside S use a separate one-dimensional coordinate. Take the direct sum over disjoint coordinates. Support intersection is subspace intersection. Support union followed by completing any S blocks is subspace sum, and vector-space dimension is the rank just defined. This use of binary vectors is a counting argument for both physical laws.

Connectivity is retained under nonempty intersection. Indeed the ordinary tree-spans of two supports intersect in a subtree, and the point nodes of a span are exactly its support. Closure under completing S is retained under intersection. Two touching supports have connected closed join. An intersection that contains an auxiliary point but no other originally occupied point cannot occur between supports that each contain a genuine original: the unique way from that auxiliary component to any original contains its same root.

## Covering and removal

An immediate extension means adjoining one ordinary neighbor, with completion of its S block if needed, increasing rank by one. Conversely every connected containing support of rank one more is an extension of this form, by following a shortest connection to the added part and using the rank bound. At a nontrivial S coordinate the one-to-full move can add two points at once. A connected immediate predecessor is its reverse, and must keep an original.

For any support of rank at least three the different admissible one-rank removals modify distinct coordinates. The only possible exception in the coordinate product is downgrading one full S block to different singletons. Any nonempty exterior attaches to that block through specific member points, since the incidence structure is a tree. If two of its member points have exterior attachments, no such downgrade keeps the whole remainder connected. If exactly one has exterior attachments, only that surviving singleton is possible. With no exterior the support was the rank-two S block itself. Hence at ranks at least three two distinct removals have intersection two ranks below the original. This intersection is connected and contains an original by the preceding paragraph.

A support with at least two real points has at least two immediate predecessors, including the rank-two S block with its three real singletons. One can see this by taking the terminal blocks of its finite occupied span. In an occupied free terminal block, remove one exterior point. In an occupied full S terminal block, retain its unique attachment point and remove its other two points together. A one-point occupation at S uses the ordinary adjoining blocks only. The two ends give distinct removals, or a support in just one block has the direct list of removals just given. A peripheral auxiliary chain can instead lose its last point. A real at a peripheral terminal carrying auxiliary branches cannot be removed while those branches survive, but their outer points then supply the required removals. With only one real point the support consists of its own auxiliary terminals and initial segments of their chains. It has only one predecessor exactly when it uses just one such terminal and one consecutive chain. This also explains why no other unary group is needed.

**Unique short lifts.** A nonidentity deck transformation translates along an axis of the incidence tree by at least four successive point positions, as in the standard union lemma. If a connected closed support D contains both B and its nontrivial translate fB, their ranks differ from D by at least four. To check the rank, take the first projection of the span of B onto that axis and the corresponding first projection of fB. Between the first projection (included) and its translate (excluded) there are at least four axis points of D outside fB. A complete pair of axis points in a single S coordinate contributes two ranks: the third point projects to the intervening block, also before fB. A single new axis point in a partly met coordinate contributes at least one rank, since fB cannot already contain its full block. This counts distinct coordinate contributions, proving rank(D)≥rank(B)+4. Consequently copies differing by one, two or three additional ranks in a common support cannot describe different translates of the same physical knot. This is the earlier cover estimate with rank in place of the ordinary point count. ## All actual parents, including every swallowing group

At rank one the original witnessed group rule gives exactly the rank-two supports, including one three-parent support for a complete S block, the three distinct binary subsets of a C block, and any attached unary support. Now assume ordinary births up to rank n are identified and live, n≥2. Two current knots are witnessed candidates exactly when their supports have lifts sharing a connected rank-(n−1) predecessor. Each overlapping pair then has a unique coherent lift relative to the first and unique joined rank-(n+1) support. Indeed nonempty intersections are connected, the rank formula gives a rank-one extension of each, and incoherent copies would lie in a common closed join of at most rank n+2 contradicting the four-rank bound. The same argument lifts every pair of a candidate clique coherently relative to one fixed member: a second lift witnessing adjacency lies with the chosen copies in a closed joined support of rank at most n+3. All joins in this comparison are connected since each addition already intersects a chosen lift.

In vector spaces, a family of n-dimensional subspaces pairwise intersecting in dimension n−1 either shares a common (n−1)-dimensional subspace or lies in one (n+1)-dimensional subspace. Here is the short proof. Choose two members A,B and put H=A∩B, V=A+B. A member not containing H intersects A and B in distinct codimension-one parts that span it, so lies in V. A member outside V must meet V precisely in H, hence cannot meet that first member in codimension one. This proves the assertion. Here H is a connected admissible predecessor, and V a connected admissible extension. Thus every genuine unheld clique of at least three parents lies among the immediate predecessors of one extension.

The vector-space alternative is the standard classification of maximal cliques of the Grassmann graph in the literature. The physical question here additionally requires connected admissible intersections, unique translated lifts, true original parents and paid growth under either state law. [Kwiatkowski and Pankov, *Chow’s theorem for linear codes*, introduction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.06115)

Conversely, if an extension has at least three distinct predecessors, their pairwise intersections are admissible shared predecessors, and all of them cannot share one: their losses modify at least three different coordinates, leaving rank at most n−2 in common. The full list is maximal. An outside candidate adjacent to every member would by the same argument lie in their unique joined extension and would already be on the list. For a list with exactly two members neither can belong jointly to any larger unheld clique, since their least containing rank-(n+1) support is already this one. This exhausts native maximal groups including all possible rivals. Two different lifts of a child or of a direct parent cannot enter such a calculation by the four-rank bound. The only exceptional three-donor pencil, the full rank-two S support, was dealt with from the literal original witnesses.

For completeness, a straight subinterval has an elementary local check as well. If two consecutive true binary intervals have parent sets {A,Z} and {Z,B}, an unheld swallowing group would require a child containing both A and B. Their actual predecessor windows two positions apart do not share a parent on these routes. For the short base the pertinent original donor sets are the actual two- or three-parent sets, not a hypothetical pair at S. Reversal cannot identify two of these consecutive windows: such a reversal forces immediate repeated or reversed passage, and a forward identification at shifts one to three would force a return of at most three distinct traversed blocks. The cover proof above also covers forks, off-route siblings and arbitrary long repeated clock phases.

## Funding and the state laws

The number of distinct immediate extensions of a singleton equals its true original loose supply. It counts its funded groups and its surplus auxiliary terminals. At each subsequent cut, one loose distinction is required for each multi-parent extension containing the member. An ordinary group with at least two parents gets its demand as fresh supply, since every support has an extension into the infinite connected cover and the minimum fresh requirement is just one. Such a group has no unary extension, by the removal classification. A solitary terminal attached to one real point with original supply h≥3 has h−1 prospective extensions already issuing through multiple parents; this number is at least two, the unary's minimum, and it has no outward chain. The only remaining one-parent supports are a real point of original supply two with its assigned terminal and part of its one linear chain. There is one funded extension toward its other original service and one further unary extension along that chain. Demand one and the unary floor of two produce both. The same accounting covers a separate reserve of two loose names attached to a non-gate holder. Distinct extensions give distinct physical children by the cover estimate. This verifies funding and induction at every age.

All original live holders have at least two private names, spent witness supports are disjoint, and every spent witness has paid connections to multiple originals. A prescribed permitted word on one spent witness extends by freely satisfying the other witnesses, then using each real's private distinctions. For a prescribed permitted word on a live donor, satisfy each incident spent witness using its free inputs from a different donor, then the other spent witnesses and private inputs. Both parity and not-all-equal extend, with their actual nonempty proper relation and all one-place-forgotten projections free. Distinct source inputs taken from separate live donors are jointly unrestricted: prescribe them, complete the spent constraints, and use another private distinction at each donor (choose its remaining value for parity, an opposite value if required for not-all-equal). At each later birth a jointly free old input list and a positive fresh supply give the full projected newborn relation. One can simultaneously prescribe a single then-loose output on each of several parents: for any such parent with a second free output finish with it. If an input parent has only one output, parity constraints on all loose names of a connected history have just the complete parity equation, by cancellation of internal names. A proper subset is free. For not-all-equal a whole selected set taking one loose from each parent leaves every old holding at least two other places; the old-state coloring argument extends any prescribed values. In a residual odd cycle every point is an addressed parent, since other knots kept at least three places. Pick any one of them, give both incident values the opposite of its prescribed bit, and alternate on the rest of the cycle. Every other point in the cycle sees both values, regardless of its own prescribed bit. Thus these inputs are free under both laws. There are always distinct surviving source and receiver intervals, excluding sole-birth parity closure, while not-all-equal never implies such an all-input newborn. Old allowed states always extend through a new ordinary knot with positive fresh supply.
