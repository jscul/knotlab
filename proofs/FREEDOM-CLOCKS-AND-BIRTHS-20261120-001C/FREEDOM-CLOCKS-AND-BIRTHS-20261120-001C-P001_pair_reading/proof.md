# Recovering wider holdings from pairs in a class of first reached stages

Supply the uniform weighting of all allowed binary words and the exact disagreement rate on every named pair. Consider any connected reached not-all-equal stage in which contracting each connected group of three-place holders leaves a tree of parts, joined at one name per meeting. A part can be a whole connected group of three-place holders or one wider holder. The table determines the allowed words among these stages. Full-state recovery for ordinary reached not-all-equal holdings then determines all later birth counts, even if a later stage leaves the class. [Previous full-state theorem](../../../research/THREE-READERS-AND-GROWTH-20261120-001B/classification.md).

For signs on names a and b, write C(a,b) for one minus twice their disagreement rate. A simultaneous complement preserves the allowed words. At a single shared name t, the two choices of its value therefore admit the same number of completions on either side. The parts on either side are independent conditional on t, each keeps its uniform marginal and C(a,b) equals C(a,t)C(t,b). Every two distinct names have full binary projection in a physical not-all-equal holding of the stipulated arities. In particular their correlation has magnitude strictly less than one. [Pair projection argument](../../../research/FREEDOM-CLOCKS-AND-BIRTHS-20261120-001C/proof.md#what-the-uniform-rates-measure).

Read every triple whose three disagreement rates sum to two. A nonconstant triple gives exactly two disagreements and a constant triple gives none. Thus these are precisely the triples that cannot be constant. No such triple spans a one-name join. A side with two pins can realize their values, and the other side can match the resulting shared name and the third pin by full pair projection. A triple that includes the shared name requires just a pair projection on each side. Every actual three-place holder qualifies, and a wider single holder permits all words on each triple. Grouping intersecting qualifying triples recovers the name sets of every ternary part. Requiring all those triples to be mixed recovers its words, because that collection contains every physical ternary rule.

Consider two names outside any common ternary part. They belong together in a wider holder precisely when no third name c satisfies C(a,b) = C(a,c)C(c,b). If they have no common part, an intervening single join supplies such a c. In a common wider holder of size q their correlation is the negative number u = −1/(2^{q−1}−1), by counting its mixed words and using its uniform marginal. Another name in that holder gives product u². For an outside name c reached through z in that holder, the product is u C(z,c)² if z equals a or b, and u² C(z,c)² otherwise. Neither can equal u. The maximal sets of four or more pairwise co-held names are the wider holders. A third name outside a proposed holder could associate with two of its names only by making a cycle among parts. This recovers the missing rules. The result respects a simultaneous renaming of all distinctions.

Finite disjoint unions of these presentations can also be read with no prior knowledge of their components. Distinct components have zero correlation by independence and complement symmetry. Inside a wider holder every pair has a nonzero correlation. In each physical three-place holder the three correlations sum to −1, since the triple is always mixed. At least two correlations are nonzero because their individual magnitudes are strictly less than one. Thus named pairs with nonzero correlations connect the three names in every ternary holder and the full set of names of each wider holder. Their connected groups recover the original components. Recover their constraints separately and apply the ordinary funding and closing rules to the whole holding. [Disjoint-union argument](../../../research/FREEDOM-CLOCKS-AND-BIRTHS-20261120-001C/proof.md#reading-disconnected-members-of-this-class).

This proof uses a named binary separator and the ternary rate identity. Ordinary growth gives each continuing newborn a fresh distinction, but it can also give it several old attachments. A private name on a four-place holder has a conditional sign that involves a product of the three other signs. Their correlation with an outside name can require a four-name moment. This explains why the proof does not cover arbitrary cycles that pass through wider holders.
