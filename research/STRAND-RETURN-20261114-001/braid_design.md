# Can a later reader undo a crossing after a fold?

On November 14, 2026 (Pacific) the owner suggested braiding and untangling specifically for the test of what a downstream reader can distinguish. The earlier historical manuscript treats braids as a picture. This follow-up gives a reversible local operation an explicit meaning and also makes folding a distinct, potentially irreversible operation. The two candidate examples and the binary census were identified during design. All results here have exploratory status. [Original manuscript](../../archive/p-vs-np/non_deterministic_topological_logic.tex#L280)

## Candidate operations

On a pair of bits use the signed swap `(x,y) → (y,1−x)`. On a pair of values in {0,1,2} use `(x,y) → (y, 2y−x mod 3)`. For each, exhaust all original pairs in both orders of crossing and taking the inverse. Check what happens if the crossing is repeated in place of undoing it. On three neighboring values compare the adjacent crossing sequences 12, 23, 12 and 23, 12, 23 on every input. For binary values also examine each of the 24 possible bijections on two bits. Test the three-move identity and whether either output depends on both input values. These are classical finite actions of braids when the tests pass. Interleaving independent cursor actions in the other experiment changes their schedule alone. [Eisermann, section 4](https://msp.org/pjm/2007/231-2/pjm-v231-n2-p04-s.pdf)

## Delayed reading and lost correlations

Cross every joint set of possibilities, including the empty set, over two binary values and over two ternary values. Hold each joint set fixed. Recover the possible original first values after undoing the full relation. Then give the recipient only the individual possibilities on each strand after crossing. Use all consistent pairings (the product of the two marginals) and count exact recovery. Also check which marginal messages are intrinsically ambiguous across different original allowed sets. Test the two records in which the original first value is respectively 0 or 1 while the second is free. Record both the destination marginal messages and the actual inverse. No constraints identify the two strands in this experiment.

For individual fully specified crossed states, the eventual reader asks whether the first value is zero. Compare a public promise to stop, a public promise to undo, and a choice between the two that is revealed after the packet has been committed. Exhaust the answer signatures and count the minimum fixed payload in bits. Count best one-bit accuracy with decoder and sender chosen for this finite menu under uniform states and equally likely eventual choices. Exact knowledge of one later yes/no answer, all possible later answers, the entire state, and a packet restricted to the two marginals are separate communication tasks. The move, inverse, readout and question family are supplied. This experiment performs no learning.
