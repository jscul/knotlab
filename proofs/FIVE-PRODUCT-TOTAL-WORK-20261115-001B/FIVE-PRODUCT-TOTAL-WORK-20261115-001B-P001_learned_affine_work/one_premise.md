## One affine equation as premise and one as conclusion

On this five-product example, **23 is the optimal total cost if at most one scalar affine implication may be trained and stored**. Each premise is one equation in the nine visible bits, as is each conclusion. Queries may ask any visible affine parity, both evaluator checks remain independent, and a citation costs one each time it is used. An ordinary tree of cost 23 is available. The result uses the fixed sketch masks \(1,6\). Guards containing several independent equations and proofs with several stored facts remain open.

Use the invertible affine change to three triples \((x_1,x_0,u),(x_2,x_1+x_3,v),(x_4,x_3+x_5,s+u+v)\). In a triple \((y,w,p)\), put \(e=p+yw\). Rich means all three errors vanish. Plain means their sum equals one. The raw rich and plain sets both span all nine coordinates. For rich, each copy of the four true points \(T=\{0,1,2,7\}\) spans its three variables. For plain, fix any odd error pattern and take the Cartesian product of the three corresponding full-span local sets. The first useful operation in the main proof is therefore an affine question whenever the premise is a proper nonconstant equation.

Write \(G,L\) for the two equations and \(F=G\cap\neg L\) for the training premise. If the coefficient rows are independent, \(F\) is a codimension-two affine flat. The checker finds the exact ordinary training cost \(T(F)\). Without a learned rule only the surviving source rows matter. A rich call sends \((A,B)\) to \((A,B\cap\operatorname{aff}(A))\), and a plain call gives the symmetric update. At a nonempty union, a source with no surviving rows closes for one. A binary parity split with one empty-union child can be suppressed. The other effective transitions either shrink a source set or split the union. The initial affine span has dimension at most seven. In dimension seven, an effective source call or nonconstant question gives children whose surviving rows together span at most six dimensions in each child. The search on these children compares both individual source calls and all affine questions, charging one per operation.

For a first main question \(q\), choose any one of its touched triples. The local part of \(q\) takes both values on \(T\). Select \(t_0,t_1\in T\) giving the two opposite local answers. On answer \(b\), embed the other two full triples \(z\) by filling the touched triple with
\[
t_0+(b+q_{\rm outside}(z))(t_0+t_1).
\]
This affine injection lands in the chosen child and preserves both sources pointwise. Its inserted triple contributes zero error. An arbitrary residual odd error is still a plain row, including rows on which sketch bits disagree with their products. On all 64 inputs of every injection the checker reverses the nine-bit change of variables and verifies both original definitions. The original plain relation leaves both sketch bits free.

On a lifted subproblem, give the pulled-back implication \(E^*G\Rightarrow E^*L\) to the proof at zero training cost. Retain the unit citation cost and require the actual affine context to entail the premise. Every proof of the large child restricts to a proof of the smaller child of no greater cost. Maintain the smaller affine context inside the preimage of the large context. A source call can only strengthen the smaller context because an affine map takes the hull of the smaller source rows inside the hull of the large source rows. A question that becomes constant has a unique child. An entailed premise pulls back to an entailed premise. A closing citation or empty source call is paid. This simulation permits repeated and interstitial source checks and arbitrary later visible affine questions.

Let \(U(g,l)\) be the exact two-triple price with one available fact. Compute its value at every one of the 26,387 nonempty affine subsets \(H\subseteq\mathbb F_2^6\) in increasing dimension, starting at zero for the empty context. Compare every genuine shrink by a source hull, every nonconstant affine question, and each legal citation. If \(H\subseteq g\) and \(H\not\subseteq l\), a citation costs one plus the price on \(H\cap l\) with the same available rule. The simulation retains a source operation that changes the geometry relevant to premise entailment even when the surviving rows of both sources stay the same. Symmetries of the two triples identify equally priced ordered pairs. Without an effective rule the two-triple cost is 11.

With \(U\) evaluated on the full six-bit cell, the lower bound for training and use is
\[
 T(F)+1+\min_{q\ne0}\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}}\max_{E\ \mathrm{as\ above}} U(E^*G,E^*L).
\]
For the three triples the group \((S_4)^3\rtimes S_3\) permutes the four true local states and the three whole triples. Each local \(S_4\) acts affinely and also preserves the four false local states. Consequently the group preserves both sources, every allowed operation, and its price. There are \(4(511)(510)/6=173{,}740\) different forbidden flats. A traversal under explicit group generators gives 126 orbits. Each flat has three containing hyperplanes, one for each nonzero linear combination of the two defining rows. Each may serve as the guard. Given that guard, the other two ways of writing the conclusion coincide throughout it. The computation thus covers all 521,220 forbidden-flat and guard pairs through 378 representative orientations. The displayed lower bound is at least 23 in all 378 and at least 24 in 357. These numbers bound proofs that train the displayed fact. The untrained solver remains available at cost 23.

For dependent coefficients or a constant equation, \(F\) is empty, the whole space, or an affine hyperplane. Empty \(F\) changes no legal context. Whole-space training already costs 23. For hyperplane \(F\), restrict the main proof to the complementary hyperplane. Every legal citation there has a conclusion already implied by the current context. If the proof uses \(k\ge1\) citations, an ordinary root split on the hyperplane costs one, can use the training proof on one side, and costs at most the old main cost minus \(k\) on the other. The trained proof thus costs at least 23. With zero citations, the ordinary main tree alone costs at least 23.
