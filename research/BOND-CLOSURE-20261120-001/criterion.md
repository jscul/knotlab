# Which inputs reveal parity closure?

**Any one pair of inputs decides a genuine ternary birth.** Its pair bond distance is two exactly when the birth takes all loose distinctions. Otherwise it is at least three. This holds on every connected lawful parity start and its reached stages. At arbitrary arity \(p\ge3\), one query on any prescribed \(p-1\) inputs suffices, and that number of distinct inputs is sharp for an input-only reader under arbitrary renaming. In the proved cluster class, **any pair still suffices at every arity**. All new claims await the program referee. [Observation contract](preregistration.md)

## The physical cut and the ternary proof

Let \(H\) be the connected ordinary graph of knots, counting each shared distinction separately. Attach all loose distinctions to one auxiliary exterior \(O\). A parity equation is exactly the cut of a set of ordinary holders. A bond is a nonempty cut with both sides connected, equivalently a nonzero equation with no nonzero equation on a proper subset. For a query \(T\) of loose distinctions let \(b(T)\) be the size of a smallest bond containing them. Its ordinary side \(S\), away from \(O\), must contain every holder of \(T\). The cost is
\[
 C(S)=\#\{\text{loose distinctions at holders in }S\}+|\delta_H(S)|.
\]
The complete ordinary side is connected and has just \(O\) opposite it, so the full set of loose distinctions is always a bond at a nonempty end. All the loose-mark minima below are finite with the registered parity convention. A cut containing only loose distinctions must include every loose distinction, since \(H\) is connected. [Parity derivation and connected-shore criterion](../EDDY-WORLD-20261119-001/bending.md)

**One shared crossing cannot divide a legal group of three or more parents.** Each pair of parents \(i,j\) has an ordinary two-step route \(i,w,j\) through a common witness. Suppose one ordinary bridge \(a b\) divides the parent set, with \(a\) and \(b\) on opposite sides. For any pair across the bridge at least one is the corresponding bridge endpoint, since otherwise two steps could not cross. The two endpoints themselves cannot be a candidate pair because a distinct common witness would give a route avoiding their bridge. Thus one side has a single parent which is its endpoint, say \(a\), and \(b\) is not a parent. Every parent on the other side has to meet \(b\), by its two-step route to \(a\). Now \(b\) meets every parent, including \(a\), contrary to the requirement for a larger group. It follows that any ordinary cut separating members of the group has at least two shared crossings. This proof uses actual witnesses and does not require the parents to have only one witness per pair.

Fix either pair of loose inputs in a legal triple. A shore containing their holders and omitting the third pays at least two loose distinctions and two shared crossings. A shore containing all three pays at least three. It can cost exactly three only if it contains no other loose distinction and has no shared crossing, in which case it is the entire ordinary presentation. Conversely the full loose set realizes three when the inputs exhaust it. Thus \(b(T)=3\) in the closing case and \(b(T)\ge4\) in the continuing case. The previously reached four-knot example attains three and four. A true singleton query there has value three in both arms. The three-place parent star supplies the upper bound and the wired cube subgraph requires at least three crossings. The distance of a mark from itself is zero and supplies no singleton minimum. [Literal four-knot example](../ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/rulers.md)

**Why physical connectedness matters.** If one requires only K1–K3 at each source star and permits disconnected physical unions, even the complete bond data on the full parent supports cannot test global exhaustion. Take the genuinely reached closing ternary component above, alone or in direct product with an independently continuing bare five-ring having two initial loose distinctions at each holder. Both components can be grown from lawful sources to the common age. Every bond meeting the marked component lies wholly in it. A projection involving two independent components factors, and any nonfull projection contains a nonfull projection in one component. All marked bond readings of every order therefore agree. In both products the ternary parity is implied in its own component and writes zero fresh. Only the single-component stage as a whole closes. Under a disconnected law the appropriate implication test is exhaustion in the **ordinary physical component**. A connected physical source with several disconnected *growing-end* components, as in the four-knot example, is already covered by the positive theorem.

The proposed group and the complete round's funding are hypotheses. For example give each of three leaves one loose distinction and two shared meetings with one spent center. All three loose inputs together exhaust the end, and every pair has \(b=3\). The center holds them together, so they are ineligible as a ternary. Each leaf owes two residual pairs with only one loose distinction, and the round stalls. Every declared source star is a bond, since deleting any holder leaves the others connected through the wired exterior. A whole-stage bond on three proposed marks therefore cannot by itself authorize a round.

## Higher arity and the optimal prescribed subset

For \(p\ge3\), fix \(p-1\) of the inputs of a legal group. If a shore misses the remaining parent it pays at least \(p-1\) marked loose distinctions and two ordinary crossings. If it includes every parent, cost \(p\) is possible exactly when \(S=V(H)\) and there are no other loose distinctions. The exterior cut proves the converse. Therefore
\[
 b(T)=p \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the }p\text{ inputs exhaust the loose distinctions},
 \qquad |T|=p-1.
\]
Querying all \(p\) inputs also works, even without legality of the parent set, by the same full-loose argument. Under parity a **funded** exhaustive birth is the sole birth, has implied parity, takes zero fresh, and closes. A funded nonexhaustive birth has at least one fresh. Its precise number also depends on its arity and on the groups that its siblings will form. The numerical threshold is a statement about parity. Under not-all-equal a funded nonempty birth needs a fresh distinction even when it takes every loose distinction. [Full update and its two laws](../ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/end_operator.md)

**Sharp lower bound, including multiple queries on the same inputs.** Let \(q=p-2\ge2\). Give one loose distinction to each of \(q\) main parents and two further parents \(v,w\). Every main parent meets the two spent witnesses \(H_1,H_2\) once. Parent \(v\) meets \(H_1\) once and witness \(W\) twice. Parent \(w\) meets \(H_2\) once and \(W\) twice. Compare zero versus one loose distinction at \(W\). The ordinary graph consists of \(q\) two-step routes from \(H_1\) to \(H_2\) and the additional route through \(v,W,w\). Every ordinary star has at least three places. Deleting any one vertex leaves a connected ordinary graph with a loose parent, proving the exact K1–K3 parity projection at every original star.

The \(p\) input parents are pairwise candidates and no witness meets all of them. Their full set is the unique maximal group in that component, uses each opening once, and is funded. The additional live \(W\) in the comparison has no candidate among the \(p\) parents, as its ordinary neighbors are parents and theirs are witnesses. It gives one separate unary birth. The true \(p\)-parent birth has zero fresh in the first preparation and one in the second.

The **complete query deck on the \(q\) main loose inputs** agrees in both preparations.

- Each singleton minimum is three. Its three-place star is a bond. Neither a loose-only check on at most two marks nor an ordinary one-bridge separation can give a smaller cut.
- Every subset of two or more main inputs has minimum \(p\). A connected shore holding their parents must contain at least one hub \(H_i\). With exactly one inside, the \(q\) main parents each contribute a crossing on one of their two hub contacts, and at least two marked loose distinctions also cross. With both hubs inside each main parent costs at least one, and each route from \(H_i\) through its exceptional parent to the exterior costs at least one more. For the upper bound take all main parents and both hubs. The two exceptional parents, \(W\) and \(O\) form a connected other side. The cut costs \(q+2=p\) in both arms.

Consequently there is no guaranteed answer from a single query on any prescribed \(p-2\) or fewer inputs. Even if any number of adaptive subqueries is allowed but only \(p-2\) *different* anonymous input names can be inspected, an adversarial renaming can place each newly inspected name among the \(q\) main inputs. Every answer along the resulting transcript matches. For \(p=3\) the four-knot singleton example gives the corresponding lower bound. This sharpness proof is for lawful **starts** and thus for the requested general domain, which includes starts. It makes no positive-age realization claim for these two-hub examples.

The observers must be distinguished. In this family the exceptional pair \(v,w\) is identifiable if the common-witness rosters on the parent set are given, since it is the unique two-member maximal witness block. Its own two-mark reading is four closing and five continuing at every \(p\ge4\). The connected shore \(\{v,W,w\}\) realizes both. Any shore through that pair which omits another parent pays at least two shared crossings. If \(W\) is inside, its extra loose distinction raises the cost to at least five in the larger preparation. If \(W\) is outside, its four shared contacts to \(v,w\) already cross. A shore containing all \(p\) parents costs at least \(p+1\) in the larger preparation. Thus the example deliberately leaves the arms distinguishable by the deck of **all** pairs on **all** inputs. A parent-support table also supplies entries on shared distinctions that an input-only table never queries.

## An upper bound for deck order across all inputs

There is an additional uniform upper bound for the observer who can query different subsets across all \(p\) names. If a shore \(S\) contains \(s\) input parents, then
\[
 |\delta_H(S)|\ge \min(s,p-s).
\]
Otherwise each side has a parent incident to no crossing, since each crossing has only one endpoint on each side. Those two parents cannot have their required two-step route across the partition. In particular \(C(S)\ge p\) whenever \(s\ge\lceil p/2\rceil\). This lower bound holds even for disconnected shores.

Put \(m=\lceil p/2\rceil\) and \(k=m+1\). At exhaustion every \(k\)-mark query has \(b=p\), using the full loose bond. Conversely suppose **all** \(k\)-mark queries have \(b=p\). Take one of their ordinary witnessing shores \(S\). If some input \(j\) lies outside, query it together with \(k-1=m\) inputs already inside \(S\). The new witnessing shore \(S'\) costs \(p\). Each of \(S\cap S'\) and \(S\cup S'\) contains at least \(m\) inputs and therefore costs at least \(p\). Cut cost is submodular. The two original shores count each crossing at least as many times as the intersection and union do together, and the loose counts add exactly. Hence the union costs precisely \(p\) and contains the additional input \(j\). Continue using the union, whose connectedness is unnecessary. Eventually a set of cost \(p\) contains every input, forcing exhaustion. Consequently a deck through order \(\min(p-1,\lceil p/2\rceil+1)\) suffices across all input marks. This is an upper bound, distinct from the sharp number of different inputs needed by an arbitrary-subset reader. The full deck can test whether every value at the indicated order equals \(p\), without receiving any witnessing shores.

## When one pair works at every arity

A local structural certificate applies when two input holders \(i,j\) have the following routes in the ordinary presentation. For every other input holder \(x\) choose one ordinary two-step route to \(i\) and one to \(j\), with the whole family of routes sharing no physical distinction. This condition is checkable from the actual witnessed parent incidence. A shore through \(i,j\) that omits \(t>0\) of the input parents then pays at least \(2t\) ordinary crossings and \(p-t\) marked loose distinctions. Its cost is at least \(p+t\). A shore containing all inputs costs \(p\) exactly at exhaustion. Hence this one pair gives \(b=p\) versus \(b\ge p+1\).

Every \(p\)-parent birth with \(p\ge3\) in the **identified class** has this certificate at **every pair**. The initial no-first-triple condition excludes such a group at source age. At positive age its parents are the clusters \(Y\setminus\{x_i\}\) for distinct removable points, using the compatible lifts for this child \(Y\). Each pair has an earlier common parent given by \(Y\setminus\{x_i,x_j\}\). These are distinct physical witnesses for distinct unordered pairs. Two different deletions cannot be literally equal. If they represented the same orbit via a nonidentity translation, the cluster \(Y\) would contain a cluster and its nontrivial translate while exceeding its size by only two points. The identification theorem requires at least four. The witnesses are one layer earlier and so are also distinct from the input holders. The two-step routes through these different witnesses use disjoint physical distinctions, as required. After a finite top relays, every birth on its two chains has at most two parents. The one-unit gap occurs at genuinely **reached** stages for every \(p\ge3\). Give the \(p\) leaves of a single center \(p-1\) loose distinctions apiece and compare zero versus one extra loose at the center. Each source star is a bond by deletion, and leaf pairs have the center as their unique common witness. Their identified piece is one finite block on \(p\) points. Its knots at age \(t\) are precisely the \((t+1)\)-subsets of the leaves. At age \(p-2\) the \(p\) subsets omitting one leaf each hold one loose distinction. The next join takes all \(p\) inputs. In the first start it closes. In the second the possibly live center had no candidate at the first round and made a unary on its extra port. Its subsequent component persists, and this single spent port joins that whole branch to the old center. Taking all ordinary knots in the leaf growth, including the original center, supplies a bond with exactly the \(p\) marked distinctions plus this one port. Both shores are connected and the separate branch reaches the current exterior. Thus each pair minimum is exactly \(p\) versus \(p+1\). The first parity growth is finite, the second has two-chain growth by formation size.

## One and two parents

**Two inputs.** For a funded binary birth, \(b(\{e,f\})=2\) exactly when it takes all loose distinctions. A two-cut containing the two loose marks can contain nothing else. Otherwise the bond of all loose distinctions gives a finite minimum at least three. The registered convention includes a two-place algebraic bond. If the reader excludes all supports smaller than three, the closing pair instead has value **infinity** because its two-mark check is a proper dependent subset of *every* larger candidate support. In a continuing case the full loose bond has size at least three and contains both marks, giving a finite reading. Separately, a *growth law* that forbids a two-place final birth has changed the archived closing exception.

Both actual singleton minima together also decide the binary case at a lawful live stage, when that instrument supplies them. Closing gives two at both marks. Suppose the two marks are at \(u,v\) with common witness \(w\), and that some extra loose distinction exists. A size-two bond through \(e\) must use one ordinary bridge, with \(u\) alone among loose holders on its side. It cannot be incident to \(u\), since its two-place check with \(e\) would then restrict a proper subset of the at-least-three-place star of \(u\). Along \(u,w,v\) the bridge must therefore be \(w v\). All extra loose distinctions must be on its \(v\)-side. A size-two bond through \(f\) would analogously have to use \(u w\) and put every extra on its \(u\)-side. Those two sides are disjoint. This contradiction proves the claim. At any live reached parity stage the needed star lawfulness persists. Old states extend, each new nonimplied star receives at least one fresh distinction, and its inputs are a proper subset of the preceding loose set and therefore free. Any allowed assignment on that new star extends at each other birth via its own fresh distinction.

One prescribed singleton need not suffice. Use an ordinary path \(u,w,v,r\) with respective physical meeting multiplicities \(2,1,2\). Give \(u,v,r\) one loose distinction apiece. Every star is a bond by deletion. The funded birth on \(u,v\) through \(w\) leaves \(r\)'s loose distinction to a unary. The ordinary shore \(\{u,w\}\) gives a two-bond through \(u\)'s input and the single \(wv\) meeting. Compare two leaves with one loose each, meeting their common spent center twice each. Their sole pair birth closes, and the same singleton reading is two. For a real positive-age binary comparison, use the original path \(a,W_1,b,W_2,c\), double each ordinary meeting and give \(a,b,c\) respectively \(1,2,1\) loose. Its initial pair births \(ab,bc\) carry one loose each and then join. Adding one original loose at \(W_1\) creates a separate unary at the first end. The pair now continues. The source stars are again bonds by deletion and these initial sources are identified.

Two distinctions about this binary case are worth making explicit.

- When only genuine bonds of at least three places may be queried, the singleton readings in the small closing and continuing sources above are both \(3,3\). Each closing leaf star has size three. In the continuing source the complete set of three loose distinctions is a genuine bond through either input. Hence the algebraic **two-singleton** test depends on the two-place convention, even though a query on the **pair** distinguishes the answers in either convention. For any legal group of size at least three, every input is free of such a two-place tie. The only possible tie of an input to an ordinary distinction would put a bridge between that one parent and the other parents. The ternary and higher input readings agree under both conventions. - At a positive-age binary, supplying **both parent opening counts** strengthens the algebraic singleton reader. If either exceeds one the birth cannot exhaust. Otherwise both newborn parents came from at least two older parents each, since a nonimplied unary birth brings at least two fresh distinctions. Each old-to-new incidence they hold lies on an ordinary cycle through an alternate earlier parent and the connected preceding ordinary stage. Neither of the two links to their common witness is a bridge, ruling out an algebraic singleton minimum of two while extra loose distinctions exist. Without the opening counts, one prescribed algebraic singleton can fail even at positive age. Take a source path \(X,D,Y\), double both meetings and give the three holders respectively \(2,0,1\) loose. The first layer gives a binary child \(u\) of \(X,Y\) with one fresh and a unary child \(v\) of \(X\) with two. Their binary child cannot exhaust. The loose distinction of \(u\) forms a two-bond with the shared distinction \(Xv\) by taking every ordinary holder except \(v\) as the inner shore. All source stars are bonds by deletion. The positive-age closing example above gives the same singleton minimum.

**One input.** Algebraically, a singleton loose mark has \(b(\{e\})=1\) exactly when it is the sole loose distinction, by the connected cut argument. Such a source cannot be lawful under the three-place minimum because its sole loose value would be fixed in a proper singleton subset of its holder. Nor can an open stage reached under the registered parity law have just one loose distinction. A funded step with one birth is implied and gives zero. A nonclosing step produces at least two births with at least one fresh each. Thus a unary exhausting birth never occurs on the lawful parity domain, even though nonexhausting unary births do occur. A diagonal pair distance of zero carries no information about that mark's least containing bond.

These results concern a specified funded birth. Exact parent-group eligibility, next demand, a shortage anywhere in the end, and the supplied radius of a bond-distance neighborhood remain separate observations. No fixed numerical radius follows from the number of marks queried.
