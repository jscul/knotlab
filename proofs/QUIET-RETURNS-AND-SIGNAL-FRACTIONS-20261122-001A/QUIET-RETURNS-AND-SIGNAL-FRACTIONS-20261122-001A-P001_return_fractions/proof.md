# Which fractions of a continuing receiver’s returns can carry a signal?

**Theorem.** On the physically marked five-founder preparation, the same first-anchored receiver returns indefinitely from a wholly quiet founder. Under either even parity or not-all-equal, its sequence of received signals can be *any* infinite binary sequence. The minimum lower limiting fraction of signals among positive returns is 0 and the maximum upper limiting fraction is 1. Both are attained. A newborn contains the two values whose disagreement defines a signal. Identifying its return and counting earlier signals use additional, specified access to the retained holdings. ## The quiet history and its actual returns

The five original knots, numbered here only for display, have ordinary meetings 01, 12, 23, 34, 40 of multiplicities 2, 1, 1, 1, 3. Each original also holds two loose distinctions. Mark exactly one distinction at each of the five meetings with value 1 and set every other founding value to 0. This is both the actual word and its reference. Each founder sees two ones and at least its two loose zeros, so the same complete founding assignment satisfies both laws. The canonical knot tests concern the unconditioned relations on these holdings. The chosen word is one lawful assignment. [Physical preparation and original projections](../../../research/CALIBRATIONS-CLOCKS-AND-WIDER-KNOTS-20261120-001B/phase_clocks.md#what-the-holdings-identify)

The unique holder on both the doubled and tripled meetings is original 0. Follow the doubled meeting through 1 to 2 for the first witnessed step. The ordered phases of the witnessed five-cycle are 0, 2, 4, 1, 3. From here on a phase index $p$ means position in that order modulo five. Write $K_t(p)$ for the knot whose oriented word of original occurrences starts at phase $p$ and has length $t+1$. The age $t$ is a proof index. At cut $t+1$ the five true parent pairs are
$$
\{K_t(p),K_t(p+1)\},\qquad p=0,1,2,3,4 .
$$
Each old knot appears twice and can pay both obligations. Each new pair shares an old payer with each of its two neighboring pairs only. This gives the same five-cycle of proposed groups at the new cut, with two necessary fresh distinctions at every newborn. There are five births at each cut, so the parity rule for a sole closing birth never applies. This induction gives all cohorts and their true parents for both laws. Neither the values nor the choice of the bijection between a payer’s two names and its two children enters the induction. The two true end deletions identify the new oriented word. [Bare-cycle proof and reader](../../../research/CALIBRATIONS-CLOCKS-AND-WIDER-KNOTS-20261120-001B/phase_clocks.md#turns-and-witnessed-separation)

Enroll $R_t=K_t(0)$ at every cut, beginning at the physically marked founder. Its next member is an actual child of $R_t$ and of $K_t(1)$. Its last original occurrence is at phase $t$ modulo five. A positive return requires that last occurrence to be the founding anchor again. The returning knots are exactly $R_5,R_{10},R_{15},\ldots$. Every one is a new knot on this one continuing enrollment. The founder enrolls once. All legal fills leave all these members funded.

The first positive cohort receives two zeros at each knot. Give every newborn two ones. The next cohort then receives two ones at every knot, regardless of the payment binding, and can write two zeros. Inductively every age-$t$ fresh value is $t\bmod 2$. Each newborn has exactly two ones among its four values and hence satisfies both laws. All these actual writes agree with the stipulated reference. They give a single compatible, completely quiet history with a real return every five cuts. Minimum-deviation filling preserves it by induction, since its simultaneous reference completion is uniquely at zero difference when the past is quiet. More generally, after any fixed lawful old word and physical payments the set of next completions is the product of the newborns’ legal fresh banks. Distinct newborns introduce disjoint names and all old values persist. ## What the continuing receiver can read

A streaming reader can identify the next enrolled child from actual shared distinctions. The oriented founding holdings identify the first child with original 2. Thereafter let $U_{t-1}=K_{t-1}(1)$ be the outside payer at the current enrolled birth $R_t$. At the next complete cut follow the two fresh payments of $R_t$ into its two children. In each child identify its other payer from its second incoming distinction. These other payers are $K_t(1)$ and $K_t(-1)$. The first shares a real paid distinction with $U_{t-1}$ because its parent phases are 1 and 2. The other has parent phases $-1$ and 0, so shares none with $U_{t-1}$. Choose the child of the first, and retain that outside payer for the next handoff. This is $R_{t+1}$ at every step. The comparison uses the prior enrollment and its outside payer, and uses no bit values or display names. A reader given the whole finite presentation can instead peel the complete cohorts and recover the same sequence from its two-parent words. For the already enrolled $R_t$ there is a return test on **present physical holdings**. Count the least number of ordinary coholding steps from this knot to the unique founder on the doubled and tripled meetings. The count is exactly $t$. The continued-parent chain attains $t$, and each sharing between knots changes their formation age by at most one. Sharings between two founders change it by zero. No later parent and child have a repeated meeting that could imitate the original mark. The first positive multiple of five in this read is the first return, with one further return per five steps. A variable number of contacts may have to be traversed. A reader recording the enrolled handoffs can also count them from their physically identified start. Neither calculation gives a passive member a stored phase or counter.

At its birth a returning member has exactly two *paid* distinctions, each shared with its own true older payer, and two *fresh* loose distinctions. Read 1 if the paid values disagree in the fixed Boolean convention, and 0 if they agree. This is a local property of its own holding that is unchanged on interchanging the two payers. The next returning member has different actual payments. Once one name has two holders it can never be paid anew. At any later complete cut the full-holdings reader can recover every earlier enrolled return and identify each of its two input names by its actual older parents. Those names still carry the same two values. Thus the whole return and signal tally can be reconstructed from that single growing presentation with values, without an external past log. Each new member inherits one previous member as a true payer. The model supplies no operation that writes the cumulative tally into that member.

The reach needed for the return reading matters. Fix any finite number $r$ of ordinary coholding steps available from a newborn, and allow its incident names, shared or loose status and values, up to coherent naming. On the quiet history take $t=5j$ sufficiently larger than $r$. The rooted observations at $R_t$ and $R_{t+2}$ are isomorphic. Match positively aged holders by the same phase and relative age below the latest cohort. Every parent and child incidence is the same, the two fresh distinctions have equal values, and shifting by two preserves the alternating values of all payments. The observation cannot reach the original mark. The first member is a return and the second is between returns. Likewise $R_t$ and $R_{t+10}$ give the same view at two different return counts. This holds even if the immediately preceding handoff and its outside payer are pointed out inside the observation. It rules out a phase decision by a fixed local newborn inspection on every legal fill. [What one fixed holder retains](../../../research/REPEATED-SENDS-20261122-001A/results.md)

## A free bit at each of the two actual payers

Fix a structural allocation of the two outgoing names of every knot to its two children, independent of the values to be chosen. Allow any such fixed allocation. Consider any positive newborn whose actual paid values are $x,y$. Prescribe a bit $b$ on **one** of its two fresh names. The following choices for the other fresh bit complete its entire holding lawfully.

| Law | Other fresh bit | Why it works for every $x,y,b$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Even parity | $x\mathbin\oplus y\mathbin\oplus b$ | All four entries have even parity |
| Not-all-equal | $1-b$ | The two fresh entries already differ |

In particular, equal as well as unequal old inputs allow either value at the designated payment. The parity companion sometimes changes with it. That companion physically funds the payer’s other child. At each subsequent birth both laws admit a completion on every paid input pair, including whatever that other child receives. Fixing the previous whole word, the payers’ writes coexist with independently lawful writes of all their siblings by the product argument. There is no prescription on an old, already written name. [Local freedoms on a fixed holding](../../../proofs/QUIET-START-AND-FIRST-WRITE-20261122-001B/QUIET-START-AND-FIRST-WRITE-20261122-001B-P001_quiet_fill/proof.md#what-can-first-be-written)

Let $s_1,s_2,\ldots$ be *any* binary sequence. For the $j$th return, at the preceding birth cohort $t=5j-1$, consider $R_t$ and $K_t(1)$. They are distinct newborns and both are genuine parents of the coming $R_{t+1}$. Give the outgoing name that will be paid to $R_{t+1}$ value 0 in the bank of $R_t$, and value $s_j$ in the bank of $K_t(1)$. Use the table for their two companions. All other banks at this cohort, and all banks at intermediate cohorts, can be completed by the following deterministic convention. Designate the slot going to the child with the same first phase, put 0 there, and use the same law’s table for the remaining slot. This convention and the prescribed slots assign every positive fresh name once. No two return prescriptions compete for a newborn. The initial founders’ values are fixed, and all designated payers for returns have positive age.

Induct over the actual funded cohorts. The construction always extends the very same assignment and never changes an existing value or restarts the receiver. The two paid names received at return $j$ have values $0,s_j$. Each prescribed arrival is therefore realized. Existence can equivalently be witnessed by choosing a single allocation in advance for the whole construction. A choice of fills based on the prospective recipients proves attainability of a history. A later value-dependent reassignment of the physical payments can change that history’s arrivals.

For a concrete matched first signal, stay quiet through age 3. At age 4 both future payers of $R_5$ have received 11 and have reference bank 00. Keep the bank of $R_4$ at 00. Under parity let $K_4(1)$ write 11. Under not-all-equal let it write 1 on the actual payment to $R_5$ and 0 on its companion. In both cases all other age-4 banks can remain at reference. $R_5$ now receives 01. Staying completely quiet gives the very same $R_5$ two zeros. The parents, supply, first return and binding agree in the two arms. Repeated 1s and a mixed or nonperiodic record use the induction above, which checks all siblings on the changed history.

The per-law qualification is sharp. A positive four-place holding lawful under *both* laws must have exactly two ones. From the common founded word, the first newborns receive 00 and must therefore write 11. In the next cohort they receive 11 and must write 00. All their payments remain homogeneous under arbitrary bindings. Induction shows that the only jointly lawful valuation on each fixed growth history is the quiet alternating one. The two laws separately permit the same full set of *signal sequences* and require different valued histories to realize any sequence with a 1.

## Bounds on all returns

For the first $N\ge1$ actual returns write $S_N=\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_j$. Every history satisfies $0\le S_N/N\le1$. The quiet construction attains 0 at every $N$. Taking $s_j=1$ for every $j$ attains 1 at every $N$. In fact any finite $N$ permits every count from 0 through $N$, with a compatible infinite continuation in each case. Intervening births, enrollment, and repeated inspections of a paid pair add nothing to this denominator. A finite observed prefix has its ordinary finite count. Every full growth history in this class returns infinitely, since its next complete cut is funded and every new local word admits an extension. A genuinely missing target in another preparation has no trial at that cut. [Earlier missing-target comparison](../../../research/QUIET-START-AND-FIRST-WRITE-20261122-001/first_write.md)

For a requested limiting fraction $a\in[0,1]$ take $s_j=\lfloor ja\rfloor-\lfloor(j-1)a\rfloor$. The first $N$ returns then contain exactly $\lfloor Na\rfloor$ signals. More generally, **every pair** $0\le a\le b\le1$ occurs as the lower and upper limiting fractions. For $a<b$, alternate finite blocks of the rounding construction at densities $a$ and $b$, starting the rounding at zero within each block. Give the first block length one and every subsequent block length $(1+M)^2$, where $M$ is the number of preceding returns. Within block $k$ the cumulative signal count differs from the sum of the chosen block densities times their respective prefix lengths by less than or equal to $k$. Since $k/N\to0$, every subsequential limit lies in $[a,b]$. Each completed final block outweighs the preceding total by a factor tending to infinity. Its ending fraction approaches its chosen density, so the stated extremes are attained, including a history oscillating between lower limit 0 and upper limit 1. Finitely many prescribed initial readings affect the fraction by at most their number divided by $N$. From any fixed finite lawful past, the same construction applies at each later return whose paying banks have yet to be written. No distribution is used.

An exact cumulative **signal** count is also unavailable to a fixed-radius current newborn inspection for all histories. On the all-signal history that total takes arbitrarily large values at returns. Away from the founders the four-place holdings have bounded ordinary degree, binary values and finitely many rooted, valued observations of each fixed radius up to renaming. Two of these distinct cumulative counts must therefore have the same local observation. Whole-present recovery through the retained names, or memory of the enrolled handoffs, grants more access.

Permuting holder names, permuting distinction names with their actual bindings transported, or exchanging the order of the two reported inputs preserves each return and disagreement. A common Boolean complement preserves the comparison when the source, relations and reference are transported together. Independent value recodings need extra care. In parity, complement the two outgoing names of a positive-age parent, one paid to each of its children, and one fresh name at each child. Every affected holder through that cut has two flips, while exactly one input of either child has changed convention. The parity symmetry can be continued forward by compensating any odd number of input flips at one fresh name. Raw disagreement at the child is reversed. Thus comparing its two physical bits presupposes the frozen common value convention. A registered reference for each individual name could transport disagreement of *deviations* even under independent recodings, but reading such references would require further access.

The sharp range describes possible local arrivals along a recognizable material succession. It establishes neither a weighting on the histories nor an internal source that selects a bit sequence.
