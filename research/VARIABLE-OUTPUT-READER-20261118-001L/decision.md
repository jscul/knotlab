# Four is enough when the third three-output join takes two parents

**Both predictions were frozen before testing. The sharp cutoff of four held. The prediction that a hidden junction could coexist with a legal triple was refuted.** The source starts with a ternary parity junction and two one-parent joins that each introduce three distinctions. Exactly one further join introduces three, and it takes two parents. All remaining joins introduce two. At every finite size in this family, any reachable history matching the present distance table through four has exactly the same three-parent choices. If even one triple may join, the same matching also identifies every actual junction. This allows arbitrary renaming, arbitrary rival histories under the mixed law, and a single common value for every distance greater than four. **Why a single legal triple is enough.** Through the third shrinking join, where a join takes three and introduces two, the short pair readings force the entire allowed-state relation. This gives the full collection of junctions together with the set of loose distinctions. If two histories assigned different members of that collection to be the exterior, all distinctions loose in both would belong to just one ordinary junction. A legal triple needs three different holders. Its existence settles that last choice. At the fourth shrinking join only four distinctions remain loose. The proof checks every later binary continuation and excludes a legal triple even in a rival with a different arrangement or choice of output counts.
