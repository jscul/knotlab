## What stands, in four parts

1. **(a) Bindings exist after ordinary growth, on built starts.** On all 32 built ring families (three or four rings
   of four to seven knots joined through connecting knots that hold no loose distinction), at rounds 2 and 3, each
   ring's new carriers are class facts read by no single knot under both readings of a small change, and together
   they obey one parity rule with every proper part free (62 rows read; 2 past the size cap not read). Also on the
   advisor's 14-knot start, and under the shortest changes on the binding referee's sampled ten-knot start. None on
   the census or the held-out starts.
2. **(b) The relation is the start's own sum of knot laws.** Every carrier has a start representative, the
   representatives obey the same relation on the start's allowed states, and its sum is the sum of the start's laws.
   One loose distinction placed far from every ring dissolves it. With the advisor's argument (its premises held on
   every birth record and its spanning set on every stage of 24,616 rounds read), ordinary growth makes no relation
   among class facts on any stage read.
3. **(c) Growth turns that relation into one among class facts, with carriers of its own.** Before growth the rings'
   parities were read by no single knot but were not class facts under changes of at most five distinctions on any
   family, and under the shortest changes only on the double-link families: a five-distinction change ran from one
   ring's loose distinction through the connecting knots to another's. Growth consumes the loose distinctions, so after
   it every parity is a class fact under both readings, carried at each round by distinctions born the round before.
4. **(d) The forbidding cuts make a relation the start lacked.** On the 8 three-ring detour families with the middle
   knots joined, both cuts give one three-parent birth no fresh distinction, and its law binds the rings' parities
   (8 joint values at the start, 4 after); ordinary growth leaves them free; the cut runs stall the next round.

**On the owner's rungs.** Repetition: met. The tower's first step (whole-held facts bound by a rule that passes the
knot test and that nothing outside supplies, where before the step they were not such facts): met on built starts,
with the start's own laws as the rule and growth making the facts; under the cuts the rule is new. The tower's every
step (the bound layer keeping facts of its own, step after step): not shown. Recursion: not shown.

**Not shown.** That such starts arise under any sampled weighting; capped group rules; rounds past 6; a next layer's
own small changes; a proof (the argument is a sketch checked by computation).
