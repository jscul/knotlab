# Referee R1 · Reading ordinary growth from the start’s finite map

**Refuted — Block.** The absence of both flags does not suffice for the implemented reading. An accepted, connected 36-knot start has neither flag even when parents and ranks are taken from the actual inclusion order. Both readings fail in round one under both knot laws. All five frozen predictions about the seven-knot samples and the three replays hold.

## What holds

I first tested 55 fresh eight-knot starts under a different weighting and seed, before reading the study. All 48 with neither flag agreed in both readings through round nine under both laws. All six with a split pair alone and the one with overlapping cells differed in round one under both laws. All 55 were distinct up to renaming and differed from the study’s two eight-knot replays. This blind test supported the criterion in both directions on that sample. The constructed start below breaks sufficiency. I found no flagged start whose reading agreed for all rounds under both laws. The converse remains open.

I reproduced both frozen seven-knot sample lists, recomputed their flags, and rebuilt all 358 comparisons from the stored growth rows and new predictions. The two readings are the multiset of birth kinds, parent counts, fresh counts and place counts, and that same multiset with each birth’s parents’ kinds and counts attached. Their first differing rounds agreed in every case. Counts in this table are retained records under **each** law.

## Why a flag-clear start fails

Take seven knots numbered 1 through 7. Use the seven triples 123, 145, 167, 246, 257, 347 and 356. Give each triple a different private knot. Each triple together with its private knot is one four-knot group cell. One retained block holds the seven numbered knots. Two different cells have exactly one numbered knot in common, and every pair of numbered knots belongs to one triple.

Here is an ordinary start realizing this map. One knot with no loose distinctions meets all seven numbered knots. For each of the 21 memberships of a numbered knot in a triple, a separate knot meets that numbered knot and the triple’s private knot. Link those 21 knots in one cycle so each has four places and no loose distinctions. Give each numbered knot three loose distinctions and each private knot one. Each meeting uses its own shared distinction. There are 36 knots, 14 live. The map accepts one piece with no chains. Its two counts for admission are both 14. [Construction and checks](obstruction.py)

Exhaustive subset enumeration gives just **22 valid sets**: 14 singletons, seven complete cells and the whole set. In the inclusion order the whole set covers the seven cells. Each pair of these parents shares a singleton parent. The true inclusion ranks are 0, 1 and 2.

The code computes size as cardinality less two for each complete four-knot cell, then computes rank as size less one. The whole set therefore gets size (14-7(4-2)=0) and rank −1. There is no computed rank-two set for the split-pair test to examine. A whole set that contains seven cells has been assigned a smaller size than any of them. Both frozen flags return false, as does the older route-cycle flag. The true-cover split-pair test also returns false.

| Knot law | Round one predicted | Round one grown | Round two grown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Not-all-equal | Seven four-parent joins, zero fresh each, plus two offshoots | Seven four-parent joins, one fresh each | One seven-parent join, one fresh |
| Parity | Seven four-parent joins, zero fresh each | Seven four-parent joins, one fresh each | One seven-parent join, zero fresh |

Those seven first-round births share a parent pairwise, and no parent belongs to all seven. Ordinary growth groups them together in round two. That demand accounts for the one fresh distinction on each first-round join. The implementation counts no size-one-larger successor for any cell. Under not-all-equal it also starts the relay at the wrong size. Under parity it predicts no second-round birth. Even imposing the fresh-count minimum afterwards would leave the missing birth. [Not-all-equal rows](obstruction_nae.jsonl) · [Parity rows](obstruction_parity.jsonl)

A smaller, 14-knot start independently gives **eight predicted births against seven grown** in round one under both laws. Here the four cells and the full ten-member set all receive computed size two. The full set is counted as an additional first-round birth with ten parents. In this example a split pair *is* visible after recomputing inclusion ranks. The 36-knot witness above survives that change to the flag. A third construction has 30 knots and five cells. Its computed order has a rank-two set with four parents, every pair sharing a parent. It too differs in round one under both laws, so the failure also occurs when the split-pair check inspects actual pairs at the required rank. Minimality is open. ## Corrections to the study

1. **Block — the proposed sufficiency statement.** “In the late-views predictor's class, a start whose graded order has no split pair and no two group cells sharing two knots has as its births exactly the valid sets of its graded order, each with the sets it covers as parents and the sets covering it, plus one per chain, as fresh distinctions, with the relay and closing of `late.py` and the fresh count's floor max(D, 3 − p, 1).” Both constructed starts pass the implemented admission test and refute the statement about that predictor. The two flags classify the frozen samples correctly. Assigning ranks by the actual covers would put the seven-cell witness’s full set at round two. That would be a different reading whose general validity remains open. Any proposed narrower theorem must first justify the computed size on every inclusion cover. It must then address births with positive chain lengths, their parents, fresh demand, implied parity births, relay and closing. Agreement of two multisets by itself does not identify individual births. [README.md, statement to prove](../README.md#L133) · [decision.md, conclusion 3](../decision.md#L18) · [Size and rank-step code](../../LATE-VIEWS-20261126-001/late.py#L215)

2. **Block — the order lemma, even where size grades inclusion correctly.** “A proof needs Lemma R (proved) and an order lemma: any two parents of a valid set share a parent, and every set of births that pairwise share a parent and lies in no one parent's children is the parent set of exactly one valid set.” The first clause needs a rank-two minimum. For the second, let one knot with no loose distinctions meet four live knots, each with three loose distinctions. The finite map is one block. Its valid sets are all nonempty subsets of 1234. The births 123, 124 and 134 share parents 12, 13 and 14 pairwise, with no parent common to all three. Their only common successor, 1234, also has parent 234. Ordinary growth selects all four. Both laws agree with the predictor on this control. [decision.md, conclusion 3](../decision.md#L19) · [Control rows](order_lemma.jsonl)

   The precise obligation is to compare the **entire parent roster at each rank**. For later-round live births, form candidate pairs by shared parenthood. Take inclusion-maximal pairwise-compatible families of size at least three that have no common parent, then take every candidate pair outside all selected families. The size-at-least-three families must be maximal among *all* pairwise-compatible families. Account separately for offshoots from remaining loose distinctions. To obtain identification, prove that this roster gives exactly the proposed parent sets with their multiplicities, and check the initial roster and fresh counts as well. The reduction lemma supplies the growth rule. It does not establish equality with the order. [Growth roster](../../../experiments/WHAT-A-BIRTH-CARRIES-20261125-001B/WHAT-A-BIRTH-CARRIES-20261125-001B-E001_link_through_a_made_knot/eng.py#L335) · [Reduction lemma, items 1–5](../../../proofs/REDUCE-BLOCKS-20261119-001A/REDUCE-BLOCKS-20261119-001A-P001_reduce/proof.md#L24)

3. **Narrowing — split pairs and overlapping cells explain different observed failures.** “The growth joins only births that pairwise share a parent, so two parents with no common parent enter two joins.” They cannot belong to the *same* later join. Their number of joins depends on the whole roster. In the eight-knot replay with four pairwise-meeting knots, the four-parent joins in round two had six fresh distinctions each against three predicted. Different compatible parent groups in the next round have the same union in the map. The eight-knot replay with two cells meeting at one knot did give the forecast five fresh distinctions per cell against two predicted, then four four-parent joins where the reading counted one six-parent join. “The reading then makes one birth for both cells in round 1 where the growth makes two” describes a particular overlap. In a frozen tree-plus-extra-pairs start with three overlapping cells (source sample 2323), the reading made one seven-parent join for those three distinct cells. In the blind eight-knot sample with two cells sharing three knots (source sample 1266), it predicted two eight-parent joins for two actual five-parent joins. Both lost the individual valid cells, with different effects on the count. The score for F3 survives. 4. **Not a block — denominators and closure.** “On 254 fresh start-and-law cases and the six replays” should count **352** fresh law comparisons when reporting agreements and failures together. The 254 count covers agreements alone. “Every count below is up to renaming” applies within each weighting. “39 with a split pair” counts the split-pair-only starts from the first weighting; its total with a split pair is 40. “42 closed earlier, by rounds 2 to 12” gives the last nonempty rounds; closure occurred in rounds 3 through 13. [README.md, population](../README.md#L62) · [Results and denominator](../README.md#L120) · [Deduplication and renaming witness](dedup.md)

5. **Not a block — descriptions of starts and scoring.** “Four eight-place knots meeting pairwise, each with a three-place knot hanging from it” should say four-place for the four accompanying knots. “Bare sizes: open equals the round-1 demand” omits the three-place minimum and the weighting’s demand calculation. Eighteen of its 83 retained starts have chains. “`compare` scores only the rounds grown” overlooks the explicit check for zero predicted births from closure to the horizon. A guard or cap, in contrast, leaves later rounds unread. The two-cell replay ended with 226 and 1,358 births in its last two observed rounds. Its eventual growth rate remains unclassified. ## How it was checked and what was not run

- The blind weighting sampled labelled shared pairs on eight knots at one of four probabilities, selected live knots and assigned loose distinctions around their group demand. Seed 41182619, 16,000 attempts, 4,163 generated starts, 2,481 passing admission and the size cut. I took the first 12 per occupied combination of stratum and flags, or all available if fewer. Each piece had at most ten members and three chains. I enumerated valid subsets, tested inclusion covers directly and compared both multisets separately. A closure was compared as empty through round nine. The first 45 attempts took 0.023 s at that configuration, the complete pass 3.63 s. [Independent code](fresh.py) · [Counts](fresh_timing.txt)
- The frozen sample replay used the original seed and weighting functions. Exact permutation comparison counted 214 and 724 distinct eligible starts in the two realized streams. The hash merged no distinct eligible starts in either. The 2,000-attempt pilot took 0.52 s and the complete 240,000 attempts took 33 s. The static score audit used independent rank-one subset and literal cover comparisons. Three frozen starts had some difference between these parent relations. On the successive-knot start with two four-knot cells sharing two knots (source sample 199207), even the split-pair flag changes with true covers at the computed rank. The overlapping-cell flag still detects it. All stored *implemented* flag values and first differences reproduced. One row per file took 0.081 s; all rows took 1.06 s. [Deduplication replay](dedup_full.jsonl) · [Score audit](records.py)
- The two constructed starts were grown afresh through round two, the clean four-member-block control through round four or closure. The first law was the timing pilot for the second. Each 36-knot check, including the predicate and subset enumeration, took about 0.11 s. The original replay explanations were also checked against short new growths. An auxiliary search compared bare-core rosters on 1,860 admitted descriptions with up to seven live knots, and on 8,000 constructed descriptions with a retained block. The latter yielded the separate five-cell witness. Static checks and timings are saved with [the search results](challenge_notes.md). The full original 24-round growth trajectories were **read**, not regrown. An exhaustive census, every later round on open starts, and a proof of the converse were **not run**. No larger run is needed for the Block.
