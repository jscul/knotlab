# The two remaining families under uniform words

The uniform reading on the same reached 31-name preparation leaves a precise secondary question. The realized family of six newest knots gives seven named two-parent births. The only other family surviving the necessary tests consists of the single holder \(\{8,29,30\}\) with loose names 29 and 30. Its next roster, if realizable, would contain **two unary births**, both with that sole parent and two fresh names. An admissible history realizing this alternative is unknown. The primary result with recursive division excludes it. If the single unary were the complete latest cut, every underlying 29-name word would have exactly three complete extensions, regardless of the sign of input 8. Consequently the restriction of the observed uniform table to names 0–28 would be the **uniform** pair table of that preceding presentation, including when it had itself reached positive age. Its sole loose name would be 8. Set \(C_{ij}=1-2r_{ij}\). The minimum in row 8 is \(-q=-1332653/20039377\), attained precisely on \(A=\{3,5,6,7\}\). Its actual companions are a subset of \(A\) with constant-event probability exactly \(q\). Every two- or three-name subset of \(A\) already exceeds that probability. Therefore \(H=A\cup\{8\}\) is a mandatory physical holder. Erasing \(H\) and 8 then makes all four members of \(A\) loose. In the resulting uniform relation, their complete pair rows are exactly readable from the original pairs. For \(y\in A\),

\[
 C'_{yz}=\frac{C_{yz}-C_{8z}}{1+q}.
\]

For a residual event \(B\) its new probability is at least \(\Pr(B)/(1+q)\). These are the direct counting formulas for one versus two allowable extensions to 8 according as \(A\) is constant or mixed. Complement symmetry determines each individual three-name pattern probability, \((1+s_i s_j C_{ij}+s_i s_k C_{ik}+s_j s_k C_{jk})/8\). The least common multiple of all their reduced denominators is 200,393,770. The hypothetical uniform 29-name word count must be a positive multiple of it. The five forced constraints alone allow exactly \(30\cdot15^3\cdot7\cdot2^9=362,880,000\) words: choose a nonconstant word at \(H\), complete four mutually disjoint other holdings with one assigned place in each, then choose the remaining nine names freely. Hence the preceding stage has **exactly 200,393,770** words if it exists. The other 28 names would have to be shared. Any original source with these properties and the full restricted uniform table could be taken as founder, since its single loose 8 necessarily pays the unique unary birth with two fresh names. The conditional three-extensions count also gives the full 31-name table from its old pair rates: the new two signs have mutual correlation \(-1/3\) and their exterior rows are each \(-C_{8z}/3\). Any positive-age 29-name rival would yield such an admissible static presentation as well. Thus the uniform alternative reduces to the existence of this one type of 29-name source with the measured table. Resolving it goes beyond the inherited stop.
