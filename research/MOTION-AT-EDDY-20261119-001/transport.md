# A local parity pulse with one arriving distinction

Strict C1 transports one marked four-response in both directions on the plain backgrounds. It runs to the end of each finite path and returns in five or 31 updates on the isolated rings. An approach to a seam under the strict rule stops at the preceding centre. C-all reaches the attached parts by keeping every choice. This result uses the frozen first grown layer of each of the 14 starts. ## State and local update

At a fixed stage, the visible response \(R\) is an allowed change on four shared distinctions. The pulse carries one arriving distinction \(i\) in \(R\). Every response \(S\) with \(R\cap S=\{j\}\) and \(j\ne i\) gives the possible next state \((j,S)\). One such state gives a strict C1 move. Zero gives an end and more than one gives a tie. The rule excludes every alternative that leaves by the arriving distinction, including a move to a different response. The launch supplies one ordered overlapping pair. All later updates depend only on the current pair and its local meetings.

If \(x\) is the current allowed state, replace it by \(x\mathbin{\triangle}R\mathbin{\triangle}S\), where \(\triangle\) denotes toggling the named distinctions. Every tested support is an ordinary four-cycle with four holders, each meeting two of its changed distinctions. The replacement toggles six real distinctions and preserves all parity equations and every loose value. Two four-change events suffice, and one is too small for this particular replacement. The two toggles may be applied in either order. The route and arriving distinction belong to C1's declared state, so this decomposition is separate from B's intrinsic choice of one event from an unmarked binary state.

These fixtures have no nonzero ordinary change of size one, two or three. An even change decomposes into minimal circuits, so four is the smallest ordinary nonzero visible support here. Freeing loose distinctions admits 1,407 two-distinction changes across the panel. Adding a fixed allowed background to every state commutes with the replacement under the same given \(R,i\). For any given binary state every response is possible as its difference from some allowed background. Recovering the visible response therefore requires the reference or the mark.

Full enumeration gives one response per pair of old meetings at a common holder, with their common child. Every such response is minimal through an old distinction. Thus the full catalogue agrees with the catalogue restricted to these pairs on the tested stages. The old shared distinctions can also be read at this layer as those with both holders exhausted. That structural reading is used to grade response centres and source parts. The rule itself uses the full catalogue, with no preselected path or ring.

## Scattering and causal reach

If \(e\) is a shared distinction of old holders \(u\) and \(v\), its response roster contains \(d(u)-1+d(v)-1\) pairs, where \(d\) counts old meetings. After subtracting the current response, an exit through \(e\) has \(d(u)+d(v)-3\) choices. Thus a path interior advances, and an exit to a four-meeting seam from a two-meeting neighbour has three choices. Approaches from both sides stop there in strict C1. All pure-part launches on an attachment stay in their prepared part under strict C1. This also holds for the cut attachment C1. The pulse's basic tie reads the local meetings, which are present even on the cut control.

C-all closes a set of causal records under the same full exit relation, keeping both arrival roles wherever produced. Every record added lies on a finite directed route from the launch. Conversely a fair worklist adds every record on such a route by induction on its length. The same sets were recovered by FIFO, LIFO and a seeded arbitrary processing order in 2,600 comparisons. Minimum causal depth counts links of a shortest generating route. Each branch record is a possible arrival, including when two responses overlap. Strict C1 stops at those branching choices.

For J0, J1 and J2, 14 of 28 pure-path launches produce fan records belonging wholly to the ring. The longer J81 has 78 of 156. For T4, T8 and T81 respectively 18 of 28, 22 of 28 and 98 of 156 pure-path launches produce records on both rings. The remaining pure path launches reach neither. Every pure-ring launch on a single or double attachment produces path records. On the doubles each such launch also produces records on the other ring. From the two inward path endpoints the first pure-ring records have these exact depths after launch.

One local link moves the old meeting centre by at most one ordinary holder step. Any new distinction in the response is within two co-knotted-pair steps of the previous support, since both responses meet at a common changed distinction. The same radius around the current support contains every candidate response. All 752 unique strict links attain both bounds. The full fan roster has 1,010 one-step links and 264 links that keep the centre, all attaining the support bound of two. At causal depth \(k\) the corresponding net bounds are \(k\) and \(2k\). Finite straight runs attain the centre bound. Counting a composition of \(k\) successive straight links as one larger local application gives a larger displacement per application and keeps the cost of each constituent link. These counts concern the specified neighbourhood and update. The formation law that keeps an eddy at its source has no input from this pulse rule.

## Verification

The replay compares sparse response contacts with all literal response pairs, checks parity on every possible replacement, and recovers the same catalogue and exits in three independent recodings of each fixture. That tests 2,844 recoded arrival states. The reflection controls retain the literal permutations of all junctions and all distinctions.
