# What remains after a recurring tip and its companion are removed?

**Starts 03 and 14 each send exactly four pair children per round into the remaining junctions from age two onward. Their heavy widths are at least 73 and 100, respectively, at every age from seven onward.** The latter bounds come from complete, stationary restrictions around the tip. The same four-child interface occurs in connected examples of constant heavy width and in connected examples whose heavy width tends to infinity.

The heavy width counts every birth except the marked recurring one-parent tip and its forced companion, counting any overlap of marked births once. All distances below are ordinary adjacency counts in the finite candidate graph. A block at a positive age consists of the children of a common parent. A group of size at least three is a maximal candidate clique contained in no block. A candidate pair outside all such groups also gives one child. Each spare distinction gives a separate one-parent child. On a reached nonclosing stage a junction with \(p\) parents and group demand \(d\) has \(\max(d,3-p,1)\) loose distinctions. These statements use the funded rule and the earlier recurring-tip theorem. ## The exact boundary rule

Write \(e_t\) for the tip and \(b_t\) for its only candidate. The two children of \(e_t\) are \(e_{t+1}\), with just that parent, and \(b_{t+1}\), with parents \(\{e_t,b_t\}\). Take an age at which \(b_t\) already has this two-parent form. For both starts this holds from age two. Write \(A_t=N(b_t)\setminus\{e_t\}\) and \(R_t=V_t\setminus\{e_t,b_t\}\). Call the members of \(A_t\) the marked junctions of the remainder.

Every member of \(A_t\) is another child of \(b_{t-1}\). Thus \(A_t\cup\{b_t\}\) is held. The only groups containing \(b_t\) are its pair with the tip and a separate pair with each member of \(A_t\). Its two parents and positive demand give it exactly those distinctions. Hence

\[
A_{t+1}=\{\text{child of }\{b_t,a\}:a\in A_t\}.
\]

This transport is a bijection, so \(m=|A_t|\) stays constant. It marks the same \(m\) continuations however large the remainder becomes.

The ordinary internal groups are computed on \(R_t\) by restricting every old block to \(R_t\). In particular \(A_t\) stays held. A clique extendible by \(b_t\) is wholly in \(A_t\), so is already held. No group of size at least three touching the erased points can cover an internal pair. Thus the group list internal to \(R_t\) is exact. Its demand at a marked \(a\) is one less than the full demand at \(a\), accounting for its pair with \(b_t\). All other internal demands agree with the full ones.

For 03 and 14 the complete first birth roster has exactly one unary child. Its component is connected, as are all subsequent candidate rosters. Each other point has at least two parents and a candidate, hence at least one group and exactly enough distinctions for all its groups. Induction leaves \(e_t\) as the only unary, with its one spare. On those starts the next remainder therefore consists of exactly the ordinary internal groups **plus \(m\) marked pair children**, each with one visible parent \(a\) in the remainder and its second parent \(b_t\) outside. Keep the parent blocks formed by all the children of each retained parent, including these marked children. Also keep \(A_{t+1}\) as one held block formed by their common erased parent \(b_t\). This gives the exact next restricted down-closure. For any further unary junctions in a different source, first add their actual spare children with multiplicity using the full demand just described. The surviving tip and companion always exclude the unique globally implied closing birth. [Full-roster checks on 03 and 14](../GROWTH-CONTINUES-20261119-001/starts.md)

Erasing only \(e_t\) leaves \(b_t\) in the count and makes the retained width exactly one larger. In that description the forced child of \(\{e_t,b_t\}\) has the single retained parent \(b_t\) and true arity two. Erasing both gives the \(m\)-child rule above. Erasing all descendants of the original tip can remove the whole generation, as the example below shows.

For several tracked tips take the union of the marked births at each step. Two reciprocal tips can initially produce the same pair child. After one step every new tip is unary and each companion is a pair child. One more step gives distinct companions, since two such pairs with distinct unary parents cannot be equal. After the third step their parent pairs are disjoint, so companions of different tips are incompatible. On the fourth step the sets \(A_t\) for distinct tracks are disjoint as well, since a shared new member would have two incompatible old companions as parents. These properties persist. The same restricted-block recurrence now applies with one marked set per tip. In each of the intervening steps the literal union handles coinciding names. Two reciprocal tips produce a path of lengths in vertices \(2,3,4,5,\ldots\). The two tips share a companion at width three and the two marked sets share a remaining vertex at width five.

## One more layer settles at every finite interface

Once the marked junctions are \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\) with their transported labels, the parents of \(a_i\) are the preceding companion and the preceding \(i\)-th marked junction. Let \(X\) be the junctions at candidate distance three from the tip. For each \(x\in X\), put \(I(x)=\{i:x\sim a_i\}\), which is nonempty. Its members are exactly the preceding marked junctions that are parents of \(x\), with their labels transported. The neighbors of \(a_i\) split into two held blocks through \(a_i\). One consists of the companion and the marked junctions. The other consists of \(a_i\) and the \(x\) whose \(I(x)\) contains \(i\).

Consequently every large group through a marked junction contains at least two marked junctions, indexed by \(S\), and at least one member of \(X\). Put \(X_S=\{x:S\subseteq I(x)\}\). All of \(X_S\) are mutual candidates through any one index in \(S\). The maximal group with those marked indices is exactly \(S\cup X_S\), with \(S\) interpreted as junctions, and exists precisely when \(X_S\ne\varnothing\) and \(S=\bigcap_{x\in X_S} I(x)\). It is unheld because any two marked junctions have only the common old companion as a shared parent and no \(x\in X\) has that parent. A singleton profile \(I(x)=\{i\}\) gives exactly the residual pair \(\{a_i,x\}\). Each incidence \(a_i\sim x\) with \(|I(x)|\ge2\) is already covered by the group indexed by \(I(x)\). The pair \(a_i,a_j\) receives its own child exactly when no \(I(x)\) contains both indices. No point beyond \(X\) can belong to a group through \(a_i\).

The next layer at distance three thus preserves the number of singleton profiles separately at each index. For profiles of size at least two it has one child per distinct nonempty-family intersection of the old profiles of size at least two whose intersection has size at least two, plus one two-index profile for each uncovered pair. This operation is idempotent. All pairs are now covered, the intersections are closed under further intersections of size at least two, and the added two-sets cannot introduce any further such intersection. Once the profiles are fixed, the displayed parent rule fixes their incidence after one further application. The outer junctions' demands can still depend on distance four. This proves eventual stationarity of the first three layers for any such tip. Multiplicity of identical singleton profiles is retained.

## Stationary five-layer restrictions on 03 and 14

For the distinguished child \(e'\), distances of all other children \(y\) obey

\[
\operatorname{dist}(e',y)=1+\min_{p\in P(y)}\operatorname{dist}(e,p).
\]

An old candidate path to the closest parent gives the upper bound by choosing one child witnessing each successive pair. Conversely, choose one shared old parent along each step of a new path from \(e'\) to \(y\). Its first shared parent must be \(e\) and the last belongs to \(P(y)\). Consecutive old choices belong to the same child, so are equal or old candidates. This gives the lower bound.

Fix \(r\ge1\). A new point in the closed ball of radius \(r\) is either the new tip or a child of an old point at distance at most \(r-1\). Every group through such an interior point, every possible extension, and every witness group covering one of its candidate pairs lies entirely in the old radius-\(r\) ball. Its entire group demand is therefore local. On a general positive reached stage, mark which points are unary. With this information one reconstructs the exact spare count at each interior point, including multiple children with the same single parent, using the funded fresh-count formula. The unary flag suffices because \(3-p\le1\) for \(p\ge2\). An outside or boundary unary contributes no unary child to the new ball. Every parent of every child in the new ball is in the old ball, so the shared parent blocks of the reconstructed children are also locally exact. The flag of every child is read directly from its true parent list. Thus the rooted ball, its restricted down-closure and the unary flags have a deterministic local successor. A rooted isomorphism with this successor proves stationarity through all future rounds. Outer demands and global group counts need not repeat.

Transporting the full maximal restricted blocks through each bijection gives equality. The portable certificates also store every parent's position in every local birth. ## Identical boundary widths permit bounded and unbounded growth

For each \(m\ge0\) there is a stationary single-tip example with \(m\) marked arrivals, heavy width \(2^m-1\), and \(2^m-m-1\) births of arity at least three in every round. Label its points \(E\) and \(v_I\) for \(I\subseteq[m]\). Across consecutive generations prescribe the parent lists

\[
P(E)=\{E\},\qquad P(v_\varnothing)=\{E,v_\varnothing\},\qquad
P(v_I)=\{v_I\}\cup\{v_{I-\{j\}}:j\in I\}\quad (I\ne\varnothing).
\]

The held blocks are the pair \(E,v_\varnothing\) and the upper stars consisting of \(v_I\) and its immediate supersets. Among subset labels a candidate pair is one inclusion adding a single index or a swap between equal-size sets. A clique meeting two consecutive cardinalities lies in an upper star or a lower star. Indeed two different lower sets determine the only possible upper set containing both. Within one cardinality, fix two members \(C+a,C+b\). Every other member either contains \(C\) or is contained in \(C+a+b\), and a member of the first kind strictly outside \(C+a+b\) cannot coexist with one of the second kind missing an index of \(C\). Thus each clique of mutually swapped sets extends into one of these stars. The unheld maximal groups are exactly the lower stars with top size at least two. The only uncovered pairs are \(E,v_\varnothing\) and \(v_\varnothing,v_{\{i\}}\). The demands are \(1,m+1\), and \(1+m-|I|\) at \(E,v_\varnothing,v_I\) with \(I\ne\varnothing\), respectively. The fresh rule gives just one spare, at \(E\). This proves exactly the stated roster and its repetition.

Every later \(v_I\) can inherit from \(v_\varnothing\) by adding one of its indices at a time and can then use its same-labelled parent indefinitely. By age \(m+1\) the *whole generation* descends from the one original tip. The heavy count still equals \(2^m-1\). At \(m=2\) the restricted internal roster has three points, the three separate pair blocks and one unheld triple. Each point has exactly one distinction left after reserving the two boundary contributions at their respective holders. Run in isolation, the sole triple consumes all three distinctions and closes. Inside the full stage the two marked arrivals give a stationary three-point remainder. This directly tests the danger in applying the autonomous growth law to the heavy vertices alone.

These controls have physical parity starts. Make one old dead junction per parent label, and share one distinction with each of its named children. Give every live child its specified loose count. Connect each old dead junction to two additional dead junctions, and join those two together. Every ordinary junction has at least three places. In the graph that wires all loose distinctions to a common exterior, deletion of any single vertex leaves the remainder connected. If a dead or padding vertex is deleted, the other padding vertex still links the surviving old junctions and each live point has an exterior connection. If a live point or the exterior is deleted, every remaining live point still reaches an old junction and the old junctions remain linked. Every old junction has a live child, and there are at least two old labels, so the exterior stays attached after any applicable deletion. Hence each even assignment at one ordinary junction extends to a complete parity state by pairing its incident changes along paths avoiding that junction. This verifies the local projection as well as the arity floor. The stationary examples have trivial filled fundamental group. Each swap step can be replaced by two single-index steps through the intersection, using a filled triangle. Different index changes then commute across squares, each filled by two triangles, until repeated changes cancel. The tip is a pendant.

For every \(m\ge1\) there is also a connected one-tip example with exactly \(m\) marked arrivals and heavy width tending to infinity. Start with a leaf \(e\) at \(b\), with \(m\) further neighbors of \(b\). Include one such neighbor in a four-cycle with incompatible opposite vertices. Cone the four-cycle to one additional point and hold each of its four triangles as its own block. Hold each tree pair separately. The filled candidate graph at the start is contractible. Every required group is a pair. Give the tip two distinctions and every other point exactly its pair demand. At any other degree-one point add a private singleton incidence to meet the arity floor. The same padded physical realization verifies the parity projections. There is exactly one offshoot throughout.

The pair children of the consecutive rim pairs form a new induced four-cycle. Two consecutive such children share the middle rim parent. Any other child meeting both without this parent would need to contain the incompatible outer rim endpoints. Every clique through the two is therefore held at their shared parent, and their pair birth is required. Induction continues the cycle. The minimum distance of its four members from the tip increases by one every round by the distance formula. An ordinary shortest path to the nearest cycle member has at least that many distinct vertices besides the tip, so deleting the two light points leaves a heavy count tending to infinity. A fixed \(m\), a single unary tip, a connected candidate graph and a trivial filled fundamental group thus permit both outcomes, including at the observed \(m=4\). The cyclic construction supplies a separate protected interior condition.

There is also a direct limitation on increasing the depth of local stationarity checks. On the path with two recurring tips every step adds one vertex. At the chosen left tip every fixed finite-radius restriction eventually repeats for all time, while the heavy width after deleting both ends and their companions grows by one per round. Even success at every radius separately allows the growing end to move beyond each resolved radius. The earlier chain and relay identifications count paths and rays under their own block hypotheses. Here the exact boundary and local update also apply amid unrestricted overlapping groups.
