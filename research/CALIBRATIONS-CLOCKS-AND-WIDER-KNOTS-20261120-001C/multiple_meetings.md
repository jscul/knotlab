# Reading arbitrarily many wider knots with repeated meetings

The [main theorem](proof.md) recognizes individual parts from exact uniform pair rates. One additional kind of part can be recognized on those same named observations. Inside the part let there be three or more holders. Forget the multiplicity of each meeting and require the resulting pattern of holders to be a tree. Each such meeting shares at least two names. Each holder also has at least two names used only there *inside this part*. Those names may acquire a second holder through a one-name attachment outside the part. This condition allows arbitrarily many larger holders, parallel distinctions and arbitrary branching. It is a promise on competing stages. The extension and its membership checks were [separately specified before their outcomes](multiple_meetings_freeze.md).

## The hidden groups can be read

Group together the private names of each local holder. Also group together all the names in each meeting of two holders. These groups partition the local names, and each group has at least two members. A mixed word inside a group has already satisfied every rule containing it. Consequently each mixed word there has the same number of exterior completions. Complementation exchanges the two constant group words. Write $p_U$ for the probability that all names of U are equal. For a pair of names in U, conditional on mixed the pair correlation is $-1/(2^{|U|-1}-1)$. Thus the within-group pair correlation $C$ gives
$$
p_U=C+(1-C)2^{1-|U|}.
$$
Let $M_U$ be the common sign in a constant group and zero in a mixed group. For x in U and y in another group V,
$$
C_{xy}=\mathbb E[M_U M_V],\qquad K_{UV}=C_{xy}/\sqrt{p_U p_V}.
$$
To see the identity, first condition on which groups are constant and on their constant values. Patterns in each remaining mixed group then have independent uniform choices, so the contribution involving a bit in a mixed group is zero. Each $p_U>0$ because U can be constant while all other groups are mixed. Strictly $|K_{UV}|<1$: U can be mixed while V is constant, and vice versa. These events give nonproportional $M_U,M_V$ in the Cauchy–Schwarz bound.

Removing a physical holder H splits its physical tree into independent preparations on each incident group. In this removal the groups on H are mutually independent, and every group has complement symmetry. Apply H again. Its only rejected assignments on its own names are the two constant words. For any two different groups of H the signed expectation, zero before imposing this last rule, decreases by the same amount. Its new value is $-u_H<0$. For these incident groups $p_U>u_H$: choose x in U and y at another group in H, condition on U being constant, and use the full mixed projection on H. Because H has at least two positions outside U, both values of y occur under that conditioning. Consequently $|\mathbb E[s_y\mid U\text{ constant at }s]|<1$. The absolute correlation of x,y is strictly smaller than $p_U$. Full local projection and essentiality used here follow directly for this constructed class: prescribe any mixed word on H, then make the private group of each other holder mixed. A local constant word violating H alone extends in the same way.

For two distinct physical groups on opposite sides of a shared meeting U, the normalized correlations multiply at U:
$$
K_{XY}=K_{XU}K_{UY}.
$$
After a word on U is given, its two sides are independent. On mixed U the conditional expected sign from either side is zero, since the incident knot is already satisfied and each outside side may be complemented. On U constant with value s, each such expectation is an odd linear function of s. Multiplying and averaging proves $C_{xy}=C_{xu}C_{uy}/p_U$ for any $u\in U$, and the displayed normalization follows. In particular the sign between different groups is $(-1)^d$ where d is the number of holders on their connecting route, with one holder counted for co-held groups. No such correlation is zero. Within a private group the individual bits are independent before H is imposed, so any two of its names also have correlation $-u_H$.

The groups need not be marked. Two names in one group have interchangeable rows in the pair table when their mutual entry is left out. Two co-held names from different physical groups are distinguishable: one of their groups also belongs to another holder K, since H has just one private group. A private name in K has negative correlation to that shared name and positive correlation to the other. For two noncoheld names x,y linked through an odd number of holders, a name in the first intervening shared group has negative correlation to x and positive to y. For an even number of holders take a private name z of the first holder distinct from x. There are at least two such private names when x itself is private. Then $C_{xz}<0$, even in the same private group, while $C_{yz}>0$. This proves that the sets of interchangeable rows recover exactly the physical groups.

## The normalized reading determines the holders

Two different recovered groups X,Y belong to a common holder exactly when $|K_{XY}|>|K_{XZ}K_{ZY}|$ for every third group Z. Without a common holder, an intermediate shared group gives equality. For co-held X,Y, a third group Z of the same H gives the product in unnormalized units $u_H^2/p_Z<u_H$. A group farther away enters H at one group W. If W equals X or Y, the product through Z equals $K_{XY}K_{WZ}^2$ in magnitude. Otherwise it equals $K_{XW}K_{WY}K_{WZ}^2$. The already proved strict bounds apply in both cases. No square roots need be estimated numerically: compare $|C_{xy}|p_Z$ directly with $|C_{xz}C_{zy}|$.

Maximal sets of pairwise co-held groups are exactly the holders. Distinct holders meet at just one *group*. A pairwise co-held set of groups without a common holder would require a closed sequence of at least three holders in the assumed tree. Each holder contains its own private group, so none can be a subset of another. The union of the names in each recovered set is therefore precisely one physical holding.

To apply this calculation after arbitrary single-name attachments, note first that the original, unnormalized strict-product relation contains all pairs from different groups of the same physical holder. For a third name in one of those two groups the product is strictly smaller by $|C|<1$ inside a group. Through a third group Z it is bounded by the normalized inequality times $p_Z\le1$. These named pairs keep the whole part connected after deleting any one name, because every group has at least two members and the holder tree is connected. Hence the global single-name decomposition in the main theorem detects the boundary of this entire part before any normalization.

Every triple in this class can be assigned zero on all three names. Put one on other names first, then give a private name value zero at every still-constant holder. Each such holder was untouched by the pins and has a private name available. These changes do not affect any other holder. Its minimum arity is four. The measured part therefore has no forced mixed triple. It also has positive pairs between the private groups of adjacent holders. In a double holder, its positive pairs form exactly a complete bipartite relation on its nonisolated names. Here choose successive holders L,H,R. A private name of L, one of H, one of R and a shared name of L,H form a three-link sequence of positive pairs, yet its first and last names are co-held and negative. This is impossible in a complete bipartite relation. Thus the new parts can be distinguished from the other analytic possibilities. A previously certified finite entry has a unique named state set against *all* connected physically lawful rivals on those names and so cannot give a conflicting result.

## An actual first and second reached stage

Take three holders, two successive double meetings, and three private names at each holder. This thirteen-name part exceeds the fixed ten-name catalogue. A three-ternary preparation $x12,134,234$ has just its attachment x loose. Use distinct copies to close all private names of the wider part except one. Attach a new ternary to the remaining port; give it a second attachment to a fresh copy of the three-ternary preparation and one private name of its own. Only this new ternary is live. The wider part is preserved at its original named single attachments while genuine ordinary growth takes place outside it. Its first two steps give one and two children. The next cut makes three. Every pair in the full structure can be calculated exactly by multiplication over its single-name attachments, using the local complete word sets. The decoder was given only that resulting exact table. It recovers the same allowed words through both actual cuts. Taking more wider holders gives arbitrarily large examples outside both the former analytical classes and the ten-name dictionary. The theorem neither assumes nor estimates a correlation from a sample of words. The fresh samples choose physical source structures to measure how many reached stages satisfy the structural premise. An arbitrary wider part with no private names or with a cycle after meetings are reduced to one per holder pair lies outside this proof.

Reconstruction by pair observations on a hidden tree has established precedents. Here the measurable group-agreement probability supplies the necessary normalization, and the equal negative correlations between different groups in a common physical knot identify the holders.
