# The copier study, ticket C: the claim, stated before it is tested

Epic `MADE-COPIER-20261125-001`, ticket `MADE-COPIER-20261125-001C` (assumption A249, with A248). Words are the
glossary's and those of [ticket A's definitions](definitions.md) ("D"), [ticket B's definitions](definitions_b.md)
("DB") and [ticket C's definitions](definitions_c.md) ("DC"); no new noun is used. The tests are registered in [ticket
C's pre-registration](../MADE-COPIER-20261125-001C/preregistration.md) ("PC"). Tickets A and B ([claim](claim.md),
[claim_b](claim_b.md)) are not changed here. Versions 1 and 2 of this text are filed in the advisor folder as sent;
where a reading changed, the change is said where it is made.

## The owner's words

His third addition, 24 November 2026 (Pacific time; the record's tickets carry 25 November, its date in UTC), while
tickets A and B were registered and waiting for their engine:

> "What if we need to statistically hit a 'copier' like DNA and primordial soup?"

His standing words for the study: "copying would not be a law. Laws are for how the knots interact. A copy function,
like other functions, would be a logical structure that has a meeting pattern with other things." And: "a law is the
rule the growth follows (which knots join, what a new knot receives, what a short knot does); a copier is a pattern the
growth makes." DNA and a primordial soup are his pictures; no claim is made about living things, minds, physics or time.
His inkling (A248) is kept apart from the evidence, as in tickets A and B.

## 1. How his words are read

His addition changes the method, not the question. Tickets A and B build a first pattern X and try it on a registered
class of second patterns, with the takes fixed by a stated rule. Ticket C lets the growth make both patterns and their
meeting, samples very many growths, and asks how often the growth makes a pattern that leaves two of what it meets. A
copier is a pattern; how often the growth makes one is a rate under a weighting.

- **Three things are sampled or enumerated, each under a named weighting** (DC 1). The starts: the record's census of
  three to five knots with single meetings, under the census weighting (each start once) and the labelled weighting
  (each start by its number of knot numberings). The laws: a stated family of 42 laws under each knot law, each reported
  on its own; no rate is averaged over laws, since that would need a weighting of laws, which nothing supplies. The
  histories: each take sampled under the take-by-take weighting (D 2.7's) and, as its swap, the holder weighting. No
  take is fixed by a rule and no history is picked.
- **The scale**, set by the owner's rule of 25 November 2026 on compute (no run longer than about an hour; PC, "Runs",
  with costs measured at the registered bounds): about 135,700 sampled growths of at most 8 rounds under the
  take-by-take weighting in the main runs (under parity, every one of the 3,344 starts once under the 21 laws with MET,
  and the first 2,000 starts of one random order under the 21 with STEPS-1; under not-all-equal the first 1,000 under
  MET and the first 120 under STEPS-1), and 21,000 under the holder weighting (the first 500 starts, the 42 laws,
  parity); about 160,000 in all, with the checks R0b, R7 and R8 besides, in about three and a half hours.
- **His "primordial soup", read.** Every pattern that meets is descended from one supplied start of three to five knots,
  and the meetings are made by the law's takes, not by mixing. A start of the census is a few knots meeting singly, and
  the census holds every such start, each once: no arrangement is chosen. Two parts in different connected structures
  are never brought together by either taking rule (C-S6). **Not read here**, and left to a later ticket of this study:
  two census starts, each sampled under the census weighting, joined at round 0 by one new shared distinction between
  one uniformly sampled knot of each; for it, "the round-0 meeting is supplied by the weighting; every later meeting is
  made". Meetings supplied during the growth would be a supplied rule of the law's kind, and would need the owner's
  leave.
- **No taking rule is left out on purpose.** With no taking rule, parts that share nothing never become one part (FD
  3.1), so nothing the growth makes meets anything after the start; ticket B is the census of that case.
- **His "hit", read: a case** (DC 4.3), a pair of a sampled growth (X a made pattern, Y a part wholly apart from it)
  that the growth makes one part, after which a claimed reading is met: two parts the same as Y, Y's form not X's form,
  each with every member descended from X only (form) or from both (from both); or, under parity, two usable loose made
  distinctions tied to one place of Y, both descended from X only (values, both from X). The values readings with a
  distinction from both, and ticket A's values reading (at least one from X), are reported, each with its own verdict.
- **A case is one sample; whether it is met more often with Y is a comparison of rates** (DC 4.3, 4.4). From the state
  after the round the pair is read, continuations are run with nothing barred, with Y barred, with X barred (Y's own),
  masked (X and Y unable to join, FD 5.6) and fully masked (unable to join, and neither able to take a distinction held
  by the other). A case **passes at 100** when, in 100 continuations of each of the first three kinds, the reading is
  met after the meeting more often with nothing barred than with Y barred and than Y leaves two of its own; it is
  **confirmed**, **a case met more often with Y**, when on 1,000 fresh continuations it is met more often than with Y
  barred, than Y's own and than fully masked, at levels that keep the chance of confirming any case not met more often
  than all three at most 0.01 over the main runs. With it its **way** is reported: **through joining** when it is also
  met more often than masked, **through takes** when it is not. A case above both barred controls but not above fully
  masked is reported as **with Y's births, not through a meeting**, and is not counted. One met in none of the 1,000
  continuations with Y barred and none of the 1,000 fully masked is **a case that needs the meeting** (each rate below
  0.3% at 0.95). By SD line 14 and FD 1.2 a birth that takes a distinction held by a knot of Y meets that knot, so a
  meeting runs through joining or through takes; the masked control alone would test only the joining. The fully masked
  kind was added after the scratch; it leans toward a positive (a case passes it more easily than version 2's masked
  control), and no scratch case of a claimed reading changes verdict by it. For from both, the Y-barred, masked and
  fully masked controls are empty by construction, and only Y's own tests it.
- **No positive control made by the growth exists.** Ticket A's designs under sampled takes give no case that passes at
  100 (PC, "Known before the runs"), so **the test's sight rests on a planted case**: two parts of Y's form placed by
  hand into some continuations of one registered pair, read and never grown, counted toward nothing (DC 4.7; PC, R0b),
  with a machinery control and negative controls beside it.
- **What turns cases into a copier**: one form of made pattern with cases met more often with Y for at least two forms
  of second pattern, each seen again in at least 3 of 100 fresh growths of its start, and not of the others (a
  **candidate seen again**, DC 5.2).
- **What a zero bounds**: each law separately, at 0.95, the rate of growths holding a case that passes at 100 (or, when
  some pass and none is confirmed, a confirmed case): below 3/N for a prefix of N starts of the random order, 3/(K N)
  for every start read K times, about 1.6 times that under the labelled weighting (DC 4.5).

## 2. The law, fixed first

The family (DC 1.3): rations FLOOR1 (COPY-LAW, the fold study's name, not a law of copying), FBP (FLOOR1 with the fresh
count by parents), CAP-2, CAP-3, CAP-4, ORD and RATION-2, each with CANCEL-RETIRE, CANCEL-OFFSHOOT and CANCEL-WAIT, with
each of ticket A's two taking rules, MET and STEPS-1, and RELEASE, under parity and under not-all-equal: 84 laws with
their knot laws, each read, none sampled. STEPS-1 is in the family, not only a swap, because in the scratch, under
parity on the starts of three and four knots and the take-by-take weighting, the roster had two parts or more in 1% of
the rounds read under MET and in 35% under STEPS-1; it was added after the scratch, which favours a positive, and is
disclosed. The swaps HOLD for RELEASE, STEPS-2 for STEPS-1 and every pair are conditional runs, run only if a case of a
claimed reading is confirmed; the holder weighting runs in the main study, reduced. If the swaps do not run, the claim
says (PC, "Runs"): "Not run: HOLD, STEPS-2, every pair. The negative is under RELEASE, MET and STEPS-1, with at most
eight pairs read a round; [n] rounds had more. The drafter's scratch on the starts of three and four knots (unreviewed,
6 rounds) saw no case met more often with Y under those swaps." The law is never changed for a pattern, and no rule is
added for ending one.

## 3. The claims

> **CC-1(L; T): a case met more often with Y is made.** Under law L, with takes sampled under the weighting T, some
> sampled growth has a confirmed case of a claimed reading (DC 4.4), in a growth with no failing knot.

> **CC-2(L; T): a candidate seen again.** Under L and T, some form F of made pattern is a candidate seen again (DC 5.2):
> his "copier", read under sampling.

Every result is also given as **rates** (DC 4.5): for each law, knot law, reading, weighting of starts and weighting of
takes, by start size, the share of sampled growths holding a confirmed case, with sample sizes and seeds, over all
growths and over growths with no failing knot. A zero is stated, when no case passes at 100, as: "No growth held a case
that passes at 100. Under the [weighting], the rate of growths holding one is below [bound] at 0.95, each law
separately. Smaller differences are not bounded." (DC 4.5 gives the sentence when some pass.)

## 4. Settled by argument (PC, "Settled by construction"; the runs check them)

Arguments of the drafter, not refereed.

- **C-S1, renaming (PC, Lemma C-S1).** Under every law of the family and both swaps, with takes sampled under either
  weighting, the weighting of the growth's states from a renamed start is the image of the weighting from the start, so
  every rate of DC 4.5 is the same for every numbering of the start. The state is the structure with its barred marks,
  retirements and birth record (DC 1.6). The steps of the round are nameless except in which loose distinction goes to
  which birth and in HOLD's "first" (SD lines 36–54, FD 2.1); a sampled take gives each candidate a chance read off the
  state. The result will be worded: **"argued for every law of the family and both swaps (the drafter's outline, not
  refereed); checked on every history through round 3, or fewer where said, of [n] small starts under parity, up to a
  Weisfeiler–Lehman class, [M] of the checks holding a take with two or more candidates; and by sample at 8 rounds on
  one statistic."** A single sampled history can still differ under a renaming; that is the sample's, not the rate's.
- **C-S2, not-all-equal: no values case.** Every distinction of a start of the census has at most two holders, so no two
  are tied under not-all-equal (the record: `READS-HOLDERS-SOURCES-20261120-001/2`; ticket A's S6); a birth with a
  single distinct input receives at least two fresh distinctions; a birth with one fresh distinction has two or more
  inputs, never all tied equal while nothing is tied; so by ticket A's S2 (which, with three or more holders, is
  unrefereed) no made distinction is tied. **None of the values readings is met under not-all-equal, for any start, law
  of the family or history.** Checked: no tie at round 0 under not-all-equal in any of the 3,376 starts (PC).
- **C-S3, COPY-LAW with a taking rule: what forms can be left.** By ticket A's S4, under FLOOR1 with MET or STEPS-1
  every knot made by a birth other than an offshoot of a start knot holds at most one loose distinction. By D 2.6, X's
  own members count as descended from X only, and one of them can be an offshoot of a start knot holding two. Under
  CANCEL-RETIRE and CANCEL-OFFSHOOT with RELEASE every member after a round was made in that round, so the parts of a
  form or from-both case have members holding at most one, and so has Y, whose form is theirs: **argued for those four
  laws**. Under CANCEL-WAIT (and under HOLD) a member made earlier can wait and sit in one of the two parts, so there it
  is **checked**, not argued.
- **C-S4, no taking rule**: as section 1; covered by ticket B.
- **C-S5, the bound**: DC 4.5.
- **C-S6, one connected start**: a birth takes only a distinction held by a knot its parent meets (MET) or by a parent
  of its parent (STEPS-1), each in its parent's connected structure, so every knot made stays there; two parts in
  different connected structures are never one part. It leaves unread only the reading of his "primordial soup" that
  section 1 leaves to a later ticket.

## 5. What is tested, and predicted (by census; computed, bounded)

The drafter's scratch runs (PC, "Known before the runs": the census's samples under the 21 laws with MET, 23,554 sampled
growths; 4,536 each under STEPS-1, HOLD and the holder weighting on the starts of three and four knots under parity;
ticket A's made designs under the 42 laws; 6 rounds, at most 300 knots) give these predictions for the registered runs
(8 rounds, at most 500 knots, DC 1); PC's table of predictions holds them with their marks.

- **Under MET, pairs are few**: under each of the 20 laws other than COPY-LAW(CANCEL-OFFSHOOT), under each knot law and
  on each start size, at most 10% of sampled growths have a pair in rounds 1 to 4 (scratch: at most 1.1% on each size,
  pooled over those laws; 0.2% to 4.5% by law). In the scratch, under parity on the starts of three and four knots and
  the take-by-take weighting, the roster had no part or one part in 99% of the rounds read. Under
  COPY-LAW(CANCEL-OFFSHOOT), between 20% and 55% on five-knot starts (scratch: 36% under each knot law) and between 25%
  and 65% on three and four (scratch: 37% to 51%).
- **Under STEPS-1, pairs are many**: at least a fifth of sampled growths have one, under each law, on the starts of
  three and four knots (scratch, parity: 33% to 91% by law); on five-knot starts not seen.
- **No confirmed case of a claimed reading** in the main runs, under any law or weighting: CC-1 and CC-2 are not found,
  and each rate is given as its bound (DC 4.5). In the scratch no case was confirmed. Under MET the 92 form cases, each
  read at 100 continuations of each kind, were met with Y barred as often as with nothing barred or more often in 90;
  none passed at 100. Under STEPS-1 (52 cases), HOLD (6) and the holder weighting (2), none was shown to be met more
  often with Y; the three that were met in none of 20 continuations with Y barred or with X barred were one-history
  cases (at most 1 of 200 with nothing barred). No case of from both was seen.
- **Ticket A's values reading, its own verdict, with a prediction**: under COPY-LAW(CANCEL-OFFSHOOT) and parity some
  cases pass the Y-barred and Y's-own comparisons; whether any is confirmed rests on the fully masked control, not run
  in the scratch; none is "through joining". In the scratch, of its 4 cases, read at 1,000 fresh continuations of each
  kind, two pass the first two (with nothing barred 83 and 58 of 1,000; with Y barred 33 and 2; Y's own 33 and 13), and
  masked, with X and Y unable to join, the reading is met 89 and 85 times: as often or more, so the excess over the
  Y-barred control comes not from the joining; takes were not blocked. In the second case every one of the 58
  continuations that met it had one of the two distinctions tied to Y's place made by a birth descended from X only and
  the other by a birth descended from Y only; by ticket A's S3 the X-only birth's tie runs through its inputs (whether
  through a take was not read). In the first case, of 83, 37 had both made by X-only births, 42 one of each, and 4
  three. Read as values, both from X, neither passes the Y-barred comparison (39 against 33, masked 38; and 0, 2). That,
  and one of the two being Y's own, is why they are not a copier; the masked result is not the reason.
- **Second patterns of form cases, in the scratch** (its samples of starts, the take-by-take weighting). Under MET, 87
  of the 92 have a second pattern of one member holding one loose distinction, joinable with none (a lone member holding

- **The controls of the rate rule** (PC, R0b; DC 4.7). Ticket A's made designs under sampled takes give no positive
  control: in 183 of their 210 cells (design and law, parity) the claimed readings were met after the meeting with
  nothing barred in 2, at 1 of 100, and none passes at 100. Registered instead: the planted case, whose three arms must
  give pass, fail, fail; the machinery control under its own rule (Y's own values on made-4 and made-5 under
  COPY-LAW(CANCEL-OFFSHOOT) with STEPS-1: 78 and 86 of 100 with nothing barred, 0 with Y barred); and negative controls,
  none of which may be confirmed.
- **What would overturn the predictions**: one confirmed case of a claimed reading under any law; one candidate seen
  again (CC-2), which would also bear on the first sentence of his inkling if its second-pattern forms were some and not
  all (with the others read, not only low in power); a planted arm 1 that is not confirmed (the test would not see what
  it should).

## 6. The owner's inkling (A248), as it bears here

Ticket C reads no pattern of a registered class, so no failure here says that a copier cannot be generalized, and no
case is built for a second pattern to change. A candidate seen again for some second-pattern forms and not others, each
"other" met often enough to bound its rate, would be an instance for his first sentence under sampling; his second
sentence is not read.

## 7. Verdicts, as three questions (DC 6)

Who made the two (the reading's descent); what fixed the takes (**sampled**, under the take-by-take or the holder
weighting: a third answer, a stated rule and a picked history being the other two); where X, Y and their meeting come
from (X made by births, its members at s = 1 children of start knots; Y made, or holding start knots; the knots through
which they meet, made by births or start knots). A case is called **made** only when X and Y are both made patterns, the
knots through which they meet are made by births, and the takes are sampled; otherwise it is reported as supplied in
that part (DC 6).

## 8. Law and pattern, kept apart

The laws are fixed in section 2 and never adjusted; the rations are of the law, and a part is never said to have one. X
and Y are parts the growth makes; what is left is made by births under that law. The weightings are of the sampling, not
of the growth: they say how often, under a declared random process, and nothing chooses a history (lesson 7.3 of the
Intuitions guide; A71 and A110). No claim is made about living things, minds, physics or time.
