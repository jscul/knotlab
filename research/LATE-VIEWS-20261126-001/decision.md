## What stands

1. **A local reader's view is read off the start's finite map, for nearly every accepted start.** Each kind of birth
   counted per round, in both readings, is a finite sum of binomial coefficients over the valid sets of the start's core
   (live knots and pendant points, with each start knot's places for round one), and the settling round follows once
   each kind's terms are summed and their last departure from the eventual polynomial is located. On a held-out
   uniform sample of 2,000 six-knot census starts, 705 of the 706 accepted agree with their growth in every round, under
   both knot laws (705 of the 747 that grow; under parity 704 of 705 when each start must pass K1–K3); so do all 70
   accepted seven-knot starts of a second weighting and all 100 grown to round 40 (95 reached it in the run, the
   referee's replay the other five).
2. **The one-or-all reading of groups carries most of it.** Of the 706, 125 are covered by the proved identification
   (39 directly, 86 after the reduction lemma removes repeated blocks); the other 581 need round-1 groups read as cells,
   the cluster-count study's rule, and 580 of them agree. On the starts it gets right, dropping the cells introduces 580
   errors and dropping the relay 216.
3. **The group-cell reading as frozen fails where two cells share a knot**: all 40 tested starts with cells sharing a
   knot fail from round one (42 such starts in the whole six-knot census); two other starts, each with a block meeting a
   four-knot cell, fail from round two. A reading found afterwards, the valid sets as a graded order with parents the
   sets a birth covers and fresh distinctions the sets covering it, fixes the 40; it is post hoc and untested.

**Not established.** Pieces with a cycle (1.8% of the six-knot census, not predicted); the two misses the revised reading
leaves; any proof of the cell reading; starts of eight knots or more (one hand-built eight-knot start was tested, and
missed; no sample at that size).
