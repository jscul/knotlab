# Recognizing loose distinctions under either weighting

On the selected 29-name observation, the loose distinctions are exactly 9 through 28. The five current live knots give ten births, one for each pair, each receiving three fresh distinctions. This conclusion holds separately under uniform weighting of complete words and recursive division of each older word's mass amongst its extensions. The rival may start at any physical source and have any positive observation age. The proof uses the two pair tables separately, with formation ages withheld. ## One marked loose distinction identifies its siblings

Write \(C_{xy}=1-2r_{xy}=\mathbb E[s_xs_y]\), with signs \(s_x=\pm1\). Call two distinct names *twins in the table* if \(C_{xz}=C_{yz}\) for every \(z\ne x,y\). A latest knot \(P\) takes one previously loose input from each of its distinct parents and receives \(q\ge1\) fresh names. All earlier loose names are consumed. Latest siblings use disjoint names. A loose \(f\in P\) has strictly negative row minimum, attained exactly on \(P\setminus\{f\}\). Under either weighting, the loose siblings in \(P\) are table twins by permutation of their bits. The converse within \(P\) also holds. [Ordinary growth and loose-row minimum](../STATE-DISTANCE-AND-REACHED-GROWTH-20261120-001B/reading.md#what-ordinary-formation-guarantees)

Under uniform weighting, if \(u\in P\) were an old input with former holder \(H_u\), every \(z\in H_u\setminus\{u\}\) would give \(C_{uz}-C_{fz}<0\). The swap calculation appears below. Under inherited weighting, restriction to old names gives exactly the preceding measure. A previously loose \(u\) has a negative row minimum there, attained on every former companion. Its two bits have equal weight when these companions are mixed, and it is forced opposite when they agree. If \(P\) has at least two old inputs and \(u,f\) are twins, the minima in \(u\)'s old row are exactly the other old inputs of \(P\), by the strict minimum for \(f\). None can be a former companion of \(u\) since the inputs were loose in distinct former holders. If \(u\) is the sole input, the conditional mean of \(s_f\) given \(s_u\) is \(-s_u/(2^q-1)\). Twinning would force \(C_{uz}=0\) for every old \(z\ne u\), again impossible at a companion. Thus the entire loose set in \(P\) consists precisely of its names whose exterior rows equal the marked loose row, including the mark itself.

## Two signed inequalities for proposed old inputs

Suppose distinct old inputs \(u,v\in P\) are twins and choose a loose \(f\in P\). Write \(A=H_u\setminus\{u\}\), \(B=H_v\setminus\{v\}\) for their previous companions. Both sets have at least two names, all outside \(P\). The following implications use every allowed word with positive weight.

**Uniform weighting ⇒ \(C_{uv}>C_{uf}\).** On the event that \(A\) is mixed, swapping \(u,f\) preserves the whole word set and weight. Thus the minimum of \(D_u(z)=C_{uz}-C_{fz}\) equals
\[
-\mathbb E[|s_u-s_f|\mathbf1_{A\ {\rm constant}}]<0.
\]
It is attained on \(A\). Equality at another \(z\) requires its sign to equal the common sign of \(A\) in every word where \(A\) is constant and \(f\ne u\). For strict negativity, full projection gives a previous word constant on \(A\) with the opposite value at \(u\). Set \(f\) opposite to \(u\) and complete every other birth at its own loose name. The same bound applies to \(D_v\) with \(B\). Since \(D_u=D_v\) outside \(u,v,f\) and this domain contains both \(A\) and \(B\), each element of \(B\) minimizes \(D_u\), and conversely. Every older word with \(A\) constant extends with \(f\ne u\). Hence \(A\) constant with sign \(s\) implies \(B\) constant with the same sign in **every preceding word**. The converse follows by symmetry.

On the common constant event we have \(u=v\). For an explicit strict witness, choose an older word with every bit in \(A\) equal to 0 and \(u=1\), using full projection. The implication forces every bit in \(B\) to 0, hence \(v=1\). Set \(f=0\), which satisfies \(P\), and meet every other new rule at its own private name. This complete word has positive mass. Off the common event both preceding holders are mixed without \(u,v\). Conditional on all the other names, \(u,v,f\) are then exchangeable because their only remaining rule is the symmetric rule at \(P\) and words are uniform. Consequently \(\mathbb E[s_us_v-s_us_f]>0\). The swap calculation requires uniform current words. [Old-holder difference formula](../STATE-DISTANCE-AND-REACHED-GROWTH-20261120-001B/witness_recovery.md#a-pair-reading-for-a-spent-holder)

**Inherited weighting ⇒ \(C_{uv}>0\).** Restrict the table to preceding names. There the minimum of \(C_{uz}\) is \(-\Pr(A\ {\rm constant})<0\), and for \(z\ne u\) equality requires \(z\) to share the common sign of \(A\) on every word where \(A\) is constant. This follows by summing over \(u\) at a fixed exterior. As before, the two rows agree outside \(u,v\), and each other's companions attain the minimum. Thus their constant events coincide with the same sign on every previous word. On that event \(u=v\). Otherwise the two signs are independently fair at each fixed exterior. At the source all four assignments to these two loose names have equal weight. At a reached preceding stage they were fresh at distinct holders in the same cut, and all four assignments have the same ancestor word and extension denominator. Thus \(C_{uv}=\Pr(A\ {\rm constant})>0\). Preservation of the entire preceding marginal gives the identity in the observed table as well.

**Unmarked consequence.** Suppose independent evidence establishes \(P\) as latest and a pair \(x,y\in P\) has equal exterior rows. Suppose also that each row's minimum, together with its own index, gives \(P\). If both were old, any loose \(f\in P\) would give \(C_{xy}=C_{xf}<0\). This contradicts the strict uniform inequality and the positive inherited value independently. Thus one is loose and the marked result identifies its entire loose class. This conclusion needs neither a promised loose mark nor advance knowledge of the number of loose names.

## Why two old inputs cannot be twins at an ordinary birth

For either weighting the previous argument gave the same-sign logical equivalence of the constant events on \(A,B\). There is a further structural restriction, independent of the signed inequalities.

**One-way implication.** In any finite physical preceding presentation, if every word with all \(A=0\) has all \(B=0\), then
\[
             |A\setminus B|\ \ge\ 4\,|B\setminus A|.
\]
Here \(u,v\) are loose in distinct holders, every holder has at least three distinct names, and each name has at most two holders. Delete \(H_u,H_v\) and their private names. Every residual solution extends by choosing \(u,v\) separately, so the implication survives deletion. Every name of \(A\triangle B\) now belongs to at most one remaining holder, and a name shared by \(A,B\) belongs to none. Pin \(A=0\), which is feasible by full projection on \(H_u\).

Repeatedly apply the forced assignment at any holder all of whose places but one are fixed to the same value. The remaining place must be opposite, permanently satisfying that holder. At a fixed point every unsatisfied holder with pins has at least two free places and needs only one opposite bit. Every untouched one has at least three and needs both values. The finite two-colour completion lemma extends this partial word. Its possible odd chain closing on itself with two free places per holder includes a pinned holder, at which the two free places may both take its required opposite value. [Completion lemma and its proof](../THREE-READERS-AND-GROWTH-20261120-001B/recovery_argument.md#completing-a-partly-specified-state)

If \(b\in B\setminus A\) remained free at this fixed point, pinning its value to 1 would still allow completion. It occurs at most once. A new assignment can affect only that single active holder at first. If it forces a further assignment, its holder is now permanently mixed and its output occurs at most once elsewhere. This continues as a single sequence. Each newly visited unsatisfied holder had at least two free names before that sequence reached it. Its first new pin can satisfy it, stop without forcing, or force its last free name. Previously traversed forcing holders are already mixed. Hence no conflict is possible, and a new fixed point completes. This contradicts the implication. Every \(b\in B\setminus A\) must therefore already have been forced to 0 by the original pins.

Record which single holder produced each forced name and its earlier fixed antecedents. A primitive pin can contribute to at most one such holder. A produced name can contribute to at most one further holder after its producing occurrence. The antecedent histories of distinct outputs in \(B\setminus A\) are disjoint, since those outputs have at most one residual occurrence and the records only use earlier assignments. For each output 0 its producing holder required at least two previously assigned 1s. All primitive pins were 0, so each such 1 needed at least two earlier 0s. The histories of those four earlier 0s each contain a distinct primitive pin from \(A\setminus B\). This proves the factor four.

Apply the implication in both directions. The two inequalities force \(A=B\). Every name in this common set already occurs in both \(H_u,H_v\), and their two private names occur in no other former holder. They consequently form a separate two-holder component. There is no third holder meeting both. Every multi-parent ordinary birth requires a witness for each parent pair, even when the full group has no common witness. The two former holders cannot have contributed inputs to the same birth. Thus arbitrarily repeated source meetings still preclude two old inputs of a latest knot from having equal exterior rows under either weighting. In an independently verified latest \(P\), **any pair of table twins in \(P\) identifies the full loose class** by the marked result, even without separately checking their minima. The condition that \(P\) is latest remains essential to the inference. [Ordinary witness rule](../ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/end_operator.md#the-operator-on-the-present-end)

**A single loose name under inherited weighting.** If a verified latest ordinary \(P\) has no twins in its table, it has exactly one loose name \(f\), because any two fresh names would be twins. Since \(|P|\ge3\), it has at least two old inputs. A putative old centre \(x\) whose exact minimum set were \(P\setminus\{x\}\) would then have smaller correlation to another old input \(a\in P\) than to any \(z\in H_x\setminus\{x\}\). Such a \(z\) exists outside \(P\), and is a row minimizer of \(x\) among all old names. Preservation of the preceding marginal contradicts \(C_{xa}<C_{xz}\). The latest loose-row minimum therefore identifies \(f\) uniquely. Combined with the twins case, **under recursive division every independently established latest ordinary knot has its entire loose set determined by the pair table at every positive age**, regardless of previous repeated meetings.

Two controls test the scope of this argument. Across all 2,349 archived connected presentations on three through ten names, 5,379 pairs of loose distinctions had different holders. All 318 directed implications obeyed the factor-four inequality. The seven pairs with equal events had equal companions and a separate two-holder component. A ten-name presentation with holders \(012345,617,238,459,897\) attains four against one. Of its 322 words exactly one has \(12345=0\), and it has \(17=0\). The latter event holds in 53 words. Its five holders pass full projection and each rule is necessary. A four-name repeated meeting \(023,123\) gives two genuinely loose twins in separate holders with \(C_{01}=1/5\) in its ten uniform words. Those two holders lack the witness needed to contribute their private names together. ## Recovering the 29-name priced parent roster

For each possible loose \(f\), form \(P\) from its row minimum and itself. Within \(P\) take its full class of equal exterior rows as the fresh set \(F\), then \(U=P\setminus F\) as the proposed inputs. Given a preceding word, the signs on \(F\) are uniform on all assignments except one when \(U\) is constant. They are uniform on all assignments when \(U\) is mixed. This remains true after fixing the other births' fresh names and under either weighting. Thus for \(|U|=p=1,2\) and \(z\notin F\),
\[
 C_{fz}=-\frac{\sum_{u\in U} C_{uz}}{p(2^{|F|}-1)} .
\]
The mean on \(F\) follows from the common input sign when it exists, which for \(p=2\) is \((s_u+s_v)/2\) including zero on disagreement.

This identity orients every independently established latest **ternary** under uniform weighting as well. Cases with twins were settled above. In their absence there is one private place and two inputs. If \(f,x\) were both possible private places in the same observed ternary with third place \(y\), then for every \(z\ne f,x\) the two identities \(2C_{fz}=-C_{xz}-C_{yz}\) and \(2C_{xz}=-C_{fz}-C_{yz}\) would give \(C_{fz}=C_{xz}\). The equations also apply at \(z=y\), using \(C_{yy}=1\). This would make \(f,x\) twins.

Each support is the union of the two columns. At least one is present since each last funded birth has a fresh name. Every present live knot has one or two inputs and four fresh names. Hence its price \(q=\max(3-p,1,d)=4\) requires exactly four groups through it in the proposed next cut, where \(d\) counts those groups.

For a selected knot, remove its own name from each group of parents containing it. The remaining nonempty subsets of its witnessed neighbours are incomparable. Larger groups were chosen inclusion-maximal and a fallback pair occurs only when uncovered by any larger group. With at most three neighbours, such a family has size at most three. Indeed a whole triple excludes every other member. If there are \(s\) singletons, all remaining pairs use the other \(3-s\) elements, giving at most \(s+\binom{3-s}{2}\le3\). Four groups therefore require at least four other knots. There are only five possible supports. All five must be present and every pair must be witnessed. If the full five were unheld, it would be the unique maximal unheld group, giving demand one to each. They consequently have a common witness, so every subset is held, and precisely the ten pairs give births. Each knot spends its four loose names on those four groups.

A birth on \(i,j\) now belongs to exactly three unheld groups, one with the births on \(i,k\) and \(j,k\) for each remaining \(k\). Indeed, pairwise intersecting two-element parent sets either share one index or form the three pairs of a triple, which no fourth distinct pair can meet. Every witnessed pair lies in one of those triples. Thus the prospective demand is three for each two-parent birth, giving three fresh names apiece. The decoder reproduces all ten complete named parent sets and prices before comparing against the reference. The proof recovers the selected stage's live roles and next roster without bounding hidden source size or observation age inside the reached population. Recognition of an arbitrary apparent latest holder from row equality alone and unrestricted inversion of all reached observations remain open.
