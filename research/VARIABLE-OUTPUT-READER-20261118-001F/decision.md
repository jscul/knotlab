# Three-parent joins are determined by pair distances through 26 distinctions

**Yes at every size tested.** We exhausted every reachable arrangement with 21 through 26 distinctions under the serial parity law that allows two or three fresh distinctions per join. At each width, two different arrangement types always had distinguishable full bond-distance tables. Within each individual table, every renaming that preserved all entries preserved the set of legal triples. This answers a question about a proposed unordered triple using the current table, without knowing which distinctions were loose or how the arrangement grew. [Proof and counts](../../proofs/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F-P001_finite_interval/proof.md) · [Per-width receipts](../../proofs/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F-P001_finite_interval/comparisons.json)

The six widths contain **6,898,145** arrangement types and **3,635,999** legal triple occurrences. The strongest test came from 311 arrangements in which a legal triple and a forbidden one agreed on the first summary of their surrounding distances. In every one, the full table distinguished the two. An independent calculation reconstructed their allowed-state equations, their histories, and all the ways a legal triple could be renamed. It recovered 786 distinct groups of equivalent legal triples across those 311 arrangements. It detected a deliberately substituted forbidden triple even when the total number of choices was held fixed. [Independent checks](../../proofs/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F-P001_finite_interval/audit.md) · [Hard cases](../../proofs/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F-P001_finite_interval/residual_cases.jsonl)

Completeness was checked a second way by counting histories. At width 26 every one of the 8,585,343,953 sequences of parent sets and fresh counts was accounted for. The smaller widths 15, 16 and 17 were also replayed, and the compiled comparison matched the earlier results at 18, 19 and 20. A triple first becomes possible at width 15. Together these facts put a first counterexample at **27 distinctions or more**, if one exists. [History-count proof](../../proofs/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F-P001_finite_interval/history_balance.md) · [Lower-width proof](../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001E/decision.md)

**What survives at every size.** There is a restricted family for which even a rival with unrestricted mixed counts must give the same answers. If every junction in one history meets at most four distinctions and at most one addition introduces three fresh distinctions, the short pair distances force every allowed-state equation and hence every three-parent verdict. This includes histories of arbitrarily large width. The proof starts by finding the three- and four-distinction equations from the table, then uses the maximum possible number of independent equations at that width. [Restricted theorem and its hypotheses](../../proofs/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001F-P001_finite_interval/structural.md)

**What remains open.** We have a finite reader for three-parent joins through 26 and a proof for the stated family at every size. A general history may form a five- or six-place junction. It is still unknown whether a renaming that hides such an equation can change an actual three-parent choice. The known width-32 swap hides an equation and preserves all eight triple choices in that table. Whether any triple requires an additional three-way reading remains open. The full menu has a different answer already at width 18, where identical allowed states permit different one- and two-parent joins.
