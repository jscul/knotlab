## The question

The owner asked on 24 November 2026: "is there a growth law under which parts that have come apart can meet again, so
that what one part builds depends on whether another part is there? Under every law we have studied so far, parts that
come apart act alone for ever, which is why the copies are a population and not a machine." His first step: take the
three laws of the other thread in which a birth may take a distinction that is already shared (rule R, A230; rule Q, A235;
the move recorded in A225), run them on the ring that returns and on the two-knot part that copies itself, and grow each
start twice, once with the second part present and once without it. "A part reads another when its course differs
between the two runs." Stop at one part whose course depends on another, or at a reason this cannot happen under these
laws; and if a part reads another, say whether the reader was supplied by us or made by the growth.
([His request, as given](../PARTS-MEET-AGAIN-20261124-001/owner_request.md).)

He added, the same day, as his intuition and not as evidence (A241): "There's no way it wouldn't work. It might just cost
complexity. Programming is a thing in our reality, there should be a way to reference information like joining 2
different clusters of child logic from the parent via something like an 'axis point'. So we can essentially reference
chunks of logic." He said afterwards that "access point" may be what he meant. In the program's terms: two sets of
descendants become one part again through a knot that both descend from, an **ancestral common knot**. So the study has
a second arm, a ladder of laws that take a distinction from further along the record of descent, and a negative for the
three recorded laws is a result about those three only. The study then goes on, inside this pre-registration, to the
laws of the ladder at which a part's course does depend on another, with their cost.

He then carried it further: "This is why we may need 'function-like' logical operations where we can reference/sample
logic. These are basically meeting patterns that are complex in nature. Maybe even so complex as to the indexing operation
being logic based iterators rather than incremental. So like maybe intelligence as a fold has access to something like
'the golden ratio', but it needs to index what part of the golden ratio it's looking at?" In every law here a birth takes
one of several shared distinctions, and **the choice** says which. Under the laws that take from beyond the parent (MET
and the ancestor-taking laws) the choice decides which knots the new knot meets, and so stands in for his index there.
The study registers, for every law, what supplies the choice: the start's numbering, the order of births, the structure,
the record, or an enumeration.

**Existence is cheap.** A law under which any two members are joinable, whatever is built, "works" at once (here
ALL-JOINABLE: nothing then ever comes apart). What the study measures is the cost of the least laws under which a part's
own births depend on another part through meetings the growth makes, how robust those cases are to the scaffolds, and
whether what is built are knots.

## In standard terms

In the literature's words, not the program's: a synchronous hypergraph-rewriting system in which each step adds
hyperedges built from degree-one vertices of earlier ones, and under the laws studied a new hyperedge may also attach to
a vertex of degree two or more. The live part splits into components of a "common neighbour" relation. The question is
whether two components that have separated can reconnect, and whether one component's evolution can depend on the
presence of another. Under the earlier laws the components evolve as a context-free substitution system; a dependence
would be interaction between components (see "Prior work outside the program"). The main fact argued before the freeze
is an invariant: if every vertex a new hyperedge attaches to is a vertex of one of the hyperedges it is made from,
components that share nothing never reconnect. A rule that reconnects attaches to a vertex of another hyperedge: one
further back along the record of descent (a back-reference), or one that a hyperedge it is made from is adjacent to.

## Prior work outside the program

Each from memory, the literature's, not checked:
- A reference "k binders up" is a de Bruijn index (de Bruijn, 1972); in compilers, the static link followed k times.
  STEPS-k is that move on the birth record.
- Taking from a knot that the parent meets is the embedding step of node-replacement graph grammars (Janssens and
  Rozenberg, about 1980). MET is that move.
- Sharing in term-graph rewriting lets two subterms refer to one node (Barendregt, van Eekelen, Glauert, Kennaway,
  Plasmeijer and Sleep, 1987).
- Lindenmayer's systems (Lindenmayer, 1968) include, from the start, systems with interaction, in which how a cell is
  rewritten depends on its neighbours.

What this would add: whether, on a structure that makes its own places and whose every piece is tested by the canonical
knot test, a back-reference of this kind can be made by a stated growth law, at what cost, and how much of the result is
the scaffolds'. Not searched further.

## Settled by construction (stated, not tested)

Arguments for the referee; the runs check them. The advisor checked a first form of them and asked for the repairs made
here; the form below has not been checked by anyone but the drafter and the thread.

1. **Parts that share nothing stay apart.** Let a law satisfy H1 and H2 (definitions, section 6). Two facts: holders never
   decrease, and a barred distinction is never unbarred, so a knot that is no member never becomes one (definitions,
   section 1, item 1). Let X and Y be two parts of the roster after round s that are wholly apart, and let 𝒳 and 𝒴 be
   the two sets of parts of the split (definitions, section 1, item 6), with Δ𝒳 the places of the members of the parts in
   𝒳, and Δ𝒴 likewise. **Invariant**, for every round t ≥ s: every knot made after round s has all its parents among the
   members of 𝒳 and their descendants, or all among those of 𝒴; a knot of the first kind holds only distinctions of Δ𝒳
   and fresh distinctions made after round s for knots of the first kind; likewise for 𝒴; and no member descended from
   𝒳 is joinable with a member descended from 𝒴. At t = s it holds, since Δ𝒳 and Δ𝒴 are disjoint. Step: at round t + 1,
   by H2 every group lies in one part, and parts are unions of joinable members, so by the invariant every birth has all
   its parents on one side; by H1 what it takes is held by one of its parents, so it lies on that side's distinctions; its
   other places are fresh. Then a knot meeting a member on each side is impossible: a knot made after round s holds only
   one side's distinctions, and the two sides' distinctions are disjoint; a knot made by round s has fixed places, so it
   would hold a distinction of Δ𝒳 and one of Δ𝒴, and at round s it already met a member of each set (which would then
   have been joinable, so in one part) or was a member of one set holding a distinction of the other (against the
   split). So no met set holds members of both sides, and by H2 none is joinable.
   **Conclusion**: no member descended from X and none descended from Y are ever in one part, and no birth has parents
   descended from both.
2. **When parts are wholly apart.** Under RELEASE, after round 0, two members that hold a common distinction lie in one
   part, except two start knots: with three or more holders a third holder meets both; with exactly two, under H1, they are
   a parent and its child, or two start knots; and under RELEASE a member that makes a birth spends every usable
   distinction (definitions step 5; a short member under CANCEL-OFFSHOOT or SHORT-TAKES too, since it owes more than it
   holds; under CANCEL-WAIT and CANCEL-RETIRE a short member makes no birth), so a parent is never a member beside its
   child. So under RELEASE, after round 0, every two different parts share nothing and are wholly apart, unless two start
   knots that meet directly are both members, which does not happen after round 0 on the registered starts (on Ring(5, 3)
   every start member owes 2 and holds 3). Under HOLD it can fail, which is why HOLD is its own arm.
3. **So rules Q and R cannot make parts that have come apart one part again**, under any choice, any start round, any
   ration, treatment, extra or closing: each takes only distinctions the birth's own parent holds (H1) and keeps H2; nor
   can CARRY. The reason: what a parent holds is already met by everything the parent meets, so taking it can only rejoin
   what is still joined. The A225 move, stated as a growth law, is on this side whenever the birth's parents are the holders
   of what it takes, and off it only when a birth takes a distinction none of its parents holds.
4. **The course.** Under H1, H2 and RELEASE, from a round s after which X and Y are wholly apart, the births descended from
   X look only at X and its descendants and at older knots that meet them, except through round-wide rules: STALL (any short
   member anywhere), the residual stall of SHORT-TAKES, and the parity closing (IMPLIED looks at the span of every knot's
   rule). Three further conditions make runs A and B(i) comparable: they are compared only through the last round both
   reached, with the same bounds; no paired test is made under LITERAL (barring switches it off everywhere); and births are
   ordered lexicographically by knot names, so the renaming between the runs preserves order, and OLDEST and NEWEST compare
   only names whose order is the same in both runs. So under not-all-equal with a cancelling treatment the course of X is
   the same in A and B(i); under parity it can differ only through IMPLIED, and under STALL or SHORT-TAKES only through a
   stall.
5. **The record law cannot either.** Under RECORD-k with k fixed, two parts wholly apart after round s with no common
   ancestor at most k parent steps above any of their members never acquire one: a common ancestor of two later members at
   most k steps above each lies at most k steps above members of each part after round s. Structural joinability is handled
   by argument 1 in the same induction. So RECORD-k delays coming apart and never makes parts that came apart one part
   again; ALL-JOINABLE lets nothing come apart.
6. **What breaks it.** A law outside H1, under which a birth takes a distinction held by a knot none of its parents is, can
   make a new knot of one side meet an older knot that already meets the other side: MET (a knot the parent meets),
   STEPS-k and UP-TO-k (an ancestor above the parent), FIRST, and the A225 control.
7. **Checks, true by construction**: with no taking rule the engine is the ring study's; under not-all-equal there is no
   closing and every round extends; a birth that takes nothing extra has as many inputs as parents.

## What a result would and would not show

- **Supplied or made**, three verdicts (definitions, section 5, item 7): through a knot the growth made; through a start
  knot (the start's own meeting reopened); supplied (a round-wide rule, a rule that looks for the other part, or the record
  alone). In every case the taking rule, its choice, the record it looks at, the base law's ration and treatment, the start
  and the complete round are supplied. "Made by the growth" means that the meeting and the dependence are made by births
  under a stated law; it does not mean that the law is the model's.
- **A positive would show** that a supplied rule reaching back along descent, or to a knot the parent meets, can put two
  separated parts in one part again, and that the first part then builds differently. In the smallest case, with the
  second copy barred the first copy makes a three-parent join; with it present, two of the first copy's members retire and
  it makes one offshoot.
- **It would not show a reference.** One bit crosses: whether the other part is there or not, by crowding (the meeting
  makes members short). Nothing is built jointly in the smallest case; joint births appear under a few laws (P17).
  What the other part is does not cross (the larger-founder contrast). The smallest case goes through the start's dead knot,
  not an ancestor; it vanishes under NEWEST and under the ration swap; under the start's names reversed it goes through a
  knot the growth made. Every case found for parts that came apart vanishes when the ration is swapped. The one case that
  survives every swap joins the two-knot start's own halves, which were never one part, through the distinction its two
  start knots share, and the course differs there only five rounds after the halves are one part.
- **Is the phrase honest?** "A part whose course depends on another" is literally true of these cases. "Reads" and "made by
  the growth" are not earned by the smallest case; the cases through a knot the growth made earn the second in the sense
  above, and each of them depends on a scaffold (P12).
- **A null for arm A** shows the reason, under those laws: taking what a parent holds cannot reach a part that shares
  nothing with it. It is exact for H1 and H2, not for every law of the record's kind.
- **Not shown in any case**: anything about the golden ratio's "parts". The record already has that a knot's own holdings
  give the round it was made in to within three values in the golden-ratio eddy (r = f + 2p − 3 − 2e,
  `RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001B/results.md` line 25), so a place in that growth is carried by the structure and no rule
  looks at it. A pattern that steps the choice, the owner's "logic based iterators", is outside this study (lead B326).
- **The plain sentence for the owner** (the advisor's, adapted to the findings): "Under the three recorded laws, no, and we
  can say why: a birth that takes only what its own parents hold can never reach a part that shares nothing with it. If a
  birth may also take a distinction from a knot one or two steps back in its own descent, or from a knot its parent meets,
  two separated copies do become one part again and the first then builds differently. But the smallest case comes back
  through the start's dead knot, the second copy only stops two of the first copy's members, every case for parts that
  came apart disappears when the ration is changed, and we supplied the rule, the choice of distinction and, for most of
  these laws, the record of descent. Births with a parent in each part appear under a few of these laws, and in the
  smallest case there are none."
