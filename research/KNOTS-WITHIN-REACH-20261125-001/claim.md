# Claim: how many knots lie within reach, and whether the growing end carries a distance of its own

Epic `KNOTS-WITHIN-REACH-20261125-001` (sub-goals B261 and B269; goals G1 and G2 with G5; assumptions A110, A97, A98,
A105). Written before any registered run, after the advisor's two passes. [Definitions](definitions.md) ("KD"),
[survey](survey.md), [pre-registration](../KNOTS-WITHIN-REACH-20261125-001A/preregistration.md) ("PR"). Law, pattern,
clock and weighting are kept apart: the laws are rules we supply; the structures they build are patterns; nothing here is
a clock; every share names its weighting.

## 1. The owner's words, and how they are read

The owner asked (24 November 2026, Pacific time): "Could you work your way through all 4 of these? You can work in
parallel." The direction taken here, as the thread put it to him: "Space and dimension from the growth (goals G1, G2) ...
Two leads sit in the backlog: how many knots lie within reach, and whether the growing end carries a distance of its
own." Behind both stand his founding premise, "Distance is the only form of information" (A97, a reading under
challenge), his "if you're at 3 on the number line, you can't just skip 4 to get to 5, so there is an order" (A98's
reading), and his direction that blow-up is expected and is read by sampling (A110).

How the thread reads them:

- **The count is of the growth's history, not of what exists at one round.** "The knots within r steps of a knot" is
  read as the history count H(u, r): every knot of every round within r holder steps of u, the knots that have stopped
  growing as much as the members (KD 2.1). Under a law with no taking rule the holder graph is the birth record read in
  both directions plus the start's own meetings, so a power of H includes one degree from the rounds; where the number
  of parts is fixed and the roster is the same after every round, a POWER(j) of H is read "j − 1 from the roster and 1
  from the rounds". The bare ring shows it: the roster after every round is the same ring of n knots, and its "2" is 1
  from the supplied ring and 1 from the rounds run, up to the ring's own size; beyond it H reads 1.
- **What exists at one round** is read separately, by **the roster count** P_t(u, r): the members of u's part within r
  witnessed steps in the roster after round t, a stage reading, with the number of parts and the members per part beside
  it (KD 2.2). H and P are never added or combined.
- **"A space with a dimension"** is the program's target, not a verdict. The verdict that bears on it: the same power j
  from every knot examined, each knot examined within a factor of 2 of c_u·r^j on one common range, with a range that
  lengthens when the rounds double and is not set by the start's size (KD 4). Anything less is reported as its verdict.
- **"The growing end carries a distance of its own"** = among members of one part, how strongly a loose distinction of
  one member and one of another are related, either sign (|κ|, under the weighting "each allowed state of the structure
  after round t once"), is ordered as their witnessed steps and their arrival delay are: fewer steps or sooner never less
  strongly related (KD 5.6). The share ρ is always a pseudo-distance (a mean of 0/1 differences), so "carries a distance"
  is trivially true; the questions are whether |κ| is graded beyond the members' own loose and shared-place counts, and
  whether it keeps the order of steps and of delay.

The literature's name for H is volume growth (the literature's, marked). Nothing here is named by space, and no claim is
made about physical space or time, living things or minds.

## 6. What a result would and would not show

- A POWER(j) of H counted from a knot says that the knots of the growth's history within r holder steps of that knot
  number p(r), a polynomial of degree j, on the stated segment of a finite structure. One degree of it comes from the
  rounds. It is not a bond distance or any distance read from the allowed states.
- **No limit object.** Every count is of a finite structure at its last round. Nothing is said of a structure "in the
  limit"; the kept range says only that later rounds do not change the count up to its end.
- **The count depends on the knot counted from.** The bare ring shows it: from made knots the count is quadratic to the
  ring's size, from start knots only to half of it. Only the gate's form says more, and only for the knots examined.
- **The birth order read in both directions gives the roster's exponent plus one**, where the number of parts is fixed.
  The study reads the roster (P) and the history (H) apart; it does not read the birth order by chains and intervals,
  which a later study could.
- With hubs the step convention changes the counts by more than a bounded factor; claim 3's verdicts are given under each
  of the four steps.
- A discordance on a stage shows that how strongly two members' loose distinctions are related does not keep the order of
  steps or delay there; the headline levels say whether loose counts and shared-place counts were held equal. Agreement on
  every stage examined would be a finite census under the cap, not a theorem.
- Under parity, Lemma P says pairs of loose distinctions are tied or unrelated; a rule of Λ_t on fewer than all loose
  distinctions shows a rule among a few exists under a taking rule, not that it is a distance.
