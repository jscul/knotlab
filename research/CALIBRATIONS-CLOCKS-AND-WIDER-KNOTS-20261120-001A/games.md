With original fixed signs, two split even knots admit none of the individual proposed local changes. Their guarded permutations are identities. The third-row last question was $YY=-(X_1Z_1)(X_2Z_2)$. After primitive guarding the explicit minus remains, every shared pair agrees, and Alice's third row has the wrong parity. Thus $9/9$ agree but only $6/9$ win. If one instead defines that last entry by multiplying the first two terms in the third row, its sign changes on every physical word and the last column fails instead. Rebuilding it from the negative last-column product recovers the original minus in this case. Applying different context definitions to the two readers can supply a legal local strategy that loses only the common last entry. On the full word space with the original queries all three expressions coincide, owing to one order-dependent minus when a sign crosses its change. Guarding the primitives can destroy this identity. Guarding each **combined** translation simply uses its previously established sign and does not reconstruct a sequential primitive word. With three separate one-bit readers of the isolated not-all-equal knot, $F_i$ always preserves every other ordinary bit. Different $F_i$ can fail to commute, so the literal whole-space trials with two or three $X$ questions are undefined. In particular $XXX$ has no value under that simultaneous spectral prescription and the literal whole-space score is undefined. After fixing any actually read $Z$ values, the positive uniform words within that sector are invariant under each of the remaining guarded permutations. Each $X$ sign is therefore plus with certainty on that prepared sector, in any order. This establishes the joint table on the generated subspace of each preparation and its common explanation: sample one allowed word, answer each $Z$ by its bit, each $X$ by plus. For not-all-equal the three possible pairs of distinct ordinary signs have expectations $-1/3$ each. For parity they are all zero. The corresponding scores are $2$ and $1$. The 64 deterministic ternary answer lists split evenly between scores $-2$ and $2$. If a reader instead reports whether its offered move really changes a *shared initial word* before any moves are made, the scores are $2$ and $-1$, respectively. Matching one summary score does not identify the rule. These prepared, scalar and movement completions are individually well-defined.

With nine named scalar answers shared by both readers and no context-dependent change of their values, a single word can satisfy at most five of the six row/column parities. Failing any one parity invalidates three of the nine trials even if every shared answer agrees. An optimal word wins $6/9$, while a uniform nine-sign word wins $1/4$ averaged over all 512 words and nine trials. Under the usual game rule that each reader can answer as a function of its entire question, each can satisfy every local parity. All-plus in the even contexts, and one suitably placed minus in Bob's odd column, gives $8/9$. Winning all nine would force a single consistent nine-entry assignment satisfying all six parities, which is impossible by multiplication. The old $8/9$ local bound thus describes a different choice from insisting on nine scalar context-independent signs. Ordinary movement, scalar mixed signs and a legal local game strategy each supply their own answers. This study establishes no physical meaning for any of these extra instruments.
