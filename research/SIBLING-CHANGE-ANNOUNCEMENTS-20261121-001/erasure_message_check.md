# An eraser needs all sixteen single-launch messages to keep the maximum

The eraser receives the four actual change signs on the spent names. Before it knows the eight ordinary sibling values or the ninth sibling's change sign, it supplies the writer with one of a fixed number of distinct messages. The focal readers later see just the writer's go or decline bit. This study asks what message capacity preserves the **rate**; there can be several different flags with the same full rate. [Prospective design](../SIBLING-CHANGE-ANNOUNCEMENTS-20261121-001C/preregistration.md) · [Predecessor rates](erasure_access_check.md)

If a fixed encoder uses at most 15 messages, at least two of the 16 erasure records have the same message. Reveal all the other labels separately. The new writer can simulate the old encoder and its decoder for every sibling record. This gives a finite upper bound by the maximum over the 120 possible tag pairs that could have been merged; at 15 symbols each of these bounds is attained by its corresponding encoder if its decoder is optimal. For each pair, add its two subnormalized focal matrices separately for each of the 512 sibling records, and reoptimize the entire decoder. This permits changes to every decision, including ties, and preserves the pooled threshold. Comparing the 16 rows of one chosen flag would not prove a bound on all flags. This is related to the literature's one-use coding with decoder side information. Here incompatibility is determined by a strict loss of extremal probability on the physically reached source. [Witsenhausen, 1976](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1976.1055607)

Under not-all-equal the best among all fixed encodings with fewer than 16 symbols gives **51935/1075648** for a deterministic writer and **657035/13599264** for an independently randomized writer. The same pair merger uniquely attains both upper bounds among the 120 single mergers. In physical paid-name order its records are 0111 and 1110, with one denoting the minus sign. Both ordinary-only sibling payers report minus. The focal payer and the payer of the changed sibling exchange their plus and minus signs. Either fixed focal orientation gives the same prices. The full rates are **1629/33614** and **164785/3399816**. The respective gaps from the best proper encoding are **193/1075648** and **2105/13599264**, measured per original launch.

A shared independent lottery over which encoder to use also cannot preserve either full maximum. Let S = 56v − 2w denote the surplus of an individual record of integer mass w and unnormalized focal excess v. For each pair merger sum max(0, w + 163S/354) over its merged records. The largest bound, across all 120 choices, is exactly **657035/13599264 per original launch**. The *same* multiplier supports each component before the joint constraint is imposed. Therefore a lottery with different surpluses on its component launches cannot exceed that value at a nonnegative pooled surplus. A fixed best merger and a randomized decoder attain it. The independent calculation also minimizes the envelope over its rational supporting lines. The proper-code maximum even with shared randomness is below the seedless full maximum by **14083/95194848** per launch. Private noise at the eraser is also covered by the upper bound: it can be sampled as a random lookup independently of the source. Simulating the original four-sign message literally attains each full maximum. [Independent pair prices, integer recurrence and shared-lottery envelope](message_price.py)

Under parity, even the unrestricted record has no positive probability at the required excess. A constant eraser message and an always-decline flag attain the zero rate. The not-all-equal result requires all 16 messages, equivalently four fixed-length binary digits per launch in the worst case. A variable-length record stream across independent launches would ask a different cost question. Any distinguishable silence or refusal in the admitted single-launch device already counts among its messages. [Earlier parity obstruction](erasure_access_check.md)

The location of the independent coin changes the price of the randomized maximum. Suppose only the eraser may toss a private coin and the writer must act deterministically. Sample the emitted symbol independently for each possible h to obtain a lottery over lookup tables with the same conditional symbol probabilities. Every fixed lookup has supporting value at most the full value. Every lookup with a merger has strictly smaller supporting value at the *same* multiplier, as the 120 pair prices show. Attaining the full randomized rate therefore forces every lookup of positive probability to be injective. The 16 conditional symbol supports must be mutually disjoint, since a symbol shared by two supports would occur as a merger with positive probability in the independent lookup. With 16 symbols they are singletons. The writer then accepts an integer mass, whereas the mass required is **9227960/177**, in units of one launch divided by 1075648. Thus 16 cannot suffice.

Seventeen symbols do suffice with that coin placement. Order the records by their exact threshold prices, accepting whole records until a single fractional record remains. Split that record's h into two emitted symbols by a coin tossed before k is available. The writer treats the symbols identically except at the one fractional k. In the independent replay's zero-based ordering the split is h = 0, k = 48, coin probability 2/177 for one fixed focal orientation. For the other it is h = 0, k = 500, coin probability 539/708. Summing the actual emissions and the deterministic decisions gives mass 9227960/177 and zero surplus in both cases. This costs five binary digits with fixed-length messages. With an independent coin available at the writer, sixteen symbols suffice. These prices describe the supplied announcement devices.
