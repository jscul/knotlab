# How early an anonymous ordinary ball can distinguish a load

The single-ring source gives a **first-layer structural difference at every tested offset at least two**, while the attachment itself is still outside the ball. At offset $d$ the first distinguishing radius in that layer is $2d$ incidence hops. The old ring first enters at $2d+1$. This holds for a single shared mark and for an unordered adjacent pair, measured from the nearer marked holder. At offset four, for example, the ball of eight hops around `e8,e9` shows **54 quiet or 51 loaded distinctions**, with **18 knots in each arm**. An explicit rooted isomorphism matches the seven-hop truncations. ## Which information distinguishes the two arms

We expose the induced incidence between the displayed knots and distinctions. Each physical distinction is one vertex, even when two distinctions have the same holders. An unordered adjacent pair uses one common root color. Every vertex otherwise receives only its knot or distinction type. All original positions, birth ages, ancestry and historical names are scoring aids. Boundary holders and ports expose precisely the incidences inside the ball. We also compare two augmented observations, one giving each displayed distinction its true number of holders and one giving every displayed vertex its full degree. The latter reveals more about a boundary knot. Lastly, the actual-value reading labels each displayed distinction by its current value relative to the transported reference.

| View | First strict radius at layer one for $d\ge2$ | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Induced incidence | $2d$ | Three fewer displayed distinctions under load |
| True number of holders on each distinction | $2d$ | Same first distinguishing radius |
| Full degree of each displayed vertex | $2d-1$ | The visible boundary child has degree six quiet or three loaded |

| Knot count alone | No strict difference at layer one | The first difference is at $2d+3$, already past the old attachment |

At exact offsets zero and one, every strict induced, true-degree and actual-value comparison agrees at every age. The largest usable radii there are zero and two. Within two incidence hops of an original shared mark one can see original holders and their existing distinctions. The original holders' total degrees stay fixed. Their spent distinctions acquire a second holder in both arms at the first growth layer and retain it thereafter. The new holders themselves are outside these strict radii. By contrast a search over any offset *at least* zero or one can take offset two and distinguish the histories at the first layer. Supplying an adjacent pair whose common holder is the desired anchor shifts its nearer-holder offset by one on the same side.

With arbitrary radius, every root distinguishes the structures in the first layer, which adds eleven more knots under load. Whole-stage differences persist whenever both finite histories complete. The current loose distinctions identify the live knots. In these open histories one can remove the live cohort and its fresh distinctions to recover the preceding layer, retaining the distinctions previously spent by old holders. A rooted isomorphism at a later stage would therefore induce one in the first layer. A full actual-value reader instead distinguishes at layer zero, one hop beyond the first original ring holder. The original background marks themselves retain quiet values. Their projection of allowed states remains free, since the original holders have loose inputs and every nonclosing birth has a fresh distinction for extending an old assignment. ## Why layer one suffices on a padded path

Reflect to marks on the right, with the nearer marked holder at $b_{s+d}$. The first child shared by $b_{s+2}$ and the seam $b_s$ exists while quiet. It uses two spent distinctions and gets four fresh ones. Under load the proposal is vetoed and its place at $b_{s+2}$ is taken by a unary child with one spent and two fresh distinctions. The quiet child's demand is four. Its parents have two and four loose distinctions, and its one outward sibling at $b_{s+2}$ shares no additional parent with any sibling at the seam. The unary floor gives two fresh distinctions because the loaded child has just one sibling. These are the only differing displayed child supports at the first distinguishing radius.

In fact every child shown through radius $2d-1$ has an old parent of offset at least two on that side. For offset at least three the original proposals through that old holder are retained in both arms. Its first children have identical fresh counts. For the inward odd pair on offsets one and three, its two-open outer parent has just one other sibling whose parent set cannot participate in another shared-parent triple with the four-open inner parent's siblings. The retained pair therefore gets four fresh distinctions irrespective of the veto near the ring. For retained pairs of two-open path holders, each parent contributes one sibling and the pair gets two fresh distinctions. The same counts hold next to the physical ends because an end spare supplies the missing unary sibling. These facts leave the quiet joint and its loaded unary replacement as the only change through the shown cohort that can alter an outward ball.

The new child at $b_{s+2}$ first appears at hop $2d-1$ in both arms. Its distinction at that outward parent appears in both. Every other distinction on the child first appears one hop farther out in the incidence reading. At $2d-1$ match the old shared path, the retained outward children and the visible parent ports, with the quiet child paired to the loaded unary. No outside incidences are supplied. At $2d$ the quiet child shows its extra spent distinction and two extra fresh distinctions. Their three missing vertices distinguish the loaded ball. The seam itself and the ring's occupied distinctions remain outside. These arguments use only the fixed attachment neighborhood and the stated end spares, so padding extends the first-layer result to arbitrarily large offsets. The increasing radii are part of that result. A fixed-radius ball can only show knots born through $\lfloor(r-1)/2\rfloor$ and distinctions born through $\lfloor(r-2)/2\rfloor$ besides the original ones. The induced graph stabilizes when those births have occurred, because a new holder gained farther out does not alter an induced incidence between two already displayed vertices. The readout at layer one with radius chosen as $2d$ gives no speed for a fixed-radius observer. ## Finite frontier and controls

On all 1,600 positive-layer cells with $d\ge2$, the first induced distinguishing radius at layer $t$ equals

$$R(d,t)=2d-2\bigl(\min(t,\lfloor d/2\rfloor)-1\bigr).$$

This is a verified finite profile through layer eight. True port arity gives the same profile, full displayed-vertex degrees give one hop less, and actual values give the same strict profile.
