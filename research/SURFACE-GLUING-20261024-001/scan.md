# Bounded scan: space from a law

Date 2026-10-24. Scope: one bounded idea-and-barrier scan before activating evidence tickets. External sources calibrate selection only;
nothing here is admitted as proof evidence.

## Question

How can question 9 ("which law, not written on a space in advance, gives a shape that looks like a space of low dimension") be posed
without drawing a space, under the owner's no-selection framing? What is known about gluing relations into surfaces and about laws
whose glued results depend only on topology?

## Query families

- Frobenius algebras and Frobenius objects in the category of sets and relations.
- Emergent dimension from local rules without a background (dynamical triangulations, causal sets, quantum graphity).
- Background known without search: two-dimensional topological field theories correspond to commutative Frobenius algebras (Abrams
  1996; Kock, *Frobenius Algebras and 2D Topological Quantum Field Theories*, 2004); pants decompositions of surfaces and their dual
  cubic graphs (Hatcher and Thurston 1980).

## Imported ideas and local discriminators

- **Topological gluing = Frobenius structure.** If a three-place relation, with a pinned or free leg as disc, satisfies the unit,
  associativity and Frobenius laws in Rel, every hub-free network's glued result depends only on its surface. Discriminator: test the
  laws for every three-place Boolean relation, and test invariance directly over all small gluing patterns.
- **Prediction from the groupoid classification.** Among the 34 knots only the two parity knots should pass. All-equal also passes,
  but it is not a knot.

## Overlaps, barriers and limits

- **The parity case is classical.** It is mod-2 homology of surfaces, a Dijkgraaf–Witten style gauge theory for the group of order
  two. The program's contribution can only be the census of Boolean three-place knots and the reading.
- **No law selects a surface.** Every gluing exists. The question answered is what a law makes of each gluing, not which gluing
  occurs.
- **Gluing along single distinctions only ever builds surfaces.** Higher-dimensional cells would need gluing along sets of
  distinctions. Recorded as an open question, not a claim.
