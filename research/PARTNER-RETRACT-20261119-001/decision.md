# A retraction can preserve the growing end and lose runaway growth

**Two more rounds of numerical domination are guaranteed from a funded block retraction onto a canonical partnered stage. The third can fail.** Here a retraction maps each source block inside a target block and fixes a copy of the target. The source has enough loose distinctions for every pending join. In the explicit failure both starts have that property. Each of the six embedded starting junctions has exactly its target's four loose distinctions. At round one there is again a retraction between the two *reached* stages. Starting at that comparison, the source falls below the target on the **third subsequent round**. A new source group contains all four members of a transversal already held on the target, together with a fifth junction mapping to one of those four. Its child becomes a common parent of the next 28 junctions. They enter a single-block tail and eventually close. The target runs away indefinitely. The witness is a connected, meet-once start with 28 junctions and 95 distinctions. The target starts with 18 and 60. The literal parity replay and the independent roster computation agree. Two rounds survive because each free target transversal lifts to a source group containing its embedded members. Lifts of complementary transversals are disjoint, other pairs meet through an embedded parent, and common parenthood on these selected children agrees with common parenthood on the target. This gives the second injection. It leaves other source children available at the following stage, precisely where this history fails. If candidate pairs are also forbidden to collapse under the map, the embedded open counts agree and the current round is funded, the protected children form a separate partnered component after one round. That stronger condition gives domination at every later age. It concerns counts. A particular map can still lack a compatible image on every new junction.
