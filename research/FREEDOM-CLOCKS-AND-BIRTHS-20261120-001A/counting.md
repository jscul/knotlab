# How to count answers to a change question

With the second question supplied, allowed words alone determine the proposed probabilities. The following statements apply to any nonempty finite binary relation $S$, with one equal unit per allowed word and no weights carrying signs. Ordinary read $Z_U$ gives the sign $(-1)^{s\cdot U}$ on physical word $s$. Change $X_T$ reverses all and only the distinctions in $T$. Distinctions outside the questions remain unread. The compared six-rule and three-reader records originally obtained chances by applying sign projectors to the positive uniform weighting. ## 1. From freedom counts to probabilities

With the second question supplied, define its signed product expectation, with $T$ and $U$ disjoint, by

$$C(T,U)=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{s\in S,\,s+T\in S}(-1)^{s\cdot U}.$$

The indicator in the sum asks whether that *exact* flip stays allowed when every other distinction retains its value. For no ordinary reads, $C(T,\varnothing)$ is the fraction of free words. A sign with mean $c$ has **yes probability $(1+c)/2$**. Thus the literal proposal that the answers multiply to yes with probability equal to the free fraction fails. A single flip of an isolated parity ternary is free on zero of four words, while the yes probability is one half. On an isolated not-all-equal ternary the corresponding values are $4/6$ and $5/6$. All signed correlations of the older positive uniform weighting agree with $C$: on a basis word, the sign is read there and the flip then sends it to $s+T$. Only two allowed endpoints contribute to the inner product against the uniform indicator. Summing over the unmeasured values enforces equality of those values on the endpoints. The normalization is $|S|$.

For a setting with $k$ binary signs write $a_i=0$ for a plus answer and $a_i=1$ for a minus. Let $T_J,U_J$ be the marks whose changes and values appear in a subcollection $J$ of the questions. The indicator for the complete answer word $a$ is the product of the $k$ individual sign indicators. Expanding it gives the unique joint probabilities

$$P(a)=2^{-k}\sum_{J}(-1)^{a\cdot J}C(T_J,U_J).$$

Summing this equation over any omitted answer cancels every term containing its question. All remote-setting marginals agree and $C(0,0)=1$ enforces normalization.

For a counts-only positivity proof, write $F$ for all marks asked as changes, $R$ for all marks read ordinarily, $z$ for the observed ordinary values, and $h$ for an assignment to all unasked marks. Hold $h,z$ fixed. For a proposed change-answer word $a$ on $F$, let $N_+(h,z,a)$ and $N_-(h,z,a)$ count allowed words with these fixed values and even or odd $a\cdot s_F$. Expanding the ordinary-read subcollection first pins $z$. Expanding the change subcollection then sums over all ordered pairs of allowed words with this $h,z$. Every pair appears exactly once, at $T=s_F+s'_F$. Matching signs contribute plus and opposite signs contribute minus. Thus

$$P(z,a)=\frac{\sum_h\bigl[N_+(h,z,a)-N_-(h,z,a)\bigr]^2}{2^{|F|}|S|}\ \ge 0.$$

The two integers count ordinary words. The square follows from pairing them. Inverting the signs or expanding each older squared projector gives this same expression. Summing all $a$ cancels the off-diagonal pairs and returns the ordinary count for $z$.

With the canonical K2 test, every assignment to the remaining variables extends after forgetting any particular variable of the *written knot*. This does not require that the forgotten bit can change by itself at a fixed full word. An isolated even ternary satisfies K2, has no single-mark flip at any word and admits every two-mark flip everywhere. The isolated not-all-equal ternary also satisfies K2 but its single flips are partial. K2 is a statement about projection. The proposed rule counts exact changes with the other values held fixed. ## 2. Which combined questions have answers together?

With the second question supplied, encode a real combined question by a flip mask $t$, an ordinary parity mask $u$ and an answer orientation $e\in\{0,1\}$. On input $s$ it reads $(-1)^{e+u\cdot s}$ and sends the word to $s+t$. Applying $(t',u',e')$ first and then $(t,u,e)$ gives

$$(t,u,e)(t',u',e')=(t+t',\,u+u',\,e+e'+u\cdot t').$$

The order sign arises because a bit read after it flips has the opposite value. Exchanging the questions costs $(-1)^{u\cdot t'+u'\cdot t}$. A single read and change on the same mark cost minus. One real combined question squares to the identity exactly when $u\cdot t=0$. A word with odd overlap squares to minus the identity and does not by itself define a real two-answer measurement in this protocol. Full questions with an even number of such overlaps can commute. The order sign is in the question, including the prescribed orientations of the six contexts. The physical word counts remain unsigned.

For any commuting family of such involutions $Q_i$, form each ordered product $Q_J=(t_J,u_J,e_J)$. Its moment is $(-1)^{e_J}C(t_J,u_J)$ with the same endpoint count, now allowing overlapping masks. The order-independent result holds because the completed questions commute. Sign inversion again gives all the chances, including dependent questions. Positivity can be checked without a Hilbert-space assumption. For each endpoint $w$ count pairs $(s,J)$ with $s\in S$ and $w=s+t_J$, split according to the sign $(-1)^{a\cdot J+e_J+u_J\cdot s}$. The sum over $w$ of the squared differences of these two integer counts, divided by $4^k|S|$, equals the sign-inverted table. To see the equality, expand each square over $J,K$. Equal endpoints set the two starting words in correspondence by $Q_JQ_K=Q_{J\triangle K}$ with its computed sign, since the $Q_i$ commute and square to the identity. For each symmetric difference there are exactly $2^k$ ordered choices. Thus precisely its moment remains with coefficient $2^{-k}$. This includes the minus demanded by the last six-rule context. The ordering and the admissible combined questions extend beyond the original disjoint masks.

The underlying sign algebra is the literature's stabilizer operator description and the inversion is Fourier inversion on binary words. The physical content added here is the endpoint counting prescription, the exact equality to the previous reader tables, the operational rivals, and the comparison with a real birth's inputs. The general non-affine support in the count formula need not itself be a stabilizer preparation. ## 3. What would select these counts?

With the second question supplied, agreement with the ordinary reads, multiplication of compatible answers, independence from other readers' settings and certainty of plus for every globally free change leave other rules available. Let $D=\{d:S+d=S\}$. It partitions $S$ into equal-sized sets $s+D$. Choose one of these in proportion to its cardinality and use the positive counting rule inside it. The result has exactly the original ordinary read chances, admissible simultaneous products and unchanged remote marginals. Every $d\in D$ permutes each individual part and has plus answer with certainty. For any shift $t\notin D$ the parts contain no pair at that displacement, so every mixed moment with that shift vanishes. This rival uses only unsigned word counts, respects renaming, and factors on independently prepared Cartesian products because their translation groups factor. An isolated not-all-equal ternary has three such two-word parts. It still gives three changes with product plus, but all three single-change/two-read moments vanish and its score is 1. A change of answer names cannot take a zero expectation to $-2/3$.

Within the comparison class of real signed uniform preparations, write $\sigma(s)\in\{+1,-1\}$. The endpoints now contribute the factor $\sigma(s)\sigma(s+t)$. Global plus certainty forces $\sigma(s+d)=\sigma(s)$ for every $s$ and $d\in D$, because every term of the average must equal one. Conversely this condition is sufficient. In the finite affine parity case $S=a+C$, $D=C$ acts transitively, so only a global sign remains in this particular comparison class. At the not-all-equal ternary any orbit-constant pure sign can also be absorbed by a character sign on the words and a consistent local answer relabeling. To test a genuine alternative at fixed ordinary frequencies, negate only 0001 and 1110 on the four-place not-all-equal support. These signs are constant under global complement. No parity character, up to a global sign, has this pattern on the fourteen words. The magnitudes of some one-change expectations differ. Claiming that unsigned weights alone force the rule would also miss the previous unsigned partition rival.

A short sufficient extra reading is available. For any nonzero proposed shift $t$, reveal the identity of the unordered pair $\{s,s+t\}$ containing the current word, with ordinary word frequencies. This identity is determined by the ordinary parities $u$ with $u\cdot t=0$, all constant across the pair. Require that the reveal retains the unchanged unconditional change distribution, including conditional mixed moments with any such ordinary parity, and calibrate a full pair as a fresh two-word preparation with certain plus. Calibrate a singleton as fair. Each full pair then contributes two units to the numerator of the change mean, and a singleton contributes zero. A compatible ordinary sign is constant in the pair and simply multiplies that contribution. Normalizing by $|S|$ yields every proposed moment. Compatible multiplication and sign inversion determine the full tables in the stated domains once the order law is supplied. Merely announcing the pair or preserving an unconditional marginal is insufficient: the rival can retain its old within-pair coherence, which can vanish even on a full revealed pair. The additional calibration resets how such a full pair answers. Fairness on singletons also does work. For disjoint questions, “always plus for changes” with ordinary word sampling retains ordinary answers, compatible answer products in that limited domain, no signalling and global certainty, and even full-pair plus calibration, while differing on singletons. This is a sufficient list and an explicit failure of the weaker proposed list. Absolute minimality among all possible operational descriptions has no specified criterion.

## 6. Where the rule ends

With the second question supplied, the proposed moments and chances apply to **a nonempty finite allowed set with uniform unsigned preparation**, in a fixed ordinary basis, and the declared ordinary and change questions or real mutually commuting binary products of them. They contain no additional information about how to implement an arbitrary local question, arbitrary measurement effects, incompatible simultaneous questions, or what physical response records a change answer. The disjoint rule by itself does not supply the order law for the combined questions. A nonlocal predetermined answer distribution for all incompatible choices at once is also a different object; the isolated not-all-equal table expressly rules it out for separate readers.

Ordinary support alone never specifies arbitrary weights or relative phases. On the same six allowed ternary words, giving squared mass four to each word in the pair 001 and 110 and mass one to each of the rest changes a named single-flip expectation from $2/3$ to $1/3$. A signed preparation can keep even the ordinary frequencies identical, as can the mixture over translation classes. On a single unrestricted mark, the positive equal preparation has $\langle X\rangle=1$, whereas equal components with relative phase $+\mathrm{i}$ or $-\mathrm{i}$ both give zero for the two real questions $X$ and $Z$. They give opposite answers with certainty to the imaginary-phase question called $Y$ in the literature. A diagonal mixture gives the same $X,Z$ statistics there and gives mean zero to $Y$. Real combinations such as two $Y$ factors can still have the real order signs reached in this study. It is the full complex choice of preparations and measurements that the ordinary set fails to specify. The proposed probabilities concern one preparation and one compatible set of questions at a fixed holding stage. Conditioning a stored outside report is justified when the prior protocol specifies one joint compatible question and includes its reporting rule. It does not provide a general post-measurement state rule, dynamics between incompatible reports, evolution of a physical state through parent steps, an inherent orientation, or a population weighting on arbitrary sources. Each growth stage here receives the positive uniform preparation on that stage's allowed words. The formulas say nothing about how a branch or continuing physical preparation acquires that distribution. Infinite unrestricted sets can lack a uniform probability and are outside the finite theorem. The result is therefore a counts-only derivation within a declared measurement fragment and preparation, with the availability of the second question still supplied.
