# The exact weighting on groups of one growing end

Use the live junctions and their maximal blocks. The shared distinction carried into a new junction determines which old blocks it meets. Within the current roster, two live junctions are compatible precisely when some block holds both. A maximal group here means a maximal set of compatible junctions, including held ones for the moment. The separate birth correction removes held groups and accounts for residual pairs and offshoots.

Choose two blocks and count all maximal groups of their induced part. For a two-block part, the names on each side already meet. Choosing a subset of one side excludes a union of neighborhoods on the other side. The maximal completion is determined by that union. Different unions have different completions. A single set of independent partner pairs gives the simple case of one binary choice per pair. In the general case the choices can constrain one another. The count and rankers incorporate those constraints exactly. [Two-block calculation](structure.md)

Let $L$ be the number of groups in the counted part and $P$ the particle population. Draw initial ranks so that their combined expected mass is uniform over the $L$ groups. The implemented stratification draws an independent uniform integer $U_i$ from $0$ through $L-1$ and takes rank $\lfloor (iL+U_i)/P\rfloor$ for stratum $i=0,\dots,P-1$. Over all $P$ strata, each rank is represented by exactly $P$ of the $PL$ equiprobable positions. Thus giving each of the $P$ particles weight $L/P$ gives each initial group expected weight one.

Now restore one further junction. Removing that junction from any new maximal group and completing in the fixed older order determines one old maximal group. An old group has one or two children, and these families are disjoint. In a direct single-path version, uniform choice among children would give group $G$ probability $1/(L2^{b(G)})$, where $b(G)$ counts the two-child decisions along its unique path. The run used a population so that many children could be carried simultaneously. [Extension proof](formal.md)

At each thinning, an integer mass of $m_g$ before thinning has expected outgoing multiplicity $P m_g/m$, where $m$ is the total integer mass. Multiplying the common sampling weight by $m/P$ exactly compensates. Conditional on the entire preceding population, its unnormalized weighted group measure is preserved in expectation. Copying a parent's contribution to each actual child has the correct first moment because every child has one parent. Induction proves that each physical maximal group has expected final weight one, including groups whose restriction to the initial part is not maximal. All normalization factors are recorded as integers.

For a single final group drawn from one completed run, let $m_g$ be its integer multiplicity, $m$ the final total multiplicity, and $W$ the run's estimated total after rescaling. Its conditional sampling probability is $m_g/m$. Its marginal probability under this design is the expectation of that conditional probability over all runs. For every fixed group $g$, the expectation of $W$ times the indicator that the draw is $g$ is exactly one. The same identity gives an unbiased total for any fixed structural predicate. Sample means use the untrimmed arithmetic average. The target count is unchanged by renaming; a particular finite sample and its spread can depend on insertion order.

There is also an explicit reading that returns a lawful birth with its weight. On start-03, choose between two strata with equal probability. In the first, draw a final particle in proportion to its mass and return weight $2W$ for a legal large group, zero for a held group. In the other, draw uniformly from the 1,241 residual pairs and the single offshoot and return weight 2,484. Every lawful birth has expected contribution one. The numerical count uses the same structural information more efficiently by including the exact birth correction deterministically. For any finite roster this construction also explains why a general impossibility assertion would need an additional restriction on allowed work. If $s$ sites remain after the seed, the single-path weight is at least $L$ and at most $L2^s$. Its variance is finite, and independent averages converge to the true finite total. This existence argument gives no practical budget at start-03. The reported empirical spreads use the actual budgets in the locked panels.

For an exactly counted whole-stage part $F$, an optional exact adjustment is $|F|+\widehat C-\widehat C_F$, with the two estimates from the same weighted population. Linearity gives expectation $C$. This is useful when a reader needs that particular marked total. The locked primary uses its original weights and the separately exhausted birth correction.
