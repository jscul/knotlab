# What persists when the split source stays lawful

The coherent even source cannot implement either nonzero exact signed outcome by serial local operations, even if safety is tested only on the actual intermediate state. The coherent not-all-equal source can implement both with total herald probability \(1/3\), and that probability is optimal. Both conclusions include local clean memory, arbitrary finite adaptive classical communication and all fine records. The targets start independent of the source and of the apparatus. ## Sector persistence on the actual state

Write \(p=a\oplus c\). Define \(Q_s\) by the two simultaneous physical conditions \(p=b=s\), leaving all work registers unrestricted. The lawful parity subspace is \(Q_0\oplus Q_1\). Its prescribed pure preparation is
\[
  \frac{|u_0\rangle|0\rangle+|u_1\rangle|1\rangle}{\sqrt2},
  \qquad
  u_0=(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)/\sqrt2,\quad
  u_1=(|01\rangle+|10\rangle)/\sqrt2.
\]
Fix the shared randomness and refine every operation into real Kraus outcomes, keeping local environments or recording their fine indices. A lawful coarse density has support in the lawful subspace on every positive fine constituent, by positivity. For a current lawful fine vector \(\psi=\psi_0+\psi_1\) and any one-reader Kraus operator \(K\) with lawful output, \(Q_sK\psi=K\psi_s\). When the first reader acts, the components still have orthogonal physical \(b\); when the second acts, they still have orthogonal physical \(p\). A forbidden contribution from one component cannot be cancelled by the other. This is an identity on the reached vector. It remains valid with a private memory coherently correlated with the physical names.

By induction down a fine transcript, the final vector in sector \(s\) is the product of the transcript's local Kraus operators applied to that initial sector alone. On the product target \(00\), each initial sector is product across readers. Tracing either persistent physical record removes interference between sectors. The resulting target at any fine or coarsened herald is a sum of positive product operators. In contrast \(P_t|00\rangle=(|00\rangle-t|11\rangle)/2\) is a nonzero entangled vector for each sign. Announcing a narrower branch, retaining a private copy, ignoring its record or resetting a local workspace cannot change this refusal. Any normalized separable target has squared overlap at most \(1/2\) with this ideal Bell output.

For any target on which the serial protocol is lawful, its fine outcome probabilities equal those from dephasing just between \(Q_0,Q_1\). The dephased resource is a mixture of real products across the split. Every fine effect on a target is then a sum of real positive product effects. Since \(\operatorname{tr}(EJ)=0\) for each real symmetric local \(E\), these effects give identical probabilities on the opposite-sign mixtures \(P_+/2,P_-/2\), including at all heralds. Here \(J=XZ\). The reality condition matters to this sign-probability statement. Dephasing every physical word also gives this refusal under either source law. A word-preserving copy into an unread environment has the same effect if it distinguishes the two parity sectors, or, in the not-all-equal source, each first-reader row. A local unmeasured coherent copy can instead be uncomputed while the original names are still available.

Prepared-state safety is genuinely weaker as a test of a primitive. For example, a real first-reader unitary can exchange \((00-11)/\sqrt2\) and \((01-10)/\sqrt2\) while fixing \(u_0,u_1\). This leaves the actual parity preparation unchanged but leaks from the individual input \(000\) into forbidden words. It provides no advantage for either signed outcome. A coordinate calculation revealing a Bell pair needs a further lawful way to erase or measure the physical records of its two paths. ## Full-law safety, heralds and reversible implementations

For a first-reader row \(x=ac\) let \(S_x\) be its permitted set of remote \(b\) values. A matrix element from \(x\) to \(y\) on an independently ready local memory can be nonzero in a full-law forward primitive only when \(S_x\subseteq S_y\). Test separately each old basis word, its fixed remote value and each allowed ready memory vector. For a restricted history the corresponding test ranges only over feasible word–memory pairs. Requiring a Cartesian product of physical words and memory states is a stronger assumption.

| First-reader row | Even \(S_x\) | Not-all-equal \(S_x\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 00 | \(\{0\}\) | \(\{1\}\) |
| 01, 10 | \(\{1\}\) | \(\{0,1\}\) |
| 11 | \(\{0\}\) | \(\{0\}\) |

For not-all-equal, the remote first-reader sets at \(b=0,1\) are respectively \(\{01,10,11\}\) and \(\{00,01,10\}\). They are incomparable. If every primitive must preserve the full physical relation on the Cartesian product with its ready local memory, the second reader cannot change \(b\). Each original \(b\) sector of the pure source is product across readers, so tracing the physical \(b\) gives the same refusal as in the parity argument. Under the reached-word reading a public herald that guarantees an unequal physical first row allows the second reader to reset \(b\).

For the single-name restriction, the primitive acts on one physical name and private memory alone. It may neither read another physical name nor use one as a control. Test each value of the addressed name and each independently ready memory state against every compatible pair of values on the other two names. For either law the two sets of compatible pairs are incomparable, so such a primitive must leave the addressed value unchanged. A gate reading both first-reader names while changing one uses two-name access. The successful not-all-equal protocol below instead uses private controls correlated with the actually reached words.

A full-law two-name first-reader operation can take either restrictive not-all-equal row to an unequal row, preserving the old local information privately on a blank-ready isometry. A finite-dimensional unitary that preserves the entire lawful physical space on **every** ancillary input also preserves it in reverse. At each fixed \(b\) its allowed first-reader subspace is invariant in both directions, hence so are the intersection (the unequal rows) and the two exclusive rows. Such a unitary cannot move a restrictive row into that intersection. A blank-ready implementation can extend to a unitary on a larger register whose action on other incoming memory values fails full-law safety. On the reached image the inverse returns the original lawful words. Reversibility alone does not specify which safety test was passed.

## The not-all-equal source and its success bound

The first reader may coherently test whether \(a=c\), retaining one common success flag on both equal rows. These two source words are \(001,110\), each of initial weight \(1/6\). On success store \(m=1-a\) privately, which equals \(b\) on both words. First change \(00\) to \(01\) by flipping \(c\) controlled by \(m\); then change the surviving \(11\) to \(01\) by flipping \(a\) controlled by \(1-m\). The physical intermediates are lawful for each source word. After certification that \(ac=01\), the second reader swaps \(b\) into a blank. The physical source is now \(010\), and the private registers carry \((00+11)/\sqrt2\). The equality flag itself can be prepared by accumulating \(a\oplus c\) into a private blank with two single-name controls. A first-reader gate measuring the two success rows as *distinct* fine outcomes would erase the required coherence.

On the complementary herald, of probability \(2/3\), the normalized source is \((01+10)/\sqrt2\otimes(0+1)/\sqrt2\), and the independent target has not yet been coupled. The successful pair gives each of the two fine records of sign \(t\) the subnormalized map \(P_t\rho P_t/6\) per launch. Thus on \(00\) each fine successful outcome has weight \(1/12\), and under the success herald it has weight \(1/4\). If the source is diagonal, the same herald still fires on \(1/3\) of launches but yields a fair mixture of two classical paths. Every fine analyzer record then has the same map \((\rho+M\rho M)/24\) per launch. An unread orthogonal tag distinguishing the two equal rows gives this same result. The two local analyzers and keeper writes are the ones from the previous instrument. Once a flag has fired, a missing third-writer input can leave that keeper at \(110\) while the earlier physical analysis persists. [Fine signed instrument and keeper histories](../TWO-READERS-ONE-SURVIVING-SIGN-20261121-001A/results.md)

There is also a deterministic, reached-word-safe transfer of the **entire** coherent not-all-equal source to private memories. Copy \(ac\) and \(b\) locally without changing them. Using the stored row, send \(00\) and \(11\) to \(01\) as above, leaving \(01,10\) alone. The physical first row is always unequal, so the second reader can reset \(b=0\) from its copy. Now take a residual \(10\) through \(11\) to \(01\), controlled by its original stored row. All intermediate named words are lawful and each update addresses at most one named bit. The last two updates need the already completed reset. This distinction between a full resource transfer and extracting a *maximally* entangled pair matters here.

The not-all-equal reduced density at the second reader is \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}3&2\\2&3\end{smallmatrix}\right)/6\), with eigenvalues \(5/6,1/6\). For a subnormalized pure branch use \(e=\operatorname{tr}\rho-\lambda_{\max}(\rho)\) on either one-reader marginal. The two values coincide by Schmidt decomposition. A local instrument cannot increase their sum over its fine outcomes: take the untouched side, whose marginal is the sum of the outcome marginals, and use subadditivity of \(\lambda_{\max}\). Each exact success on \(00\) leaves a pure Bell target, which factors out even with a retained environment, so its \(e\) is at least half its branch weight. Hence \(p/2\le1/6\). The attained \(p=1/3\) is optimal even with unrestricted local operations, including under prepared-state-only safety.

The same \(1/3\) bound applies to unambiguous **sign alone** on equally likely \(P_+/2,P_-/2\), even when the target may be destroyed. A conclusive real effect \(E_t\) has support in \(\operatorname{ran}P_t\) by zero wrong-sign probability and positivity. Supply each reader with a private real Bell pair, sending one half into its target port. The untouched spectator pair has unnormalized density \(E_t^{\mathsf T}/4\). Refine all remaining local systems and environments in real product bases. Every resulting nonzero spectator vector is real and belongs to \(\operatorname{ran}P_t\). Every such normalized real vector is maximally entangled: its coefficient matrix is \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\tb&-ta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), whose rows are orthogonal with equal norm. Thus the same tail bound applies. The product of the two local Bell preparations contributes no initial entanglement across readers, and its target marginal \(I/4\) is exactly the equiprobable mixture. This conclusion uses the real fine description.

If a sign must be guessed on **every** launch, the optimal success probability on those equiprobable mixtures is \(1/2+\sqrt5/6\). To see the upper bound, write the pure source in its two-term Schmidt basis as \(\sigma\), with squares \(\lambda,\mu\), and put \(\Delta=P_+/2-P_-/2=M/2\). A real separated effect is invariant under transposition on the first reader's entire input, while \(\Delta^{T_A}=-\Delta\). Its outcome probability difference therefore pairs the effect with \((\sigma-\sigma^{T_A})/2\otimes\Delta\). The factors have trace norms \(2\sqrt{\lambda\mu}\) and \(2\). The two-outcome success bound is \(1/2\) plus one quarter of their product. For attainment, first perform the deterministic transfer, use local real Schmidt bases, and measure \(J_{\rm source}\otimes J_{\rm target}\) at each reader. Each local observable is real symmetric with eigenvalues \(\pm1\). The negative product of the two reported signs has mean \(2t\sqrt{\lambda\mu}\) on sign \(t\). This guesses a sign and need not preserve a target state. On the diagonal controls and on serial lawful parity, the corresponding best guess is \(1/2\).

## What a synchronized product adds

First copy \(p=a\oplus c\) and \(b\) privately at the respective readers, leaving every source name unchanged. Now, at one atomic event, flip \(a\) controlled by the stored \(p\) and flip \(b\) by its stored value. On even input the new named word is \(cc0\). The residual source is the coherent sum of \(000,110\), independent of the private Bell pair. This yields the earlier complete meter at probability one. Either clearing half by itself makes both parity-one local rows illegal, with total probability \(1/2\). When only one offer is present, requiring the joined event gives a refusal and leaves the physical names untouched. On the six not-all-equal inputs the joined action makes three words \(000\), so it fails even as an atomic product there.

The pair of controlled flips is self-inverse and preserves parity on the entire joint memory subspace where the stored controls agree, even if no further named-word correlation is assumed. Mismatched controls flip total parity. A fully reversible product preserving the entire parity relation on **all independently chosen ancillary values** can only implement one common permutation of the two physical sectors. On each product basis input both output labels must be definite and equal, by the product structure and certainty of equality. Varying either side independently shows that the common output label depends only on the old sector. Local unitarity on every memory input rules out sending both sectors to one. Such a permutation leaves orthogonal physical records of the two paths.

An atomic product has a further distinction from a continuously applied product of local unitaries. Under intermediate safety on each basis word, the common definite outgoing sector cannot change continuously. Even on the actual pure parity state and the product probe alone, a continuous local product of unitaries preserves its two Schmidt squares \(1/2,1/2\). A lawful state's Schmidt matrix is block diagonal in the two matching physical sectors. Each sector weight must therefore be \(0,1/2\) or \(1\). Continuity keeps both weights at \(1/2\), with a single Schmidt term in each sector; tracing the physical source again leaves a separable target. This last statement concerns continuous unitary paths. The finite, serial instrument obstruction above already permits irreversible outcomes and private discards.
