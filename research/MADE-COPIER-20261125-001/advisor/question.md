## 3. Three senses of "two of it", each decidable by a program

Read after round t > s, in the run with X and Y both present ("run A").

### (i) The same form (the owner's "the same form")

**Two of Y in form** at round t: at least two parts of the live roster after round t, each **the same** as Y was after
round s (SD lines 29–30: a one-to-one renaming of members carrying h, k and the family of inclusion-maximal met sets),
each holding a member made after round s.

Decided by a graph isomorphism of labelled rosters (members labelled by h and k, one node per maximal met set). It looks
at the live roster only and says nothing of the allowed states; the knot test is applied, separately, to every knot made.

### (ii) The same built structure (the owner's "the same built structure")

**A copy of Y's build** at round t: a set K′ of knots made after round s with one-to-one maps φ from the knots of Y's
build to K′ and ψ from their places to the places of K′, such that a distinction d is a place of knot u exactly when ψ(d)
is a place of φ(u), and each d and ψ(d) agree on: the number of holders inside the set, whether some holder lies outside
it, whether it is loose, whether it is barred. Y's build is read after round s, the copy after round t. **The strong
form** adds the allowed states: the allowed states of the whole structure after round t, restricted to the places of K′,
are carried by ψ onto those after round s restricted to the places of Y's build (the same projection the knot test looks at).
**Two of Y in build**: two disjoint copies.

Decided by a labelled incidence isomorphism (knots and distinctions as the two kinds of node), and, for the strong form,
by comparing two projections: under parity two subspaces over the field of two elements, under not-all-equal two sets of
words by a solver. The build of a one-member part is the member and the knots it meets; a build of members alone is too
weak (it matches any made knot with as many places, loose and shared ones, which the prototype found under CAP-3).

### (iii) The same values (the owner's "the same values")

**A bearer of v** (proposed word) at round t: a distinction made after round s that is **tied** to v after round t
(glossary: equal in every allowed state, or unequal in every allowed state), where v is a place of a member of Y, not a
place of a member of X, and v is free (takes both values in allowed states). **Two of v in values**: two bearers of v
that are **loose** after round t (one holder each), so that each can still be taken by a later birth: the note's
"hand it to two users" (`research/THIRD-HOLDER-20261123-001/third_holder_moves.md` line 23). For a set V of Y's places:
two disjoint sets of loose bearers, one bearer per place of V in each.

Decided exactly: under parity by the kernel of the knot rows over the field of two elements (two distinctions are tied
exactly when their columns over a basis of the allowed states are equal); under not-all-equal by a solver (no allowed
state with v and the bearer unequal, or none with them equal). A tie is a two-place relation of the allowed states; a
pair never makes a knot (lesson 3.2), so a bearer is never a knot, and no passing knot has two of its own places tied.
"v still free" is automatic in every law used here: the allowed states on v's distinctions never change after they are
made (`survey.md`, 2.1).

   a place of X, and free. **Two of it in values** (the owner's "the same values"): two **loose** bearers of one v after
   round t. For a set V of Y's places: two disjoint sets of loose bearers, each with one bearer for every place of V.
   Ties are computed exactly: parity by a basis of the allowed states (two distinctions are tied exactly when their
   columns over that basis are equal; frozen when the column is zero); not-all-equal by a solver (no allowed state with
   the two unequal, or none with them equal). Bearers that are not loose are reported (a value handed down a chain),
   but they do not count as two.
4. **Handed on** (the note's words, kept): a birth hands v on when one of its fresh distinctions is a bearer of v.

## 4. The paired runs

For a design with X, Y and s:
1. **Run A**: the start grown under the law.
2. **B(i), the second pattern barred** (FD 5.1, primary): run A through round s; then every usable loose distinction of
   Y's members is barred; then the same law.
3. **B(ii), the second pattern removed** (FD 5.1, swap): as defined there, or "not defined".
4. **B_X, the first pattern barred**: the same as B(i) with X in place of Y.
5. **The second pattern replaced**: run A from the start in which Y is replaced by another second pattern of the class,
   the rest of the start unchanged (the design's own variants, section "Starts" of the pre-registration).
6. Comparisons are through the last round both runs reached, with the same bounds (FD 5.1).
7. The fold study's marks are reported for X against B(i) (its course differs, the way, one part again or for the first
   time, a joint birth) and for Y against B_X; the masked runs M and M′ (FD 5.6) confirm a "course differs" mark.

## 5. The copier, and its verdicts

1. **A copier** (the owner's sense): a first pattern X such that, under one law and one stated choice rule, for every
   second pattern Y of a stated class, run A has two of Y (in the sense named), at least one of the two made by X, and:
   - B(i) has fewer than two of Y in that sense (and B(ii) where defined);
   - Y is not the same as X (form), Y's build is not a copy of X's (build), v is not a place of X (values);
   - with Y replaced by another member Y′ of the class, run A has two of Y′.
   A copier is **made** when X is made by births after the start (not a start pattern) and every knot of the copies is
   made by births under the stated law with nothing placed.
2. **Verdicts** for each case that has two of Y with fewer than two in B(i):
   - **made by X, under a stated rule**: at least one of the two made by X; the choice a stated rule;
   - **the second pattern copying itself after a meeting**: both made by Y and its descendants; B_X has fewer than two;
   - **supplied by the choice**: the two need a choice picked for that history (run 4);
   - **supplied by the start**: X is a start pattern, or the meeting is through a start knot only (FD 5.7's class of the
     linking knots) — reported alongside the others;
   - **not the second pattern's**: Y is the same as X, or B_X has two of Y as well (Y does it alone).
3. **The class**, the rule and the law are reported with every verdict, and so is what each case survives (run 5).

## 6. Facts used (each argued in the pre-registration, "Settled by construction")

1. Under a law in which every birth either receives a fresh distinction or (parity, IMPLIED) adds no rule, the allowed
   states restricted to the distinctions existing after round t never change after round t (FD 7, lines 271–274, read
   for both knot laws). So ties are decided at the round the younger distinction is made.
2. Not-all-equal, every birth with a fresh distinction, every knot passing: no made distinction is ever tied to any
   distinction.
3. Parity: a birth with two or more fresh distinctions hands nothing on; a birth with one fresh distinction f hands on
   exactly the values v for which its inputs together with v sum to a rule of the structure before its round.
4. Under MET or STEPS-1 with COPY-LAW(t): every birth whose parents are not all start knots receives exactly one fresh
   distinction, or none under parity when it is implied.

## In standard terms

In the literature's words, not the program's: a synchronous hypergraph-rewriting system (each step adds hyperedges on
degree-one vertices of earlier ones and, under the laws used here, on one vertex of higher degree), in which every
hyperedge carries a constraint: a parity equation over the field of two elements, or a not-all-equal condition (a
two-colouring condition). Sense (i) is isomorphism of a labelled component of a derived graph; sense (ii) is an induced,
labelled sub-hypergraph isomorphism; sense (iii) is **fan-out** (the circuit literature's word): two new variables equal,
in every solution, to a given old variable. Under parity a new variable of a new equation with one new variable is the sum
of that equation's old variables, so fan-out is linear algebra: v is copied to f exactly when the new equation plus v lies
in the old row space. With every variable in at most two equations the solution space is a graph's cycle space and two
variables are equal in every solution exactly when they form a two-edge cut. Under not-all-equal, when every new
constraint brings a new variable of its own, every solution of the old system extends and every new variable can be
flipped alone, so no new variable is ever functionally determined by the others.

## Settled by construction (stated, not tested)

Arguments for the referee; the runs check them. Not yet checked by anyone but the drafter.

1. **S1, ties are decided at birth.** Under a law in which every birth of a round either receives a fresh distinction or
   (parity, IMPLIED) is a birth whose inputs' parity is already a rule, every allowed state before the round extends
   (each other birth is satisfied by its own fresh distinction, which nothing else holds yet), and no state after it
   restricts to a state not allowed before it. So the allowed states restricted to the distinctions that exist after
   round t are the same after every later round (FD 7, lines 271–274, extended to parity). Whether two distinctions are
   tied is therefore settled at the round the younger is made.
2. **S2, not-all-equal: a made distinction is tied to nothing.** Let every birth receive a fresh distinction (FLOOR1,
   CAP-3, ORD) and let f be a fresh distinction of a knot u made in round t that passes the knot test. u has three or more
   places, so some allowed state after round t is not constant on u's places other than f; flipping f alone keeps u
   not-all-equal, and no other knot holds f after round t. So f is tied to no distinction existing after round t, and by
   S1 to none at any later round; a distinction made later is tied to nothing by the same argument. Hence **under
   not-all-equal no made pattern has two of anything in values**, under any taking rule, choice or start. The exceptions
   are exactly births with no fresh distinction (FLOOR0, ONE-FEWER) and failing knots.
3. **S3, parity: what a birth hands on.** A birth with two or more fresh distinctions hands nothing on (each fresh
   distinction is held only by it, beside another that only it holds). A birth u with exactly one fresh distinction f has
   x_f equal to the sum of its inputs in every allowed state, so f is a bearer of v exactly when u's inputs together with v
   sum to a rule of the structure before its round (for v made in the same round, when the two births' inputs sum to
   such a rule).
4. **S4, one fresh distinction.** Under COPY-LAW(t) (FLOOR1 with the fresh count by distinct inputs, RELEASE, no
   FILLERS) a join has two or more inputs and receives one fresh distinction. Under MET, an offshoot of a knot q that is
   not a start knot takes: q holds the distinction d it took loose from a parent g, so q meets g; g has three or more
   places, and every place of g other than d is shared (with g's own parents, or with the births g made, since under
   RELEASE a parent spends every usable loose distinction and only a retired knot holds barred ones, and a retired knot
   has no children), hence eligible and not held by q. Under STEPS-1 the same g is one parent step above q. So under MET
   and STEPS-1 every birth whose parents are not all start knots receives exactly one fresh distinction (none, under
   parity, when it is implied), and every member
   it makes holds one usable loose distinction and nothing barred; holders never decrease, so no such knot ever holds two
   loose distinctions. Consequences: **no part the same as a part with a member of h ≥ 2, and no copy of a build with a
   knot holding two or more loose distinctions, is ever made by such births**; in particular the two-knot part is made
   only by offshoots of start knots, so it does not copy itself.
5. **S5, parity with two holders** (survey 1.1): ties are two-element cuts; two loose distinctions of a connected
   structure are tied only when they are its only loose ones.
6. **S6, not-all-equal with two holders** (survey 1.2; the record's proof): every distinction can change alone.
7. **Checks, true by construction**: with no copier readings the engine is the fold study's; a bearer is never a knot; a
   passing knot has no two of its own places tied.

### 1.1 Parity, every distinction with at most two holders: a value is handed on once — confirmed, with one precision

**The note** (lines 13–25): take a connected part of parity knots, every distinction with one or two holders; an end is
a distinction with one holder inside the part. By the bond-closure criterion
(`research/BOND-CLOSURE-20261120-001/criterion.md` line 11, "A cut containing only loose distinctions must include
every loose distinction, since H is connected"; that study is "Proof and bounded experiment. Independent program
referee pending", its decision line 3), the ends satisfy exactly one rule, their total parity. So a part with exactly
two ends ties them; a part with three or more ends ties no two of them; "it cannot take a loose value and hand it to
two users".

**Confirmed, by the same argument, in the form the copier needs.** With at most two holders the allowed states are the
cycle space of the graph whose vertices are the knots and one outside vertex, whose edges are the distinctions (a loose
one joined to the outside). So:
- two distinctions are tied exactly when they form a cut of two (neither alone a cut); every parity tie is an equality,
  since the all-zero state is allowed;
- two **loose** distinctions of a connected structure are tied only when they are its only two loose distinctions (a
  cut made only of loose edges is the whole set of loose edges, the criterion's line 11);
- hence, in a connected structure with three or more loose distinctions, **no value is at two loose places**.

**The precision.** "Ties no two of its ends" holds for the part taken alone (outside holders read as loose). In the
whole structure the outside can tie two ends of a part, and a value can sit at three or more places, but at most two
of them loose: a chain of pieces carries one value through every shared distinction between them. Example found by this
survey: knots `[[4,5,9],[3,6,8],[0,4,5],[1,2,10],[1,7,9,10],[3,6,7,8]]` tie `{0, 2, 9}`: 0 and 2 loose (the
structure's only two loose distinctions), 9 shared. This does not change the note's conclusion; it says where a value
can sit.

**Direct check (this survey; computed).** 3,659 drawn connected structures of 2 to 9 knots of 3 to 5 places, two
holders at most (seed 2026112501): no tie class with three loose members; no two loose distinctions tied while a third
loose distinction exists; on 14,459 connected parts taken alone, every part with exactly two ends ties them (292) and
none with three or more ends ties two (14,167). Tie classes with three or more members: 2 (each with two loose
members, as above). The tie test agrees with brute force on 800 drawn structures under both knot laws (`t_ties.py`).

Evidence class: proved by hand (a cut argument resting on the bond-closure criterion, referee pending), checked by

ration that gives every birth a fresh distinction (ORD, CAP-k, FLOOR1, RATION-k) every round extends, and a knot's
relation on its places never changes after its birth. The same holds under parity with the IMPLIED closing (a birth
with no fresh distinction is one whose inputs' parity is already implied, so it adds no rule). Since adding knots never
adds states, **the allowed states restricted to the distinctions that exist after round t never change after round t**.
So whether two distinctions are tied is settled at the round the younger is made, and no later meeting can tie or untie
them. (Argued here; it uses nothing about holders, so it holds for every taking rule of the fold study's ladder.)

**Not-all-equal: a made distinction is tied to nothing (argued here; checked).** Let f be a fresh distinction of a knot
u born in round t, under a law in which every birth of every round receives a fresh distinction, and let u pass the knot
test. u has at least three places, so it has an allowed state whose restriction to its places other than f is not
constant; in that state f can be flipped alone (u stays not-all-equal, and at round t no other knot holds f). So f is
tied to no distinction existing at round t, and by the lemma to none at any later round; a distinction made later is
tied to nothing by the same argument. **Under not-all-equal no made pattern can hand on any value, under any taking rule,
any choice and any start**, so long as every birth receives a fresh distinction and every knot passes. Checked by this
survey's prototype: no tie class containing a made distinction in 480 not-all-equal runs (2,860 structures: Q, MET,
STEPS-1, STEPS-2, five base laws, four choices, six starts) and 240 histories with every take drawn; and no made
distinction tied to a place of the second pattern in the first test's 600 not-all-equal runs and 213 pairs. The only
ways out are births with no fresh distinction (FLOOR0, ONE-FEWER) and knots that fail (CARRY's join in the fold study,
its results line 150).

**Parity: a birth hands on the parity of what it takes (argued here; checked).** Under parity a birth with two or more
fresh distinctions hands nothing on (each fresh distinction is tied to nothing, since only u holds it and u holds another
that only u holds). A birth u with exactly one fresh distinction f has x_f = the sum of its inputs in every allowed state,
so **f is tied to v exactly when the inputs of u together with v sum to a rule already in the structure** (the row span
before its round). Under FLOOR1 with the fresh count by distinct inputs (A235), a join receives one fresh distinction,
and so does every offshoot that takes a shared distinction. Under MET (and STEPS-1) every offshoot of a knot that is not a
start knot takes one (its parent meets its own parent, which holds another shared distinction), so **after round 1
almost every birth hands a value on**: the parity of its inputs. Which value is set by what it takes, that is by the
choice.

### 2.2 What changes under the fold study's laws, which give three or more holders

- **Under parity, a third holder is now made where a value would need it.** The note's own diagnosis was that "sending
  one value to two places" needs a birth that takes a distinction already held twice (lines 48–50: "No recorded growth
  move gives a third holder to a value that is not already tied"). Under MET every taking offshoot is such a birth, and
  values do reach two loose places. Smallest structure by hand: knots `{v, p, q}`, `{p, q, c1}`, `{p, q, c2}`. Then
  c1 = p + q = v and c2 = v: one value at three loose places (v, c1, c2), with p and q held three times; every knot passes
  under parity (checked). Under not-all-equal the same three knots tie nothing (checked).
- **In growth, under MET, seen:** on the two-knot start under COPY-LAW(OFFSHOOT) with MET(NEWEST), parity, after round 3
  the start's shared distinction 0 (four holders) is tied to the fresh distinctions 22 and 24 of two round-3 offshoots,
  one descended from each start knot: one value at two loose places, made by births. Each chain of births re-took the
  other places of its ancestors (22 = 16 + 7 = (8 + 1) + 7 = 2 + 1 = 0, by the rows of knots 3 and 0). Without a taking rule the same
  prototype found only ties of the kind 1.1 allows (one value handed down a chain to one loose place).
- **Under not-all-equal nothing changes:** the lemma above holds with any number of holders. The third holders that the
  taking laws make never tie a made distinction. The Fano kind of tie can be a property of a start, never of what a growth
  makes when its births all receive a fresh distinction.

So: **under parity a value can be handed on to two loose places, by births with one fresh distinction whose inputs close
a rule; under not-all-equal no value is ever handed on by a growth in which every birth receives a fresh
distinction.** The owner's question, in values, is therefore unsettled only under parity, and there it is a question
about the choice.

## 1. Structures in which a distinction may have any number of holders

1. A distinction has one or more holders: **loose** with one, **shared** with two or more (definitions line 12,
   extended). A knot's places are fixed when it is made. Two facts used below:
   - **holders never decrease**: a birth adds itself as a holder of what it takes, and nothing removes a holder (run
     B(ii) of the paired test, section 5, is the one construction that does, and it is defined only where stated);
   - **a barred distinction is never unbarred**, and a knot never gains a place, so a knot that is no member never
     becomes one.
2. Two knots **meet** when they hold a common distinction, whatever its number of holders; the m holders of one
   distinction meet pairwise (the definitions' line 14, read for any number of holders; as the other thread's engines
   read it, `rlaw.py` lines 372–381).
3. Live, dead, member, h, k, met set, common knot, joinable, part, groups, owing and short are worded as in the
   definitions (lines 14–40), read with this meeting. **A knot is not in its own met set**: the met set of w is the set of
   members other than w that w meets, when there are at least two.
4. **Parents** are written by each birth (definitions lines 49–51; `LOCAL-ROUND-20261123-001/definitions.md` lines
   27–31): the birth record is not a reading of the holders. A start knot has none. One **parent step** goes from a knot to
   one of its parents. The **ancestors** of a knot are the knots reached by one or more parent steps; the **descendants of
   g** are the knots that have g as an ancestor, g not included; **X and its descendants**, for a set X, includes X.
   Start knots have no ancestors. A **common ancestor** of two knots is an ancestor of both; it says nothing of meeting.
5. An **ancestral common knot** of two members u and v is a common knot of u and v that is a recorded ancestor of each and
   not the parent of both. (The owner approved the term on 24 November 2026.) The start's own common knot of two start
   members, which is no recorded ancestor of their descendants, is not an ancestral common knot and is reported as its
   own class (section 5, item 7).
6. **Share nothing and wholly apart.** Two parts of a roster **share nothing** when no member of one holds a distinction
   that a member of the other holds. Link two parts when they share something; the **classes** of a roster are the sets
   of parts connected by such links. Two parts are **wholly apart** when they lie in different classes: the parts of the
   roster can then be split into two sets, one holding each, such that no part of one set shares anything with a part
   of the other. (A third part may not link them.)

## 2. One complete round with taking

Steps 1 to 8 of the definitions (lines 34–67), with these additions and exact readings.

1. **Order of births and names.** The joins are the kept groups: groups of three or more in lexicographic order of their
   sorted member names, then pairs in the same order. Then the offshoots, in increasing name of the member; a member's
   offshoots in increasing name of the loose distinction each takes. Each member gives its usable loose distinctions, in
   increasing name, to its births in birth order (its joins first). Under HOLD a member keeps the first of its usable
   loose distinctions after those its joins take, and its offshoots take the rest (as the ring study's engine does). New
   knots are named in birth order once every birth of the round is formed; their fresh distinctions are named after the
   demand step, in birth order, each knot's usable ones before its barred ones. A start's distinctions are named by its
   stars' labels in increasing order. The swap "order of births reversed" reverses the whole list of births before any
   distinction is given out.
2. **Eligible.** A distinction is **eligible** for a take when it has two or more holders at the start of the round. A
   distinction loose at the start of the round is never eligible. Several births may take one distinction in one round.
3. **SHORT-TAKES** (rule R, A230, as a treatment of a short member, in place of the base law's treatment). No group is
   cancelled. A short member gives its usable loose distinctions to its first births in birth order and, for each further
   birth, takes one distinction it holds that is eligible, is held at the start of the round by no other parent of that
   birth, and differs from the others it gives this round; which one, by the choice (OLDEST or NEWEST, ALL-CHOICES on small
   cases). A short member owes more than it holds, so it makes no offshoots. If some short member has no allowed
   distinction for one of its births, no birth is made in the round (a **residual stall**); the structure is then
   unchanged, so the residual stall repeats for ever and the run stops.
4. **Step 5′, the taking rule.** After the round's births are formed and before any is made, the law's taking rule (section
   3) adds distinctions to some births, read on the structure at the start of the round, birth by birth in birth order.
   Every taking rule adds at most one distinction to a birth, except CARRY, under which a join takes up to one for each of
   its parents. A take never repeats a distinction the birth already takes; the fresh count uses **distinct** inputs.
5. **The choice.** Among the eligible distinctions a birth may take, sorted by name:
   - **OLDEST**: the smallest name; **NEWEST**: the largest (A231, A236). Both use the names, and so the start's numbering.
   - **IN-TURN**: defined for offshoots: the i-th offshoot of one parent in the round (counting from zero, in birth order)
     takes the i-th, cycling. With one offshoot per parent it equals OLDEST. A join under MET(IN-TURN) takes by OLDEST;
     IN-TURN is not used with SHORT-TAKES.
   - **BY-COUNT**: the taking birth's parent's number of places (for a join, its first parent's, by name), modulo the
     number of eligible distinctions, counting from the oldest at zero. It uses the structure only.
   - **BY-RECEIVED** (a named case only): the parent's number of places less its number of parents, modulo the number of
     eligible distinctions, counting from zero. It uses the birth record.
   - **ALL-CHOICES**: every choice enumerated (small cases).
6. **Step 6, demand.** As the definitions, with the met sets read from the structure after the takes and before the fresh
   distinctions, so that a knot that took a shared distinction is already met by that distinction's other holders (the
   other thread's lookahead, `rlaw.py` lines 480–494). Swap: **demand before the takes**, read on the structure in which
   each birth holds only what it took loose (and, under SHORT-TAKES, what it was given).
7. **Step 7, fresh distinctions.** The ration's formula with p read as the number of distinct distinctions the birth takes
   (A235, `ONE-PARENT-REREAD-20261123-001A/preregistration.md` line 47). For a birth that takes nothing extra this is its
   number of parents, so nothing in the definitions changes. Swap: **fresh by parents**.
8. **Closing (parity).**
   - **IMPLIED** (primary): a birth receives no fresh distinction when the parity of what it takes lies in the span of the
     rules of the knots of the structure before the round (A230's form, `REREAD-...-001A/preregistration.md` line 47).
     Each birth of the round is tested against the structure before the round, not in sequence, so that the result does
     not depend on the order of births within the round. Several births of one round may be implied. The definitions'
     clauses "it is then the round's only birth, nothing is barred and no old member keeps anything" do not apply.
   - **LITERAL**: the definitions' exception (line 63), a swap only, never in a paired test (any barred distinction
     switches it off everywhere, definitions lines 73–74, and run B(i) always bars).
   - With every distinction held at most twice, in a connected structure, the two agree (the settled statement of
     `RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001A/preregistration.md` lines 100–101). A birth that IMPLIED leaves with no fresh distinction
     and that then fails the knot test is a failure.

## 4. Coming apart, and one part again

1. **Come apart** keeps the definitions' sense (line 87): the members after round t descended from a part after round s
   lie in two or more parts.
2. **The descendants of X after round s**, X a part after round s: the knots made after round s that have a parent among X
   and its descendants.
3. Two parts X and Y after round s that are wholly apart, and that descend from one part after an earlier round, are
   **one part again** at round t > s when a member of X or its descendants and a member of Y or its descendants lie in one
   part after round t. When X and Y have no common part in their ancestry they are **one part for the first time**. The
   owner's "meet again" is quoted only in his words.

## 5. The paired test

1. **Runs.** Run A grows the start under the law. For a round s and two parts X and Y after round s:
   - **B(i), the second part barred** (primary): run A through round s; then every usable loose distinction of Y's members
     is barred; Y stays built and makes nothing more; then the same law.
   - **B(ii), the second part never made** (swap): run A through round s; then Y's members are removed with their fresh
     distinctions, and each distinction they took returns to its other holder, loose and barred. It is defined only when
     no knot outside Y holds a fresh distinction of a member of Y and every distinction a member of Y took has exactly one
     other holder; otherwise it is reported "not defined".
   - Runs are compared only through the last round both reached, with the same bounds on rounds and knots.
2. **The trace of X.** For each round t > s: the births of round t all of whose parents are in X or its descendants (a
   joint birth, with a parent outside them, is excluded; its children are included), each with its kind, its parents, what
   it takes, which of those were shared at the start of the round, its fresh count and its usable and barred fresh
   counts; the members after round t in X or its descendants with h and k; and their met sets, each met set intersected
   with X and its descendants first and then reduced to the inclusion-maximal ones. Knots made by round s and
   distinctions existing after round s keep their names; knots made after round s for X and its descendants, and their
   fresh distinctions, are renamed in order of name (an order-preserving renaming); any other new knot or distinction is
   "other". **The course of X** is its trace. The demand of X's births is recorded beside the trace and reported on its
   own line (the first round at which it differs); it is not part of the course.
3. **The four marks of a pair** (facts about run A, and comparisons; not a scale):
   - **one part again**: the first round t > s at which they are one part again (section 4);
   - **a joint birth**: the first round of run A with a birth that has a parent in X or its descendants and a parent in Y
     or its descendants;
   - **the course differs**: X's trace in run A and in run B(i) differ (the first round);
   - **the course differs with what the other is**: X's trace differs between two runs A from starts that differ only in
     the holdings of the start knot Y descends from (renaming every knot and distinction of the trace by first
     appearance, since the two starts are numbered differently).
4. **The choice is necessary** (a control, not a mark): the same start and law, with only the takes of X and its
   descendants using another choice (OLDEST, NEWEST, IN-TURN, BY-COUNT, BY-RECEIVED) while every other birth keeps the
   law's own; reported: whether the course differs under each.
5. **The way** of a "course differs" mark, by the first differing step of the first differing round, in the order of the
   round's own steps: **a group or owing** (the groups containing a member of X or its descendants, or their owing); **a
   round-wide trigger** (a stall, a residual stall or a closing differs between the runs); **a take**; **fresh** (the
   fresh counts, through the demand).
6. **Confirmation through a meeting.** A masked run M is run A in which, from round s + 1, no member of X or its
   descendants is joinable with a member of Y or its descendants (the pair is removed from the joinable relation in the
   round and in its demand step). The difference is **through a meeting** when X's trace in M equals its trace in B(i). If
   not, an isolating run M′ makes no member of Y or its descendants joinable with any other member; when X's trace in M′
   equals B(i), the difference is **through a third part**.
7. **The verdict** of each "course differs" mark:
   - **through a knot the growth made**: confirmed through a meeting (or a third part), and at the round they are one part
     again X's and Y's descendants are joinable using only met sets that have a common knot made after round 0;
   - **through a start knot** (the start's own meeting reopened): confirmed, and joinable only through met sets whose
     common knots are start knots;
   - **supplied**: the way is a round-wide trigger; or the law is FIRST(PARTS) and the way is a take (its rule looked for
     the other part); or the law is RECORD-k or ALL-JOINABLE (the record alone);
   - **unconfirmed**: none of these.
   With each verdict, the class of the knots linking the two at that round: an ancestral common knot (made, or a start
   knot that is a recorded ancestor); a knot made by the growth that is no ancestor of both; a start knot that is no
   recorded ancestor of both (the start's common knot of the founders).
8. **Pairs.** For each round s from 1 to 6: the parts after round s in order of their smallest member name; the first eight;
   every pair (i, j), i < j, in lexicographic order, that is wholly apart, at most four per round. Each pair is labelled as
   having come apart (a common part in its ancestry) or not. In the HOLD arm, and on the two-knot start's own parts, every
   pair is taken and labelled wholly apart or not.

## 6. The two hypotheses that split the laws

- **H1, parents only.** Every distinction a new knot takes is held, at the start of its round, by one of its parents; its
  other places are fresh.
- **H2, joinable from the structure.** Two members are joinable only when some knot meets both, and every birth's parents
  lie in one part of the roster its round reads.
- The earlier laws, Q and SHORT-TAKES (every choice, every start round), CARRY, and every ration, treatment, release rule,
  extra and closing of the definitions satisfy H1 and H2. RECORD-k and ALL-JOINABLE break H2. MET, STEPS-k, UP-TO-k and
  FIRST break H1. The A225 control breaks H1 once, by hand.

## 7. The knot test with three or more holders

- **Parity**, two methods that must agree on every knot: **ranks** (the definitions' line 133), which needs no limit on
  holders; and **kernel**, a basis of the allowed states projected onto the knot's places, rank |S| − 1. The definitions'
  cut method needs two holders per shared distinction and is not used. Both are checked against brute force before any run.
- **Not-all-equal**: a SAT solver for each of the 2^|S| assignments of each knot's places (definitions lines 137–139).
  Under not-all-equal with a ration that gives every birth a fresh distinction (ORD, CAP-k, FLOOR1, RATION-k), every round
  extends (a birth with a fresh distinction forbids no old word), and a knot's relation on its places does not change
  after its birth: a knot tested at any round after its birth is tested for every later round. Under FLOOR0 and ONE-FEWER
  a birth can receive nothing, and every knot is tested at every round.
- **The birth method** (not-all-equal, for knots of any size, where every birth receives a fresh distinction). A knot u
  born in round t passes, at round t and every later round, exactly when its inputs that existed before round t are
  jointly free in the structure before round t (every assignment of them extends to an allowed state). The argument: at
  the end of round t every other birth of the round can be satisfied by one of its own fresh distinctions, which nothing
  else holds yet, so the allowed states on u's places are those whose old part is allowed before round t and which are
  not all equal; and u's own fresh distinction completes any old part to one that is not all equal. Joint freedom of a
  set I is decided exactly by removing, again and again, every knot that has a distinction held by it alone among the
  knots left and not in I (such a knot can always be satisfied); splitting what is left into pieces linked by
  distinctions outside I; and testing, for each piece, every assignment of the distinctions of I it touches (half of them,
  by the symmetry of flipping every distinction). A start knot is tested directly. The method is checked against the
  direct test on every knot of at most 12 places of run A, and on drawn grown structures with failing knots.
- Each run's knots are tested under the run's own knot law. A knot that passes has, in the whole structure, exactly its
  own rule on its places: constrained as a whole, every part free, some state allowed (K1–K3 with forgetting).
- With three or more holders a birth with a fresh distinction need not pass: its inputs may be constrained together. The
  earlier laws' settled statement (`RING-IN-BALANCE-20261123-001A/preregistration.md` lines 100–105) needs two holders.

# What gives a distinction a third holder

Note by the thread for epic `THIRD-HOLDER-20261123-001`, 2026-11-23, written while its two studies ran. One argument,
one small check and a reading of the record. Scientific referee pending.

## What a third holder is for

The 20/9 distribution asks each side to use one of an observer's values twice while the distinction that carries it
stays loose for the birth: once to compare it with a neighbour and once to hand it across. Two parity knots that share
two distinctions tie their third distinctions (even(x, p, q) with even(c, p, q) forces c = x). That hands a value on
once. A third knot on the same pair hands it on twice, and gives p and q a third holder.

## Parity knots with two holders hand a value on once and never twice

Take a connected part made of parity knots in which every distinction has one or two holders, and call an end of the
part a distinction with only one holder inside it: a loose distinction, or one whose other holder is a knot outside
the part. The bond-closure study proved that a rule of the part on loose distinctions alone must contain every one of
them ([criterion](../BOND-CLOSURE-20261120-001/criterion.md)); treating outside holders as loose, the same holds for
ends. So the ends of such a part satisfy exactly one rule, their total parity, and no other.

Two consequences. A part with exactly two ends ties them, which is the copy above. A part with three or more ends
ties no two of them. So no part of parity knots with two holders per distinction presents one value at three of its
ends: it cannot take a loose value and hand it to two users. This is proved for parity parts only. A not-all-equal
knot is not a parity rule and the argument says nothing about parts that contain one; for those the evidence is the
census of the review-leads epic (no hidden excess in 506 million pairs).

## The record already has a copy made of not-all-equal knots, and it has a third holder

The nesting study proved by hand that the Fano pattern less one knot ties that knot's three distinctions
([its note](../SUBST-NEST-20261112-001/subst_nest.md)). A direct check ([script](fano_copy_check.py)): seven
distinctions with the seven not-all-equal knots {i, i + 1, i + 3} allow no word; leave any one knot out and 10 words
remain, in every one of which that knot's three distinctions are equal. In that structure the three tied distinctions have two
holders and the other four have three. So not-all-equal knots can hand one value to three places, and when they do,
some distinction has a third holder. With two holders per distinction every not-all-equal structure allows a word
(recorded in the postulates basket, B265), and no copy made of them has been found.

## Which recorded moves give a third holder

| Move or structure | Third holder? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary growth | Never | A birth takes loose distinctions and adds fresh ones; no knot gains a distinction (checked on 403 rounds by the audit of the review-leads epic) |
| The rival fresh-count laws (joins that forbid) | No | They change how many fresh distinctions a birth gets |
| Joins across spent knots | No | The shared distinction between two spent knots permits a birth; the live neighbours pay with loose distinctions |
| A fresh distinction shared by two existing knots (B255, not yet run) | No | The fresh distinction has two holders; two existing knots gain a place each |
| Merging two distinctions the allowed words prove equal | It can, without handing anything on | The merged distinction has the holders of both, so it may have three or four; but the tie it renames was already there |
| Built patterns: the Fano pieces, the bounded-bond pattern, the nesting study's copies | Yes | Three, six and three holders; none is grown by a recorded law |

No recorded growth move gives a third holder to a value that is not already tied. The move that would: a birth that
takes a distinction which already has two holders. Under ordinary growth a value is read once, by the one birth that
takes its loose distinction; that move would let it be read again.

## The reason the recorded laws cannot

Rule R lets a short knot take a distinction it already shares; rule Q lets an offshoot also take one its parent shares. In
both, everything a new knot takes is held by one of its own parents. Whatever a parent holds is already met by
everything the parent meets. So taking it can make the new knot meet only what its parent already met: it can rejoin
what is still joined, and nothing else. The argument is written out in the pre-registration as one induction, for parts
that share nothing even through a third part, under any release rule. It is an argument for the referee. The runs bear it

The third recorded item, the move of assumption A225, is a birth placed by hand, not a law. As a law it is rule R or

## The laws that reach further

A law outside that argument has a birth take a distinction that none of its parents holds. Seven such laws were run, with
four ways of choosing which distinction is taken. Each has a cost in what it looks at.

| Law | Looks at, in holder steps | Looks at, in steps of the record of descent | Parts that came apart become one part again, and the first's course differs? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Joined by the record alone | 0 | 2 or 3 | No (and by argument never) |
| Any two members join | 0 | 0, but it looks at the whole roster | Nothing ever comes apart |
| Rule Q, rule R | 1 | 0 | No (the argument above) |
