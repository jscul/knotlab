# Referee R1 · What an ordinary-growth swap contributes to a signed reading
**Confirmed with fixes — no Block.**

**CHSH 4 survives independent reconstruction with four distinct settings, separated holders, equal nonzero normalizers and no signalling.** Two completions of one actual reading can cancel. Ordinary growth supplies the meeting pattern and its admissible swaps. Once a lift is specified, several signs are computable from a state. Turning one into a weighting and adding differently signed completions are additional prescriptions. Ordinary positive counting has one joint distribution for the four distinctions and obeys CHSH 2.

## Two tables that settle the existence question

In both examples outcomes within each row are ordered 00, 01, 10, 11. “Signed count” means sum the weights of all completions of the selected pair and divide by their common signed total (R1). “Squared sum” means square each of those four sums, then normalize the row (R2). Setting choices are passive and independent of the preparation by construction. No knot holds a mark of both readers in either example. Even the two sets of holders do not meet.

Dividing by a negative common total in the second example gives nonnegative probabilities. Both examples have two entries of 1/2 per setting and both readers have marginal 1/2 at either setting. These directly check G0–G4. In the first example the sides' internal distinctions are exactly (6,8) and (7,9). Parity gives $x_8=x_9=t$, with $u=x_6$, $v=x_7$ and $t$ independent and four completions per triple. W2 chooses a negative weight when both $u+t$ and $v+t$ are one, with addition modulo two. For the actual reading $x_6=x_7=0$, four positive completions cancel four negative ones. Once **all four** values are given, the weight is determined. After one growth only distinctions 6 and 7 remain in the respective pools, one per reader. In the second example the full word 0100 itself has one positive, two negative and 81 zero-weight completions. [Exact sample tables and full-word counts](sample_tables.json)

R1 has a signed joint weighting, so summing out the other reader always gives the same marginal. A hit requires a negative weight in the joint table of all four values. R2 adds a further choice of which unread outcomes to retain. In the usual normalized equal superposition of unread values the first example's retained fraction is $128/(2^8\cdot 32)=1/64$ for each setting. Equal retention and fair recorded marginals do not give a positive joint table for the original four values. The existence theorem for signed representations allows a suitable weighting for each no-signalling table. It does not predict success for an arbitrary weighting. [Fine's joint-distribution result](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.48.291) · [Abramsky and Brandenburger's signed-representation result](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.0264)

## Arguments, predictions and fixes

**A1 and P1 — confirmed.** Parent sets, their funding and the implication test are preserved by a knot renaming. Match births with renamed parents and equal fresh counts, invert this matching on the partner, and match leftover indistinguishable loose distinctions. This extends the knot action with order two. A particular previous matching of the distinctions may be incompatible. All **11,733 individual knot involutions** extended on the 7,383 continuing stage triples. The old minimal lift failed to extend 410 times and the maximal lift 3,598 times. The five-knot ring reflection in the advisor's answer is one concrete failure.

**A2 and P2 — necessary conditions confirmed. Narrowing on sufficiency and population.** The exact sentence in [the results](../README.md#the-answer) begins “The states do sort into kept and flipped, but the sort is the action of one renaming and gives every state the same weight, and under parity the flipped states vanish once growth makes a knot of three places.” The intrinsic read for a specified lift $g$ is the whole mismatch $x+gx$. Zero and all ones are two special values. In the three full parity rounds, **4,553, 7,584 and 11,200** representative-lift rows also have a state of neither kind. So does every counted not-all-equal row. Giving all states equal weight is a separate choice. Global flips occurred in 63/1/0 parity rows and 357/146/81 counted not-all-equal rows, with zero violations of P2. In [A2](../README.md#arguments-before-the-census), the clause “which under parity need only that every knot holds an even number of moved distinctions” needs “require”. For parity states $S$ and the mask $m$ of moved distinctions the exact test is $m\in(I+g)S$. On the three-knot start at index 16 the central knot shares two distinctions with each outer knot and every knot has two loose distinctions. Its maximal lift moves all ten. A moved-only flip would put exactly three ones on the six-place central knot. Of 128 parity states, 16 are kept and none flips.

**A3 — the structural exchange is confirmed. Narrowing on what a state reads.** [The decision](../decision.md#what-stands) says “A state sees the twist only as the sort into kept and flipped states: a flipped state is an assignment that changes value each time a twisted loop is gone round.” On the moved incidence, a chain from a knot to its partner closes when partners are identified. Changing representatives at intermediate knots cancels on the closed chain. Renaming the entire pattern preserves the existence of this exchange. The four-knot source half-turn has such a chain of length two and the six-knot half-turn one of length three. But on the four-knot ring, the legal state with distinctions 0, 4 and 6 set to one disagrees on exchanged shared pair 0/2 and agrees on pair 1/3. The zero state agrees on both along the same pattern. A local disagreement also occurs for a two-sided reflection without a chain to its partner. Thus the two-distinction read in A3 measures the *state's mismatch* on that pair. To establish the exchange one needs the chain and its lifted endpoint. A closed chain of physically named incidences returns to its own representative. Fixed distinctions elsewhere can preclude every global flip while leaving the exchange intact. [Ring witnesses](ring_rebuild.json)

**A4, P3 and P9 — class action confirmed. Narrowing on where the ring signs are compared.** All tied shortest changes through $d$ map bijectively to those through $g(d)$. Their span $H$ is preserved. The induced linear action on $S/H$ fixes zero, hence fixes both classes when there are exactly two. Each class indicator is separately invariant. The two-class quotient has one nontrivial character with value +1 on the zero class. Selecting that character as the weighting adds a choice, as does selecting the constant character. Translation by an assignment of the other class exchanges them. The parity ranks $(\dim S,\dim H)$ for four, five and six knots were (8,8), (10,10), (12,12) at the start and (14,13), (15,14), (22,21) after one growth. All **1,244 literal lifted involutions** on those six stages preserve the class read. In the census, **0/2,152** lift rows on 624 two-class stages move a class; **46/102** on 27 four-class stages and **6/6** on two eight-class stages do. In [P9](../README.md#against-the-frozen-predictions), “All 1,076 swaps” counts knot-swap conjugacy representatives. A nonidentity involution on the three nontrivial four-class characters exchanges two and fixes their product. It supplies no coefficient or sign to either class.

[P3's result](../README.md#against-the-frozen-predictions) says “**Held** on all 3 rings at rounds 0 and 1 under both laws where run”. At the source the four-knot parity half-turn has 16 kept and 16 flipped states, with positive old-shared sign on both. The six-knot source has 64 and 64, positive and negative respectively. At the source there is only one motion class, so the old-shared sign there is a character, not yet the sign of two motion classes. After growth both parity half-turns have zero globally flipped states and the study's kept-minus-flipped weighting is omitted. The four-versus-six comparison cannot be repeated there. **A5 and P4 — the character bound and quadratic hits are confirmed. Narrowing on the tie.** For an affine character, varying it along an unread change cancels the whole sum over that reading. Otherwise its magnitude is constant on the attainable read values. A nonconstant character fails R1 G0. With nonzero R2 normalizers the pair tables are the ordinary uniform marginals on its support. A character constant on its support can pass G0. This occurred on 5,118 of 23,758 enumerated parity W1k rows. All 3,343 emitted parity W3/W3m rows had zero signed total.

For a quadratic exponent, summing along the unread kernel first cancels fibres on which a change in the radical of its polar form reverses the sign. The surviving read words satisfy affine equations. On each surviving fibre a translation in the nondegenerate part gives a common magnitude and a quadratic sign. The resulting support can shrink. Independent enumeration of 307 affine sets × 1,024 ambient signs gave the same 512 presentations above 2 in both readings, each scoring 4, with 32 distinct restricted weightings up to global sign. All 16 words have eight ordinary completions each. Summation cancels precisely the words with $x_4\ne x_9$. Conditional on four reads, the two side parities are uniform on a coset. If either parity varies, at most half the words are 11 and its W2 sum is nonnegative. ## Was a native sign missed?

**Narrowing.** [The design](../README.md#predictions-frozen) says “Only a hit with W1k, W3, W3m or W4, whose signs come from the swap alone, would be a sign the model makes once the swap is given”. Let $H(x)$ be the distinctions holding the chosen value 1. On **every** assignment the set $H(x)\cap gH(x)$ is invariant under the lift. The sign of its induced permutation is exactly W1, since each fully occupied moved pair contributes one transposition. W1k restricts this same function to kept states. On those states reversing the held-value convention changes at most one overall sign. W4 needs an orientation of the two sides for a signed value, although reversing that orientation leaves its squared reading unchanged. The swap supplies an unordered pair of sides when there are two. Choosing the first side is extra. The W3 signs likewise assign opposite coefficients to two intrinsically defined subsets and zero to everything else.

There is also a sign using no preferred held value or ordering. The sign of the permutation on the configuration's own orbit is +1 if kept and −1 otherwise. It gives the same sign to both members of an exchanged pair. For this omitted sign I checked all **23,760** published parity lift rows on **6,910** stages. Neither reading hits an eligible placement. A separate exhaustion of 5,472 projected four-read cases at the observed fixed-state codimensions certified the rank test used in that search. An independently built ordinary parity growth with 256 states and 64 fixed states does give 4 with this sign under both readings if the reader pools and common-holder restriction are relaxed. The first-pass five-knot test also obtained 4 by multiplying the two possible pair signs over lift matchings. That sign depends only on whether both repeated holder sets have opposite values inside them. Its successful marks lie outside the frozen side pools. These constructions distinguish a well-defined sign from a law requiring it as a weighting. [Orbit-sign checks](natural_sign.json) · [Product of lift signs](lift_controls.json)

**A6 — confirmed for fixed support and magnitudes. Narrowing on attribution.** [The decision](../decision.md#position-and-next-directions) says “A6 closes value reads for every sign; R1 and R2 reward any sign”. In A6 each completion contributes its own squared weight. A phase disappears before summation. On each assignment a CHSH expression is +2 or −2, so averaging over any common positive weighting bounds its absolute value by 2. Changing zero weights changes the support and can change the table. Forgetting distinctions as a relation gives attainable values; counting or squaring supplies the weighting. The study establishes a need for a different read to see a phase and the existence of signed reads that work. Neither R1 nor R2 succeeds for every sign.

I reproduced the two post-run comparisons with their stated choices. On the ten-knot round-two parity stage from census index 26, **94/200** uniformly shuffled matchings of 11 pairs (seed 20261126) attained 4, and no single pair among 253 did better than 1. On the twelve-knot round-two not-all-equal stage from index 421, **156/231** products of two single-distinction signs exceeded 2 under R1, maximum 290/81, and **6/231** under R2, maximum 12/5. Both control searches allow all holder-disjoint distinctions as candidates. They establish that a swap is unnecessary for those examples. They do not establish a frequency over arbitrary signs or make a particular swap's weighting irrelevant. The lift, state law, support and reading can each change an outcome. In particular, writing W1k as a product of single-distinction signs on kept states retains a joint restriction to those states. [Rebuilt post-run controls](pairing_controls.json)

## Scope of the fixes and checks

- **Narrowing on the state cutoff.** [The results](../README.md#against-the-frozen-predictions) describe the 1,294 excluded round-two and ten round-one not-all-equal stages as “more than 2^22 states”. [The implementation](../sides.py#L178) tests whether more than 22 distinctions occur, before counting allowed states. All ten round-one exclusions and all 142 round-two exclusions of width 23 have fewer than $2^{22}$ allowed states. Examples are the four-knot start with two loose distinctions per knot at index 89 (876,906 after one growth) and the three-knot input at index 26 (1,254,582 after two). The missing signed searches are **not run**. The larger ring sector counts above fill only the state-count question.
- **Not a block on code range.** In [sides.py](../sides.py#L282) the exact expression selecting wider integers uses `> 1 << 30`. Four raw tables with two nonzero cells of exactly $2^{30}$ in the winning pattern can overflow the fixed-width squared CHSH numerator at $2^{63}$. Convert the four correlations to unrestricted integers before combining them. This did not affect the published hit replays or their verdict.
- **Narrowing on a proposed direction.** The [decision table](../decision.md#position-and-next-directions) says “only a three-reader test of changes could show a bounded excess”. The integer-correlation argument there applies to the stated two-reader value and change observables on the specified signed parity state. It gives no such lower bound on the number of readers for an enlarged choice of observables.
- **Not run.** There was no second from-scratch search for every possible table on all 7,075 starts. The corrected full hit counts use the stored hits. Mixed lifts were included in the 201-start first pass, not exhaustively in the full-population table search. The larger excluded not-all-equal table searches, partnered round-two tables, complete-record signalling, active setting changes and native probability laws were not tested. The original 60 random arithmetic controls and five unsigned controls were read; the independent four-read control, named replays and exact witness counts provide separate checks.
- **How checked.** The scripts in this folder use the repository interpreter, suppress bytecode writes and import no study module. Structural matching, assignment enumeration, binary ranks, signed transforms and direct integer marginal counts furnish separate checks. Runs were piloted at their actual settings and single-process numerical libraries were used. The all-start structural comparison took 28.45 seconds, the sample table replay 14.74 seconds at 461,324 KiB peak, and the full archive recount 4.51 seconds. One initial explosive-pattern terminology search surfaced the assumptions entry before that comparison was finished. Its implementation used predecessor sources; the named partnered replay is explicitly post-read. The main first-pass table reconstruction and the census and ring reconstructions were independent of that exposure.
