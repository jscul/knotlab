**Both setting births can be births a stated law makes. On one hand-built structure of 11 knots, a single round of
rule Q makes exactly two births, one for each observer, and reading which shared distinction each birth takes as the
setting gives a hidden excess of 20/9, the ceiling for this weighting. An independent implementation replicated every
number. The excess is still the weighting's, and the structure is wired by hand.** Rule Q: a one-parent birth takes its
parent's loose distinction and one distinction the parent shares. A hidden excess is a four-correlation score above 2
with both complete records unchanged by the far choice, every allowed word of a stage-1 structure counted equally.

## The structure

Alice: even(x1,x2,x3,c), nae(c,x2,s1), nae(c,x3,s2), even(x2,h1,h2), odd(x3,h1,h2).
Bob: even(y1,y2,y3,t), even(t,m1,m2), even(s1,m1,m2), even(y2,k1,k2), odd(y3,k1,k2), odd(s2,k1,k2).

Only x1 and y1 are loose, and no one knot meets both of their holders. Five distinctions (x2, x3, c, k1, k2) have
three holders.

## What happened

- **Round 1 makes the two setting births and nothing else.** One birth takes x1 and one of x2, x3, c; the other takes
  y1 and one of y2, y3, t. Each has one fresh distinction; the next round asks nothing more of either.
- **The excess.** With Alice's birth taking x2 or x3 and Bob's taking y2 or y3, each of the four stage-1 structures has
  144 allowed words, both complete records are hidden, and the largest score is exactly **20/9**. It is the same over
  all outcome functions of the record, over parities, and over functions of the three old values alone.
- **The third option does nothing.** The old knots force c to be the opposite of x1 and t the opposite of y1, so a
  birth that takes c or t counts every old word equally. The other eight pairs of options are hidden at 19/9 or 37/18
  because one of their settings is of that kind.
- **Hidden means the records.** Bob's choice changes the distribution of the two distinctions the sides share, s1 and
  s2, by 1/3. Alice's two not-all-equal knots hold them; nothing else of hers changes, and her four-value record does
  not show it. Alice's choice changes nothing that Bob's knots hold. The dependence runs one way.
- **The excess is the weighting's.** With the old words' distribution held fixed at that of any one choice, every
  score is exactly 2.
- **Leaving knots out.** While round 1 makes exactly the two births, no structure with one or two knots left out gives
  a hidden excess: one stays at exactly 2, and one passes 2 but changes Alice's record. If a third one-parent birth is
  allowed, leaving out even(y2,k1,k2) or odd(y3,k1,k2) gives 10 knots with a hidden excess of 55/27.
- **Signs.** Of the 512 assignments of even or odd to the nine parity knots, 128 give the hidden 20/9: exactly those
  that force x2 and x3 to differ and y2 and y3 to differ. The other five parity knots' signs do not matter.

## What this is about

One structure, built by hand. Supplied: the structure with its ties, which rule Q with not-all-equal newborns cannot
grow; the reading of the rule's open choice as a setting, each of the four choices being a separate structure; the
equal weighting of the allowed words of each; the complete record. The birth the rule makes is the knot the earlier
studies added by hand, so the number is the third-holder epic's. In the literature's terms this is a local model in
which the shared values' distribution depends on one observer's setting (measurement dependence), within its known
bounds.

**Verdict.** The setting births no longer have to be added. A law makes them, and the setting is a choice the law
leaves open. What is new is that statement and no new number. Everything else the excess needs is still supplied:
the structure and the weighting.
