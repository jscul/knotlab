## When the simpler tests stop

The mandatory-rule test uses every support whose projection is exactly not-all-equal. A support is mandatory when some forbidden word violates just its rule among that whole candidate family. If the mandatory rules together forbid every forbidden word, they give the only irredundant presentation. At ten distinctions the test succeeded on every class. The single-word test keeps a candidate when, for some forbidden word, the distinctions whose individual flips repair it are exactly the distinctions on that support. These repairs recover the whole state set for **1,650/1,659** ten-distinction classes, representing **587,043,991/588,843,271** named state sets. Of the nine classes requiring mandatory deletion, seven have a funded first step and two are already closed. ## Flipping one named distinction

For a distinction x, take every forbidden word that becomes allowed when only x changes. If x has one holder, that holder must contain x and must forbid every word on this list, since flipping x leaves every other physical rule as it was. In the entire census, this test gives exactly the actual holder on **5,981/5,981** loose distinctions and gives an empty list of candidate sole holders on **16,502/16,502** shared distinctions, counting one presentation of each type. At ten distinctions alone the counts are **4,250/4,250** and **12,340/12,340**. The two actual alternatives in the six-name case have all distinctions shared, and none can be loose in any legal named cover of those states. This is a bounded diagnostic for the proposed all-size lemma.
