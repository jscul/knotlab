# A lasting source with infinitely many separately continuing products

**Theorem.** A finite connected start under ordinary parity growth produces new finite packets at infinitely many sizes. At release every parent of every packet member belongs to one recognizable source. At every later size every parent of every member belongs to the preceding members of that particular packet. The packets and the continuing source separate by an increasing number of steps in the same growing end. The same construction works with not-all-equal. It uses a reading of the full current holdings, including spent knots. Seven knots and 25 distinctions suffice. These are the least counts in that order within the full identified class. A second construction makes the recognized source connected at each size and gives **every** source member a source child indefinitely.

An isolated packet continues to make the same cyclic pattern of parents. Recomputing ordinary growth with its actual loose counts changes the fresh counts in the very next increment. A single catalogue ring, even one with exponentially many births, cannot generate two disjoint lasting products on its one unrolled walk. These distinctions between readings and interventions are part of the [frozen question](preregistration.md).

## The physical start and the reading

First take two candidate cycles of lengths 5 and 6 with just one common point, the hub. Direct both cyclically. For each directed adjacency put a separate spent knot meeting its tail once and its head twice, using different distinctions. Give each point as many loose distinctions as its number of candidate neighbors. This realizes the two desired cycles. Each candidate pair has its own witness, there is no unheld triple, every demand is funded and there are no leftover distinctions. The hub, the two cycles, their orientations and the point $b$ halfway around the longer cycle are intrinsic. There are ten live knots, eleven spent witnesses, 33 shared distinctions and 22 loose distinctions. The same proof applies with equal multiplicity on each side of a witness if the reading returns all tied choices of orientation. A single individually oriented family in that variant needs a separate symmetry check.

For parity, deleting any one knot leaves every surviving spent witness attached to a live knot with loose distinctions, so all remaining knots connect to the exterior used in the parity cut test. Each declared support projects to its one full parity equation. Under not-all-equal, any prescribed allowed assignment at a live knot extends through each of its incident witnesses using the distinctions at the opposite end. All remaining live knots can take opposite values on two private loose distinctions. A prescribed allowed assignment at one spent witness also extends, since the other spent witnesses have disjoint supports. Thus each initial knot passes K1–K3 on its actual projection.

The identification theorem makes a knot of formation age $m-1$ exactly an orbit of a connected set of $m$ points on the unrolled incidence tree. Its parents are all single-point deletions leaving a connected set that contains an original live point, and its fresh distinctions count its one-point extensions. Here there are no pendant points or relay prerequisites. The unrolled incidence has minimum degree two. The finite subtree consisting of a cluster and its incident blocks has at least two departures into the rest of the tree, reaching different outside points. Hence every birth has at least two fresh distinctions, every later stage is funded and no sole parity close occurs. Under either law any prescribed choice of at most one loose input per current holder is free on these starts. At a subsequent layer each current holder has a second fresh distinction available to meet its own constraint with the older state fixed. This also gives the full local allowed relation at every new birth and conservativity on older states. ## Release and separation

Let $A,B$ be a full traversal of the shorter and longer cycles in their respective directions, both starting at the hub. A word records one point per step and omits the repeated final endpoint. For $k=1,2,\ldots$, repeat $A^kB$ in both directions. Its period is $p_k=5k+6$. Each turn at a hub continues without immediate retracing. The lift is a path through distinct successive blocks, so every finite window is a literal knot, and its two parents are **exactly** the windows with one end deleted. The marker occurs exactly once in a period. These $p_k$ positions give distinct phases at size at least $p_k$. A nontrivial translation would move the marker, and reversing a whole period would reverse an adjacency traversed in just one direction.

If a repeated block contains $b$, its two copies place $b$ at a displacement divisible by $p_k$. Its length is therefore at least $p_k$. Conversely, **every** window of length $2p_k$ consists of two identical blocks containing $b$. This proves simultaneous release of all $p_k$ phases at size $2p_k$, from still-accepted parents of size $2p_k-1$. At all later sizes these same $p_k$ moving windows have only their own two phase parents. Adjacent phases share one parent, with the phases taken cyclically. A single present reader recognizes the packet for all $k$. It inspects consecutive occurrences of $b$, reads the gap $p$, verifies a whole period and the interval word against $A^kB$, and requires at least $2p$ points. Other lengths and incomplete periods do not count as release.

There is quantitative physical separation even though the whole growing end remains connected. A $2p_k$-window in this packet contains a forbidden repeat and two markers separated by $p_k$. It cannot occur in a source word or in any $A^jB$ word with $j>k$, even read backwards. A same-layer step through a common parent replaces one point of a lifted cluster. Successive lifts along any route can be aligned through their actual common parent. After $d$ steps at least $m-d$ points of the starting $m$-point cluster remain. Intersecting two paths in the incidence tree gives a consecutive interval when their intersection contains points. The endpoints of a route from packet $k$ to the source or to packet $j>k$ therefore share at most $2p_k-1$ points in these lifts. Their growing-end separation is at least

$$
                    m-2p_k+1 .
$$

This holds at every common size after the respective releases. The ancestry claim uses all parents. The separation claim even allows routes through births outside the chosen families. Source sizes and product periods grow without a fixed bound. This gives no fixed-size emitter, fixed-size repeated copy, canonical unique reader, state-dependent action or physical elapsed time.

## Two stronger controls on the meaning of source

On the first frame the source as a whole is productive. An individual nearly repeated word can exhaust its two available forward path extensions. For a version in which **every** source member continues and the source members at a given size form one connected set under common-parent steps, take two directed primary cycles of lengths four and six sharing their hub. Add a separate directed four-cycle. Give each primary point one directed exit to one of two points on this last cycle, using different exits for the two classes of the primary bipartition. Realize every directed adjacency by its own one-at-tail, two-at-head exhausted witness as above, and give each live knot its candidate degree in loose distinctions. There are 13 live knots and 23 spent witnesses, hence 36 original knots and 115 distinctions.

The primary graph and the added cycle separately have no three-cycle. A mixed three-cycle would need two adjacent primary points with the same exit, or two exits at one primary point. Both are excluded. The original witnesses, funding and allowed-state checks therefore apply. The directed original graph has one terminal strongly connected part, the added cycle. The other part is the primary pair of cycles and again identifies $b$ as the antipode of the six-cycle. Use the same directed-interval source test. Once a path has gone through an exit, it stays inside the terminal cycle. A repeat containing both $b$ and a point on that cycle would have to return from the latter to the former to start its second copy. Every accepted primary-only interval can thus take its exit immediately and continue indefinitely as a source interval. Intervals already using an exit can also continue. At each fixed size, sliding a window forward along such an extension gives a route of source windows to the terminal-cycle phases. Those phases form a connected cycle. At size one use the original witnessed adjacencies for the same argument. All statements about releases, parentage and separation hold with $p_k=4k+6$.

## The seven-knot construction

Use original knots numbered 0 through 6 for the description. The ordinary meetings are $01,04,14,24,26,34,35,56$. Give only $01$ two shared distinctions. Give the seven knots respectively $3,0,3,3,3,2,2$ loose distinctions. There are 16 loose and nine shared distinctions, hence **25 distinctions**. Knot 1 is the only spent original, and it meets the two different live knots 0 and 4. Each other original has at least two private loose distinctions. Deleting any one knot leaves each surviving knot connected to a loose distinction and the parity exterior. A prescribed allowed not-all-equal row at a live knot can be completed at knot 1 with its contact at the other live neighbor, if needed. The other live knots use their own openings. A prescribed row at 1 extends as well. Each original star therefore has its full local allowed relation.

The witnessed sets of at least two live knots are
$$
  \{0,4\},\quad \{4,6\},\quad \{4,5\},\quad
  \{0,2,3\},\quad \{3,6\},\quad \{2,5\}.
$$
There are eight candidate pairs. Each has just one witness, the sole mutually adjacent triple $\{0,2,3\}$ has one common witness, and the openings fund exactly the pairs. The alternating incidence connects point 4 and block $\{0,2,3\}$ by three internally separate routes of lengths 3, 5 and 5. Omitting the witnesses that hold no pair, every role has degree at least two. The same all-size, positive-fresh cluster theorem therefore applies. The two words
$$
                    A=(4,0,2,5),\qquad B=(4,0,3,6)
$$
each return to 4 on their next step through a different witness from the one just used. They can be concatenated in any order without retracing. The complete accumulated physical incidence suffices to recover the original frame even with only $01$ doubled. At a positive complete boundary all loose holders are the newest births, each positively funded. None meet one another directly. Erase them simultaneously, delete distinctions that disappear with them, and restore their shared inputs to their remaining holders. This returns exactly the preceding complete boundary. At the original boundary two live holders do directly meet, for example 0 and 4. This gives a stopping test at the actual origin, without a date, under the stated complete-boundary promise. Once recovered, 4 is the sole original with four distinct neighbors, 1 is spent, and 0 is its other neighbor. The pair $M=\{2,3\}$ of live neighbors of 4 outside that first three-knot part is intrinsic. The remaining symmetry exchanges the two longer routes. The peeling claim applies to these frames and to complete boundaries of the specified growth.

Use the invariant source test that **at least one** $b\in M$ has no tandem repeat containing $b$ in either half. Choose $k\ge2$. Return both periodic walks with one long route used $k$ times and the other once, as an unordered pair of cycles of phases, or return their union as one invariant packet. In each returned cycle the minority route, identified on one full period, gives a uniquely determined marker in $M$. It occurs once per period $p_k=4(k+1)$. Just before release this marker witnesses membership in the source. At $2p_k$ every phase is a repeated period containing **both** markers in both halves. No choice witnesses source membership. All $p_k$ phases in each of the two cycles have their own two physical phase parents at every later size. The two cycles count the two marker identities differently in every length-$p_k$ interval. Reversal and translation preserve these counts, so their own same-layer separation is at least $m-p_k+1$. For different $k<j$, every length-$2p_k$ window contains a pair of occurrences of **each** marker exactly $p_k$ apart. In either larger-period cycle the minority marker occurs only at gaps that are multiples of $p_j$. This proves disjointness and the previous separation bound for the unordered outputs too. For $k=1$ the two route choices give one cycle up to a shift, with release at size 16. If the reader must single out one named-by-structure route, double $24$ as well. This breaks the symmetry at 26 distinctions, and the original single-marker rule applies directly.

## Withholding other births and restarting the packet

After release the prescribed packet births have no unprovided parents when replayed alone in their original fixed structure. Each has two later packet children. Delivering independent births in another parent-respecting order preserves these rosters and their inherited slots. An exhaustive fair delivery reaches each by induction on its finite parent depth. This replay retains the original cost assignment. A present-only run on reduced holdings recomputes demand. [Fixed-map delivery and ordinary serial growth](../ROUND-FREE-20261119-001/round_free.md)

For that intervention retain all $p=p_k$ current phase holders and their actual loose counts $h_i$. Retain their incoming distinction identities and the witness pairs on the phases. Each incoming witness now has precisely its two adjacent phase holders among its live neighbors. One lawful realization adds a cyclic chain of shared distinctions among those exhausted witnesses, keeping all phase holdings as specified. The complement of any star connects through the live holders to the parity exterior. Under not-all-equal, if a live holder is prescribed, its two incident witnesses can be completed using their other live contacts. If a spent witness is prescribed, every other spent witness can use its two live contacts. In either case all other live holders have two loose distinctions available. This construction specifies the boundary after withholding the other births.

On the first frame a full interval $W$ has $h(W)=2+2H(W)$, where $H(W)$ counts its occurrences of the hub. Indeed, the unrolled candidate graph is a tree with degrees two and four, and the number of one-point extensions of a finite path is $2+\sum_{x\in W}(\deg x-2)$. For adjacent phase holders $i,i+1$ let $U$ be their common parent in the full presentation. Their next full binary child costs $h_i+h_{i+1}-h(U)$. This follows from the same hub count or from the two-parent demand formula. In the standalone phase cycle the binary child costs $h_i+h_{i+1}-2$, since the common predecessor witnesses just this pair in the retained end. Every phase also makes $h_i-2$ unary births there. At release $h_i=2+4(k+1)$ and the increase in the cost of a designated binary child is $4(k+1)$ or $4(k+1)-2$, according as the point omitted from the common predecessor is ordinary or the hub. Thus even the designated costs differ immediately. Resetting every $h_i$ to two instead gives a bare cycle whose only outputs reproduce the cyclic two-parent pattern at cost two each. Under the standalone rule with the actual $h_i$, a surplus occurs only at holders with $h_i\ge3$. Each spare therefore gives a finite terminal point with no endless chain. The whole standalone presentation has **one** periodic walk with bounded finite attachments. The result below shows that the product itself cannot serve as another generator of independently persistent packets under this replay, even though its own phase pattern lasts.

## Why one decorated walk cannot do this

Consider one unrolled periodic walk of period $\ell$ in the identified class. Initially allow finite attachments repeated periodically along it, with a uniform finite bound per period and no endless chains. The backbone points are original live knots. This covers each walk of the [decorated ring catalogue](../RING-CORES-20261119-001/ring_cores.md), including a third point in a block between adjacent backbone points and its finite attachments.

Fix an observation size $q$. Every sufficiently large connected cluster contains an undecorated $q$-interval in **every** backbone phase. To see this, divide the cover into finite cells indexed by backbone position, assigning extra points of an intervening block to its left cell. Include all the finite offshoots belonging to that cell. If a cluster meets cells from $i$ through $j$, all intervening backbone knots strictly between the ends must be present. Each separates the blocks to its left and right in the incidence tree. If the largest cell has $C$ points, a cluster larger than $C(q+\ell+2)$ therefore contains at least $q+\ell-1$ consecutive backbone points, which suffices for every phase. Each retained $q$-interval is an **actual ancestor**. Extend a tree spanning that interval to a tree spanning the bigger connected cluster and delete leaves outside the interval one by one. Each deletion keeps a connected set with an original point, as the parent law requires.

Consider any packet that persists by the all-parent requirement to arbitrarily large sizes on this walk. At size $q$ after its launch it must already contain **every** one of these plain $q$-intervals, by backward application of that requirement. Two such lasting products must intersect at every common observation size. A bare walk shows the difference particularly directly. A proper run of $r$ consecutive phases has $r-1$ two-parent descendants next, then $r-2$, and reaches zero after exactly $r$ increments. An inclusion test requiring just one parent would instead let that run spread.

More generally, take a **finite** identified start in which each component of the point/witness incidence has at most one independent cycle. There is a finite size-independent upper bound on the number of disjoint lasting packets. Count one anchor type per cyclic component and one per orbit of an endless chain, including the two chains of each finite piece that relays. To prove the bound use a common observation size $q$ beyond all finite relay tops. Deep enough use of a chain forces the $q$-point prefix from the associated original knot and pendant, since a valid cluster contains an original. In a relay use its full prerequisite and relay point followed by the appropriate chain prefix to size $q$. If none of these finitely many chain prefixes occurs, all chain depths are bounded at this fixed $q$. Each cyclic part now has bounded finite cells, so a sufficiently large cluster in it must contain the chosen plain $q$-interval. A part with no cycle is then finite at that bound. Every indefinitely continuing packet must contain at least one of the listed actual anchors at size $q$. Disjoint packets cannot use the same anchor. In particular, an odd catalogue ring can support at most one such packet and an even catalogue ring at most two, one per unrolled walk. This gives a lower bound specifically for the identified class. Infinitely many disjoint lasting products require some ordinary point/witness component with at least two independent cycles. Its incidence has girth at least eight. Two cycles that meet in at most one role already have at least fifteen roles in their union. Otherwise take a cycle and a route between two of its points, internally outside it. This gives three internally disjoint routes between two roles. Every pair of route lengths sums to at least eight, so the three lengths sum to at least twelve and use at least eleven roles. Each original physical knot supplies at most one point role and at most one witness role. At least six original knots are therefore necessary **inside the identification class**.

## Sharp minimum in the identified class

Write $L$ for the live originals. The incidence on their point roles and the witness roles of those same originals is the alternating double cover of the simple ordinary graph on $L$. Each spent original adds one witness on its live neighbors. Witnesses on fewer than two live knots change no cycle. Parallel ordinary meetings affect the number of distinctions and the source lawfulness, but do not alter this incidence.

A component with two independent cycles needs **at least five point roles**. Two cycles meeting in at most one role already have at least seven point roles. For three separate routes with endpoints in the same role class, their lengths are $2a,2b,2c$. Girth eight forces $a+b+c\ge6$, and each role class has at least $a+b+c-1$ members. For endpoints in opposite classes the lengths are $2a+1,2b+1,2c+1$. Every pair of the half-lengths sums to at least three, so each role class has at least $1+a+b+c\ge6$ members.

The ordinary graph induced on $L$ contains no simple cycle of length 3, 4 or 6. A three-cycle would yield a six-cycle of point and witness roles; the other two directly yield four- and six-cycles there. On at most seven vertices a simple graph with these exclusions has at most one cycle per component. Two cycles meeting in at most one vertex would need at least nine vertices. Otherwise use three separate routes of lengths $a\le b\le c$ as above. Seven vertices would require $a+b+c\le8$. If $a=1$, then $b,c\ge4$; if $a\ge3$ the sum is at least nine. The only remaining possibility is $2,3,3$, whose long routes make a forbidden six-cycle.

If all originals are live, doubling the roles of an ordinary tree or single-cycle component gives pieces with at most one cycle each. One can see this by first removing leaves, then alternating along the one remaining cycle. This rules out all-live starts through seven knots. With five live knots their graph is a forest or a five-cycle. In a forest the double cover consists of copies of its ordinary trees. Two point roles in the same component are joined by an even route of length at most four. A spent witness cannot meet both, as this would create an incidence cycle of length at most six. In the five-cycle case the cover is a ten-cycle; again every two point roles have distance at most four. One added spent witness therefore cannot create a second cycle in either case. This deals with every six-knot start.

For seven original knots and five live, two spent witnesses remain. In the five-cycle case neither can join two of its live points. In the forest case, shrink each tree component to one bookkeeping vertex. Each spent witness touches each such component at most once, as just proved. Two independent cycles would require at least three old tree components touching **both** spent witnesses. Let $d_i$ be the distance between the two entry point roles in each of three such trees, allowing zero. The three routes between the spent witnesses have lengths $d_i+2$, so $d_i+d_j\ge4$ for each pair and $\sum_i d_i\ge6$. Their old paths use at least $\sum_i(1+d_i/2)\ge6$ different live point roles in the separate trees. Only five exist.

The remaining case has six live originals and one spent. If their ordinary graph is a forest, two point roles in one copy still have even distance at most four, so the spent witness creates no cycle. Otherwise there is one five-cycle and a sixth live knot $x$ that is isolated or joined as a leaf to a cycle knot $a$. The ten-cycle on the five old point roles admits no two live neighbors at the new witness. An isolated $x$ cannot help it create another cycle. For a leaf $x$, its point role meets the witness role of $a$ on the ten-cycle. The latter's distances to its five *point* roles are $1,1,3,3,5$, with the opposite role being the point role of $a$ itself. Thus the only possible new witness pair at old distance at least six is $\{x,a\}$. This forces exactly the seven-knot shape exhibited above, up to renaming. Its eight distinct ordinary meetings must use at least nine shared distinctions, since the spent knot has only two neighbors and needs three places. The eight candidate pairs also require at least sixteen loose distinctions. This proves the lexicographic minimum of **seven knots and 25 distinctions** without a census.

The distinction bound is conditional on using seven knots. An eight-knot, 24-distinction control makes this explicit. Take five live knots forming the ordinary path $a,b,c,d,e$, with openings $2,3,3,2,2$. Add distinct spent witnesses on $\{c,d\},\{b,a\},\{b,e\}$. Give the first two spent witnesses one shared distinction with each other, and double the last witness's meeting with $b$. This makes twelve shared distinctions. The six candidate pairs are all the pairs between $\{b,c\}$ and $\{a,d,e\}$, each separately witnessed. There is no unheld triple. Each spent knot remains attached to a live knot when any other knot is deleted. For not-all-equal, contacts at unpinned opposite live ends complete any spent row touched by a prescribed live row. When a spent row is prescribed, each other spent knot has two independently assignable contacts at live ends. The live knots all have two private loose distinctions available when their row was not prescribed. Each initial support is lawful. The direct meetings of live knots detect the source boundary on peeling, and the doubled meeting fixes the second holder on the path. The two point words $(b,a,c,d)$ and $(b,a,c,e)$ give the same construction with $e$ as marker. Direct projection and the K1–K3 checker also pass on each of these eight stars under both laws.

Without the identification promise, one or two connected original knots cannot make an unbounded number of lasting products either. No two distinct live knots then have a common holder. With at least two loose distinctions the identified class applies immediately, with no cycle in the candidate map. Zero loose distinctions leave nothing to form. With just one, parity closes on its single birth and not-all-equal gives a unary birth with two fresh distinctions. Its next two unary children each have two loose distinctions and share just the one parent, giving the identified two-ended continuation. A lone original knot of ordinary arity at least three necessarily has at least three loose distinctions. This gives an unrestricted lower bound of three original knots. The unrestricted minimum and the least distinction count with arbitrarily many original knots remain open.
