**What goes wrong in the five failures.** Number the chain's 17 distinctions from 0 through 16, with knot `i` using `(2i, 2i+1, 2i+2)`. All five checked failures have an extra law on (0,1,5) in this numbering. If A₀ and B₀ both agree, their values on (3,4,5) and (0,1,10) must both oppose distinction 2. The extra law would then have all three values equal. Thus 110 as well as 111 is missing on (A₀,B₀,C₀), and the pair (A₀,B₀) loses 11. The five occurrences represent four chain identities on two starts, the four-knot census entry 90 and five-knot entry 2030. Exactly the same rule explains all **33** lower failures among **10,696** chain occurrences. On five-knot census entry 3609 a *different* extra condition preserves the lower triples but makes some single-law removals insufficient.
