# Lone lines at two and three places

Status: Proved

Ticket `TRIPLE-SHIELD-20261023-001B`, 2026-10-23.

**What it uses:**
- from `TRIPLE-TWIST-20261023-001A-P001`:
  - the twisted three-place model;
  - complement closure;
  - doubly allowed assignments and fitting twists;
  - Theorem 5 (minimal contradictions wind along classes on which the twist differs from every fitting twist);
- from `NAE-INVARIANT-20261022-001A-P001` (with Aspvall, Plass and Tarjan): a two-literal world contradicts exactly when a strong
  component of its implication graph contains both literals of one variable;
- from `ODD-LOOP-20261023-001B-P001` and `LOOP-SHIELD-20261023-001C-P001`:
  - Lemma B (a swap-invariant strong component contains both literals of every vertex it meets and both arcs of every edge
    between them);
  - Theorem 1 (the wall: |L_q(c)| = |L_{q+PD(c)}(c)| for fair independent orientations);
- from `TRIPLE-SHIELD-20261023-001A-E001` (frozen, exhaustive): the counts in Proposition 6.

## Standard statement

**Setting.**
- **Worlds.** A world is a tiling placement R on the L × M triangular torus, a twist q and a configuration x of the visible bits,
  as in `TRIPLE-TWIST-20261023-001A-P001`. A twist fits when a doubly allowed assignment exists.
- **Cylinders.** For a deck class c, the cylinder along c is the cover of the torus whose deck group is generated by c's period
  (c₁L, c₂M), with the world pulled back. It contradicts when it has no hidden assignment.
- **Lone lines.** For the three classes (1, 0), (0, 1) and (1, 1), lone_c(q) is the set of configurations for which the
  cylinder along c contradicts and the other two do not.
- **Orientation worlds.** These are placements 63 and 119 (a clause of two literals of the same sign) and 187 and 207 (an
  implication).
  - Their single clause sits on a pair of corners joined by an edge of the square lattice inside the triangular one.
  - Each square-lattice edge carries exactly one clause, with its own fair bit.
  - Their effective twist is q′ = q for 187 and 207, and q′ = q + (L, M) mod 2 for 63 and 119.
- **Line events.** L_{q′}(c) is the line event of `LOOP-SHIELD-20261023-001C-P001`, taken in the implication graph: every
  cluster of cyclic edges has periods in ℤc, and some period is nonzero. It is defined when q′(c) is odd and is empty
  otherwise.

**Theorem 1 (cylinders and fitting twists).** If q_f fits and q − q_f is even on c, then every configuration has a hidden
assignment on the cylinder along c. Hence:
- no world contradicts in a fitting twist;
- the cylinder along c contradicts only in twists that are odd on c relative to every fitting twist.

**Lemma 2 (parity).** In an orientation world, follow a walk in the implication graph whose projection is a closed walk of class
γ. The value of the literal changes by q′(γ) mod 2.

**Lemma 3 (rank-two contradictions are seen along every odd class).**
- **Hypothesis.** A strong component S of an orientation world's implication graph contains both literals of a variable, and
  the period lattice of its projection is ℤ².
- **Conclusion.** For every class γ with q′(γ) odd, S contains walks from one literal of that variable to the other, and back,
  whose projections are closed walks of class γ. So every cylinder along a q′-odd class contradicts.

**Lemma 4 (clusters run parallel).** On the torus, clusters with nonzero periods all have their periods in one line ℤδ, δ
primitive. The exception is a cluster whose period lattice has rank two; then every other cluster has period 0.

**Theorem 5 (lone lines are line events).** In the four orientation worlds, lone_c(q) = L_{q′}(c) for every twist q and every
class c of the three. Consequently, for every dual pair {q, q + PD(c)}, |lone_c(q)| = |lone_c(q + PD(c))|.

**Proposition 6 (the identity fails wherever one coin decides three places).** Consider every dual pair of a three-place or
shared-coin placement that has a nonzero count and that no symmetry map relates. On the fresh tori 3 × 4 (all 54 tiling
placements) and 4 × 4 (one placement per orbit of the maps), each such pair has unequal lone counts.
- **How many.** 36 three-place pairs and 18 shared-coin pairs. The 4 × 3 torus repeats 3 × 4 with the axes swapped.
- **Examples on 4 × 4.**
  - Three-place: 640592 against 4736 (placement 54), 75916416 against 768848 (62), 18117008 against 27271632 (111).
  - Shared-coin: 118368 against 129184 (47), 9205568 against 8957504 (55).
- **No bijection.** The counts are exact. So on these tori no bijection carries the lone lines of one twist onto those of the
  other, and in particular there is no wall. The identity of Theorem 5 is not a law of these worlds.
- **The seen torus agrees.** On 3 × 3, 22 three-place and 4 shared-coin pairs are all unequal.

**Remark 7 (the boundary is how many places one coin reaches).** Theorem 5 and Proposition 6 split the tiling placements by how
many hidden places a single fair coin decides.
- **Orientation worlds.** Each coin decides one clause on two places, independently across edges. The identity holds on every
  torus.
- **Shared-coin worlds.** Every clause has two literals, but one coin decides two or three clauses of a triangle at once: a
  contact on three places. The identity fails, by factors up to 2.
- **Three-place worlds.** One coin decides a three-place relation. The identity fails, by factors from 1.08 to 2114 on the
  fresh tori.

So the identity separates contacts whose coin reaches two places from contacts whose coin reaches three. Clause width is not the
dividing line.

**Why the two-literal proof stops there.**
- **What the wall needs.** The wall of `LOOP-SHIELD-20261023-001C-P001` flips the coins of edges next to the loop, edge by edge.
  The seam carrying the dual twist then slides without creating or destroying a directed cycle. That needs each coin to touch
  exactly the two ends of one edge.
- **What a three-place coin does instead.** It couples a wall edge to an edge off the wall (shared coins), or has no single edge
  to flip (three places).
- **What a seam does.** A seam crossing a triangle changes the gauge of one or two of its corners, which no coin flip
  reproduces.

These are reasons the proof does not transfer, not a proof that nothing else could relate the twists. On the tested tori the
exact counts settle that.

## Nearest known result

- **Theorem 1.** A covering-space and gauge argument. It is the cylinder form of `TRIPLE-TWIST-20261023-001A-P001`, Theorem 5.
  Low folklore risk as stated; the technique is standard.
- **Lemma 3.** It combines four classical facts:
  - the skew symmetry of implication graphs (Aspvall, Plass and Tarjan 1979);
  - directed ear decompositions of strongly connected digraphs (for example Bang-Jensen and Gutin, *Digraphs*, Theorem 5.3.2),
    whose ear cycles form an integer basis of the cycle lattice;
  - a cone argument;
  - the purity of period lattices of subgraphs of the torus.

  The conclusion about cylinders is new to the program. Moderate folklore risk.
- **Theorem 5.** New to the program. It is what makes the cylinder count of A comparable to the shielding theorem.
- **Proposition 6.** A recorded exact computation, frozen and independently checked. The reading in Remark 7 (the reach of one
  coin) is the project's.

## Proof

### Theorem 1

- **The two twists differ by a coboundary on the cylinder.**
  - Let h_f be doubly allowed in twist q_f on the torus, and lift it to the cylinder along c.
  - On each lifted triangle T, corner k carries the gauge t_q(T, k) = q · deck(corner k) relative to the first corner.
  - The difference d(T, k) = t_q(T, k) − t_{q_f}(T, k) is the corner form of a cocycle whose value on a closed walk of class γ
    is (q − q_f)(γ).
  - Closed walks on the cylinder have classes in ℤc, where this value is even by hypothesis. So the cocycle is a coboundary:
    there is a function G on the cylinder's vertices with d(T, k) = G(v_k) + G(v_0) on every triangle (built along a spanning
    tree; consistent on every closed walk).
- **The shifted assignment is doubly allowed.** For h = h_f + G, the q-gauged pattern of T is
  (h_f(v_k) + t_{q_f}(T, k) + G(v_0))_k. This is the q_f-pattern of h_f, complemented when G(v_0) = 1. It lies in R ∩ R̄,
  which is closed under complement. So h is allowed on every triangle for both values of x.
- **On the torus.** For a fitting q, h_f itself does this. ∎

### Lemma 2

**The clause of a triangle.**
- In an orientation placement each relation (R or its complement) forbids exactly two patterns. They agree on two dependent
  corners and differ on the third.
- So it is the clause "not (h_u + t_u = β₁ and h_w + t_w = β₂)" on the dependent corners u, w.
  - The constants β₁, β₂ are equal for 63 and 119 (a clause of two literals of the same sign).
  - They differ for 187 and 207 (an implication).

**One step of a walk.**
- The implication arcs go from (h_u = a) to (h_w = 1 − b), and from (h_w = b) to (h_u = 1 − a), where a = β₁ + t_u and
  b = β₂ + t_w.
- Along either arc the value changes by 1 + β₁ + β₂ + t_u + t_w. That is t_u + t_w for an implication, and 1 + t_u + t_w for
  a clause of the same sign.

**Around a closed walk.**
- The sum of t_u + t_w is q(γ), since the gauges come from deck offsets.
- On the L × M square lattice a closed walk of class γ has length γ₁L + γ₂M mod 2.
- So the total change is q′(γ). ∎

### Lemma 4

- **Pure lattices.** The period lattice of a connected subgraph of the torus is 0, ℤδ with δ primitive, or ℤ². The cokernel of
  H₁ of a regular neighbourhood in H₁ of the torus is torsion-free, by Lefschetz duality.
- **Simple cycles.** The lattice is generated by the classes of simple cycles (fundamental cycles). These are simple closed
  curves, so their classes are 0 or primitive.
- **Disjoint clusters.** Clusters are vertex-disjoint, hence disjoint as subsets of the surface. Disjoint simple closed curves
  have intersection number 0, and on the torus that is the determinant. So their classes are parallel.
- **The rank-two case.** A cluster of rank two contains simple cycles of two independent classes. A disjoint simple cycle must be
  parallel to both, so it has class 0. ∎

### Lemma 3

Write p and p̄ for the two literals (h_v = 0 and h_v = 1) in S, P for the projection of S, and G[P] for the graph induced on its
vertices.

**(a) Skew symmetry.** Negation τ reverses arcs: ℓ → m is an arc exactly when τm → τℓ is.
- So τS is a strong component, and τS = S because S contains p and p̄ = τp.
- A walk W in S from ℓ to m gives the reversed walk τW^rev from τm to τℓ. Its projection is W's, traversed backwards, so its
  class is −[W].

**(b) What S contains.** S contains both literals of every vertex of P, by (a), and both arcs of every edge of G[P]. Each such
arc joins two literals of S, and an arc between two vertices of one strong component lies in it (Lemma B).

**(c) The monoid E.**
- Let E be the set of classes of closed walks in S from p back to p. It is a monoid, contained in ker q′ by Lemma 2.
- Take a directed closed walk C anywhere in S, with walks A from p to its start and B back. Then A Cⁿ B gives classes in E for
  every n ≥ 0.
- So [C] = [ACB] − [AB] lies in E − E, and [C] lies in the closed cone K generated by E.

**(d) E generates ker q′.**
- The underlying graph of S is a connected double cover of G[P], by (b).
- Its closed walks at p project onto the closed walks of G[P] of even q′-class. Every γ in ker q′ occurs, because P has period
  lattice ℤ² and a walk of even class lifts closed.
- A strongly connected digraph has a directed ear decomposition. Close each ear by a directed path through the earlier ears.
  - The resulting directed cycles each contain a private edge, the first edge of their ear, that no earlier cycle uses.
  - The non-private edges form a spanning tree.
  - So these cycles form an integer basis of the cycle lattice.
- So every class in ker q′ is an integer combination of classes of directed cycles. By (c) these lie in E − E, so the group
  generated by E is ker q′.

**(e) E is a group.**
- **Its cone is the whole plane.** By (a), each closed walk at p reverses to a closed walk at p̄ with the negated class.
  Conjugating it to p as in (c) puts −e in K for every e in E. So K contains E, −E and, by (d), a spanning set: K = ℝ².
- **A monoid with that cone is a group.** For e in E, −e is a nonnegative rational combination of finitely many elements of E.
  Clearing denominators gives −Ne = Σ mᵢeᵢ, so −e = (N − 1)e + Σ mᵢeᵢ lies in E.
- **So E is the whole kernel.** E is a group, and by (d) it equals ker q′.

**(f) Walks of every odd class.**
- The set O of classes of walks in S from p to p̄ is nonempty and q′-odd (Lemma 2).
- Appending closed walks at p̄, whose classes form −E = E by (a), gives O ⊇ o + ker q′: every q′-odd class. The same holds for
  walks from p̄ to p.
- So for each q′-odd γ, both walks exist with projected class γ. On the cylinder along γ they join the two literals of one lifted
  vertex in both directions, so the lifted world contradicts. ∎

### Theorem 5

**(⊆) A line event is a lone line.** Let x be in L_{q′}(c), with q′(c) odd.
- **The cylinder along c contradicts.** By Lemma B there is a strong component S* containing both literals of a vertex. Its
  walks between them project to closed walks in one cluster, with classes in ℤc. These walks close on the cylinder along c, so
  that cylinder contradicts.
- **The other two cylinders do not.** A contradiction on the cylinder along another class c″ would need such walks with
  q′-odd classes in ℤc″. Their cluster's periods lie in ℤc, and ℤc ∩ ℤc″ = 0, which is even.

So x is in lone_c(q).

**(⊇) A lone line is a line event.** Let x be in lone_c(q).
- **The torus contradicts.** A torus assignment would lift to every cylinder.
- **No contradicting cluster has rank two.**
  - Then q′ ≠ 0, and a nonzero form on (ℤ/2)² is odd on exactly two of the three nonzero classes.
  - If a contradicting component had a cluster of rank two, Lemma 3 would make both of those cylinders contradict, which lone_c
    excludes.
- **The cluster's class is c.**
  - So contradicting clusters have rank one.
  - No other cluster has rank two either. By Lemma 4 that would give every other cluster period 0, including a contradicting
    one, whose periods are nonzero.
  - So by Lemma 4 all clusters with nonzero periods lie along one primitive δ.
  - The contradiction on the cylinder along c comes from walks with classes in ℤc inside such a cluster, so δ = ±c.

Hence every cluster has periods in ℤc and one is nonzero: x is in L_{q′}(c).

**The counts.**
- **Both members of a dual pair are odd on c.** The fitting twist of an orientation world has q′_f = 0: it is 0 for 187 and
  207, and (L, M) mod 2 for 63 and 119 (the doubly allowed patterns are constant, respectively alternating). Both members of a
  dual pair are odd on c relative to it, so q′(c) is odd for both.
- **The identity.** |lone_c(q)| = |L_{q′}(c)| = |L_{q′+PD(c)}(c)| = |lone_c(q + PD(c))|. The middle equality is
  `LOOP-SHIELD-20261023-001C-P001`, Theorem 1, on the square torus.
- **Why that theorem applies.** Its hypothesis holds: one fair independent bit per square-lattice edge.
  - The implication graph of 187 or 207 is the twisted double cover D(x, t_{q′}) of that proof.
  - For 63 and 119 it becomes D(x, t_{q′}) after the flip of the vertices with i + j odd. That flip is the map of
    `TRIPLE-SHIELD-20261023-001A-E001` that turns a same-sign clause into an implication and adds (L, M) to the twist. ∎

## Checks

`check.py` uses the standard library only (seed 20261023). Every result below was obtained:

- **Theorem 1 on the 3 × 3, 3 × 4 and 4 × 3 tori.** There are 210 fitting (placement, torus, twist) cases among the 54 tiling
  placements.
  - For all 1260 (class, twist) cases with an even difference, the gauge G exists on a strip of 12 periods, and h_f + G is
    doubly allowed on every strip triangle.
  - For all 1260 cases with an odd difference, no gauge exists.
- **Theorem 5 and Lemma 3 on the orientation worlds.** 1091 configurations of placements 63, 119, 187 and 207 on the four tori, in every
  twist, chosen at random and by the cluster computation.
  - On the 506 that contradict, every cylinder is certified by the check's own two-literal solver (a strip of 24 periods
    without an assignment, or a cyclic cover of at most 16 sheets with one); none is undetermined.
  - lone_c equals L(c) in all 1518 class comparisons.
  - All 316 rank-two contradictions make exactly two cylinders contradict.
- **Exhaustive on 3 × 3.** For placements 63 and 207 in all four twists, the number of configurations in L(c) equals the
  lone-line count recorded by the frozen experiment, in every class.
- **Controls, all rejected.**
  - The cylinder event without the lone condition differs from L(c) on 632 comparisons.
  - A rank-one contradiction leaves a cylinder along an odd class free on 190 configurations.
  - A twist odd on c admits no gauge (1260 cases).

## What this does not show

- That the identity fails for every three-place world on every torus. It fails on the tested tori, which is enough to show it is
  not a law there. Larger tori could only be sampled.
- Anything about other triangulations, or about worlds where one coin reaches three places in other ways.
- That no other exact relation between three-place twists exists: only the dual pairs of lone lines along three classes are
  tested.
- Anything about space or physics. Lattices and twists are declared.
