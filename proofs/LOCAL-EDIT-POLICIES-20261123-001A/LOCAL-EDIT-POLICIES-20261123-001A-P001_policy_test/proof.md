# Testing every instruction that uses the local pre-edit record

**Statement.** In a finite experiment, equality of the complete local record for every admissible local policy is equivalent to equality of the receiver's conditional record laws on each supported pair of pre-edit observations. This gives a Bell bound for local readings of old visible values gated by a common visible birth. For the designated old-bit reading, a sender can turn an adjacent correlation difference into a message using just its own old bit. Concealing which edit will fund the round does not conceal this message.

**Prior result.** A common distribution of four local potential answers obeys the Bell inequalities. The preceding ordinary-growth study found a message when each controller could read its own two readiness values before choosing an address. The present argument permits arbitrary correlations between hidden readiness, both old marks and both observations. Its tests use information each party actually possesses. ## 1. Which distributions every policy tests

Let \(I=i,J=j\) be the complete observations available at the two controllers before the attempted round. Include any correlated preparation information or coin already available for choosing an action. Let \(w_{ij}\) be the common pre-edit distribution. It precedes and is independent of the input messages. Let \(x\in X(i),y\in Y(j)\) be the locally feasible actions with each menu nonempty. Use \(K_{ij}^{xy}\) for the conditional probability law of the pair of complete post-edit records at those inputs and old observations. In particular a local post-record includes its own old observation, its own chosen action, all later values and holdings within its declared access, and a visible gain or stall when the experiment makes it available. Marginals are denoted \(K_{ij,A}^{xy},K_{ij,B}^{xy}\).

The consistency required here is physical. On a prepared case with policy choices \(x=\alpha(i),y=\beta(j)\), use precisely the intervention that the same chosen addresses would perform on that case. The message label itself makes no further change. Thus the tables for these policies sum \(w_{ij}K_{ij}^{\alpha(i)\beta(j)}\). Full-record privacy means that changing the far policy leaves the receiver's *whole* distribution unchanged, with its own policy fixed. If the receiver can use more information after growth than before it, place it in the post-record. The receiver's old observation remains available for decoding.

**Conditional test.** There is no signal in either direction for any locally informed policies if and only if, at every \((i,j)\) with \(w_{ij}>0\), both conditions hold:

\[
K_{ij,A}^{xy}=K_{ij,A}^{xy'}\quad(x\in X(i),\ y,y'\in Y(j)),\qquad
K_{ij,B}^{xy}=K_{ij,B}^{x'y}\quad(y\in Y(j),\ x,x'\in X(i)).
\]

For necessity fix \(i,j,x,y,y'\). Let Alice use \(x\) at \(i\). Let Bob's two policies agree everywhere except at \(j\), where they choose \(y\) and \(y'\). Alice can test any event consisting of her *own* old observation \(i\) and an event \(D\) in her subsequent record. Its difference in probability is exactly

\[
w_{ij}\big[K_{ij,A}^{xy}(D)-K_{ij,A}^{xy'}(D)\big].
\]

It must vanish. Interchange the parties for the other condition. There is no joint observation of \((i,j)\) or rejection of trials. Bob alone recognizes \(j\), and Alice's event is defined on every trial. A single binary message choosing two policies differing on just **one** sender-observation class suffices for any failure. When the menu is common at all prestates a fixed receiver address suffices. More generally choose any locally feasible default at other observations. For sufficiency apply the exhibited equalities term by term in the sum over \(i,j\), including the actions selected by an adaptive receiver. An independent randomized instruction is a mixture of deterministic policies. Conditioning on the random seed proves the same result even when the receiver remembers its own coins. An extra signal about the preparation available before editing belongs in the corresponding old observation.

For two sender addresses there is an exact finite maximization. Put \(D_{xij}(r)=w_{ij}[K_{ij,A}^{x1}(r)-K_{ij,A}^{x0}(r)]\), with \(r\) the receiver's post-record beyond its stated \(i,x\). Then the best sender-to-receiver total variation, optimizing the receiver's locally feasible choice too, is

\[
\varepsilon_{B\to A}=\max_{e_j\in\{-1,1\}}\ \frac12\sum_i\max_{x\in X(i)}\sum_r\left|\sum_j e_jD_{xij}(r)\right|.
\]

Two policies differ by signs in \(\{-1,0,1\}\) at the sender. Holding the receiver policy fixed, total variation is convex in each such coefficient, so a maximum occurs at signs \(\pm1\). The receiver knows \(i,x\), so its row contributions are disjoint and its best action can be selected separately for each \(i\). For a fixed receiver address remove its maximization over \(x\). An address pair unavailable at \(j\) contributes zero there. For more actions use a pair of feasible sender addresses per \(j\). The conditional test remains unchanged. If the receiver's only new observation is a binary flag with chance \(h_{xy}(i,j)\), the half-sum over its two outcomes reduces to the single absolute value with \(D_{xij}=w_{ij}(h_{x1}-h_{x0})\).

## 2. When common funding gives a common response model

Suppose each complete receiver record contains the same binary flag \(H\). Write \(h_{xy}(i,j)=P(H=1\mid i,j,x,y)\). Ordinary funding may be determined by hidden, arbitrarily dependent readiness values. The conditional test implies first independence from \(y\), then from \(x\), at each supported \((i,j)\). There is one \(h(i,j)\) for all its feasible pairs. The entire conditional output law at \((i,j,H)\), whenever that branch has positive mass, must also have local marginals independent of the far input. Indeed equality of the full conditional marginal including \(H\) permits restriction to that flag value and division by its common positive mass.

The flag can still change on each actual preparation. For example let one unobserved fair bit \(c\) determine whether Bob's chosen address equals \(c\), with Alice always ready. Both controllers have constant old records, and both see just their own action and \(H=\mathbf1\{y=c\}\). Half the cases fund at either address, and every case changes its flag on switching addresses. Every policy on the given information is non-signalling. Handing Alice the old value \(c\) after the round would let her decode \(y\) from \((c,H)\), although the flag alone would still have its fair law. Equality of the conditional *flag* laws by itself need not give equality of full records.

For outputs determined by the local pre-record, own address and \(H\), draw \((i,j)\) once and draw one Bernoulli variable with probability \(h(i,j)\). Use that same flag for all four potential local responses. This reproduces every pair of measured answers, including stalled trials. The construction also works if, at each \((i,j,H)\), the two post-record generators have a common local model using further randomness whose distribution at that stratum is independent of the settings. Draw that randomness once too. On one potential list of four signs the signed CHSH expression is \(A_0(B_0+B_1)+A_1(B_0-B_1)=\pm2\). The signed and all eight oriented bounds survive averaging. This proves that all-policy privacy of the common flag excludes a Bell excess from the two old visible marks and any postprocessing of the stated kind. A Bell excess compatible with all-policy privacy needs a positive conditional branch with no such joint local response model if the only remaining effects are in the reported values.

Private fresh coins drawn from the locally known inputs and the visible flag are covered. Fresh values constrained by formerly unseen old distinctions can disclose those distinctions or their correlations. They need their actual conditional law. The same observation criterion applies to a parity-closing event, but a Bell deduction from that event as the sole flag requires it to be locally readable at both sides and requires the stated post-record hypothesis. A private closure predicate or a change of the ordinary proposal roster does not itself give those premises.

## 3. A quantitative message using the old marked bits

Let \(u(i),v(j)\) be *the same* two visible old bits at all addresses. The reported bits are \(H u,H v\), with zero on every stall. Use \(a_i=(-1)^{u(i)}, b_j=(-1)^{v(j)}\). Since a stalled pair contributes correlation \(+1\),

\[
 E_{xy}=1+\sum_{ij} w_{ij}(a_i b_j-1)h_{xy}(i,j).
\]

Fix Alice's address \(x\) and write \(d_{ij}=w_{ij}(h_{x1}-h_{x0})\). Bob's two *constant* addresses change the probability that Alice sees a gain by \(L=\sum d_{ij}\), in address order one minus zero. Bob can also send a freely chosen message \(M\) by choosing address \(v(j)\mathbin\oplus M\). Alice tests the single bit \(H\mathbin\oplus u(i)\). On a given \((i,j)\) the address order is reversed when \(v=1\) and the event difference is reversed again when \(u=1\). The signed probability change for this decoder is \(C=\sum a_i b_j d_{ij}\). Consequently

\[
 E_{x1}-E_{x0}=C-L.
\]

This gives two simple, explicit messages. When the fixed addresses preserve the gain probability, the mark-based message and decoder attain TV **exactly** \(|E_{x1}-E_{x0}|\) on the decoded bit. No readiness value is used. For arbitrary gain probabilities at least one of these messages attains half the adjacent difference in absolute value. The whole receiver record has TV at least that of either decoded bit. Under the mark-based message itself, the signed decoder change also equals the change in the gain probability minus twice the change in the probability of reporting one, because \(H\oplus u=H+u-2Hu\) and the old value does not change. Thus comparing the gain alone and the reported bit alone separately can cost information. The statements with Alice sending follow by interchanging the observers.

For any odd-minus sign pattern, up to an overall sign there is a single exceptional CHSH cell. Pair it with either its horizontal or its vertical neighbor. The other two correlations each have absolute value at most one, so the oriented magnitude is at most two plus the absolute difference between the paired neighbors. Put \(S_{\max}\) for the largest oriented CHSH value and \(z_+=\max(z,0)\). The best complete-record policy TVs in the two directions satisfy

\[
(S_{\max}-2)_+\le 2\min(\varepsilon_{B\to A},\varepsilon_{A\to B}).
\]

If every fixed address preserves the far gain probability at a fixed own address, the factor **2 becomes 1**. Both bounds are attained in §4. They apply regardless of how the ordinary process produced the flag. In particular a future check that the earlier conditional correlators \((1,1,1,3/4)\) persist at a quantitatively concealed-readiness preparation already guarantees a message of TV \(1/4\) in **each** direction at own address one, provided the fixed-address gain probabilities also agree. A complete post-record may offer an even stronger decoder.

## 4. Sharpness and the importance of which output is known before the edit

**Without fixed-address privacy.** Let the two old marks always disagree and let funding succeed just at \((1,1)\). The correlators are \((1,1,1,-1)\), while each best complete-record signal has TV one. Mixing this preparation with a permanently stalled one with common weights \(q,1-q\) gives excess \(2q\) and TV \(q\) in each direction if the mixing label is concealed. The table is a sharp probability calibration for the coefficient two.

**With fixed-address privacy.** Conceal independent fair readiness labels \(s,t\). Prepare \(u=r, v=r\oplus st\) from an independent fair bit \(r\); only one's own old mark is available to each controller. Let \(H=\mathbf1\{x=s,y=t\}\). This is a supplied probability model. Each fixed input gives gain probability \(1/4\), with the gain independent of either single old mark. Even including both the mark and the flag, the fixed-address records are non-signalling. The correlators are \((1,1,1,1/2)\) and \(S_{\max}=5/2\). At Alice's address zero, both horizontal conditional signed differences vanish. At address one the matrix \(d_{uv}\), in bit order zero, one, has diagonal entries \(-1/8\) and off-diagonal entries \(+1/8\). Choosing opposite sender signs on the two marks makes each row contribute \(1/4\) to flag-and-mark TV. No receiver policy can increase this because it may only use the nonzero row comparison at address one. The best TV is \(1/2\) in either direction, exactly the Bell excess. In the attaining test the gain alone has TV zero and the reported bit has TV \(1/4\). The full old-mark-plus-gain decoder attains the larger value.

**A post-only quantum calibration.** Keep the fair hidden labels and matched funding, and take both controllers' pre-records to be constant. Prepare private bits \(u,v\) jointly with \(s,t\), with fair single-bit marginals and conditional correlations \(q_{st}=(1,1/2,1/2,-1/2)\). Explicitly, the conditional chance of each pair is \((1+(-1)^{u+v}q_{st})/4\). Only after the inputs, on a gain, reveal one's own payload bit. A stall yields zero on both sides. Each full record consists of own action, gain, and its reported bit. All four complete tables are the mixture of a common stall of weight \(3/4\) and the indicated \(q_{xy}\) table of weight \(1/4\) with a common visible gain. Every policy on the declared prior information is non-signalling. The unconditional correlators are \((1,7/8,7/8,5/8)\), and the score is **17/8**. All trials count.

These complete tables have a quantum realization. Share a classical bit which announces a gain with probability \(1/4\). In that branch use \(\lvert\Phi^+\rangle=(\lvert00\rangle+\lvert11\rangle)/\sqrt2\), with \(A_0=B_0=Z\), \(A_1=(Z+\sqrt3 X)/2\), \(B_1=(Z-\sqrt3 X)/2\). The state has unbiased local measurements, \(\langle ZZ\rangle=\langle XX\rangle=1\), and the two mixed-axis expectations vanish. This yields the four specified correlations. In the remaining branch give the common stall flag and two zero outputs. The constructed hidden source assigns its conditional payload tables before the external choices. Neither this assignment nor this timing of the reveal has been derived from ordinary growth. Giving the payloads to the controllers before the edit enables the old-mark message. Adding a sender action which *forces* a stall reveals a message even with empty pre-records: compare a compulsory address with that action, giving gain-probability TV \(1/4\) at a fixed receiver address. Whether omitting an edit forces a stall must be verified on each ordinary source.

## 5. Address-dependent binary readings

For a wider class, suppose the two response signs are \(A_x^h(i),B_y^h(j)\), arbitrary functions of the local pre-record, own address and flag value \(h\). We give a dimension-independent upper estimate. Write \(b=\varepsilon_{B\to A}\), \(a=\varepsilon_{A\to B}\), and use \(\mathbb E\) for the joint pre-record weighting. For any pair of local sign functions \(s(i),t(j)\),

\[
|\mathbb E[s(i)t(j)(h_{x1}-h_{x0})]|\le b.
\]

Bob chooses two complementary addresses in the order specified by \(t\), and Alice tests the gain or its complement as specified by \(s\). Interchanging parties gives the vertical bound \(a\). Allowing zero values in a test follows by local randomization or omitting the affected observations from its decoder event.

For each Alice address put \(q_x=(h_{x0}+h_{x1})/2\) at each \((i,j)\). Temporarily replace the four gain chances by \(q_x\) at both Bob addresses. Each resulting correlation changes by at most \(b\): the difference is half a horizontal gain difference multiplied in turn by \(A_x^1 B_y^1\) and by \(-A_x^0B_y^0\), each of which factors into two local signs. Thus the signed CHSH value changes by at most \(4b\). For each \(j,h\), define \(r_h(j)=0\) when \(B_0^h=B_1^h\) and 1 when they differ. At that fixed input only the CHSH parenthesis in Alice's row \(r_h\) is nonzero. Its magnitude is two. The temporarily modified signed score is therefore bounded above by \(2+2\mathbb E[q_{r_1(j)}-q_{r_0(j)}]\). Under fixed-address privacy of the Bob record \((J,H)\), each vertical gain difference has mean zero within every \(J=j\). So the last expectation vanishes. Without that privacy it is at most \(a\) in absolute value, by the vertical test just established applied to a sign-or-zero function of \(j\) and to both vertical differences in \(q_0-q_1\). Relabelling local response signs gives every odd-minus orientation. Interchanging the sides gives the other bound. In particular,

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 S_{\max}-2\le\min(4b+2a,4a+2b) & \text{for these unrestricted local response functions},\\
 S_{\max}-2\le4\min(a,b) & \text{under fixed-address privacy of }(I,H)\text{ and }(J,H).
\end{array}
\]

The estimates concern responses that are locally known as functions of \(h\) and their old record. Independent local coins can be averaged over pre-sampled local responses. The proof gives upper bounds, without establishing their optimal coefficients. The sharper old-bit bounds in §3 use the same marked distinction at both addresses. For example, on a hidden fair selector, let \(H=x\) on one half and \(H=y\) otherwise. This is a mixture of two product funding rules, each with its other participant always ready. Both old observations are constant. At \(H=0\) all response signs are +1. At \(H=1\) take Alice's signs \((+1,-1)\) and Bob's \((-1,+1)\). Then the four correlations are \((1,1,1,-1)\). Either party's largest policy TV is \(1/2\). This address-dependent reader would violate the first bound in §3 if its mark-independence assumption were dropped.

## 6. Ordinary growth on two persistent live components

This consequence was derived after the freeze. Consider a finite connected ordinary presentation, selected with an arbitrary setting-independent weighting of types and old allowed states. Each participant may do nothing or append private distinctions to its own already-live holders. For each prepared type suppose the edit sites and all births observed by the respective parties belong to two disjoint collections of witnessed live components, each containing at least one live marked holder. The live roster and witnessed adjacencies persist under all permitted additions. The global funding flag is visible at both sites. Condition on the full old presentation and its assignment whenever a local update needs an old value. This latent conditioning is for constructing potential answers; it grants the controllers no extra information.

Use per-parent allocation of its carried old names fixed across other components' edits. Private additions use the local state-preserving lift from the protocol, and each edited state divides its weight equally among its own growth extensions. Each post-record may include every old input, fresh value and ownership newly disclosed by a local birth. Its other incidence readings and names must be determined by the old case and its *own* edit and local births. Names can be transported by their parent and place in a birth. An externally assigned global birth number, a reading of all new holdings at a far holder, or a jointly selected local edit value would give additional inputs outside these premises.

The ordinary group roster is unchanged by the additions. Funding with both empty is pointwise at most funding with any permitted pair. If no far policy changes either observer's flag probability, all these expectations equal the empty/empty probability. A nonnegative difference of mean zero vanishes on every case of positive weight. The menus and pre-observations are finite, so the same probability-one event works for all policy pairs. This argument also covers locally varying feasible menus by extending each feasible action to a policy with the empty action as its default. Thus a single flag \(F\), fixed by the old case, gives the actual decision at every choice. [Optional-edit calculation](../../../research/LOCAL-EDIT-POLICIES-20261123-001/optional_edits.md)

When \(F=0\), after the local edit every proposal stalls. When \(F=1\), every loose distinction is used once, in a group birth or its own remaining unary birth. Thus at least one birth belongs to *each* of the two live components. No group crosses components since every two of its parents are adjacent through a common witness. The ordinary connected parity exception requires a sole birth consuming every loose distinction. Two births in different components exclude it everywhere on these cases. Unsigned not-all-equal has no implied nonempty birth under the registered arity and occurrence bounds. For each birth the number of fresh distinctions is consequently the ordinary nonimplied count \(\max(D,3-p,1)\) where \(p\) is its parent count. The next demand \(D\) is determined inside the same component: the provisional children are witnessed by old parents, and two child parent sets intersect only inside a single old component. Thus all group rosters, old inputs, future demand and fresh counts relevant to a party follow from the fully specified old case and that party's edit. [Ordinary group, demand and closing rules](../../../research/ORDINARY-DYNAMICS-20261120-001/end_operator.md)

Given one edited old assignment, the allowed fresh tuples form a Cartesian product over births. Their new distinctions have precisely one holder at installation and an old input retains its assigned value wherever it occurs. Uniform sampling within this product makes the fresh choices independent between births conditional on the complete edited old state. One can pre-sample all local lifts and fresh draws needed at every own action. The two observers can now be assigned complete potential records using the common old case, its fixed funding decision and these draws. An old input first disclosed by a new child is already in the common old case. It may be unknown to either controller and correlated across components. The construction reproduces those correlations too. All four binary readings of the allowed post-records obey CHSH, for either ordinary knot law. This extends the old-visible-mark obstruction to *newly disclosed* values in this optional-edit class. A free choice of the compulsory address alone, an edit that changes the live roster, or a final-state weighting that changes the old source does not supply the proof.

**A connected check with hidden old inputs.** Use a spent witness knot on each side and let the two witnesses share one distinction \(z\). Each witness meets two live ternary parents through two distinctions per parent. Each parent also has a single loose distinction. Label those loose distinctions \(u_A,v_A\) and \(u_B,v_B\). Each local witness sees the pair of its own live parents. The only two initial groups are those two pairs. At either side the observer initially reads its \(u\)-parent and may add one private distinction there. Allocate the original \(u\) to the pair group. The pair birth takes \(u,v\) and has one fresh distinction at either address. An addition makes one further unary birth with two fresh distinctions. Every cell funds.

In parity, summing the three old equations on either side gives \(u_A\oplus v_A=z=u_B\oplus v_B\). Hence the two separate pair births acquire *identical* fresh bits. Even the joint record of the two observed parents admits either value of \(z\). Both readers learn that bit, and the previously unobserved \(v\), from the newborn holding. This perfect correlation survives ordinary local simulation by the shared old case. For both knot laws the checker verifies the projections of every old support, the target and optional births and their fresh counts, and the full product of allowed new words given old inputs. No global enumeration number is part of the observed child names.

## Evidence and scope

The [standalone checker](check.py) builds finite records directly, compares conditional equalities with enumeration of policies, tests the old-mark message formulas and the two sharpness examples, and checks the post-only control and its changed information and action menus. It does not assume access to a joint pre-record, infer non-signalling from the reported bit alone, or substitute a uniform weighting after a context-dependent update. Validation of the new physical incidence, newly exposed inputs, and numeric optimum for each specified accessible region is in the experiment families. Independent referee pending.
