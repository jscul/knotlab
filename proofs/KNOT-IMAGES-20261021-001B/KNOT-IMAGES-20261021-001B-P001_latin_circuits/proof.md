# Latin circuits, Reidemeister, and the 3-nets that compose like cycles

Status: Proved

Ticket `KNOT-IMAGES-20261021-001B`, 2026-10-21. It uses:
- the definitions of `KNOT-CIRCUITS-20261020-001B-P001` ("the circuits proof"),
  including its Theorem 1;
- `CYCLE-TOPS-20261021-001B-P001` ("the tops proof"): Lemma 1 (reflexive
  relations lie below the top), Theorem 2 (the top lemma) and Corollary 4 (knot
  top images are functional or overlapping);
- the census `KNOT-IMAGES-20261021-001A-E001` ("A").

## Standard statement

**Laws with sorts.** Laws here may be multi-sorted.
- Each relation has a signature of sorts, and a variable fills only positions
  of its own sort.
- A single-sorted encoding takes the disjoint union of the sorts as one
  domain. Triples that mix sorts never meet a relation, so a polymorphism may do
  anything on them.
- Ranges, tops, circuits and composing like cycles are as in the tops proof.
- **The tops proof applies.** A system of the encoding that puts one variable
  in two sorts is empty, so it has no circuits. The systems used in the tops
  proof keep each variable in one sort. So its Lemma 1, top lemma and
  Corollary 4 hold for multi-sorted laws, with ranges inside sorts.

**Functional modulo an equivalence.** Let C be a circuit of a system and e ∈ C.
Then C is **functional modulo θ at e** when every fibre of C's projection over
the other variables lies inside one θ-class of e's range. At the top level
(θ = the top) this is the tops proof's "functional" case.
- **A Latin hypercube** is a circuit that is functional at every variable.
- **Overlapping** is the other case of the tops proof's Corollary 4: every two
  top classes share a fibre.

**3-nets.** A Latin square (Q, ∘) gives the **3-net law**: three sorts X, Y, Z,
each a copy of Q, and the one relation {(x, y, x ∘ y)}.

**This proof shows the following.**
1. **Latin circuits compose like cycles** (Theorem 1). Suppose each range
   carries a proper pp-definable equivalence, modulo which every circuit of
   every system is functional at each of its variables. Then the law composes
   like cycles.
   - So a law composes like cycles with no overlapping circuit exactly when
     every circuit is a Latin hypercube modulo the tops (Corollary 2).
   - The only open part of the characterization is whether overlapping
     circuits can occur. The **Latin conjecture** says they cannot.
2. **Reidemeister** (Theorem 3). For a Latin square, the relation among the
   four entries of a 2×2 subsquare is functional exactly when Reidemeister's
   closure condition holds, that is, exactly for group isotopes. So a non-group
   square's 3-net has a circuit that is functional at no variable.
3. **Abelian 3-nets** (Theorem 4). The 3-net law of a finite abelian group
   composes like cycles exactly when the group is a p-group. Its systems then
   carry GF(p)-representable matroids.
4. **Groups generated by small elements fail** (Proposition 5). Let some m
   make the elements with uᵐ = 1 generate the group without exhausting it.
   Then the group's 3-net law does not compose like cycles. This covers S₃, D₄
   and Z₆, but not Q₈.
5. **Q₈ separates the two conjectures** (Proposition 6). No ternary polymorphism
   of the 3-net law of Q₈ is a Mal'tsev operation modulo ±1, the census-certified
   top. Census A found no failure, and every knot top image functional.
   - So either that law fails in systems larger than any sampled, or the tops
     proof's Conjecture 7 (Mal'tsev on the tops of knot ranges) is false.
   - In the second case the Latin conjecture would survive: composing like
     cycles would mean Latin circuits modulo the tops, without a Mal'tsev
     operation there.

## Nearest known results

- **Closure conditions in 3-webs.**
  - Reidemeister's condition characterizes the quasigroups isotopic to groups.
  - Thomsen's condition characterizes those isotopic to abelian groups.
  - Both are classical; see the [Encyclopedia of Mathematics entry on closure
    conditions](https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Closure_condition).
- **Zaslavsky's biased expansions.** [Associativity in multary quasigroups: the
  way of biased expansions](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0411268) shows that a
  biased expansion of a 3-connected graph is a group expansion.
  - There, every circle's balanced lifts form a multiary quasigroup, which is
    our Latin case.
  - The census shows why our setting differs. With distinct labels, a non-group
    square's 3-net still gives the graphic matroid on K₃,₃, and it fails only
    once labels are shared.
- **Fully invariant subgroups.** These are the subgroups mapped into themselves
  by every endomorphism; for finite abelian p-groups they are classical
  (Baer). The argument used here is the short one through G/pG.

**Classification: new to the program; elementary.**
- Theorem 1 is short. It turns the dichotomy of the tops proof into an
  equivalence, up to the Latin conjecture.
- Theorem 3 is classical closure-condition theory restated for circuits.
- Theorem 4 combines the circuits proof's Theorem 1 with a description of the
  3-net's polymorphisms. I found it nowhere, but it is the kind of statement an
  expert in Mal'tsev constraints would derive quickly.

## Theorem 1 (Latin circuits compose like cycles)

Let Γ be a law. Suppose each range B with |B| ≥ 2 carries a proper
pp-definable equivalence θ_B. Suppose also that in every system, every circuit
is functional modulo θ at each of its variables. Then Γ composes like cycles.

*Proof.* Let C₁ ≠ C₂ be circuits of a system S sharing a variable e.
- Put A = C₁ − e, B = C₂ − e and I = A ∩ B. Neither circuit contains the other,
  so A − I and B − I are nonempty.
1. **The shared part reaches every class.** I ∪ {e} is a proper subset of C₁,
   so it is free.
   - So for every value i on I and every value t of e, some value a′ on A − I
     has (a′, i, t) in C₁'s projection.
   - By functionality, the fibre of (a′, i) lies in one θ-class, which is then
     the class of t.
   - Hence, for each fixed i, the class map a′ ↦ class of the fibre of (a′, i)
     reaches every θ-class. The same holds for C₂.
2. **Disjoint fibres.** θ is proper, so there are two classes c₁ ≠ c₂. Fix i,
   choose a′ with class c₁ for C₁, and choose b′ with class c₂ for C₂.
3. **The excluded value.** Suppose (a′, i, b′) extended to a solution s. Then
   s_e would lie in a fibre of class c₁ and in one of class c₂, which is
   impossible.
   - Yet each coordinate of (a′, i, b′) lies in its variable's range.
   - So (C₁ ∪ C₂) − e = A ∪ B is constrained. ∎

The equivalence must be **common**: one θ_B per range, for all circuits
through a variable with that range. Over Z₆, the circuits are functional
modulo x ≡ y (mod 2) or modulo x ≡ y (mod 3), but no single proper equivalence
serves both, and elimination fails (the checks' control).

**Corollary 2 (the characterization).** For a law Γ the following are
equivalent:
- (i) Γ composes like cycles and no circuit is overlapping modulo the tops;
- (ii) some proper pp-definable equivalences on the ranges make every circuit
  functional at each variable;
- (iii) Γ composes like cycles and every circuit's top image is a Latin
  hypercube.

*Proof.*
- **(ii) ⇒ (i).** Theorem 1 gives composition like cycles, so the tops exist.
  Each θ_B is a proper pp-definable equivalence, so θ_B ⊆ μ_B. Fibres inside
  θ-classes lie inside top classes, so every circuit is functional modulo the
  tops, hence not overlapping.
- **(i) ⇒ (iii) ⇒ (ii).** By the tops proof's Corollary 4, a circuit that does
  not overlap at a variable is functional there. So (i) gives (iii), and (iii)
  gives (ii) with θ = μ. ∎

**The Latin conjecture.** Every law that composes like cycles satisfies (iii).
- **Equivalently,** composing like cycles means exactly that circuits are Latin
  hypercubes of their tops.
- **It is the top-level form of the local converse.** That converse is false
  below the tops (K₄ is not rectangular).
- **Evidence.**
  - Census A: every surviving law had only functional knot images, and every
    overlapping candidate failed.
  - Scoping: the equality indicator and e = x + yH fail in systems of six to
    eight variables.

## Theorem 3 (Reidemeister)

Let (Q, ∘) be a Latin square of order n. Let

  P = {(x₁∘y₁, x₁∘y₂, x₂∘y₁, x₂∘y₂) : x₁, x₂, y₁, y₂ ∈ Q},

the entries of a 2×2 subsquare. Every three coordinates of P are free. The
following are equivalent:
- (a) P is functional at its last coordinate;
- (b) Reidemeister's condition: x₁∘y₁ = x₂∘y₂, x₁∘y₃ = x₂∘y₄ and
  x₃∘y₁ = x₄∘y₂ imply x₃∘y₃ = x₄∘y₄;
- (c) (Q, ∘) is isotopic to a group;
- (d) P is functional at every coordinate.

*Proof.*
- **Three coordinates are free.** Given the first three entries, choose x₁
  freely, then solve for y₁, y₂ and x₂.
- **(a) ⇔ (b).** (a) says that two quadruples with the same first three entries
  have the same fourth. Name the second quadruple's variables x₂, y₂, x₄, y₄
  and the first's x₁, y₁, x₃, y₃. This is (b) word for word.
- **(b) ⇔ (c).** This is the classical theorem on the Reidemeister closure
  condition (the reference above).
- **(c) ⇒ (d).** Write x ∘ y = γ(α(x)β(y)) for a group and bijections α, β, γ.
  Then P's entries satisfy γ⁻¹(z₂₂) = γ⁻¹(z₂₁) γ⁻¹(z₁₁)⁻¹ γ⁻¹(z₁₂). Each entry
  is determined by the other three.
- **(d) ⇒ (a)** is immediate. ∎

**Corollary (non-group 3-nets).** If (Q, ∘) is not a group isotope, P is
functional at none of its coordinates. In its 3-net law, P is the projection
onto the labels of the 4-cycle system with hidden vertices. So P is either
free or a circuit that is not Latin.
- **In the order-5 non-group class (census A).** P is a circuit that overlaps
  at every coordinate.
- **If the 3-net composed like cycles.** Its top quotient would carry P's top
  image. Under the Latin conjecture, that image is functional, so the quotient
  quasigroup would be a group isotope.
- **What census A found.** Every non-group square tested fails or collapses.

## Theorem 4 (abelian 3-nets)

Let G be a finite abelian group with |G| ≥ 2. Its 3-net law composes like
cycles exactly when G is a p-group. Then every system carries a
GF(p)-representable matroid.

*Proof.*
1. **Polymorphisms.** Let (f_X, f_Y, f_Z) be an n-ary polymorphism, one
   operation per sort.
   - It preserves the relation exactly when f_Z(x̄ + ȳ) = f_X(x̄) + f_Y(ȳ) for
     all x̄, ȳ ∈ Gⁿ.
   - Put ȳ = 0̄, then x̄ = 0̄. The map g(w̄) = f_Z(w̄) − f_Z(0̄) is then
     additive on Gⁿ, so g(w̄) = Σ aᵢ(wᵢ) with aᵢ ∈ End(G).
   - Hence f_X(x̄) = Σ aᵢ xᵢ + c_X, and likewise for Y and Z, with
     c_Z = c_X + c_Y.
   - Conversely, every such triple is a polymorphism.
   - So on each sort the operations are exactly the End(G)-affine maps with
     constants.
2. **A difference operation and the ranges.**
   - x − y + z is an operation on each sort; on mixed triples any Mal'tsev
     choice will do. So the law has a Mal'tsev polymorphism, and the circuits
     proof's Theorem 1 applies: it composes like cycles exactly when every
     subalgebra with two or more elements has a unique maximal congruence.
   - **The ranges.** The constant maps are polymorphisms. So a nonempty range
     inside a sort is the whole sort.
   - **The range of an unconstrained variable.** It is the whole union. Its
     congruences relating two distinct values are total, because the binary
     polymorphisms that are first projections on each sort may take any values
     on mixed pairs (the argument of the tops proof's Proposition 5). Its
     unique maximal congruence is equality.
3. **Congruences of a sort.** A congruence of an affine algebra with all
   constants is x − y ∈ H for a subgroup H. Invariance under the unary maps
   x ↦ ax makes H fully invariant, and every fully invariant H works. So the
   law composes like cycles exactly when G has a unique maximal proper fully
   invariant subgroup.
4. **That happens exactly for p-groups.**
   - **Two primes.** If two primes divide |G|, then G = P ⊕ P′ with both parts
     nontrivial. End(G) = End(P) × End(P′), so the fully invariant subgroups
     split. M ⊕ P′ and P ⊕ M′ are two distinct maximal ones.
   - **One prime: every maximal one contains pG.** Let G be a p-group and M a
     maximal proper fully invariant subgroup. Then M + pG is fully invariant.
     If it were G, then G = M + p^k G = M for large k. So pG ⊆ M.
   - **The maximal ones correspond to invariant subspaces.** Write
     G = ⊕_a (Z_{p^a})^{m_a}. End(G) acts on V = G/pG = ⊕_a V_a as follows:
     - by all matrices on each V_a;
     - by all maps V_a → V_b for a > b, since x ↦ x is a homomorphism
       Z_{p^a} → Z_{p^b};
     - by zero for a < b, since homomorphisms raise divisibility.
   - **Uniqueness.** So the invariant subspaces are 0 and ⊕_{b ≤ c} V_b. The
     largest proper one omits the top exponent and is unique.
5. **Matroids.**
   - By the tops proof's top lemma, each system has the free sets of its top
     image. The top is G/M ≅ F_p^m, with End(G) acting on it as all of
     M_m(F_p).
   - A top image is a coset of an M_m(F_p)-invariant subgroup of
     (F_p^m)^V = F_p^m ⊗ F_p^V. Such a subgroup is F_p^m ⊗ U for a subspace U.
   - Its free sets are those of U, an F_p-linear code. ∎

Census A agrees on every abelian group it ran.
- **The p-groups survive.** Z₂, Z₃, Z₄, Z₂×Z₂, Z₅, Z₇, Z₈ and Z₂×Z₄ had no
  failure.
- **Their tops** are the cosets of the predicted maximal fully invariant
  subgroup:
  - 2Z₄ for Z₄;
  - 2Z₈ for Z₈;
  - Z₂ × 2Z₄ for Z₂×Z₄;
  - zero for the elementary groups.
- **Z₆ collapses.**

## Proposition 5 (groups generated by small elements)

For a finite group G and m ≥ 1, let Ω(m) = {u : u^m = 1}. If Ω(m) generates G
but Ω(m) ≠ G, then the 3-net law of G does not compose like cycles. The same
holds when Ω(m₁) and Ω(m₂) are both proper and together generate G.

*Proof.*
1. **The m-cycle gadget.** Take x₀, x₁ in X, y₀, …, y_{m−1} in Y, and labels
   z₀, …, z_{m−1} in Z. Impose x₀∘yᵢ = zᵢ = x₁∘y_{i+1}, with indices mod m.
2. **It defines ρ_m.** Its projection onto (x₀, x₁) is
   ρ_m = {(x, xu) : u ∈ Ω(m)}.
   - **Every pair has this form.** u = x₀⁻¹x₁ equals every yᵢ y_{i+1}⁻¹, and
     the product of these telescopes to 1, so u^m = 1.
   - **Every such pair occurs.** Conversely put y_{i+1} = u⁻¹yᵢ.
3. **The contradiction.**
   - ρ_m contains the diagonal, and it is proper because Ω(m) ≠ G. Its
     transitive closure is the cosets of the subgroup generated by Ω(m), which
     is the total relation.
   - By the tops proof's Lemma 1 this is impossible in a law that composes like
     cycles.
   - With two values of m, the join of the two relations is total, which is
     again impossible. ∎

**Instances.**
- S₃ and D₄ are generated by involutions, with m = 2.
- In Z₆, Ω(2) and Ω(3) are proper and generate Z₆. This is an independent proof
  of Theorem 4's Z₆ case.
- In Q₈, Ω(2) = {±1} is normal and proper, and Ω(4) = Q₈. The obstruction does
  not apply, and census A's Q₈ 3-net survived, with top Q₈/{±1} ≅ Z₂ × Z₂.
- So a non-abelian group can have a 3-net law that composes like cycles, as far
  as the census sees.

## Proposition 6 (Q₈: Latin tops without a Mal'tsev operation)

The 3-net law of Q₈ has no ternary polymorphism that is a Mal'tsev operation
modulo ±1 on its three sorts.

*Proof.*
1. **Every polymorphism acts on the classes mod ±1.** x′ ≡ x (mod ±1) is the
   m = 2 relation of Proposition 5, since Ω(2) = {±1}. So every polymorphism F
   preserves it and induces an operation F̄ on Q₈/{±1} ≅ Z₂ × Z₂ on each sort.
2. **F̄ is x + y + z.** F̄ preserves the image of the relation,
   {(ā, b̄, ā + b̄)}, which is the 3-net of Z₂ × Z₂.
   - By step 1 of Theorem 4, F̄ is End(Z₂²)-affine on each sort.
   - The Mal'tsev identities then force F̄ = x + y + z on each sort.
3. **The lifts reduce to signs.** Write Q₈ as pairs (sign, class) with
   multiplication (s, a)(t, b) = (s + t + f(a, b), a + b), where
   f(a, b) = a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + a₂b₁.
   - Any lift has the form F_X(a, b, c) = a b⁻¹ c · (−1)^{s_X(a,b,c)}, and
     likewise on Y and Z.
   - Preserving the relation means F_Z(x̄ ȳ) = F_X(x̄) F_Y(ȳ). Over F₂ that is
     s_X(x̄) + s_Y(ȳ) + s_Z(x̄ȳ) = β(x̄, ȳ), where β records the sign by which
     (x₁x₂⁻¹x₃)(y₁y₂⁻¹y₃) differs from z₁z₂⁻¹z₃.
4. **No lift exists.** Some 416 of these equations together contain every
   unknown an even number of times, and their β sum to 1. ∎

**What it means.**
- **If the law composes like cycles.** Its tops on the three sorts are mod ±1,
  certified in census A, and its knot ranges are the sorts. It would then be a
  law that composes like cycles but is not Mal'tsev on its knot tops, which
  refutes Conjecture 7 of the tops proof.
- **Why small systems look affine.** By the Galois correspondence between
  polymorphisms of the top law and its relations, some top image of the law is
  not closed under x + y + z. The certificate shows such a relation needs a
  large system.
- **The evidence so far.**
  - Census A found no failure or collapse in 304 systems, and every one of its
    1,341 knot top images was functional.
  - In scoping (not evidence), there was no failure or Lemma-1 violation in
    about 3,700 further systems of up to 12 variables, and the 1,412 top images
    small enough to test were all affine.

## What this does not show

- **The Latin conjecture** is open. No law that composes like cycles with an
  overlapping circuit was found.
- **Non-group Latin squares.** Theorem 3 shows that their 4-cycle circuits are
  not Latin. Whether they always fail is open, though every one tested did.
- **Non-abelian groups.** The exact class whose 3-net laws compose like cycles
  is not determined. Q₈ is a candidate member and D₄ is not.
- **Q₈.** Whether its 3-net law composes like cycles is open. That question now
  decides Conjecture 7 of the tops proof.
- **No protected structure.** No native recurrence, composed topology in the
  protected sense, path or distance is claimed.
