# A reachable matching that fails after one complete round

**A bond-distance renaming can fix the entire growing end and fail to extend.** Six connected parity junctions grow to ten, then fourteen, by the complete-round cheapest-count law. At the ten-junction stage, exchanging distinctions 0 and 2 preserves all 210 current bond distances. It fixes the full support of every live junction pointwise. After the next round it would have to carry distance two to distance three on distinctions that had been shared since the start. Every choice of fresh names and every allowed re-pairing of loose distinctions leaves those old readings fixed. **Scope.** This resolves the universal claim about continuing a specified matching on positive-age stages. The two histories are the same lawful history, compared by a nontrivial renaming. The new junctions attach in corresponding places. The identity always carries this history forward. Whether two distinct reached arrangements can match on the table and growing end yet admit no matching of their successor tables is a separate question. Some junctions in this start share two distinctions. The case restricted to starts with at most one meeting per pair remains open. The construction also gives an example for every starting junction count of at least six by adding exhausted junctions. Six is a verified size. Its global minimality is untested. - The proposal was tested beyond the earlier at-most-five-knot census and the three- and four-knot multiplicity hunt. Peeling recovers this six-knot start uniquely, and two of its live knots already meet. This history cannot reappear in that earlier census at a greater age. A separately designed seven-junction start realizes the same loss through a three-parent join.
