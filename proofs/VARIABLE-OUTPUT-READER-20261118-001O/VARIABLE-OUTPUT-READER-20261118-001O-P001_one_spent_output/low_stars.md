# What survives at arbitrary width when at most one join shrinks

**Theorem.** Consider the one-spent source defined in the [frozen question](../../../research/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001O/preregistration.md). If no join takes three parents and makes just two outputs, bond distances through five identify every actual junction and every three-parent choice under every bijection to any reachable mixed-law presentation. This cutoff is sharp on the live sources. The same full recovery holds when the *only* three-parent, two-output join is the child spending the fourth junction's output, provided there is just one six-place junction. Arbitrarily many later two-parent joins can use the child's outputs or link to their descendants.

There is a further result with the single shrinking join anywhere. With just one six-place site, the same observations identify all other physical junctions and the two loose distinctions at the six-place site. They reduce its four remaining attachments to at most 330 candidate sets, independently of width. Distinct candidates for an exact match must overlap on one of the four attachments. Any shared attachment in a genuinely different completion must belong to a six-bit boundary around at least three ordinary sites. This last reduction alone does not certify that all candidate menus agree.

## Reading the smaller junctions

Let \(K=D+1\) denote the least cardinality of a bond containing a pair. Use the ordinary incidence representation in which every loose distinction meets a single exterior when cuts are measured. A cut with the ordinary sites in \(U\) has support \(\partial U\), the sum of their parity equations. Each actual site is a bond, the sites form an independent basis, and joins use at most one bit at each parent. The ordinary graph is connected, simple and bipartite.

A set of at least three ordinary sites starts in chronological order with a boundary of size at least seven. Their degrees sum to at least eleven, with at most two internal meetings. A later site with \(p\) inputs, \(h\) outputs and \(j\) parents already selected changes this size by \(h-p+2(p-j)\). With at most one shrinking site, every such boundary has size at least six. The five-bit theorem therefore forces every source site of size at most five as a rival equation. It also makes every rival physical site of size at most five an actual source site. In this reverse assertion a seed-plus-four sum cannot serve as a rival site because it repeats two bits of the common seed. [Five-bit transfer in both directions](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001J-P001_seven_to_six/inverse.md#five-bit-equations-at-the-first-shrink)

Suppose the sole six-place site is \(H\). Write \(F=\{a,b\}\) for the two outputs still loose there. All other \(n-1\) source equations are forced and vanish on \(F\). A rival has at most \(n\) independent sites by the four-birth rank bound through distance four. Some rival equation uses each coordinate, so the rank is exactly \(n\), and the rival also has exactly four three-output additions. Restriction of its equation space to \(F\) has one-dimensional image generated by \(11\). Each site containing one bit of \(F\) therefore contains both. Distinct ordinary sites cannot share both. The rival consequently holds both loose at one site. Their pair has \(K=6\) in the source: a cut containing one puts \(H\) on the shore, and a one- or two-site shore involving \(H\) costs at least six, as does every larger shore. Its own site attains six. The holder in the rival has six places. [Four-birth rank bound](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001N/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001N-P001_fourth_at_the_parents/rank.md#the-rank-bound-from-distance-four)

There cannot be a second six-place rival site. Such a rival would have spent its four three-output births on the two obligatory unary additions and the two six-place sites. Its number of loose bits would be \(7-t'\), where \(t'\) is its total number of shrinking joins. The whole loose frontier is a bond since all ordinary sites form a connected shore. It contains both transported bits of \(F\) and must have size at least six. Thus \(t'\le1\). The five-bit theorem would then apply also with the rival as source, identifying all \(n-1\) small physical sites of the original in the rival. Two six-place sites would leave room for only \(n-2\). Every matched presentation therefore has just one six-place site and precisely the same \(n-1\) small physical stars.

The small sites can be read from the through-five bond sizes (that is, \(D\le4\)) using the short-shore reader on the source. At sizes three to five a short bond is a star or the sum of the seed and an adjacent four-place star. Remove the cross-pairs of such sums when recognizing the stars. The preceding argument certifies against arbitrary rivals the sites recognized by this procedure. The two coordinates appearing in none of them give \(F\), so their identities and the location of the large site were never supplied as input. [Short-shore reader](../../VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001I/VARIABLE-OUTPUT-READER-20261118-001I-P001_cycle_obstruction/nonshrinking.md#exactly-when-three-sites-can-have-a-short-boundary)

## Four potential attachments

Remove \(H\) from the source. Its four used bits each have one remaining holder and form a set \(A\). All those holders are distinct. The ordinary graph of the remaining small sites is connected. All sites before \(H\) have a connected prefix. Every site born after \(H\) has at least one parent other than \(H\), including the child that uses its sole spent output. This also supplies a common coloring on the small sites for all candidate completions, up to exchanging the two colors.

Let \(T\) be the set of bits with exactly one small holder. It consists of the four old attachments and all loose bits outside \(F\). The source's number of loose bits is eight or nine before subtracting its shrinking joins, since the third three-output addition is either binary or unary. Thus \(9\le|T|\le11\) here. Each rival must complete \(F\) with exactly four different members of \(T\), using distinct holders of the same color. There are at most \(\binom{11}{4}=330\) candidate supports. One can test literal reachability of these finite presentations by their possible birth orders and remeasure their tables. The bound concerns the number of candidate supports, without a claim on the time to test each one.

Call an ordinary shore with at least three sites and size-six boundary a large six-shore. With no shrinking site there are none. A short bond containing a loose bit at \(H\) must include \(H\) on its shore. An adjacent pair involving \(H\) has boundary at least seven. With no large six-shore, the closed \(K\le6\) neighborhood of either surviving output is exactly the six bits of \(H\). Hence there is a unique completion in the no-shrink single-high case.

With one shrinking site \(Z\), every large six-shore starts with the seed and two four-place sites and contains \(Z\) later, with all three of its parents inside. All later increments other than its saving of one are zero. Unions of such shores again have boundary six: the intersection contains \(Z\) and its three parents, so the union and intersection each cost at least six, and cut submodularity gives equality.

Write \(A'\) for a proposed rival attachment set. If \(A'\) is disjoint from \(A\), all four members of \(A'\) are loose at small sites in the source. Each must read at most six to \(a\), since the rival puts them on its six-place star. For each member choose an attaining source bond. It must come from a large six-shore containing both \(H\) and that member's small holder. Their union has size-six boundary, which must consist precisely of \(F\cup A'\). This is a source equation. Subtracting \(F\cup A\) in the source gives the eight-bit set \(A\cup A'\) as an equation in the span of the small stars, because both high words have the same nonzero restriction to \(F\). A sum of small stars that uses only bits in \(T\) must be either empty or all of \(T\), by connectedness of the ordinary small-site graph. Since \(|T|\ge9\), this is impossible. Thus every possible rival retains at least one true attachment.

Now suppose \(A'\ne A\). Fix a retained attachment \(i\in A\cap A'\) and a new attachment \(z\in A'\setminus A\). In the source, \(z\) is loose at a small holder. Its holder differs from, and has the same small-graph color as, the small holder of \(i\). Otherwise the rival would take two bits from one ordinary neighbor or violate bipartiteness. The pair \(i,z\) has \(K\le6\) in the rival and therefore in the source. A one-site shore through both is impossible: the low holders differ and \(z\) is loose. A two-site shore through \(H\) would cost at least seven. A two-site bond using just the two small holders would require those same-color holders to meet. Thus some large six-shore must cross \(i\). This proves the promised necessary condition on every retained attachment.

## Spending at the only shrinking join

When \(Z\) is the spending child, every large six-shore contains that child and all its parents, including \(H\). A six-place site cannot be among the initial three of this shore; its higher degree would put their boundary above seven. When \(H\) subsequently occurs its increment must vanish, so all three of its own parents are also inside. The three input attachments of \(H\) and its single attachment to its child are therefore internal to every such shore. None can cross a large six-shore. The preceding necessary condition excludes every different \(A'\). This fixes the large junction, all small sites, their allowed states and all marked permissions. It places no restriction on whether either of the child's two outputs has subsequently been used. The 29-distinction example has used both and still has two legal triples. Finally suppose there are two six-place sites, and no shrinking site. The first two of the four three-output additions are unary and the other two take three parents, leaving no five-place site. The reverse five-bit assertion shows that an unrestricted rival has no five-place site either. It cannot have a shrinking step, each of which would have size five. Both presentations now meet the nonshrinking theorem's hypotheses, since the common seed is the unique three-place site. The through-six reader recovers all ordinary junctions directly, even when every output of the earlier six-place junction has already been used. Together with the single-high case, this proves the nonshrinking theorem stated above. By the independent [formation closure](frontier.md), every live one-spent source with two six-place sites is in fact nonshrinking. Thus the reader covers that entire live source branch. The width-23 opposing mark through \(D=4\) gives the necessary cutoff five for the nonshrinking class as a whole. [Sharpness example](census.md)
